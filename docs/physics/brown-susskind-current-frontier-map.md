# Brown–Susskind · live proof routes

**Exponential plateau onset and the first return from that plateau remain open.** Typical excursions already have doubly exponential waits. At the first robust crossing, Lean gives a doubly exponential upper deadline and an exponential lower wait. Matching linear growth and concentration near the same-error maximum also remain open. [Target](second-law.md) · [Killed routes](brown-susskind-killed-routes.md)

**Model.** Draw independent standard Gaussian onsite and nearest-neighbour Pauli coefficients once on an infinite open chain. On its $K$-qubit prefix, $D=2^K$ and $C_\epsilon(t)$ is the minimum number of arbitrary two-qubit gates, without ancillas, at projective operator-norm error $\epsilon$. A visit uses error $1/10$ unless specified. Write $m=\max_t C_{1/10}(t)$, $P=\lfloor D/[64(K+1)]\rfloor$ and $G=2^{D^c}$ for a deterministic $c$. The first time the orbit reaches distance $9/80$ from the $P$-gate set is $\sigma$. Statements below hold almost surely on sufficiently long prefixes unless specified.

| **Empty-circuit returns and long gaps** | The shortest enclosing eigenphase arc of length $L$ radians gives empty-circuit error $2\sin(L/4)$. For every $K\ge3$, circular spacings give the exact occupation at every arc tolerance $x\in[0,1]$ (error $2\sin(\pi x/2)$). It equals $p_0=Dx^{D-1}$ for $x\le1/2$ and approaches the Gumbel tail $1-e^{-e^{-y}}$ at $x=1-(\log D+y)/D$. For $0<x<1/2$, as resolution $h\downarrow0$ and $jh\to\infty$, mean spacing divided by $h$ tends to $1/p_0$. Gaps longer than $h$ have vanishing count fraction, while gaps longer than any fixed $Mh$, $M\ge1$, carry time share $1-p_0$.
