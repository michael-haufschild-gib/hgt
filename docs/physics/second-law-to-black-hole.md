### How a stationary black hole can have a growing interior

The clean example is an eternal, two-sided black hole in anti-de Sitter space. Its boundary description is two quantum systems in an entangled state called the *thermofield double*. As both boundary clocks advance, the reduced state on either side remains thermal, while the joint state changes through relative phases. Measurements confined to either side therefore see equilibrium, even though the joint state keeps evolving. [doi.org](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.90.126007?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

On the gravity side, fix the two boundary clock readings and find the maximal-volume spatial slice connecting them through the Einstein–Rosen bridge. As both clock readings advance, that slice extends farther along the interior and its volume grows roughly linearly at late classical times. The horizon area can stay constant throughout. The growing quantity is the volume of a particular, geometrically defined slice anchored to the boundary clocks. [doi.org](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.90.126007?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### What would have to be proved

1. **Establish growth for the relevant boundary complexity.** The earlier second-law statement concerned the *operator complexity* of \(e^{-iHt}\). The usual complexity–volume proposal concerns the *state complexity* of the evolving thermofield double. A hard-to-implement unitary can act simply on a particular state. One therefore needs a lower bound on the shortest circuit that prepares the evolving joint state, or a theorem relating that cost to the operator complexity. The reference state, allowed gates, approximation error, and field-theory regulator all need to be specified. [journals.aps.org](https://journals.aps.org/prd/pdf/10.1103/PhysRevD.97.086015?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

2. **Derive a quantitative dictionary between that circuit cost and a bulk quantity.** The *complexity–volume* proposal identifies changes in boundary state complexity with changes in the renormalized maximal-slice volume, in units set by Newton’s constant and a length scale. The competing *complexity–action* proposal assigns complexity to the gravitational action of a spacetime region called the Wheeler–DeWitt patch. Linear growth alone cannot select the dictionary. Even the *switchback effect*, a delay associated with the partial cancellation of forward and backward evolution around a perturbation, is shared by many candidate bulk observables. A derivation, or quantitative predictions that distinguish the candidates with the normalization fixed, would go beyond matching the shape of the growth curve. [doi.org](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.90.126007?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

3. **Extend the dictionary to finite entropy if it is meant to explain the plateau and recurrences.** The classical maximal volume keeps growing in the eternal solution. If the corresponding boundary complexity saturates and occasionally falls, the bulk theory needs a quantum definition of interior size and a calculation of its late-time behavior in the same boundary theory. Two-dimensional gravity models already exhibit an interior-length plateau at a time exponential in the black-hole entropy. Establishing the full correspondence would require matching the late-time statistics, including rare returns, for a specified boundary theory. For **why growth continues after exterior equilibration**, the first two steps are the essential ones. They would identify the growing bridge volume with a specific microscopic cost that keeps increasing even after each side separately looks thermal. The third step would tell us whether that interpretation survives when the classical picture of the interior gives way to quantum gravity.
