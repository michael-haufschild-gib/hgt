Brown and Susskind’s proposed second law of quantum complexity is that the circuit complexity of \(U(t)=e^{-iHt}\), starting from the identity and evolving under a generic chaotic, time-independent \(k\)-local Hamiltonian on \(K\) qubits, grows roughly linearly for exponentially long, fluctuates near an exponential plateau, and makes rare returns to low complexity on doubly exponential timescales. The full trajectory remains conjectural for that fixed-Hamiltonian setting. Several parts have been proved in precisely specified random models.

The unproved second law is a claim about **the minimum number of gates needed to reproduce the evolution of one fixed, generic chaotic local Hamiltonian**. It predicts that this minimum grows roughly linearly for exponentially long, then spends most of its time near an exponential plateau, with rare returns to low complexity on a doubly exponential timescale. A proof has to establish all three claims for the **same Hamiltonian ensemble and the same definition of complexity**. [journals.aps.org](https://journals.aps.org/prd/pdf/10.1103/PhysRevD.97.086015?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### What a theorem would need to establish

First, specify a family of \(k\)-local Hamiltonian ensembles \(H_K\), a normalization of their interaction strengths, a universal gate set, and a fixed approximation error \(\epsilon\). Define \(C_\epsilon(t)\) as the minimum gate count to approximate \(e^{-iH_Kt}\) to error \(\epsilon\), up to global phase. Draw \(H_K\) **once** and hold it fixed. Then prove that the following hold with probability tending to one over that draw as \(K\) grows.

| Claim | What needs to be proved |
|---|---|
| **Linear growth** | Over a specified time window extending to \(t_{\rm sat}=\exp(\Theta(K))\), give matching upper and lower bounds of order \(v_Kt\). The crucial lower bound must rule out **every substantially shorter circuit** throughout the window. Brown and Susskind’s schematic normalization takes \(v_K\) of order \(K\). |
| **An exponential plateau** | Show that \(C_\epsilon(t)\) reaches a level \(C_{\rm plat}=\exp(\Theta(K))\) around \(t_{\rm sat}\) and stays close to that level for a quantified fraction of later times. Specify what “close” means and bound the frequency and size of downward fluctuations. The plateau claim concerns the unitaries this Hamiltonian actually visits. |
| **Rare low-complexity returns** | Choose a low threshold, for example \(L_K=\operatorname{poly}(K)\). After the trajectory reaches the plateau, prove that the first return to \(C_\epsilon(t)\le L_K\) occurs at a time of order \(\exp(\exp(\Theta(K)))\). This requires both a lower bound excluding earlier returns and an upper bound establishing a return. If the claim includes renewed linear growth after a dip, give a quantitative bound for that phase too. |

This is a **candidate theorem statement** for the qualitative law. The original proposal leaves choices such as the Hamiltonian ensemble, the allowed size of plateau fluctuations, and the low-complexity threshold to be made precise. [journals.aps.org](https://journals.aps.org/prd/pdf/10.1103/PhysRevD.97.086015?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The main gap is a **circuit lower bound along a fixed Hamiltonian’s trajectory**. Its eigenvectors stay fixed and only its eigenphases change. Even if the phases explore their torus uniformly, a proof still has to bound how much of that torus lies within \(\epsilon\) of short circuits, and how soon the trajectory can hit those regions. Ordinary quantum recurrence gives eventual returns near the identity. It leaves open how soon the evolution might come close to *any* short circuit, which is what determines the first low-complexity dip. For generic time-independent local Hamiltonians, typical high-temperature product states evolve to states of exponential robust circuit complexity outside a doubly exponentially small limiting fraction of times. The finite-time estimate depends on a high-order spectral gap. The time of first exponential complexity and the growth rate along the way remain open. [Sustained growth of quantum circuit complexity in many-body Hamiltonian dynamics, Theorems 1 and 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.26885v1)

For one infinite iid Gaussian onsite and nearest-neighbour Pauli chain, almost surely on all sufficiently long $K$-qubit prefixes, the minimum gate count is between $cK$ and $J_mK$ throughout $[A/K,K^{-1/m}]$ for every fixed integer $m\ge2$ and at each error in $[1/20,1/4]$. Put $D=2^K$ and $P=\lfloor D/[64(K+1)]\rfloor$. Let $m$ be the orbit maximum at error $1/10$. At this same error the fraction of time below $m^{1/3}$ has upper limit at most $2^{-\lfloor D/1024\rfloor}$. Outside a fraction $2^{1-\lfloor 3D/16\rfloor}$ in every sufficiently long interval, for every budget $s\le P$, the first robust crossing after the preceding $s$-gate visit has a wait longer than $2^{\lfloor D/4\rfloor}$ until the next visit. From the first robust crossing after the identity, the proved lower bound for budgets up to $P/2$ is $2^K/[2^{5\lfloor\sqrt K\rfloor+26}(K+1)^2]$. Establishing doubly exponential waiting from that particular crossing remains open. [Gaussian-chain frontier](brown-susskind-current-frontier-map.md)

For the same chain, every budget $s_K=o(D/K)$, including every polynomial budget, has an exact long-time occupation exponent. At projective operator error $0<\epsilon<\sqrt2$, put $x=2\arcsin(\epsilon/2)/\pi$. Both the upper and lower limiting fractions of time affordable with $s_K$ gates are $x^{D+o(D)}$. Recording the first affordable visit at least one time unit after each previous visit gives mean spacing $x^{-D+o(D)}$, including when observations start at the first robust crossing. Both estimates take the long-observation limit before the qubit count grows.
