# Descent of functional-equation reflection

- **ID:** galois-zero-reflection-descent
- **Stage:** 3-formalized
- **Type:** THEOREM (Lean)
- **Questions:** Integer zero relations; geometric action on the original zero motive.

## Theorem

Let `Δ⊆ℂ` be the integer span of every original zero. Form the augmented
relation group `R⊆ℂ×ℤ` generated by `(ρ,1)`. Its first projection `p:R→Δ`
is onto; its second projection records total coefficient weight.
Let `D⊆ℤ` consist of weights of relations whose complex value is zero.

Reflection `ρ↦1−ρ` descends to an additive automorphism of `Δ` exactly
when `D=0`. Its lift on `R` is unconditional. The quotient `Δ/Dℂ` is
universal among additive quotients supporting reflection. Precisely the
characters annihilating `Dℂ` have reflected characters.

## Paper argument

The map `F(z,n)=(n−z,n)` is an additive involution of `ℂ×ℤ`. It preserves
`R` because functional-equation reflection permutes all zero generators.
For `(0,n)∈ker p`, its reflected complex value is `n`. Thus `F` descends
through `p` exactly when `D=0`. Equivalently, the coefficient weight
descends to an integer homomorphism `d:Δ→ℤ` with `d(ρ)=1`. The descended
reflection is then `τ(z)=d(z)−z`, uniquely, and `d(1)=2`.

For general `D`, every `n∈D` lies in `Δ`, since subtracting `(0,n)` from
its reflection gives `(n,0)∈R`. Let `Dℂ⊆Δ` be this embedded subgroup.
Modulo `Dℂ`, the value of `n−z` depends only on `z`, giving an involution
of `Δ/Dℂ`. Any additive quotient of `Δ` admitting reflection kills `Dℂ`;
this quotient is therefore universal. A character of `Δ` has a reflected
character exactly when it kills `Dℂ`.

For the real-time flow, this condition is `exp(un)=1` for every `n∈D`.
Since the real exponential is injective, it holds exactly when `u=0` or
`D=0`. Thus reflection of even one nonzero real-time character forces
reflection to descend on the entire integral relation group.

## Centered obstruction

Let `Γ` be the centered spectral integer span. The defect vanishes exactly
when `(1/2)ℤ ∩ iΓ = {0}`. A vertical relation `(0,n)` has centered value
`−n/2`, proving one implication. Conversely, if `m/2∈iΓ`, lift it to
`(z,N)∈R` with `z−N/2=m/2`. Subtract `(N+m)` copies of `(1,2)` from
twice this lift. The result is `(0,−2m)∈R`, so `−2m∈D`.

This is an additive obstruction, weaker than the field obstruction `i∈L`:
its absence is equivalent to reflection descent even when products of
centered zeros might generate `i`.

## Boundary

The actual subgroup `D` is not determined. Functional-equation symmetry
of the zero set alone does not imply `D=0`. The field-theoretic reflection
constructed under `i∉L` does not remove this unresolved arithmetic input.
