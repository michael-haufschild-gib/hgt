## Theorem A -- source measure and complete monotonicity are equivalent `[PROVED]`

For a real sequence `b=(b_m)_(m>=0)`, let

```text
(Delta b)_m=b_(m+1)-b_m.
```

The following are equivalent.

1. There is a finite positive Borel measure `nu` on `[0,1]` such that

   ```text
   b_m=integral_[0,1] y^m d nu(y).                          (6)
   ```

2. The sequence is completely monotone:

   ```text
   (-1)^k (Delta^k b)_m >= 0       for every m,k>=0.        (7)
   ```

This is Hausdorff's moment theorem.  The representing measure is unique because polynomials are
dense in `C([0,1])`.

The necessity has the exact identity

```text
(-1)^k (Delta^k b)_m
  = integral_[0,1] y^m (1-y)^k d nu(y).                    (8)
```

Indeed, finite differences pass through the integral and each application replaces `y^n` by
`y^n(y-1)`.  Formula `(8)` also proves strict positivity when `nu` is nonzero and supported in
`(0,1)`:

```text
(-1)^k (Delta^k b)_m > 0       for every m,k>=0.            (9)
```

Combining `(2)` and `(7)` gives direct inequalities on measurable deformation couplings:

```text
(-1)^k Delta^k
  [(-1)^m (2m+1) alpha_(2m+1)/8] >= 0.                     (10)
```

These are linear inequalities at every fixed `(m,k)`.  Hankel positivity is a quadratic shadow
of the same measure; `(10)` is the sharper Hausdorff support certificate for `[0,1]`.

## Theorem B -- one positive measure generates the entire phase germ `[PROVED]`

Let `nu` be a finite positive measure supported in `[0,R^2]` for some `R<1`, and let `b_m` be its
moments.  Define

```text
Sigma_nu(theta)
  = 8 sum_(m>=0) (-1)^m b_m/(2m+1)
      sinh((2m+1)theta).                                   (11)
```

The series converges normally on every smaller strip

```text
|Re(theta)| < -log R.                                      (12)
```

This follows from `b_m <= nu([0,1]) R^(2m)` and a geometric majorant for the hyperbolic sine.
Termwise integration of `(4)` is therefore valid and gives the exact source transform

```text
Sigma_nu(theta)=integral_[0,R^2] K(y,theta) d nu(y),        (13)

K(y,theta)
  = 4/sqrt(y) [arctan(sqrt(y)e^theta)
               - arctan(sqrt(y)e^(-theta))],               (14)
```

where `K(0,theta)=8 sinh(theta)` is the continuous endpoint value.  The arctangent branches in
`(14)` are the analytic branches fixed at `theta=0` inside `(12)`.

The map from measures to local phases is additive:

```text
Sigma_(nu_1+nu_2)=Sigma_(nu_1)+Sigma_(nu_2),               (15)
```

so the corresponding interaction factors multiply:

```text
exp(i Sigma_(nu_1+nu_2))
  = exp(i Sigma_(nu_1)) exp(i Sigma_(nu_2)).                (16)
```

By Hausdorff determinacy, every coefficient in `(1)` is recovered from `nu`, and the entire
measure is recovered from the infinite coefficient vector.  Thus

```text
positive source measure  <->  admissible coupling vector   (17)
```

is lossless, not merely a one-way parametrization.

## Extreme rays and the quantized global subcone `[PROVED]`

The extreme rays of the cone of finite positive measures are point masses.  Under `(13)`, a
point mass

```text
nu=w delta_y
```

is one positive multiple of the elementary phase kernel `K(y,theta)`.  With `y=r^2` and the
physical weight `w=r`, it is exactly the paired CDD shell `(3)`.  Therefore at the level of local
current germs:

```text
point measure           <-> one paired-shell phase ray,
finite atomic measure   <-> finite positive shell-germ sum,
infinite atomic measure <-> infinite discrete shell-germ sum,
non-atomic measure      <-> continuous local phase mixture. (18)
```

A literal global meromorphic CDD product imposes an additional multiplicity condition.  At an
atom `y=r^2`, the ratio `w/r` is the exponent of the elementary paired-shell factor.  A
single-valued meromorphic product is automatic when these exponents are nonnegative integers.
Arbitrary positive weights instead give continuous multiples of the **local** deformation
direction and can create branch points under global continuation.  The infinite quantized case
still requires compact convergence and strip regularity.  For a non-atomic measure, `(13)` proves
a local analytic current germ, not by itself a global regular factorizing S-matrix.  Branch cuts,
boundary growth, crossing, and locality must be audited separately.

For the geometric product set

## What positivity does and does not imply

The cone theorem separates three logically different properties.

### Positivity

Complete monotonicity `(10)` is equivalent to a positive compact source measure.  It is a
constraint on the local deformation vector.

### Global QFT admissibility

A positive coupling measure need not yield a regular global S-matrix.  Strip analyticity,
unitarity, crossing, boundedness, physical-strip singularities, and a locality theorem remain
separate gates.

### Hypertranscendence

Positivity does not imply HT.  One atom and every finite atomic measure give finite CDD products,
which are rational in the crossing coordinate and therefore differentially algebraic.  Even an
infinite measure can have a DA completion.  HT requires an additional global constraint, such as
the nontrivial rational q-difference cocycle plus a nonrational divisor in the geometric model.

Consequently the physical architecture is

```text
positive measure cone
  + global analytic completion
  + nontrivial difference constraint
  = candidate HT elastic law.                              (20)
```

No single line of `(20)` can replace the other two.

## Lean plan for the Hausdorff converse — worked out, ready to type

The card's open Lean gate is the converse of `hausdorffMoment_isCompletelyMonotone`:

```text
HausdorffCompletelyMonotone b  ->  exists nu : Measure HausdorffSourceSpace,
                                     IsFiniteMeasure nu and hausdorffMoment nu = b.
```

Mathlib has no Hausdorff moment theorem.  The classical atomic-approximation proof is fully
elementary and the two identities it needs have been derived; what follows is the exact plan.

### The quadrature weights

```text
hausdorffWeight b N k := (N.choose k) * ((-1)^(N-k) * (fwdDiff 1)^[N-k] b k).
```

Nonnegativity is immediate from `HausdorffCompletelyMonotone` and `Nat.choose` nonnegativity.

The expansion to use is `Mathlib.Algebra.Group.ForwardDiff.fwdDiff_iter_eq_sum_shift`:

```text
(fwdDiff 1)^[j] b k = sum over i in range (j+1) of ((-1)^(j-i) * (j.choose i)) • b (k + i),
```

so, using `(-1)^j * (-1)^(j-i) = (-1)^i` for `i <= j` (write `j = i + d`, then
`(-1)^(i+d) * (-1)^d = (-1)^i` because `d + d` is even),

```text
(-1)^j * (fwdDiff 1)^[j] b k = sum over i in range (j+1) of (-1)^i * (j.choose i) * b (k+i).
```

### Identity one: total mass

```text
sum over k in range (N+1) of hausdorffWeight b N k = b 0.
```

Expand, swap to a sum over `n = k + i`, and collect the coefficient of `b n`:

```text
sum over k <= n of (N.choose k) * ((N-k).choose (n-k)) * (-1)^(n-k).
```

`Nat.choose_mul` (for `k <= n`) turns `N.choose k * (N-k).choose (n-k)` into
`N.choose n * n.choose k`, so the coefficient is
`N.choose n * (sum over k <= n of (-1)^(n-k) * n.choose k)`, and
`Int.alternating_sum_range_choose` makes that `0` unless `n = 0`, where it is `1`.

### Identity two: the exact moment identity

For `m <= N`,

```text
sum over k in range (N+1) of hausdorffWeight b N k * (k.descFactorial m)
  = (N.descFactorial m) * b m.
```

Same expansion and same collection.  The coefficient of `b n` is

```text
N.choose n * (sum over k <= n of (-1)^(n-k) * (n.choose k) * (k.descFactorial m)),
```

and the inner sum is the `n`-th forward difference of the falling factorial `x -> x.descFactorial m`
evaluated at `0`.  Since `fwdDiff 1 (x -> x.descFactorial m) = m * (x -> x.descFactorial (m-1))`, the
`n`-fold difference at `0` is `m*(m-1)*...*(m-n+1) * (0.descFactorial (m-n))`, which vanishes for
`n < m` (because `0.descFactorial j = 0` for `j > 0`) and for `n > m` (the product has a zero
factor), and equals `m!` at `n = m`.  So only `n = m` survives, with
`N.choose m * m! = N.descFactorial m`.

### From the weights to the measure

```text
hausdorffQuadrature b N := sum over k in range (N+1) of
  ENNReal.ofReal (hausdorffWeight b N k) • Measure.dirac (node N k),
node N k := the point k/N of [0,1] (use k/(N+1) or handle N = 0 separately).
```

Each is a finite positive measure of total mass `b 0`.  Identity two plus

```text
| (k/N)^m - (k.descFactorial m)/(N.descFactorial m) | <= C(m)/N   uniformly in k <= N
```

gives `integral x^m d(hausdorffQuadrature b N) -> b m`.  For `b 0 = 0` complete monotonicity forces
`b = 0` and the zero measure works; otherwise normalise by `b 0` to probability measures, use that
`ProbabilityMeasure` of a compact metric space is compact metrizable to extract a weakly convergent
subsequence, and pass `x -> x^m` (bounded continuous) to the limit.  Uniqueness is already available
as `hausdorffSource_eq_of_moments_eq`.

Everything above is finite algebra plus one weak-compactness step; no entire-function theory and no
missing mathlib area is involved.
