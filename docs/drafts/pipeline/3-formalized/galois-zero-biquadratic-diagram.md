# The biquadratic diagram of the entire zero field

- **Stage:** 3-formalized
- **Type:** THEOREM (Lean)
- **Questions:** Complete-fibre ground field; geometric involutions.

Write `w_q=t_q+iδ_q`, with complete-fibre source field `K`, and put
`L=ℚ(all w)`, `M=L(i)`, `F=K(all δ_q)`, `E=K(all δ_qδ_r)`.
These are the fields of the full infinite divisor.

## Theorem

Unconditionally `[M:E]` is two or four.

- If `F=E`, then `M=E(i)` and `[M:E]=2`.
- Otherwise some `δ₀∉E`, and `M=E(δ₀,i)` has degree four over `E`.
  Its quadratic intermediate fields are exactly the three distinct fields
  `F=E(δ₀)`, `E(i)`, and `L=E(iδ₀)`.

In the second case, every `z∈M` with `z²∈E` lies on one of the four
`E`-lines generated by `1,δ₀,i,iδ₀`.

## Proof

The established real-width presentation gives `M=F(i)` and, for a nonzero
anchor, `F=E(δ₀)`. Realness of `F` makes its Gaussian step quadratic.
If `δ₀∉E`, its square lies in `E`, giving the other quadratic step.

Write `z=a+ib`, with `a,b∈F`. If `z²∈E`, its imaginary part forces
`ab=0`. For a real coordinate `x+yδ₀` whose square lies in `E`, comparison
of its nonbase coefficient forces `xy=0`. This proves the four-line law.
Every quadratic intermediate field has a nonbase square-root generator,
obtained by completing the square in a degree-two minimal polynomial.
The four-line law classifies its generated field. Realness and
`δ₀∉E` distinguish the three fields.
