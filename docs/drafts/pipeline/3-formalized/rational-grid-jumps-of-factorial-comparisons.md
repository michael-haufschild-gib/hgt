# Which quotient classes can factorials represent?

For periodic jumps $J(n)$ of period $q$, the step profile $A(t)=\sum_{1\le n\le t}J(n)$ is a finite real linear combination of $\lfloor\alpha t\rfloor$, $\alpha>0$, exactly when $J(n)=J(\gcd(n,q))$. In that case the coefficient of $\lfloor t/d\rfloor$ for $d\mid q$ is $\sum_{e\mid d}\mu(d/e)J(e)$. Rescaling $t$ gives the same criterion on every rational grid.

At a positive integer $n$, a slope $\alpha$ contributes a jump precisely when $n\alpha$ is integral. Irrational slopes therefore contribute zero, and a rational slope contributes the divisor indicator for its reduced denominator. In a finite divisor sum the least period is the least common multiple of the active divisors. Thus every active denominator divides $q$. Conversely, divisor Möbius inversion followed by summation recovers the displayed floor expansion. The profile $\mathbf1_{q\mid\lfloor t\rfloor+1}$ has different jumps at the two coprime residues $-1,+1$ when $q\ge3$. Every finite floor comparison then differs by a fixed nonzero amount throughout some interval of positive ratios. The prime number theorem on the reciprocal interval gives $\sum_{lX<p\le rX}\log p\,|A(X/p)-B(X/p)|\ge cX$ for some $0<l<r$, $c>0$, at every sufficiently large $X$. At $q=2$ the exact comparison is $\lfloor t\rfloor-2\lfloor t/2\rfloor$. The signed sum over all primes may involve cancellation between intervals.
