# Common prime cuts and cancellation in the long cofactor interval

The original signed prefix agrees through cubic-logarithmic error on every fragment with the reading above the conductor-dependent cutoff \(F_n\). [Verified cofactor interval](../3-formalized/acs-pvc-the-complete-cofactor-interval.md)

**A single prime cutoff for a whole fragment.** Set \(F^*(M)=\max_{1\le n\le M}F_n\). For any fixed integer \(P\ge F^*(M)\), keep first primes \(p>P\) and just the two product conditions \(U_n<pv\le k\). The difference from the original fragment is \(O(M^2(P+1)(1+\log(2M)))\), uniformly in \(J,M,P\). Sum the weighted row bound \(O(n(P+1)(1+\log(2n)))\). On each of the finitely many initial conductors the cutoff can be clamped at \(L_n\), so their constants are uniform in \(P\). Taking \(P=F^*(M)\ll M\) gives cubic-logarithmic error. At the intermediate target take the maximum of \(F^*(M)\) and \(\lfloor M^{5/3}/(1+\log(2M))\rfloor\). The error is then \(O(M^{11/3})\). All conductors in the fragment share one ordinary-prime list. [Common prime cut](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExceptionalLayerTheory/SquareConductors/OnePrimeCutForEveryConductorInTheFragment.lean)

For \(g(v)=\mathrm{innerJump}(1,v)\), write \(G_V(s)=\sum_{v\le V}g(v)v^{-s}\) and \(A_V(s)=\sum_{v=1}^V(-1)^{v+1}v^{-s}\). The finite stencil identity, four-cap estimate and selection at the moving cut passed Lean. [Cofactor interval and zero response](../3-formalized/acs-pvc-the-complete-cofactor-interval.md)

**The density pole and the signed last cap.** The same cofactors have zero limiting harmonic mass. Testing the three stencil coefficients at \(-\log(d)/d\) gives \((1+c/2)\log 2=1\), so \(c>0\). Write \(R_j=\int_0^1 x^j/(1+x)\,dx\). The finite geometric sum gives \(A_j(1)=\log2-(-1)^jR_j\). Thus, for every \(V\ge1\) and \(k=\lfloor V/2\rfloor\),
\[
  G_V(1)=(-1)^V R_V+\tfrac c2(-1)^k R_k .
\]
Since \(0<R_j\le1/(j+1)\), the harmonic mass is \(O(1/V)\). It is strictly positive at \(V=4K\) and strictly negative at \(4K+3\) for \(K\ge1\). At a right zero the same finite sums tend to one. The real density cancellation therefore leaves a sign at the actual last cofactor and preserves the right-zero response. [Alternating harmonic remainder](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/RHTower/BurnolAlternatingHarmonic.lean#L96) · [Cofactor endpoint formula](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExceptionalLayerTheory/SquareConductors/TheContinuousPrimeMassAtTheCofactorEndpoint.lean)

**The common lower prime endpoint.** Put \(D(x)=\sum_{v\le x}g(v)(x/v-1)\). For \(m=\lfloor x\rfloor\ge1\), \(k=\lfloor m/2\rfloor\), the exact unweighted sum is \((1-c)/2+[(-1)^m+c(-1)^k]/2\). Subtracting it from \(xG_m(1)\) yields
\[
 D(x)-\tfrac{c-1}{2}
 =(-1)^m(xR_m-\tfrac12)+c(-1)^k((x/2)R_k-\tfrac12).
\]
For \(j\le y\le j+1\), the alternating-tail estimate \(|R_j-1/[2(j+1)]|\le1/[2(j+1)(j+2)]\) gives \(|yR_j-1/2|\le1/(j+1)\). Hence \(D(x)=(c-1)/2+O(1/x)\). A first-prime cutoff \(P>0\) contributes continuous prime mass \(P D(b/P)\) through product \(b\): the interval for the prime variable has length \(b/v-P\) for each \(v\le b/P\). Its leading term \((c-1)P/2\) is strictly negative and independent of the product endpoint. Indeed \(\log2=7/12+R_4\ge7/12+1/10>2/3\), so \(c<1\). On each sharp product interval \(P\le a<b\), the two signed endpoint terms cancel, leaving \(O(P^2/a)\), uniformly in \(b\). At horizon \(k\), the cell for \(j=\lfloor k/(pv)\rfloor\) has lower product endpoint \(\max(U,k/(j+1))\) and upper endpoint \(k/j\). For \(P\le U\), there are at most \(k/U\) such cells and \(W(j)\) is bounded. Together their continuous density costs \(O(P^2 k/U^2)\), including the clipped last cell. For the original remaining primes take \(P=\max(4,F_n)\ll n\), \(k\ll n^2\), \(U=\lfloor n^{17/9}\rfloor\). The density costs \(O(n^{2/9})\) per horizon. Summing the \(O(n^2)\) spline differences of size \(O(n^{-3})\) and restoring the exterior \(n^2\) gives \(O(n^{11/9})\) per conductor and \(O(M^{20/9})\) on every fragment. The original prefix thus agrees, through the established cubic-logarithmic error, with the ordinary first-prime reading after subtracting the actual length of each prime interval. Bounding the remaining signed prime discrepancy is open. **Restoring the lower product interval for a fixed set of primes.** For a fixed prime cutoff \(P\), let
\[
 c_P(q)=\sum_{p\mid q,\ p>\max(4,P)}(\log p)g(q/p),
 \qquad g(v)=\mathrm{innerJump}(1,v),
\]
with ordinary primes in the sum. Divisor inversion gives \(|c_P(q)|\le \|g\|_\infty\log q\), uniformly in \(P\). For \(q\le U_M\), the conductors \(N<n\le M\le2N\) with \(q\le U_n\) form one interval. For all sufficiently large \(N\), the first returned sample vanishes there. The conductor sum on that interval has absolute value at most \(C(q^8/N^{14}+q^7/N^{13}+q^4/N^8)\), by the proved conductor-interval estimate. Summing against \(|c_P(q)|\) gives \(O(N^3\log(2N))\), since \(U_M\ll N^{17/9}\). A single constant covers the initial octaves by the same uniform coefficient bound. A dyadic sum gives the cubic-logarithmic bound on every terminal fragment, even when the fixed \(P\) depends on the terminal. Thus after the common first-prime cut one may restore all products down to one. The complete reading retains the positive quotient endpoint at quadratic scale. Its one-sided upper bound remains open. The fixed-cut inversion and all-terminal comparison are formalized. **First primes through the nineteen-tenths scale.** For \(F_n<p\), set \(h=p/n^2,\ l=\lfloor U_n/p\rfloor,\ m=\lfloor L_n/p\rfloor\). Write \(K_b(x)=V_b(x)-V_1(x)\) for \(b=L_n+1\). The signed reading of the prime, before its log weight, is \(n\sum_{v>l}g(v)K_b(vh)\). The sum vanishes past \(m\), since \(L_n\ge4e^8 n^2\) and \(K_b\) vanishes beyond \(4e^8\). For \(h\) small, \(K_b(x)=O(x^7)\) before the first spline sample whenever \(bx>4e^8\).

For every integer \(v\ge1\) the seventh forward difference satisfies
\[
 |\Delta^7[K_b(vh)]|\ll h^{-1}v^{-8}.
\]
Indeed \(K_b(x)=\sum_{1\le j<b}W(j)[f(jx)-f((j+1)x)]\). A sample \(f(jvh)\) contributes only for \(1\le j\le4e^8/(vh)\), and its seventh cofactor difference costs \(O((jh)^7)\) since \(f^{(6)}\) is globally Lipschitz. There are \(O((vh)^{-1})\) such samples and \(jh\ll v^{-1}\). This bound is uniform in the far quotient endpoint.

Take \(D=\lceil h^{-1/2}\rceil\). The sum for \(l<v\le D\) costs \(O(h^7 D^8)=O(h^3)\) by the small-mesh bound. Above \(r=\max(l,D)\), the coefficient \(g(v)\) is 4-periodic with mean zero. Extend the mesh by zero below \(r+1\) and use seven periodic summations by parts. The interior differences cost \(O(h^{-1}\sum_{v>r}v^{-8})=O(h^{-1}D^{-7})=O(h^{5/2})\). At most seven differences cross the cut, at cost \(O(((r+7)h)^7)=O((U_n/n^2)^7+h^{7/2})\) for \(p\le U_n\). For \(p>U_n\) one has \(l=0\), leaving only \(O(h^{7/2})\). For every fixed subquadratic prime cutoff the small-mesh conditions hold uniformly for sufficiently large conductors. The small primes \(p\le F_n\) were already paid at cubic-logarithmic cost.

For \(p\le n^a\), Chebyshev's bound now prices the interior over every terminal fragment by \(O(M^{(7/2)a-3})\) for \(12/7\le a<2\). The sharp lower cofactor correction costs \(O(M^{2+\min(a,17/9)-7/9})\). Thus \(a=19/10\) gives exponents \(73/20\) and \(28/9\), both below \(11/3\). At \(a=12/7\) both costs are at most cubic. These estimates concern *all* products with a first prime in the stated range. The signed contribution from larger primes remains open. Lean proves the seventh difference, the \(O(h^{-1}D^{-7})\) summed tail, the seven clipped samples, the signed mesh estimate \(O(h^{5/2}+(lh)^7)\), the fragment estimate for every \(12/7\le a<2\), the residual cofactor bound and the one-sided \(11/3\) equivalence.
