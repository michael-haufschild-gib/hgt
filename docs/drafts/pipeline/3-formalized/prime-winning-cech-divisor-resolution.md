## 1. Source cover

For \(n\geq2\), put
\[
 M(n)=\max_p v_p(n),
 \qquad
 W(n)=\{p:v_p(n)=M(n)\}.
\tag{1}
\]
The winner set \(W(n)\) is finite and nonempty. For \(m\geq1\), define
\[
 a_m(n)=\mathbf1_{\{M(n)=m\}},
\tag{2}
\]
and, for a nonempty finite prime set \(F\),
\[
 b_{F,m}(n)
 =
 \mathbf1_{\{M(n)=m,\ F\subseteq W(n)\}}.
\tag{3}
\]
Then
\[
 b_{F,m}b_{G,m}=b_{F\cup G,m}.
\tag{4}
\]
Thus the singleton faces \(b_{\{p\},m}\) cover the shell \(a_m\), and their finite intersections
are exactly the higher winning faces.

The formalized higher prime-dilation theorem proves that, for pairwise distinct
\(p_1,\ldots,p_k\) and \(F=\{p_1,\ldots,p_k\}\),
\[
 \Delta_{p_1}\cdots\Delta_{p_k}a_m
 =
 (-1)^{k-1}(b_{F,m-1}-b_{F,m}).
\tag{5}
\]
Consequently the Čech cells below are generated by actual covariance failures of the radial
source; they were not freely adjoined after analytic continuation.

## 2. Pointwise inclusion–exclusion `[PAPER-PROVED]`

For every \(n\geq2\),
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 a_m(n)
 =
 \sum_{\substack{\varnothing\ne F\subseteq W(n)}}(-1)^{|F|+1}.
 \quad}
\tag{6}
\]

If \(M(n)\ne m\), every term is zero. If \(M(n)=m\), put \(r=|W(n)|\geq1\). The right side is
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^r(-1)^{j+1}\binom rj
 =
 1-(1-1)^r
 =
 1.
\]
Equivalently, with every finite prime set allowed and the convention that terms not contained in
\(W(n)\) vanish,
\[
 a_m
 =
 \sum_{\varnothing\ne F\Subset\mathbb P}(-1)^{|F|+1}b_{F,m}
\tag{7}
\]
pointwise. The sum is finite at each integer.

For \(\sigma>1\), the double Dirichlet series is absolutely summable. Indeed,
\[
 \sum_{\varnothing\ne F\subseteq W(n)}1
 =
 2^{|W(n)|}-1
 \leq 2^{\omega(n)}
 \leq d(n),
\]
and
\[
 \sum_{n\geq1}d(n)n^{-\sigma}=\zeta(\sigma)^2<\infty.
\tag{8}
\]
Therefore summation order in `(7)` may be exchanged in the half-plane of absolute convergence.

## 3. Exact augmented Čech contraction `[PAPER-PROVED]`

For a fixed \(n\) on the radius-\(m\) shell, the nonzero cells in `(7)` are the nonempty faces of
the full simplex on \(W(n)\). Orient a face by increasing prime order. Its augmented simplicial
chain complex is
\[
 \cdots\longrightarrow
 \bigoplus_{\substack{F\subseteq W(n)\\|F|=2}}\mathbb Z[F]
 \longrightarrow
 \bigoplus_{p\in W(n)}\mathbb Z[p]
 \longrightarrow
 \mathbb Z
 \longrightarrow0.
\tag{9}
\]
It is contractible by a source-canonical homotopy. Let
\[
 w(n)=\min W(n).
\tag{10}
\]
Send the augmented generator to \([w(n)]\). For a nonempty oriented face \(F\), set
\[
 h_n[F]=
 \begin{cases}
 [w(n),F],&w(n)\notin F,\\
 0,&w(n)\in F.
 \end{cases}
\tag{11}
\]
Because \(w(n)\) is smaller than every other member of \(W(n)\), the displayed orientation has no
hidden permutation sign. The standard cone calculation gives
\[
 \partial h_n+h_n\partial=\operatorname{id}.
\tag{12}
\]
Taking the direct sum over ordinary integers produces an exact augmented source Čech complex for
each shell. Both its cells and its contracting homotopy are fixed before choosing a zero.

This is not an ordinary derived-limit defect. Every finite source fibre is explicitly
contractible. Any surviving analytic obstruction must be failure of the canonical homotopy
`(11)` to remain controlled after completion and Cartier base change.

## 4. Dirichlet series of every nonempty face `[PAPER-PROVED]`

For nonempty finite \(F\) and \(m\geq1\), put
\[
 B_{F,m}(s)=\sum_{n\geq1}b_{F,m}(n)n^{-s},
 \qquad \operatorname{Re}s>1.
\tag{13}
\]
Every prime in \(F\) has exponent exactly \(m\); every prime outside \(F\) has exponent between
\(0\) and \(m\). Unique factorization and absolute convergence give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 B_{F,m}(s)
 &=
 \left(\prod_{p\in F}p^{-ms}\right)
 \prod_{q\notin F}
 \left(1+q^{-s}+\cdots+q^{-ms}\right)\\
 &=
 \frac{\zeta(s)}{\zeta((m+1)s)}
 \prod_{p\in F}
 \frac{p^{-ms}(1-p^{-s})}{1-p^{-(m+1)s}}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
Define
\[
 U_{F,m}(s)
 =
 \frac1{\zeta((m+1)s)}
 \prod_{p\in F}R_{p,m}(s),
\qquad
 R_{p,m}(s)
 =
 \frac{p^{-ms}(1-p^{-s})}{1-p^{-(m+1)s}}.
\tag{15}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{\quad B_{F,m}(s)=\zeta(s)U_{F,m}(s).\quad}
\tag{16}
\]

## 5. Unit theorem on the strict critical half-plane `[PAPER-PROVED]`

Let
\[
 \mathcal U=\{s\in\mathbb C:\operatorname{Re}s>1/2\}.
\]
For \(m\geq1\), one has
\[
 \operatorname{Re}((m+1)s)>1
\]
on \(\mathcal U\). Hence \(\zeta((m+1)s)\) is holomorphic and nonzero there. Also
\[
 |p^{-s}|<1,
\qquad
 |p^{-(m+1)s}|<1.
\]
Every numerator and denominator in `(15)` is therefore holomorphic and nonzero. Thus
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 U_{F,m}\in\mathcal O(\mathcal U)^\times.
\quad}
\tag{17}
\]
Consequently every positive face series \(B_{F,m}\) has exactly the Riemann-zeta meromorphic
Cartier divisor on \(\mathcal U\), with multiplicity. Unlike the radial-shell zero-free theorem,
this statement holds for every \(m\geq1\), because fixing one winner removes the adjacent-zeta
difference whose nonvanishing required high shell depth.

## 6. Flat finite-face conormal chart `[PAPER-PROVED]`

For \(p\notin F\),
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 U_{F\cup\{p\},m}=R_{p,m}U_{F,m}.
\quad}
\tag{18}
\]
The transition is independent of \(F\), so the finite-subset nerve carries a flat rank-one unit
system. If \(\rho\in\mathcal U\) is a zeta zero of order \(d\), Leibniz and `(17)` give
\[
 B_{F,m}^{(j)}(\rho)=0\quad(j<d),
\qquad
 B_{F,m}^{(d)}(\rho)=\zeta^{(d)}(\rho)U_{F,m}(\rho).
\tag{19}
\]
Therefore its principal-normal transition is not postulated:
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \frac{B_{F\cup\{p\},m}^{(d)}(\rho)}
      {B_{F,m}^{(d)}(\rho)}
 =
 R_{p,m}(\rho).
\quad}
\tag{20}
\]
Multiplying all cells by the standard zero-free Gamma/polynomial completion factor transports
the same statement from \(\zeta\) to \(\Xi\).

Equation `(20)` is an algebraic/projective Xi-conormal landing theorem. It does **not** yet put
that conormal in a Hausdorff Hilbert completion or prove adjointability.

## 7. Infinite Euler augmentation `[PAPER-PROVED]`

Dirichlet transforming `(7)` and using `(16)` yields
\[
 H_m(s)
 =
 \sum_{\varnothing\ne F\Subset\mathbb P}
 (-1)^{|F|+1}U_{F,m}(s),
\qquad \operatorname{Re}s>1,
\tag{21}
\]
where
\[
 S_m(s)=\zeta(s)H_m(s),
\qquad
 H_m(s)=\frac1{\zeta((m+1)s)}-\frac1{\zeta(ms)}.
\]
The factorized form is exact:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_{\varnothing\ne F}(-1)^{|F|+1}
 \prod_{p\in F}R_{p,m}(s)
 &=
 1-\prod_p(1-R_{p,m}(s)),\\
 1-R_{p,m}(s)
 &=
 \frac{1-p^{-ms}}{1-p^{-(m+1)s}},\\
 \prod_p(1-R_{p,m}(s))
 &=
 \frac{\zeta((m+1)s)}{\zeta(ms)}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{22}
\]
Substitution recovers `(21)`. The known positive shell multiplier is therefore the Euler
augmentation of the source-generated prime-winning Čech nerve.

## 11. Weighted \(L^2\) reinterpretation: arithmetic purity, not another norm

Weighted \(L^2\)-cohomology of Coxeter/Davis complexes is relevant parent theory. It constructs
Hilbert cochain complexes with a positive multiparameter \(q\), Hecke-von Neumann dimensions,
weighted Poincaré duality, and Singer-type middle-degree concentration in proved ranges. The
prime-winning nerve is not a cocompact Davis complex, and its natural transition parameter is the
complex unit `(18)`, not a positive constant \(q\). Nevertheless the radial part
\[
 q_{p,m}(s)
 :=
 \frac{|R_{p,m}(s)|^2}{R_{p,m}(1)}
\tag{25}
\]
has
\[
 \log q_{p,m}(s)
 =
 -2m(\operatorname{Re}s-\tfrac12)\log p+o(\log p),
\tag{26}
\]
while reflection reverses the leading weight. The critical line is exactly the asymptotically
unweighted locus.

This suggests a more universal formulation of the missing theorem:

`[WILD CONJECTURE — ARITHMETIC FAILURE-HODGE WEIGHT LAW]`

> Analytic displacement from a self-dual line is cohomological weight displacement in the
> controlled completion of the source's own failure resolution. Functional equation is weighted
> Poincaré duality. A zero is a persistent failure class. For the literal additive-multiplicative
> integer source, Gamma/co-Poisson comparison makes that class pure of weight zero.

Then
\[
 \text{arithmetic Hodge weight of the class}
 =
 2\operatorname{Re}\rho-1,
\tag{27}
\]
and purity gives RH.

This is stronger and more falsifiable than “find a self-adjoint operator.” It demands:

1. a complex-weighted, phase-preserving extension of weighted \(L^2\) theory;
2. a comparison of the multiplicative winner contraction with the additive Fourier/Poisson

Positive \(q\)-cohomology alone loses the phase and cannot identify Xi. Weighted Poincaré duality
alone permits complementary off-middle classes and therefore does not prove RH. Candidate-new
mathematics is the source-specific additive--multiplicative comparison forcing **purity of the
