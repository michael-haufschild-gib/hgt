## Universal prime-face law

Let `A` be any semiring and let `f,g : Nat -> A` be arithmetic functions with Dirichlet
convolution `*`. For every prime `p`,

```text
(f * g)(p) = f(1) g(p) + f(p) g(1).                    (1)
```

Only `(1,p)` and `(p,1)` occur in the divisor antidiagonal. Therefore

```text
(f^n)(p)
 = sum_(i=0)^(n-1) f(1)^i f(p) f(1)^(n-1-i).           (2)
```

Equation `(2)` is the full noncommutative derivative of the power map at `f(1)` in direction
`f(p)`. It holds in arbitrary semirings, hence for operator algebras without finite-dimensional,
commutative, Hilbert, or scalar assumptions.

Let `A` now be any complete real normed algebra and define the coefficientwise infinite
convolution functional

```text
Phi_c(f) = sum_(n>=0) c_n f^n.                          (3)
```

Lean proves at every prime

```text
Phi_c(f)(p)
 = sum_(n>=0) c_n
     sum_(i=0)^(n-1) f(1)^i f(p) f(1)^(n-1-i).          (4)
```

The same derivative map acts at every prime. No term in `(4)` contains `f(q)` for `q != p`.
Thus arbitrary coefficientwise infinite Dirichlet-convolution nonlinearity cannot mix distinct
prime faces, even with noncommutative infinite-dimensional operator coefficients.

In the scalar case, `(4)` collapses to the exact chain rule

```text
Phi_c(f)(p) = Phi_c'(f(1)) f(p),                        (5)

Phi_c'(a) = sum_(n>=0) c_n n a^(n-1).
```

No convergence hypothesis is needed for the identity: at a fixed prime, convolution-power
coefficients satisfy the displayed derivative law term by term. For ordinary analytic series,
`(5)` is its usual convergent specialization.
