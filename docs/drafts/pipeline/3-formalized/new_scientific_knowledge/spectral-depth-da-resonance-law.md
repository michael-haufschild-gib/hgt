## Universalization attempt E: spectral-depth DA resonance

`[THEOREM — PAPER PROVED AND LEAN FORMALIZED, FINAL AUDIT PENDING]` Let `f : C -> C` be globally
smooth and write its eventual full-support Taylor magnitudes as

`|a_n| = exp(-b_n)`.

Suppose `b = b^(0)` has a finite recursively ordered geometric tower

`b^(i)_n = A_i lambda_i^n + b^(i+1)_n` for `0 <= i < m`,

with `A_i > 0`, `lambda_i > 1`, and `b^(i+1) = o(lambda_i^n)` at each layer.  If `f` is
differentially algebraic, then one nonzero `Q in Z[X]`, one cofinal subsequence `q_k`, and constants
`C,K in N` simultaneously satisfy

`Q(1) = Q(lambda_0) = ... = Q(lambda_(m-1)) = 0`

and

`0 <= Q(S)b^(m)(q_k) < C log(q_k+K+2)` eventually.

Here `Q(S)x(n) = sum_(r=0)^deg(Q) Q_r x_(n+r)`.  Consequently every layer ratio is algebraic over
`Z`.  If any layer ratio is transcendental over `Q`, then `f` is hypertranscendental.  The result is
phase-uniform: only Taylor coefficient magnitudes occur.

For the explicit two-layer case

`b_n = A_0 lambda_0^n + A_1 lambda_1^n + h_n`,

with `lambda_0 > lambda_1 > 1` and `h=o(lambda_1^n)`, the same `Q` annihilates `1`, `lambda_0`, and
`lambda_1`, while carrying the logarithmic nonnegative defect on `h`.  This lower root is invisible
to the ordinary successive log-ratio limit, which sees only `lambda_0`.

### Dependency-closed paper proof

Write `Delta_Q x(n) := Q(S)x(n)`.  The proof uses these elementary lemmas.

**Lemma 1 (geometric covariance).** For every real `lambda`,

`Delta_Q (lambda^(.))(n) = lambda^n Q_R(lambda)`,

where `Q_R` is `Q` with coefficients cast to `R`.

*Proof.* Expand the finite sum and use `lambda^(n+r)=lambda^n lambda^r`.  Factoring `lambda^n`
leaves the polynomial evaluation.  ∎

**Lemma 2 (shift stability of a little-o tail).** If `lambda>0` and `u_n=o(lambda^n)`, then

`Delta_Q u(n)=o(lambda^n)`.

*Proof.* For each of the finitely many shifts `r` in `Q`, composition with `n |-> n+r` gives
`u_(n+r)=o(lambda^(n+r))=o(lambda^r lambda^n)=o(lambda^n)`.  Multiplication by the fixed coefficient
`Q_r` and finite summation preserve little-o.  ∎

**Lemma 3 (one-layer peeling).** Assume `b_n=A lambda^n+u_n`, `A != 0`, `lambda>1`,
`u=o(lambda^n)`, and for a cofinal `q_k`

`0 <= Delta_Q b(q_k) < C log(q_k+K+2)` eventually.

Then `Q_R(lambda)=0`; moreover the identical defect inequality holds with `u` in place of `b`.

*Proof.* Divide the inequality by the positive `lambda^(q_k)`.  Since `q_k -> infinity`, the
standard estimate `log(n+2)=o(lambda^n)` applies; eventually `q_k>=K`, so
`log(q_k+K+2) <= 2 log(q_k+2)`.  Hence the normalized defect is squeezed to zero.  By Lemmas 1--2,

`Delta_Q b(n)/lambda^n -> A Q_R(lambda)`.

The same limit along the cofinal subsequence is both zero and `A Q_R(lambda)`, so uniqueness of
limits and `A!=0` give `Q_R(lambda)=0`.  Lemma 1 then makes
`Delta_Q(A lambda^(.))` identically zero, hence `Delta_Q b=Delta_Q u` pointwise and the original
defect transfers unchanged.  ∎

**Main proof.** Positivity of the leading amplitude, `lambda_0>1`, and
`b^(1)=o(lambda_0^n)` make `b` eventually positive and uniformly geometric.  Apply the existing DA
resonance theorem to obtain one `Q!=0`, `Q(1)=0`, constants `C,K`, cofinal `q`, and the logarithmic
defect on `b`.  Apply Lemma 3 to layer 0.  Its pointwise transfer leaves exactly the same `Q,C,K,q`
on `b^(1)`.  Repeat through the finite list.  Induction yields every root and the terminal residual
defect.  No choice is made between layers, so the annihilator is genuinely common.  ∎

**Algebraicity corollary.** For every recorded layer, the same nonzero `Q in Z[X]` has that real
ratio as a root, which is precisely `IsAlgebraic Z`.  If a ratio is transcendental over `Q`,
differential algebraicity would yield algebraicity over `Z`, hence over `Q`, a contradiction.  ∎

### Infinite-depth endpoint

`[THEOREM — PAPER PROVED AND LEAN FORMALIZED, FINAL AUDIT PENDING]` Suppose instead that profiles
`b^(i)` exist for every `i in N`, with

`b^(i)_n = A_i lambda_i^n + b^(i+1)_n`,

`A_i>0`, `lambda_i>1`, and `b^(i+1)=o(lambda_i^n)`.  If `f` is DA, then the one polynomial produced
from `b^(0)` satisfies `Q(lambda_i)=0` for **every** `i`, and its original signed logarithmic defect
descends to every tail `b^(i)`.  If `i |-> lambda_i` is injective, this is impossible: after mapping
`Q` into `R[X]`, its root set contains the infinite range of the ratios, so the polynomial is zero,
contradicting `Q!=0`.  Therefore every coefficient-phase realization is hypertranscendental.

This endpoint is the genuine universal law exposed by finite peeling.  It is stronger than saying
that each ratio is algebraic: all ratios may individually be algebraic.  Obstruction is global
spectral depth—one finite polynomial cannot support infinitely many distinct geometric scales.

**Proof.** Apply the existing DA resonance theorem once at depth zero.  By Lemma 3, its defect
descends from `b^(i)` to `b^(i+1)` and the same polynomial acquires root `lambda_i`.  Ordinary
induction gives both assertions at every natural depth.  Injectivity makes `{lambda_i : i in N}`
infinite.  A nonzero polynomial over an integral domain has finitely many roots, contradiction.  ∎

### Hostile attack and boundaries

- Removing cofinality invalidates the logarithmic normalization.
- Allowing a zero amplitude creates a fake layer and invalidates root extraction.
- Allowing `lambda<=1` destroys exponential domination of the logarithmic defect.
- Without the recursive little-`o` separation, different modes can survive at one normalization
  scale and only a linear combination of evaluations is forced to vanish.
- The result is necessary, not sufficient, for differential algebraicity.  Algebraic ratios and a
  residual resonance do not provide a differential equation.
- Strict numerical ordering is not an extra axiom in the general theorem: recursive little-`o`
  separation is the invariant condition.  In the explicit two-layer connector,
  `lambda_1<lambda_0` proves the required separation.

No counterexample survives these hypotheses.  The conclusion is materially stronger than a list
of independent algebraicity claims because it forces a **single** integer polynomial, with the same
degree and coefficient data, to annihilate every layer and preserve one signed residual defect.
