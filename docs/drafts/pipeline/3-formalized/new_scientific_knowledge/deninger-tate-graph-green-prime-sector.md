## Theorem

`[PROVED HERE] [DAC_TATE_GRAPH_GREEN_PRIME_SECTOR]`

The unsmoothed Green form

\[
 G_0(f,g)
 =\sum_p
   \left\langle
     \widehat A_p^{1/2}f_p,
     \widehat A_p^{1/2}g_p
   \right\rangle                                             \tag{3}
\]

has following properties.

1. It extends uniquely to a continuous sesquilinear form on
   \(\mathcal H_{\beta,\mathrm{gr}}\), with
   \[
    |G_0(f,g)|
    \le {1\over(\log2)^2}
       \|f\|_{\beta,\mathrm{gr}}
       \|g\|_{\beta,\mathrm{gr}}.                            \tag{4}
   \]
2. It is nonnegative and its radical on the oscillatory sector is
   zero.
3. For every (r>0), positive-time form
   \[
    G_r(f,f)=\sum_p
      \langle f_p,\widehat A_pe^{-2r\widehat A_p}f_p\rangle
   \]
   satisfies \(0\le G_r(f,f)\le G_0(f,f)\).  Hence heat forms remain
   continuous on graph completion without defining its topology.
4. Fix (p), choose a unit vector in the (k)-eigenspace, and graph
   normalize it as
   \[
    v_{p,k}
    ={e_{p,k}\over L_p\sqrt{1+\lambda_k}}.
   \]
   Then
   \[
    \|v_{p,k}\|_{\beta,\mathrm{gr}}=1,
    \qquad
    G_0(v_{p,k},v_{p,k})
    ={\lambda_k\over L_p^2(1+\lambda_k)}
    \longrightarrow {1\over L_p^2}>0.                         \tag{5}
   \]
   Thus normalized high frequencies do not escape the unsmoothed
   graph form.

### Proof

Every prime satisfies (L_p\ge\log2>0).  Therefore

\[
 \sum_p\|\widehat A_p^{1/2}f_p\|_2^2
 \le {1\over(\log2)^2}
      \sum_pL_p^2\|\widehat A_p^{1/2}f_p\|_2^2
 \le {1\over(\log2)^2}\|f\|_{\beta,\mathrm{gr}}^2.           \tag{6}
\]

Cauchy--Schwarz applied to `(3)` and `(6)` gives `(4)` on algebraic
finite-support vectors.  Continuity gives unique extension to
completion.

Every \(\widehat A_p\) is nonnegative.  If (G_0(f,f)=0), each
nonnegative summand vanishes, so
\(\widehat A_p^{1/2}f_p=0\) for every (p).  Kernel of
\(\widehat A_p\) is circle-constant sector, excluded by
\(E_p^{\mathrm{osc}}\); hence (f=0).  This proves item 2.

For \(\lambda\ge0\) and (r>0),

\[
 0\le\lambda e^{-2r\lambda}\le\lambda,
\]

so spectral calculus and monotone summation give item 3.  Formula
`(5)` follows directly from `(1)`--`(3)`, and
\(\lambda/(1+\lambda)\to1\).
