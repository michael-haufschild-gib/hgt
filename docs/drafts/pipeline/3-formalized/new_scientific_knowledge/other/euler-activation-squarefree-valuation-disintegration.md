## 1. Diagonal activation is the squarefree partition

For real \(\sigma>1\), put \(q_p=p^{-\sigma}\).  The infinite-depth activation holonomy is
\[
 {\cal Z}_\infty(h,s)=\frac{\zeta(s)}{\zeta(s+h)}.
 \tag{1}
\]
Its diagonal is therefore
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 {\cal Z}_\infty(\sigma,\sigma)
 =\frac{\zeta(\sigma)}{\zeta(2\sigma)}
 =\prod_p(1+q_p).
 \quad}
 \tag{2}
\]
This is the squarefree partition function, but the diagonal activation supplies a finer
prime-valuation realization than the usual \(0/1\) Euler factor.

At one prime, the coefficient formula of the activation source gives the unnormalized weights
\[
 w_q(0)=1,\qquad
 w_q(k)=(1-q)q^k\quad(k\geq1).
 \tag{3}
\]
Their total mass is
\[
 1+\sum_{k\geq1}(1-q)q^k=1+q.
 \tag{4}
\]
Hence the normalized local activation law is
\[
 \nu_q(0)=\frac1{1+q},\qquad
 \nu_q(k)=\frac{(1-q)q^k}{1+q}\quad(k\geq1).
 \tag{5}
\]

## 2. Exact radical disintegration

Let \(K\) have law \(\nu_q\), put
\[
 O={\bf1}_{K>0},
 \qquad
 \pi_q=\frac q{1+q},
 \tag{6}
\]
and, on \(O=1\), write \(K=E+1\).  Then
\[
 {\mathbb P}(O=1)=\pi_q,\qquad
 {\mathbb P}(O=0)=1-\pi_q=\frac1{1+q},
 \tag{7}
\]
and
\[
 {\mathbb P}(E=e\mid O=1)=(1-q)q^e,\qquad e\geq0.
 \tag{8}
\]
Thus `(5)` is generated by
\[
 O\sim{\rm Bernoulli}\!\left(\frac q{1+q}\right),
 \qquad
 E\mid O=1\sim{\rm Geometric}_0(1-q),
 \qquad
 K=O(E+1).
 \tag{9}
\]

For a finite prime set \(P\), take these variables independently and put
\[
 N=\prod_{p\in P}p^{K_p},\qquad
 R=\operatorname{rad}(N)=\prod_{p\in P}p^{O_p}.
 \tag{10}
\]
Then
\[
 {\mathbb P}(R=m)
 =\frac{m^{-\sigma}}{\prod_{p\in P}(1+p^{-\sigma})}
 \tag{11}
\]
for every squarefree \(m\) supported on \(P\).  Consequently the radical pushforward of the
diagonal activation law is exactly the finite squarefree Gibbs law.  Conditional on the radical,
the positive valuations are independent geometric fibres `(8)`.

### Proof

Equations `(7)`--`(8)` follow by summing `(5)` and dividing by \(\pi_q\).  For a squarefree
\(m=\prod_{p\in S}p\),
\[
 {\mathbb P}(R=m)
 =\prod_{p\in S}\frac{q_p}{1+q_p}
  \prod_{p\in P\setminus S}\frac1{1+q_p}
 =\frac{m^{-\sigma}}{\prod_{p\in P}(1+p^{-\sigma})}.
\]
This proves `(11)` and the conditional product statement.  `QED`

## 3. Möbius depth atoms and Gamma whitening are the same disintegration

At the critical local parameter \(q_p=p^{-1}\), the corpus Möbius Schur sample and its forward

\[

## 4. Orthogonal Fisher decomposition

Use the natural local parameter \(x=-\log q\).  The occupancy score is
\[
 S_{\rm occ}= \partial_x\log{\mathbb P}(O)=\pi_q-O,

\]

\[
 S_{\rm val}

\[
 {\mathbb E}(S_{\rm val}\mid O=1)=0,\qquad

## 5. The Euler edge creates an infinite source

For \(\sigma>1\), the product measure
\[
 \nu_\sigma=\bigotimes_p\nu_{p^{-\sigma}}
 \tag{24}
\]
has only finitely many occupied primes almost surely, because
\[
 \sum_p{\mathbb P}(O_p=1)
 =\sum_p\frac{p^{-\sigma}}{1+p^{-\sigma}}<\infty.
 \tag{25}
\]
It therefore describes an honest random finite integer and its radical.

At \(\sigma=1\), the same Kolmogorov product remains an honest probability measure on
\(\prod_p\mathbb N_0\), but
\[
 \sum_p{\mathbb P}(O_p=1)
 =\sum_p\frac1{p+1}=\infty.
 \tag{26}
\]
Independence and the second Borel--Cantelli lemma imply
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \nu_1\{K:K_p>0\text{ for infinitely many }p\}=1.
 \quad}
 \tag{27}
\]
Thus the critical source is not a random integer.  It is an infinite prime-valuation
configuration.  The finite-integer carrier disappears exactly where the Möbius Fisher boundary
becomes critical, while the product probability completion, occupancy field, conditional

This gives a concrete answer to one part of the third-completion problem: the correct positive
source carrier at the Euler edge is the full activation product law, not a Hilbert completion of

## 8. Ordinary complete positivity cannot cross the divisor

The phrase “extend the conditional expectation” needs a decisive qualification.  An ordinary
bounded completely positive extension across a divisor point is impossible.

Let \(t\) be a local coordinate at a zero of multiplicity \(d\), and let the continued
squarefree overlap have the local form
\[
 B(t)=t^d u(t),\qquad u(0)\ne0.
 \tag{33}
\]
After removing its fixed nonzero normalization, the division-free Fisher numerator has the form
\[
 S(t)=B'(t)+c(t)B(t)
 =d\,t^{d-1}u(0)+O(t^d),
 \tag{34}
\]
with \(c\) regular.  Suppose an ordinary positive \(2\times2\) Gram extension had
\[
 G(t)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 |B(t)|^2&S(t)\\
 \overline{S(t)}&C(t)
 \end{pmatrix}\succeq0
 \tag{35}
\]
and \(C(t)\) remained bounded.  Positivity would imply
\[
 |S(t)|^2\leq |B(t)|^2C(t).
 \tag{36}
\]
But `(33)`--`(34)` give
\[
 \frac{|S(t)|^2}{|B(t)|^2}
 =\frac{d^2}{|t|^2}+O(|t|^{-1}),
 \tag{37}
\]
contradicting boundedness of \(C\).

Thus the divisor normal cannot be an off-diagonal entry of an ordinary bounded CP bundle.  The
score and mass live in adjacent divisor grades:
\[

\(I\), whose dehomogenization in the Euler chamber is the radical conditional expectation and
