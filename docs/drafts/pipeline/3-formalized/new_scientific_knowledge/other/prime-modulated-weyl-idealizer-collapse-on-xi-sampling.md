## 1. The sampling ideal and the finite prime--Weyl algebra

Let `S` be a locally finite subset of `R`. Put

```text
I(S)={phi in Schwartz(R):phi(gamma)=0 for every gamma in S}.       (1)
```

For `h in R`, let

```text
(S_h phi)(xi)=phi(xi+h).                                           (2)
```

Define the finite shift--Weyl algebra `A_fin` to consist of operators on `Schwartz(R)` with a
presentation

```text
Q=sum_(h in F) sum_(j=0)^(m_h)
    a_(h,j)(xi) partial_xi^j S_h,                                  (3)
```

where `F subset R` is finite and every `a_(h,j)` is a complex polynomial. Equal shifts are
collected, so the elements of `F` are distinct.

Under Fourier conjugation, `A_fin` is the algebra generated by

```text
D=-i partial_x,
X=multiplication by x,
M_h=multiplication by exp(i h x),                                  (4)
```

with only finitely many modulation frequencies in each element. It contains the first Weyl
algebra and every finite prime-frequency extension obtained by taking `h=log p` for finitely many
primes.

The sampling idealizer is

```text
Id_S(A_fin)={Q in A_fin:Q I(S) subset I(S)}.                        (5)
```

Condition `(5)` is exactly what is needed for `Q` to descend to the sampling quotient
`Schwartz(R)/I(S)`.

## 2. Superlinear sampling rigidity

### Theorem A

Assume that `S` is locally finite, symmetric under `gamma |-> -gamma`, and has superlinear
counting growth

```text
N_S(T)=#(S intersection [-T,T]),
N_S(T)/T -> infinity.                                               (6)
```

Then

```text
Id_S(A_fin)=C[xi],                                                  (7)
```

where the right side means polynomial multiplication in the spectral variable. Equivalently,
after inverse Fourier transformation, the only finite shift--Weyl operators descending to the
sampling quotient are the constant-coefficient differential operators `C[D]`.

### Proof

We split the proof into the translation and jet obstructions.

#### Lemma 1: every nonzero shift misses infinitely many samples

For `h !=0`, define

```text
E_h={gamma in S:gamma+h notin S}.                                  (8)
```

We claim that `E_h` is infinite. Suppose instead that it is finite. Symmetry of `S` shows that

```text
{gamma in S:gamma-h notin S}=-E_h                                  (9)
```

is also finite. Thus, outside a finite set, every point of `S` has both its predecessor and
successor at displacement `|h|` in `S`.

The graph on `S` generated by edges of length `|h|` therefore has only finitely many exceptional
vertices. Every component avoiding those vertices is a full arithmetic progression. Every other
component meets the finite exceptional set. Local finiteness gives only finitely many points of
`S` in a fundamental interval of length `|h|`, so altogether `S` is contained in finitely many
arithmetic progressions together with a finite set. Hence

```text
N_S(T)=O_h(T),                                                     (10)
```

contradicting `(6)`. This proves the claim.

#### Lemma 2: all nonzero-shift coefficients vanish

Let `Q` have the form `(3)` and satisfy `(5)`. Fix `h in F`, `h!=0`, and `gamma in E_h`. The point
`gamma+h` is outside `S`. Since `S` is locally finite and `F` is finite, choose a small interval
`U` about `gamma+h` which is disjoint from `S` and from every other point `gamma+h'`, `h' in F`,
`h'!=h`.

For a fixed `0<=j<=m_h`, choose `phi in C_c^infinity(U)` whose jet at `gamma+h` is

```text
phi^(j)(gamma+h)=1,
phi^(k)(gamma+h)=0,          0<=k<=max_(h' in F)m_(h'), k!=j.       (11)
```

Such a function is obtained by multiplying the appropriate Taylor monomial by a cutoff equal to
one near `gamma+h`. Because `U` misses `S`, we have `phi in I(S)`. Evaluating `(3)` at `gamma`, all
terms except `(h,j)` vanish, and `(5)` gives

```text
0=(Q phi)(gamma)=a_(h,j)(gamma).                                  (12)
```

Lemma 1 supplies infinitely many such `gamma`. A polynomial with infinitely many real zeros is
zero, so

```text
a_(h,j)=0              for every h!=0 and every j.                 (13)
```

#### Lemma 3: every positive derivative coefficient vanishes

After `(13)`, write

```text
Q=sum_(j=0)^m a_j(xi) partial_xi^j.                               (14)
```

Fix `gamma in S` and `j>=1`. Choose a cutoff which equals one near `gamma` and whose support
contains no other point of `S`, and multiply it by `(xi-gamma)^j/j!`. The resulting function lies
in `I(S)` and has exactly the required nonzero `j`th jet at `gamma`. Equation `(5)` yields

```text
a_j(gamma)=0.                                                     (15)
```

The set `S` is infinite by `(6)`, so every `a_j`, `j>=1`, is identically zero. Thus `Q` is
multiplication by `a_0(xi)`. Conversely, polynomial multiplication plainly preserves `I(S)`.
This proves `(7)`. QED.

## 3. Symmetric operators and the canonical-commutation obstruction

Let

```text
q_S(phi,psi)=sum_(gamma in S)c_gamma
  phi(gamma) conjugate(psi(gamma)),       c_gamma>0,                (16)
```

on any common core where the sum converges. Its nullspace is `I(S)`. By Theorem A, every element
of `A_fin` descending to the quotient acts as polynomial multiplication `p(gamma)`.

If the descended operator is symmetric for `(16)`, then

```text
p(gamma)=conjugate(p(gamma))        for every gamma in S.          (17)
```

The polynomial

```text
p(z)-conjugate(p(conjugate z))                                      (18)
```

vanishes on the infinite real set `S`, hence is identically zero. Therefore `p` has real
coefficients. Conversely, every real polynomial multiplier is symmetric. We obtain

```text
Id_S(A_fin)_(symmetric)=R[xi].                                    (19)
```

In particular, all descended elements commute. If two descended operators `A,B in A_fin` obey

```text
[A,B]=lambda I                                                     (20)
```

on a nonzero quotient, then `lambda=0`. No local finite-prime Weyl pair on this carrier can realize
a nonzero canonical commutation relation.

## 4. Application to the real Xi divisor

Let

```text
Z_Xi^R={gamma in R:Xi(gamma)=0}                                   (21)
```

with each distinct ordinate listed once. This set is locally finite and symmetric. Conrey proved
unconditionally that at least two-fifths of all nontrivial zeta zeros are simple and lie on the
critical line. Together with the Riemann--von Mangoldt formula, this gives

```text
#(Z_Xi^R intersection [-T,T]) >= c T log T                         (22)
```

for all sufficiently large `T` and some `c>0`. Hence `(6)` holds and Theorem A applies:

```text
Id_(Z_Xi^R)(A_fin)=C[xi].                                         (23)
```

Thus no nonzero finite prime modulation, no polynomial position term, and no finite combination
of their derivatives descends to the full real-Xi sampling quotient. Only a polynomial function
of momentum survives.

*Superseded route.*  `(22)` is no longer needed: `(23)` is proved directly from Hardy's theorem
together with progression-freeness of the real Xi divisor, which is strictly weaker than a positive
proportion of critical zeros and is itself unconditional.  See the 2026-08-19 Lean progress section.

## 5. Two prime dilations force the theta metric onto the divisor

Return to logarithmic source coordinates. Let `q` be a jointly continuous positive semidefinite
sesquilinear form on `Schwartz(R)`. For

```text
(T_a f)(x)=f(x+a),                                               (24)
```

assume invariance under two multiplicatively independent integer scales `r,s>1`:

```text
q(T_(log r)f,T_(log r)g)=q(f,g),
q(T_(log s)f,T_(log s)g)=q(f,g).                                (25)
```

Multiplicative independence makes `log r/log s` irrational, so

```text
Z log r+Z log s                                                   (26)
```

is dense in `R`. The translation action is continuous on Schwartz space; `(25)` therefore implies
full simultaneous-translation invariance. The Schwartz-kernel theorem and Bochner--Schwartz
theorem give a unique positive tempered measure `mu` with

```text
q(f,g)=integral_R hat f(xi) conjugate(hat g(xi)) dmu(xi).         (27)
```

For Hedenmalm's normalized theta source

```text
k(x)=2 VarTheta_00(i exp(2x)),        hat k=Xi,                    (28)
```

assume the mandatory null constraint `q(k,k)=0`. Positivity and `(27)` give

```text
0=integral_R |Xi(xi)|^2 dmu(xi),                                  (29)
```

so

```text
supp(mu) subset Z_Xi^R.                                           (30)
```

This recovers the divisor-supported metric classification from only two discrete prime dilations;
infinitesimal `D`-symmetry need not be assumed. If the metric has full real-divisor support, then
`supp(mu)=Z_Xi^R`, and `(23)` applies to its null quotient. Full support is an additional
conservativity condition; it is not inferred merely from positivity.

## 6. Positive Hilbert--Pólya carrier law

The theorem fixes the architecture forced on a successful theta-pair realization:

```text
two independent prime dilations
  -> translation-invariant positive metric
  -> real-Xi-divisor sampling carrier
  -> collapse of every finite prime--Weyl observable to R[D].     (31)
```

Consequently the arithmetic operator which supplements Hedenmalm's differential pair cannot be a
finite-order polynomial-coefficient operator decorated by finitely many prime phases. Such an
operator either fails to descend through the theta metric or becomes only a function of the
already present momentum. A viable construction must carry arithmetic mixing through at least one
of:

1. a genuinely nonlocal matrix or kernel between divisor atoms;
2. an operator with cofinally infinite prime-frequency support and a separately proved closed
   domain;
3. the all-prime weights of the metric itself;
4. an equivalent global incidence constraint outside `A_fin`.

This is a positive carrier theorem, not merely a no-go: the missing Hilbert--Pólya datum is forced
to be a nonlocal all-prime incidence on the divisor-supported rigging.

## 7. Boundary cases

1. **Finite sampling support.** The conclusion fails: polynomial coefficients can vanish on a
   finite set without vanishing identically.
2. **Linear-density lattices.** The conclusion fails: if `S=hZ`, the shift `S_h` preserves `I(S)`.
   Superlinear sampling density is the sharp mechanism excluding finite translations.
3. **One prime dilation.** One translation gives only a discrete symmetry group. Two
   multiplicatively independent scales are needed to force full translation invariance.
4. **Infinite prime support.** The theorem does not exclude an infinite modulation series; domain,
   convergence, and nonlocality become substantive and are precisely where the arithmetic carrier
   may live.
5. **Partial divisor support.** An infinite sparse subset of zeta ordinates might possess unknown
   translation structure. The unconditional Xi application uses conservativity on the full real
   divisor.
6. **RH.** No step proves that the positive metric is nondegenerate on eigenvectors coming from
   nonreal zeros. That remains equivalent to RH in the Hedenmalm architecture.

### The mechanism

Putnam's kernel is

```text
C_a(x) = {e^x} e^{-(1/2+ia)x}   (x >= 0),      C_a(x) = e^{(1/2-ia)x}   (x < 0),
```

with Fourier transform `-zeta(1/2+i(a+xi))/(1/2+i(a+xi))`.  Its periodisation `D_a` with period
`P = 2pi/d` has Fourier coefficients proportional to `zeta(1/2+i(a+dn))/(1/2+i(a+dn))`, `n in Z`.
A one-sided progression of critical zeros kills exactly the coefficients of **nonnegative** index.

The new argument shows that this is already impossible, without ever touching the negative half of
the spectrum.  Write `cf(y) = 1 - r e^{idy}` for `0 <= r < 1`.  Two facts about the pairing
`J(r) = integral_0^P D_a(y) cf(y)^{-1} dy` collide:

1. **Boundedness.**  The conjugate kernel `conj(cf^{-1}) = (1 - r e^{-idy})^{-1}` expands in
   *nonnegative* powers of `r e^{-idy}`, so the conjugate pairing vanishes by hypothesis (this is
   the only place the hypothesis is used, and it is one-sided).  Adding the two pairings and
   subtracting the mean turns the Cauchy kernel into the Poisson kernel
   `2 Re cf^{-1} - 1 = (1-r^2)/|cf|^2 >= 0`, of total mass `P`.  Hence `|J(r)| <= ||D_a||_infty P`
   for every `r`.
2. **Blow-up.**  The imaginary part `K_r = Im cf^{-1}` is nonnegative on the half period `(0,P/2]`,
   odd under `y |-> P-y`, and has mass `d^{-1} log((1+r)/(1-r)) -> infinity`.  Folding the second
   half period onto the first turns the pairing into
   `integral_0^{P/2} (D_a(y) - D_a(P-y)) K_r(y) dy`, and by periodicity the integrand tends to the
   jump `D_a(0+) - D_a(0-)` as `y -> 0+`.  That jump is
   `sum_{k >= 0, e^{kP} in Z} e^{-(1/2+ia)kP}`, a geometric series of ratio of modulus `< 1`, hence
   never zero.  Splitting the folded integral at a small `delta` gives
   `|J(r)|/2 >= (1/2)|jump| * mass(r) - O_delta(1)`, which is unbounded.

The two facts are incompatible, so some coefficient of nonnegative index is nonzero.
