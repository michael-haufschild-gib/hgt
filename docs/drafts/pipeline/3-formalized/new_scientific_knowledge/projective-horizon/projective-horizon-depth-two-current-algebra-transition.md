# Projective horizons undergo a depth-two current-algebra transition

- **ID:** `projective-horizon-depth-two-current-algebra-transition`
- **Stage:** 3-formalized
- **Type:** DIFFERENTIAL-FIELD INTERSECTION / CURRENT-ALGEBRA PHASE TRANSITION
- **Scientific status:** `[PROVED ON PAPER] [NOVELTY CANDIDATE] [SIGNIFICANT STRUCTURAL JUMP]`
- **Parents:** `projective-horizon-building-schwarzian-complementarity`,
  `nonlinear-rational-observation-curve-horizon-reconstruction`
- **Statement:** Opposite projective derivative horizons of an HT scalar share Schwarzian
  curvature at depth one, but from depth two onward their intersection is exactly the constant
  field. The transition occurs because position-and-velocity jet gauges generate the full
  polynomial current algebra `sl_2(C[t])`.

## Set-up

Let `C` be an algebraically closed constant field of characteristic zero, let `x` be
hypertranscendental over `C`, and put

```text
F=C<x>,
x_j=delta^j x.
```

For `p in P^1(C)` and `n>=1`, choose a Möbius coordinate `M_p` taking `p` to infinity and define

```text
mathcal H_(p,n)=C<delta^n(M_p x)>.                         (1)
```

The field is independent of the choice of `M_p`, because two choices differ by a constant affine
map and every positive derivative changes only by a nonzero constant factor.

## Theorem: the depth transition

For every pair `p != q`,

```text
mathcal H_(p,n) mathcal H_(q,n)=F          for every n>=1, (2)
```

while the common field is

```text
mathcal H_(p,1) intersection mathcal H_(q,1)=C<S(x)>,      (3)
mathcal H_(p,n) intersection mathcal H_(q,n)=C  for n>=2. (4)
```

Thus depth one retains the full projective curvature field. Deleting one additional jet destroys
all nonconstant common rational differential information, although the two horizons remain jointly
complete.

## Proof

Projective covariance reduces to `p=infinity`, `q=0`, so

```text
A_n=C(x_n,x_(n+1),...),
B_n=C((x^(-1))_n,(x^(-1))_(n+1),...).                     (5)
```

Equation `(2)` is the all-depth nonlinear observation-curve reconstruction theorem applied to the
two observations `x` and `x^(-1)`. Equation `(3)` is the exact scalar projective invariant theorem:
the fixed field of `PGL_2(C)` on the free jet field is generated differentially by the Schwarzian.

It remains to prove `(4)`.

### Finite-jet gauge groups

Because the jets `x_0,x_1,...` are algebraically independent,

```text
F=C(x_0,x_1,...).
```

Let `T_n congruent G_a^n` translate `x_0,...,x_(n-1)` independently and fix all later jets. As an
ordinary rational field action,

```text
A_n=F^(T_n).                                               (6)
```

Let `iota` be differential inversion `x |-> x^(-1)`. Then

```text
B_n=F^(iota T_n iota^(-1)).                               (7)
```

Hence every element of `A_n intersection B_n` is invariant under both finite-jet gauge groups.

### Infinitesimal current algebra

Package jet variables as the formal arc

```text
x(t)=sum_(j>=0) x_j t^j/j!.
```

A translation of the first `n` jets has infinitesimal characteristic

```text
E_a=a(t) partial_x,                 deg a<n.               (8)
```

Conjugating a translation of `1/x` by inversion gives

```text
F_b=-b(t)x^2 partial_x,             deg b<n.               (9)
```

Put `H_c=-2c(t)x partial_x`. Direct Lie-bracket calculation gives

```text
[E_a,F_b]=H_(ab),
[H_c,E_a]=2E_(ca),
[H_c,F_b]=-2F_(cb).                                      (10)
```

At depth one, only constant `a,b` occur, so `(10)` generates the constant algebra `sl_2(C)` and
its nontrivial differential invariant is the Schwarzian.

At depth two, both `1` and `t` occur. In particular,

```text
H_t=[E_1,F_t],
E_(t^(k+1))=(1/2)[H_t,E_(t^k)],
F_(t^(k+1))=-(1/2)[H_t,F_(t^k)].                         (11)
```

Induction on `k` shows that the Lie algebra generated by the two depth-two gauge groups contains
all modes

```text
E_(t^k), F_(t^k), H_(t^k),   k>=0,
```

hence the full polynomial current algebra

```text
sl_2(C[t]).                                                (12)
```

Every `n>=2` contains the same depth-two generators, so `(12)` persists at all deeper horizons.

### Vanishing of common invariants

Let `f in A_n intersection B_n`. It is annihilated by the Lie algebra generated by the two gauge
actions, hence by every translation mode `E_(t^k)`. At the jet origin, `E_(t^k)` is a nonzero
constant multiple of `partial/partial x_k`. Since `f` depends on only finitely many algebraically
independent jet variables,

```text
partial f/partial x_k=0   for every k
```

forces `f in C`. The reverse inclusion is immediate, proving `(4)`.

## Exact information regimes

For distinct `p,q`, the two-horizon diagram has three regimes:

```text
n=0:  both fields are F;
n=1:  full join F, common curvature C<S(x)>;
n>=2: full join F, trivial meet C.                         (13)
```

The last line is a genuine field-theoretic two-share complementarity law: neither horizon shares
any nonconstant datum with the other, but together they recover every jet of the HT source.

## Physical interpretation

The transition is a change of gauge type, not a source evolution.

- depth one forgets position and exposes a global projective gauge `PGL_2(C)`;
- depth two also forgets velocity and promotes the gauge to time-polynomial projective modes;
- commutators generate arbitrarily high modes, producing the current algebra `sl_2(C[t])`;
- the current gauge has no nonconstant rational invariant on the free HT jet arc.

In the hyperbolic celestial-sphere picture, two geodesic endpoints share Schwarzian curvature at
the first horizon. Beyond the velocity horizon, their common observable algebra becomes vacuum
`C`, even though endpoint complementarity still reconstructs the source. This is an exact
holographic secret-sharing pattern with no entropy or quantum-mechanical claim.

## An elementary route to `(2)` that avoids differential Luroth

The cited proof of `(2)` runs through Lemma 1 of the observation-curve card, which invokes
differential Luroth to place `R'(x)` in the join, and then Lemma 2 to separate branches.  For the
projective pair `x` and `x^(-1)` the join can instead be exhibited by a linear system with an
explicit determinant certificate, using no Luroth input at all.

Write `y=x^(-1)` and `y_j=delta^j y`.  From `x y=1` the Leibniz relations

```text
sum_(k=0)^m binom(m,k) x_k y_(m-k) = 0,        m >= 1,
```

hold.  A term `x_k y_(m-k)` has both factors unknown only when `k <= n-1` and `m-k <= n-1`, which
forces `m <= 2n-2`.  So for every `m >= 2n-1` the relation is *linear* in the `2n` unknowns
`x_0,...,x_(n-1)` and `y_0,...,y_(n-1)`, with all coefficients and the constant term inside
`A_n B_n`.  Taking the `2n` rows `m = 2n-1,...,4n-2` gives a square system whose matrix is

```text
Theta[m,k]   = binom(m,k) y_(m-k),      k = 0,...,n-1,
Theta[m,n+j] = binom(m,j) x_(m-j),      j = 0,...,n-1.
```

If `det Theta != 0` then Cramer's rule puts `x_0` in `A_n B_n`, and since that field is
differential, every jet follows, so `(2)` holds.

Nonvanishing is certified by the exponential specialisation, which sends every jet coordinate to
the same value; there `x_j = 1` and `y_j = (-1)^j`, and the matrix becomes the integer matrix

```text
M[m,k] = (-1)^(m-k) binom(m,k),    M[m,n+j] = binom(m,j).
```

A kernel vector `(alpha,beta)` of `M` satisfies, for the `n` even rows,
`sum_k binom(m,k)((-1)^k alpha_k + beta_k) = 0`, and for the `n` odd rows,
`sum_k binom(m,k)(-(-1)^k alpha_k + beta_k) = 0`.  In each case the left side is a polynomial in
`m` of degree below `n` vanishing at `n` distinct points, hence identically zero; the binomials
`binom(-,k)` for `k < n` are a basis of that polynomial space, so all coefficients vanish and
`alpha = beta = 0`.  Therefore `det M != 0` and `det Theta != 0`.

The specialisation is realised on numerators rather than on the fraction field: each `y_j` equals a
polynomial over `x_0^(j+1)`, the numerators satisfy a two-term recursion, and substituting one
variable for every jet coordinate turns the jet shift into the Euler operator, sending the `j`-th
numerator to `(-1)^j` times the `j`-th power.
