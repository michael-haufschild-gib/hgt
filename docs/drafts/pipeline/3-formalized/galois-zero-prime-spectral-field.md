# The prime spectral field and the effective exponent action

- **ID:** galois-zero-prime-spectral-field
- **Stage:** 3-formalized
- **Type:** THEOREM (Lean)
- **Questions:** Prime-value coefficient fields; faithful arithmetic action; rational relations.

## Target

Let `Δ` be the entire integer zero group and
`E=ℚ(p^z : p prime, z∈Δ)⊂ℂ`. This is also the field generated by
`p^ρ` for the actual zeros `ρ`, since integer exponent relations become
products and inverses. Retain all prime translations.

Compatibility means that, for each `z`, there is one common exponent `w`
with `σ(p^z)=p^w` at every prime. It is exactly the condition for a unique
pointed lift commuting with all prime translations.

A scalar embedding compatible with every prime has a unique additive
injection `φ:Δ→Δ`. It preserves `E`. Two such scalar embeddings have
the same action on `Δ` exactly when they agree on `E`; in particular,
the kernel of the exponent action consists exactly of embeddings fixing
`E` pointwise. For compatible automorphisms in both directions, restriction
gives an automorphism of `E` and a faithful effective exponent action.

Every compatible embedding fixes `1∈Δ`, hence fixes `Δ∩ℚ` pointwise.
Consequently it fixes the entire radical subfield
`E_rat=ℚ(p^r : p prime, r∈Δ∩ℚ)` pointwise.
Ordinary conjugation preserves the full prime spectrum and both fields,
and acts nontrivially on `E`. The field `E_rat` is real and algebraic over `ℚ`,
whereas `E` contains a transcendental element.

## Paper proof

The two-prime scalar-descent theorem constructs the unique additive
injection `φ`. Commutation at every further prime is exactly
`σ(p^z)=p^(φz)`, so `σ` preserves the field generated by these values.
Field closure shows that equality on every prime generator is equality
on `E`. Conversely equality on `E` gives equality on both the two- and
three-prime generators; their joint injectivity recovers equality of the
exponent maps. Conversely, pointwise fixing of `E` automatically gives
full prime compatibility and trivial exponent action. The inverse-compatible
case gives mutually inverse
restrictions to `E`.

Since `σ` fixes rational constants, the joint spectrum of `φ(1)` is
`(2,3)`, hence `φ(1)=1`. If `nz=m` with integers `n≠0,m`, additivity
then gives `nφ(z)=m`, so torsion-freeness gives `φ(z)=z`. Clearing the
denominator of each rational exponent proves the rational fixed-field
claim.

Conjugation sends `p^z` to `p^(conjugate z)` and preserves the zero group.
For any actual zero `ρ`, its imaginary part is nonzero. If both `2^ρ`
and `3^ρ` were real, their values would agree with those at `conjugate ρ`;
two-prime separation would force `ρ=conjugate ρ`, a contradiction.
Thus conjugation is nontrivial on the actual prime spectral field.

Rational prime powers are algebraic, and algebraic elements form a
subfield, so `E_rat` is algebraic. Conjugation fixes it pointwise by the
compatibility theorem, so it is real. If all prime generators were algebraic,
the three-prime exponent theorem would make every actual zero rational,
contradicting its nonzero imaginary part. Thus `E` is not algebraic.

## Boundary

This field is defined by actual prime-time values, not by an assumed
algebraic model of zeros. Its mixed-prime polynomial relations and its
full arithmetic automorphism group remain undetermined. No equality
with the contour-trace source field, purity, or normality over `ℚ` is
claimed.
