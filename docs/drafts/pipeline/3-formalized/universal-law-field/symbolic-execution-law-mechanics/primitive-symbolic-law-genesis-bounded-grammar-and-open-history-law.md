## 1. Why this theorem is the next field law

ULF-SELM-1 separates two claims:

1. every declared failure test admits a law;
2. current legal pieces can execute that law.

The triangle proves that the first does not imply the second.  That alone leaves the central field
problem untouched: how can persistent failure produce a new legal grammar without inserting an
arbitrary operation?

The answer below is a mechanism.  The failure family itself canonically determines all
indecomposable admitted laws.  These primitives generate the entire admitted sector.  No primitive
is selected by an observer, endpoint, or route.

## 2. Symbolic laws, dominated splitting, and primitive genesis

Let `I` be a set and let `H` be a finite family of finite subsets of `I`.  A weighted law is a pair

\[
 (m,a),\qquad m\in\mathbb N,\quad a:I\to\mathbb N.
\]

It is **locally admitted** when

\[
 \operatorname{Loc}_H(m,a)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 m\le \sum_{i\in K}a_i\quad\text{for every }K\in H.
 \tag{PG.1}
\]

A positive locally admitted law `(m,a)` has a **dominated positive split** when there are
`m_1,m_2>0` and locally admitted `(m_1,a_1),(m_2,a_2)` such that

\[
 m=m_1+m_2,
 \qquad
 a_1(i)+a_2(i)\le a(i)\quad(i\in I).
 \tag{PG.2}
\]

It is **primitive** when it has no dominated positive split.  This definition depends only on
`H`, addition, and the coordinate order.

Define the canonical primitive grammar inductively:

1. every primitive law is generated;
2. if `(m_1,a_1)` and `(m_2,a_2)` are generated and
   `a_1+a_2\le a`, then `(m_1+m_2,a)` is generated.

For a cutoff `D`, the **D-bounded primitive grammar** uses only primitive atoms of degree at most
`D` and the same composition law.

## 3. Canonical primitive-generation theorem

### Theorem 3.1 -- generated laws remain locally admitted

Every law generated by the canonical primitive grammar has positive grade and is locally admitted.
The same is true for every `D`-bounded primitive grammar.

### Proof

A primitive atom is positive and locally admitted by definition.  Suppose the assertion holds for
`(m_1,a_1)` and `(m_2,a_2)`, and `a_1+a_2\le a`.  For each `K\in H`,

\[
 m_1+m_2
 \le \sum_{i\in K}a_1(i)+\sum_{i\in K}a_2(i)
 =\sum_{i\in K}(a_1(i)+a_2(i))
 \le\sum_{i\in K}a(i).
\]

The sum of two positive grades is positive.  Induction on the generation derivation proves both
claims.  ∎

### Theorem 3.2 -- every positive local law has a canonical primitive execution

For every `m>0` and `a`,

\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{Loc}_H(m,a)
 \Longrightarrow
 (m,a)\text{ is generated by the canonical primitive grammar}.}
 \tag{PG.3}
\]

### Proof

Use strong induction on `m`.  If `(m,a)` has no dominated positive split, it is primitive and hence
an atom.  Otherwise choose a split `(PG.2)`.  Positivity and `m=m_1+m_2` imply
`m_1,m_2<m`.  The induction hypothesis generates both smaller laws, and the composition rule
generates `(m,a)`.  The induction is on grade, so no finite-dimensional or Noetherian hypothesis is
needed.  ∎

This is a source-normal grammar-production law: it constructs a complete grammar from the admitted
laws themselves, without choosing a preferred defect.

## 4. Exact bounded-grammar classification

Say primitive degrees are bounded by `D` when

\[
 \operatorname{Prim}_H(m,a)\Longrightarrow m\le D.
 \tag{PG.4}
\]

### Theorem 4.1

The following are equivalent:

1. every primitive degree is at most `D`;
2. every positive locally admitted law is generated by the `D`-bounded primitive grammar.

### Proof

If all primitive degrees are at most `D`, take the derivation supplied by Theorem 3.2 and retain it:
every atom already satisfies the cutoff.

Conversely, let `(m,a)` be primitive and apply the assumed bounded-generation theorem to it.  A
composite final step would exhibit exactly a dominated positive split of `(m,a)`, contradicting
primitivity.  The derivation must therefore be one bounded primitive atom, so `m\le D`.  ∎

### Corollary 4.2 -- exact open primitive history

If for every `D` there is a primitive law of degree greater than `D`, then every `D`-bounded legal
grammar misses a positive locally admitted law.

Thus unbounded primitive degree—not nontermination, persistent grade, noncompactness, or
hypertranscendence by itself—is the exact obstruction to one bounded grammar generating the local
law sector.

## 5. Triangle calibration: one primitive closes the persistent family

Let

\[
 H_\triangle=\{\{0,1\},\{1,2\},\{0,2\}\},
 \qquad
 c=(1,1,1).
\]

### Theorem 5.1

The law `(2,c)` is primitive.

### Proof

It is locally admitted because every failure pair sees weight two.  Suppose it had a dominated
positive split.  The two positive grades must both equal one.  ULF-SELM-1 proves that every
grade-one triangle law has total weight at least two.  The two pieces therefore have combined total
weight at least four.  Domination by `c` bounds their combined total weight by three, contradiction.
∎

### Theorem 5.2

For every `n>0`, the persistent ULF-SELM-1 law

\[
 (2n,(n,n,n))
 \tag{PG.5}
\]

is generated by `n` copies of the one primitive `(2,c)`.  Hence a degree-two grammar closes the
entire displayed family, although the old grade-one grammar misses every member.

### Proof

Induct on `n`.  The case `n=1` is Theorem 5.1.  Composition adds grades and weights, so adjoining
one further copy sends `(2n,(n,n,n))` to `(2(n+1),(n+1,n+1,n+1))`.  ∎

This is the hostile boundary needed by the field programme: persistent symbolic/execution failure
does not imply unbounded primitive history.  One higher-degree law may explain infinitely many old
failures.

## 6. Mechanism laws

The theorem yields six route-independent laws.

1. **Admission law.** Failure kernels define a graded local law sector.
2. **Primitive-genesis law.** Indecomposability canonically creates the next legal atoms.
3. **Execution law.** Dominated addition composes legal atoms.
4. **Strictification law.** The full primitive grammar generates every positive local law.
5. **Finite-horizon law.** A bounded grammar exists exactly when primitive degrees are bounded.
6. **Open-history law.** Unbounded primitive degree defeats every bounded grammar.

This is not closure-ordinal saturation.  ULF-LSM-1 studies repeated least repairs under supplied
operations.  Here the source failure inequalities construct the legal atoms themselves.  It is also
not ULF-PCG-2: that theorem tests whether a supplied operation is effective.  Here all
indecomposable admitted laws are produced without a supplied update oracle.

## 7. Hostile scope

1. The primitive grammar may be infinite and may contain arbitrarily high degrees.
2. The theorem generates an abstract execution grammar; it does not prove a literal corpus route
   realizes its failure family.
3. Source-canonicity means no choice of one primitive is made.  It does not imply invariance under
   every change of presentation; a later theorem must type admissible equivalences of failure
   systems.
4. Primitive generation does not supply positivity, topology, physical time, a carrier, or an RH
   endpoint.
5. Unbounded primitive degree is a necessary mechanism for open bounded-grammar history, not a
   definition or proof of hypertranscendence.
6. Finite squarefree symbolic Rees algebras may have bounded primitive degree.  Their finite
   generation is compatible with the theorem.
7. The construction takes all primitives.  A computationally economical or dynamically selected
   subgrammar requires an additional theorem.
