\[

## 2. Exact regular amplification

### Theorem 2.1 -- trace and Hilbert--Schmidt dichotomy

For every (n\ge2) and (q\in\mathbb Z), (L_{n,q}) is unitary and

\[
 \boxed{
 L_{n,q}=I
 \Longleftrightarrow n\mid q.}
 \tag{RIA.2}
\]

Its trace and Hilbert--Schmidt displacement are

\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{Tr}L_{n,q}
 =\begin{cases}
 n,&n\mid q,\\
 0,&n\nmid q,
 \end{cases}}
 \tag{RIA.3}
\]

\[
 \boxed{
 \|L_{n,q}-I\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
 =\begin{cases}
 0,&n\mid q,\\
 2n,&n\nmid q.
 \end{cases}}
 \tag{RIA.4}
\]

Thus the normalized regular cost

\[
 c_n(q)=\frac1n\|L_{n,q}-I\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
 \tag{RIA.5}
\]

is exactly (0) or (2).  The scalar holonomy tends to (1) as (n\to\infty), but the regular
packet retains a uniform defect.

#### Proof

Equation (RIA.1) permutes an orthonormal basis, hence is unitary.  It is the identity exactly when
([q]_n=0).  Its trace counts basis points fixed by translation by ([q]_n).  Every point is fixed
when ([q]_n=0), and no point is fixed otherwise, proving (RIA.3).

For the Hilbert--Schmidt norm,

\[
 \|L_{n,q}-I\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
 =\sum_{x\in C_n}\|e_{x+[q]_n}-e_x\|^2.
\]

Every summand is zero in the identity case.  Otherwise the two basis vectors are distinct and each
summand is (2), proving (RIA.4). ∎

This is an amplification law: regular inertia converts an asymptotically small scalar phase into a
persistent positive operator defect.

## 3. Cycle, rank, spectrum, and recurrence

Put

\[
 d_n(q)=\gcd(n,|q|),
 \qquad
 \ell_n(q)=n/d_n(q),
 \tag{RIA.6}
\]

with (\gcd(n,0)=n).

### Theorem 3.1 -- exact cycle normal form

The permutation (x\mapsto x+[q]_n) has exactly (d_n(q)) cycles, each of length
(\ell_n(q)).  Consequently:

\[
 \boxed{\dim\ker(I-L_{n,q})=d_n(q),}
 \tag{RIA.7}
\]

\[
 \boxed{\operatorname{rank}(I-L_{n,q})=n-d_n(q),}
 \tag{RIA.8}
\]

\[
 \boxed{\det L_{n,q}=(-1)^{n-d_n(q)},}
 \tag{RIA.9}
\]

and the spectrum consists of every (\ell_n(q))-th root of unity with multiplicity (d_n(q)).
The exact recurrence period is (\ell_n(q)).

#### Proof

The cyclic subgroup generated by ([q]_n) has order (n/\gcd(n,|q|)).  Its cosets are precisely
the translation cycles, giving (RIA.6) and the cycle count.  A vector fixed by the permutation is
constant on each cycle, proving (RIA.7); rank--nullity gives (RIA.8).  A cycle of length (ell)
has sign ((-1)^{\ell-1}), so (d) cycles have sign
((-1)^{d(\ell-1)}=(-1)^{n-d}), proving (RIA.9).  The Fourier basis on each cycle gives all
(ell)-th roots once per cycle. ∎

### Corollary 3.2 -- marked versus unmarked information

\[

The unmarked unitary conjugacy class recovers only (d_n(q)), equivalently the order

## 4. Canonical cofinal action

Define the normalized regular action

\[
 \mathcal A_{\mathrm{reg}}(q)
 =\sum_{n\ge2}\frac1n\|L_{n,q}-I\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2.
 \tag{RIA.10}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 -- canonical cofinal properness

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal A_{\mathrm{reg}}(q)
 =\begin{cases}
 0,&q=0,\\
 +\infty,&q\ne0.
 \end{cases}}
 \tag{RIA.11}
\]

For (N\ge\max(2,|q|)) and (q\ne0), the truncated action is exactly

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{2\le n\le N}\frac1n\|L_{n,q}-I\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
 =2\bigl(N-\tau(|q|)\bigr),}
 \tag{RIA.12}
\]

where (\tau(k)) is the number of positive divisors of (k).

#### Proof

If (q=0), every translation is the identity.  If (q\ne0), (RIA.5) says the depth-(n) term is
(0) exactly for positive divisors (n\ge2) of (|q|), and is (2) otherwise.  For
(N\ge|q|), the interval (2,\ldots,N) contains (N-1) integers and exactly
(\tau(|q|)-1) nontrivial positive divisors of (|q|).  Subtraction gives (RIA.12), which tends to
infinity. ∎

Regular inertia therefore supplies the linear capacity missing from the scalar-character action:
dimension (n) cancels the (1/n) normalization and leaves a persistent cost.
