## 2. Source operations and their two polarities

Let `X` be a set and let `(a_i:X->X)_(i in I)` be a family of source operations.  The
**unitization relation** of `a_i` is

\[
 U_i(x,y)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad y=a_i(x).
 \tag{EU.1}
\]

Let

\[
 U=\bigcup_{i\in I}U_i,
 \qquad \widehat U=\operatorname{EqvGen}(U).
 \tag{EU.2}
\]

For a word `w=i_1...i_n`, write

\[
 a_w(x)=a_{i_n}\cdots a_{i_1}(x).
 \tag{EU.3}
\]

The relation `U` is the identification pressure generated by making every primitive operation
observer-invisible.  The exchange pair of two operations `f,g` at `x` is

\[
 e_{f,g}(x)=\bigl(f(gx),g(fx)\bigr).
 \tag{EU.4}
\]

When its two endpoints differ, the source operations themselves generate the canonical distinction
task

\[
 \delta_{f(gx)}\quad\hbox{versus}\quad\delta_{g(fx)},
 \tag{EU.5}
\]

where `delta_z` is the Dirac profile at `z`.  This reporter is natural under relabelling by
pushforward; it is not chosen after seeing a target obstruction.

## 3. Word-orbit saturation theorem

### Theorem 3.1

For every source point `x` and every formation word `w`,

\[
 \boxed{\widehat U(x,a_w(x)).}
 \tag{EU.6}
\]

Consequently every two word endpoints from the same source point are equivalent:

\[
 \boxed{\widehat U(a_v(x),a_w(x)).}
 \tag{EU.7}
\]

### Proof

Induct on the word.  The empty word gives reflexivity.  If `(EU.6)` holds for `w`, then
`U(a_w(x),a_i(a_w(x)))` by `(EU.1)`, so transitivity gives the claim for `wi`.  Equation `(EU.7)`
follows from symmetry and transitivity through `x`.  QED.

### Corollary 3.2 -- observer form

Let `q:X->Y` satisfy

\[
 q(a_i x)=q(x)\qquad(i\in I,x\in X).
 \tag{EU.8}
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{q(a_wx)=q(x)}
 \tag{EU.9}
\]

for every word `w`.  Hence every observer that unitizes all primitive laws is constant on the
entire generated formation orbit.

### Proof

Induct on `w` and use `(EU.8)` at each step.  Equivalently, `q` respects `U`, hence respects
`EqvGen(U)` by ULF-LSM-5.  QED.

## 4. Exchange self-erasure law

### Theorem 4.1

For any two source operations `f,g` and every `x`,

\[
 \boxed{\widehat U\bigl(f(gx),g(fx)\bigr).}
 \tag{EU.10}
\]

Thus every observer jointly unitizing `f` and `g` obeys

\[
 \boxed{q(f(gx))=q(g(fx)).}
 \tag{EU.11}
\]

even when the source schedules do not commute.

### Proof

Both endpoints are word endpoints from `x`, so `(EU.10)` is `(EU.7)`.  Directly, one has the
generated path

\[
 f(gx)\sim gx\sim x\sim fx\sim g(fx).
 \tag{EU.12}
\]

Applying any observer satisfying `(EU.8)` gives `(EU.11)`.  QED.

This is **self-erasure**: strict endpoint execution of both laws deletes the internal evidence that
their order mattered.  The result is stronger than forcing one selected critical pair to commute;
the whole generated word orbit is collapsed.

## 5. Exact canonical-reporter loss

Assume now that `X` is finite and

\[
 f(gx)\ne g(fx).
 \tag{EU.13}
\]

The source total variation between the two canonical Dirac profiles in `(EU.5)` is exactly one:

\[
 \operatorname{TV}(\delta_{f(gx)},\delta_{g(fx)})=1.
 \tag{EU.14}
\]

For every finite observer `q` jointly unitizing `f` and `g`, `(EU.11)` implies

\[
 q_*\delta_{f(gx)}=q_*\delta_{g(fx)},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{TV}(q_*\delta_{f(gx)},q_*\delta_{g(fx)})=0.
 \tag{EU.15}
\]

Hence the joint executable saturation loses exactly one unit of canonical distinguishability.

### Proof

Distinct Dirac masses have disjoint support, so their half-`l1` distance is one.  Pushforward sends
each Dirac mass to the Dirac mass at its observed endpoint.  Equation `(EU.11)` makes those two
pushforwards equal.  QED.

This is the ULF-LSM-5 reporter-balance law with a reporter derived from the same operations that
generate the identifications.

## 6. Sharp three-state source-generated critical triple

Let `X={0,1,2}` and define two idempotent retractions

\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
x&0&1&2\\ \hline
f(x)&0&0&2\\
g(x)&0&2&2.
\end{array}
\tag{EU.16}
\]

Then

\[
 f^2=f,\qquad g^2=g,
 \tag{EU.17}
\]

while at the middle state

\[
 f(g1)=2,\qquad g(f1)=0.
 \tag{EU.18}
\]

The individual unitization quotients are

\[
 X/U_f=\{\{0,1\},\{2\}\},
 \qquad
 X/U_g=\{\{0\},\{1,2\}\}.
 \tag{EU.19}
\]

Each preserves the canonical task `delta_0` versus `delta_2` exactly.  The joint generated relation
contains `0~1~2`, so its quotient is one point and loses the task from total variation one to zero.

Thus one pre-target algebraic signature forces:

1. two identification formations;
2. one canonical schedule distinction;
3. pairwise realizability;
4. joint incompatibility;
5. an exact response not inserted into the source.

Three states are minimal.  With fewer than three states, two equivalence relations that each
preserve a nontrivial distinguished pair cannot have a join that collapses that pair.
