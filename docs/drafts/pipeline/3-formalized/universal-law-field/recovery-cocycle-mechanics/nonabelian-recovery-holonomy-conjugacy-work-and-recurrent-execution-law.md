## 1. Why the abelian theorem was not enough

ULF-RCM-1 treats a persistent ledger as a vector and changes ledger frame by adding a
coboundary.  That model cannot distinguish two source schedules whose individual operations are
legal but whose order matters.  Replacing vector addition by a group law changes all three central
statements:

1. periodic sums become ordered holonomy products;
2. gauge invariants become conjugacy classes, not vectors;
3. harmonic averaging need not exist because a group element need not have a canonical root.

The replacement below is exact.  It does not postulate a curvature.  The persistent ledger is
generated by execution order itself.

## 2. Group-valued recovery ledgers

Let `S` be a finite set, let `T:S->S` be a permutation, and let `G` be a group.  A ledger is a map

\[
 c:S\longrightarrow G.
 \tag{NRC.1}
\]

For a state-dependent change of ledger frame `h:S->G`, define

\[
 (h\cdot c)(s)=h(Ts)c(s)h(s)^{-1}.
 \tag{NRC.2}
\]

If `C=(s_0,...,s_(m-1))` is a `T`-cycle, indexed by `Ts_i=s_(i+1)` modulo `m`, put

\[
 H_C(c)=c(s_{m-1})\cdots c(s_1)c(s_0).
 \tag{NRC.3}
\]

### Theorem 2.1 -- exact nonabelian gauge classification

1. Gauge changes conjugate cycle holonomy:

   \[
   \boxed{H_C(h\cdot c)=h(s_0)H_C(c)h(s_0)^{-1}.}
   \tag{NRC.4}
   \]

2. Changing the marked basepoint on one cycle also conjugates holonomy.  Hence the conjugacy class
   `[H_C(c)]` is intrinsic to the unbased observer cycle.
3. Two ledgers `c,d:S->G` are gauge equivalent exactly when their holonomies have the same
   conjugacy class on every `T`-cycle.
4. A ledger is gauge equivalent to the identity ledger exactly when every cycle holonomy is the
   identity.

#### Proof

In the product of the transformed edges, every adjacent pair
`h(s_i)^(-1)h(s_i)` cancels, leaving `(NRC.4)`.  A cyclic change of basepoint moves an initial
partial product from one end to the other and therefore conjugates the total product.

For the converse, work cyclewise.  Suppose

\[
 H_C(d)=aH_C(c)a^{-1}.
\]

Set `h(s_0)=a` and recursively define

\[
 h(s_{i+1})=d(s_i)h(s_i)c(s_i)^{-1}.
 \tag{NRC.5}
\]

Then `(h.c)(s_i)=d(s_i)` on every edge.  After one turn,

\[
 h(s_m)=H_C(d)h(s_0)H_C(c)^{-1}=a=h(s_0),
\]

so the recursion closes.  Doing this independently on every cycle gives one global gauge.  Taking
`d` to be the identity ledger proves item 4.  QED.

This is the nonabelian replacement for finite Livsic classification.  The invariant is not one
chosen matrix representative but the full conjugacy packet of cycle holonomies.

## 3. Exact word-metric work

Assume now that `G` has a finite symmetric generating set `A`.  Write `|g|` for the associated
word norm.  For a conjugacy class define its shortest length

\[
 \ell_{\rm conj}(g)=\min_{a\in G}|aga^{-1}|.
 \tag{NRC.6}
\]

The minimum exists because the nonempty set of candidate lengths is a subset of the nonnegative
integers.  On an `m`-cycle define

\[
 E_1(c)=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}|c(s_i)|,
 \qquad
 E_2(c)=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}|c(s_i)|^2.
 \tag{NRC.7}
\]

These raw energies depend on gauge.  Their infima over a gauge class do not.

For `L,m in N`, `m>0`, write

\[
 L=mq+r,\qquad 0\le r<m,
\]

and set

\[
 B_m(L)=(m-r)q^2+r(q+1)^2.
 \tag{NRC.8}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 -- conjugacy work and balanced quadratic work

For a ledger on an `m`-cycle with holonomy `H`,

\[
 \boxed{\min_{d\sim c}E_1(d)=\ell_{\rm conj}(H),}
 \tag{NRC.9}
\]

and

\[
 \boxed{\min_{d\sim c}E_2(d)=B_m(\ell_{\rm conj}(H)).}
 \tag{NRC.10}
\]

Both minima vanish exactly when the cycle holonomy is trivial.

#### Proof

Let `d` be gauge equivalent to `c`.  Its holonomy is conjugate to `H`, so the word triangle
inequality gives

\[
 \ell_{\rm conj}(H)\le |H_C(d)|\le\sum_i|d(s_i)|.
 \tag{NRC.11}
\]

Choose a shortest conjugate `H'=aHa^(-1)` of length `L=ell_conj(H)`.  The ledger with one edge
equal to `H'` and all other edges equal to the identity has holonomy `H'`; Theorem 2.1 puts it in
the gauge class of `c`.  This proves `(NRC.9)`.

For `(NRC.10)`, put `n_i=|d(s_i)|`.  Equation `(NRC.11)` gives `sum_i n_i>=L`.  Among `m`
nonnegative integers with sum at least `L`, the sum of squares is minimized at total sum `L` and
when all entries differ by at most one.  Replacing a pair `x>=y+2` by `(x-1,y+1)` strictly lowers
the sum of squares, proving the balancing claim and the lower bound `B_m(L)`.

Take a geodesic word of length `L` representing `H'` and cut it into `m` consecutive blocks, `r`
of length `q+1` and `m-r` of length `q`.  Every consecutive block of a geodesic word is geodesic;
otherwise replacing it by a shorter word would shorten the whole word.  Use those blocks as the
successive edge labels.  Their product is `H'` and their squared lengths sum to `B_m(L)`.
Theorem 2.1 again places this ledger in the original gauge class.  QED.

### Corollary 3.2 -- refinement separates invariant action from metric energy

Subdividing edges without changing the ordered product preserves the minimum linear work
`ell_conj(H)`.  The quadratic minimum changes with the number of available slots:

\[
 B_1(L)=L^2,
 \qquad
 B_m(L)=L\quad(m\ge L).
 \tag{NRC.12}
\]

Thus linear conjugacy work is refinement invariant, while quadratic work is a depth-dependent
realization.  A cofinal field law cannot use quadratic ledger energy as an absolute invariant
unless source depth is marked or a renormalization law is supplied.

This is the first sharp departure from abelian harmonic averaging.  The canonical nonabelian
object is a shortest conjugacy word; its quadratic realization is a balanced factorization, not a
group average or a chosen logarithm.

## 4. Common recovery remains information-neutral

Let `X` be a standard Borel local-latent space and let `K(s,dx)` be one Markov recovery kernel used
for every source law.  For a probability law `mu` on `S`, form

\[
 P_\mu(ds,dx,dg)=\mu(ds)K(s,dx)\delta_e(dg).
 \tag{NRC.13}
\]

After `n` executions, refresh the local latent state with `K(T^n s,dx)` and place in the persistent
ledger

\[
 C_n(s)=c(T^{n-1}s)\cdots c(Ts)c(s).
 \tag{NRC.14}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 -- nonabelian recovery isometry

For any two base laws `mu,nu` and every `n>=0`, with extended relative entropy allowed,

\[
 \boxed{
 D_{\rm KL}(L_c^nP_\mu\Vert L_c^nP_\nu)
 =D_{\rm KL}(P_\mu\Vert P_\nu)
 =D_{\rm KL}(\mu\Vert\nu).}
 \tag{NRC.15}
\]

#### Proof

The split chain rule gives the second equality because both packets have the same conditional
kernel `K(s,dx)delta_e`.  Executing the common Markov law gives the data-processing inequality from
input to output.  Conversely, the output observer coordinate is `T^n s`; applying `T^(-n)` to it
recovers the original base state.  Data processing in the reverse direction gives the opposite
inequality.  Therefore equality holds.  The ordered ledger product may be arbitrarily
noncommutative; it is the same deterministic conditional datum for both laws and contributes zero
conditional divergence.  QED.

Information neutrality therefore imposes neither trivial holonomy nor commutativity.  Relative
entropy measures distinguishability of base laws; nonabelian holonomy measures schedule history.

## 5. Schedule commutators generate the ledger

Let `A,B in G` be two marked reversible source operations.  On a four-step observer cycle take

\[
 c(s_0)=B^{-1},\quad c(s_1)=A^{-1},\quad
 c(s_2)=B,\quad c(s_3)=A.
 \tag{NRC.16}
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{H_C(c)=ABA^{-1}B^{-1}=[A,B].}
 \tag{NRC.17}
\]

### Corollary 5.1 -- endogenous nonabelian law birth

The schedule ledger is gauge trivial exactly when `A` and `B` commute.  If they do not commute,
every gauge representative pays at least

\[
 \ell_{\rm conj}([A,B])>0
 \tag{NRC.18}
\]

units of linear execution work per cycle, and at least

\[
 B_4(\ell_{\rm conj}([A,B]))>0
 \tag{NRC.19}
\]

units of quadratic work.

This closes the producer defect left open in the abelian ledger model: the persistent ledger need
not be appended as an arbitrary coordinate.  It is generated by the failure of two already marked
executions to commute.

## 6. Recurrent observer, open action

Let `pi:G->Q` be a homomorphism to a finite group.  After `k` turns around a cycle, the endpoint
ledger is `H^k`, while the finite observer sees `pi(H)^k`.

For the repeated experiment, keep the source marking that one turn is one copy of the same
`m`-edge execution law.  Equivalently, choose one gauge representative on the one-turn cycle and
repeat that representative `k` times.  Let `A_(p,k)^per` denote the infimum of accumulated
`p`-work under those cycle-periodic gauge changes.

### Theorem 6.1 -- finite recurrence with linearly open cycle-periodic work

The observed endpoint sequence `pi(H)^k` is periodic.  If `H` is nontrivial, then the least
cycle-periodic linear action of `k` repeated executions is

\[
 \boxed{A_{1,k}^{\rm per}=k\,\ell_{\rm conj}(H),}
 \tag{NRC.20}
\]

and the least cycle-periodic quadratic action at fixed cycle depth `m` is

\[
 \boxed{A_{2,k}^{\rm per}=k\,B_m(\ell_{\rm conj}(H)).}
 \tag{NRC.21}
\]

Both diverge linearly with `k`, even when `H` itself has finite order and the full endpoint ledger
is periodic.

#### Proof

Every sequence in a finite group is eventually periodic; powers of one element are periodic from
the identity.  The action is accumulated along the marked execution path, not recomputed from the
final endpoint.  A cycle-periodic gauge uses one representative on every turn.  Theorem 3.1 gives
its sharp per-turn minimum, and additivity gives `(NRC.20)--(NRC.21)`.  QED.

If one forgets the marked cycle decomposition and permits an arbitrary gauge on the whole
`km`-edge concatenation, the minimum linear work is instead `ell_conj(H^k)`.  It can be smaller,
and it vanishes when `H^k=e`.  This is not a defect in the theorem: it is the exact distinction
between execution-history action and endpoint refactorization.  Closed endpoint observation
forgets which of those two doctrines is being used.

Thus recurrence and open history no longer require an additive universal-cover coordinate.  A
finite nonabelian observer can close exactly while irreversible execution action continues to
grow.

## 7. Positive and functional-equation shadows

Let `rho:G->U(H)` be a unitary representation.  Every individual edge has the same positive Gram
shadow

\[
 \rho(c(s))^*\rho(c(s))=I.
 \tag{NRC.22}
\]

Nevertheless the cycle carries the ordered operator `rho(H_C(c))`, which can be nontrivial.  For a
faithful representation, positive edgewise Gram data therefore erase every one-step ledger value
while ordered holonomy still separates nontrivial gauge classes.

Now let `alpha:G->G` be an automorphism preserving the symmetric generating set.  Replace `T` by
`T^(-1)` and put

\[
 c^\sharp(s)=\alpha\!\left(c(T^{-1}s)^{-1}\right).
 \tag{NRC.23a}
\]

This is the edge label for traversing the old edge backwards and applying `alpha`.  On the reversed
cycle based at the same state, direct multiplication gives

\[
 H_C(c^\sharp)=\alpha(H_C(c)^{-1}).
 \tag{NRC.23b}
\]

A different basepoint only conjugates this value.  Since word norm is preserved by `alpha`,
inversion, and conjugation minimization,

\[
 \ell_{\rm conj}(\alpha(H^{-1}))=\ell_{\rm conj}(H).
 \tag{NRC.23}
\]

Hence both minimum works are unchanged.  Combined with Theorem 4.1:

\[
 \boxed{
 \text{functional reflection can be information-neutral and work-neutral
 without making schedule holonomy trivial}.}
 \tag{NRC.24}
\]

This rules out another scalar selector.  A reciprocal symmetry transports the nonabelian defect;
it does not cancel it.

## 9. Hostile boundaries

1. **The conjugacy packet is classical gauge data.**  Discrete connections and holonomy modulo
   gauge are established mathematics.  See Ashtekar--Lewandowski,
   [arXiv:gr-qc/9311010](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9311010), and the graph-gauge extension of
   Meusburger--Wise, [arXiv:1512.03966](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03966).  No novelty claim is made
   for nonabelian holonomy itself.
2. **Word work depends on the marked generator set.**  This is source data, not a gauge choice.  A
   presentation-free theory must replace it by a source-derived length, action, or bi-invariant
   metric.
3. **Quadratic work is not refinement invariant.**  Equation `(NRC.12)` is the exact failure, not
   an inconvenience to suppress.
4. **Finite observer invertibility is load-bearing.**  Noninvertible observers require path-groupoid
   or inverse-semigroup holonomy; cycle conjugacy alone is incomplete.
5. **Common recovery does not generate the group.**  The marked source operations `A,B` do.  If
   those operations are fitted after a target defect is read, Corollary 5.1 is theatre.
6. **Cycle marking is load-bearing.**  Arbitrary refactorization of a `k`-turn word measures
   `ell_conj(H^k)`, not `k ell_conj(H)`.  Linear open action belongs to repeated marked execution,
   not to the endpoint group element alone.
7. **No RH selection is claimed.**  An RH application must construct marked noncommuting source
   operations before divisor specialization and prove that a global source law forces their
   commutator holonomy to be trivial or finitely admitted with a proper source action.
