## 1. Source and admission domain

Let `mu` be a nonzero finite positive Borel measure on `R`. Define the extended moment response

\[
 M_\mu(s)=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{sx}\,d\mu(x)\in(0,\infty]
 \tag{CD.1}
\]

and its exponential-character domain

\[
 D(\mu)=\{s\in\mathbb R:M_\mu(s)<\infty\}.
 \tag{CD.2}
\]

The nonzero hypothesis ensures that every `M_mu(s)` is strictly positive, including when its value
is infinite. Since `mu` is finite, `0 in D(mu)`.

### Theorem 1.1 — convexity

`D(mu)` is a convex interval containing zero.

### Proof

Take `s,t in D(mu)` and `0<theta<1`. Generalized Holder gives

\[
 \int e^{(\theta s+(1-\theta)t)x}\,d\mu
 \le
 \left(\int e^{sx}\,d\mu\right)^\theta
 \left(\int e^{tx}\,d\mu\right)^{1-\theta}<\infty.
\]

The endpoint cases are immediate. Hence the domain is convex. QED.

The domain may be open, closed, half-open, bounded, or unbounded. No endpoint regularity is used
below.

## 2. Exact formation laws

For `c,d>0`, let `c mu+d nu` denote positive mixture. Let `mu*nu` be additive convolution. For
`a in D(mu)`, define normalized exponential intervention

\[
 d(T_a\mu)(x)=\frac{e^{ax}}{M_\mu(a)}\,d\mu(x).
 \tag{CD.3}
\]

Let `check mu` be reflection under `x -> -x`.

### Theorem 2.1 — character-domain calculus

For nonzero finite positive measures:

\[
\begin{aligned}
 D(c\mu+d\nu)&=D(\mu)\cap D(\nu), \\
 D(\mu*\nu)&=D(\mu)\cap D(\nu), \\
 D(T_a\mu)&=D(\mu)-a, \\
 D(\check\mu)&=-D(\mu).
\end{aligned}
\tag{CD.4}
\]

Moreover, imaginary modulation `e^{-itx}` changes no absolute exponential integrability.

### Proof

Positive mixture has

\[
 M_{c\mu+d\nu}(s)=cM_\mu(s)+dM_\nu(s).
\]

A positive sum is finite exactly when both summands are finite. For convolution, Tonelli and
`e^{s(x+y)}=e^{sx}e^{sy}` give

\[
 M_{\mu*\nu}(s)=M_\mu(s)M_\nu(s).
\]

Both factors are strictly positive, so their extended product is finite exactly when both are
finite. For intervention,

\[
 M_{T_a\mu}(s)=\frac{M_\mu(s+a)}{M_\mu(a)},
\]

where the denominator is finite and strictly positive. Reflection gives
`M_(check mu)(s)=M_mu(-s)`. Finally `|e^{-itx}|=1`, so phase modulation does not alter the absolute
integrand. QED.

### Corollary 2.2 — functional-equation symmetrization

The independently formed reflected pair `mu*check(mu)` has

\[
 D(\mu*\check\mu)=D(\mu)\cap(-D(\mu)).
 \tag{CD.5}
\]

Thus reflection does not enlarge admission. It extracts the maximal reflection-stable part of the
source domain.

## 3. Recovery neutrality and one-line reconstruction

Let `K(x,dy)` be a Markov kernel into an auxiliary standard Borel space and form the recovered
measure

\[
 \widetilde\mu(dx,dy)=\mu(dx)K(x,dy),
 \tag{CD.6}
\]

while retaining the clock `r(x,y)=x`.

### Theorem 3.1 — clock-preserving recovery is admission-neutral

\[
 D(\widetilde\mu,r)=D(\mu).
 \tag{CD.7}
\]

### Proof

Tonelli and `K(x,Y)=1` give

\[
 \int e^{s r(x,y)}\,d\widetilde\mu(x,y)
 =\int e^{sx}K(x,Y)\,d\mu(x)=M_\mu(s).
\]

QED.

### Theorem 3.2 — admitted-line reconstruction

Fix `a in D(mu)`. The complete vertical-line response

\[
 L_\mu(a+it)=\int e^{(a+it)x}\,d\mu(x),\qquad t\in\mathbb R,
 \tag{CD.8}
\]

determines `mu` uniquely.

### Proof

The value at `t=0` is `M_mu(a)`. After normalization,

\[
 \frac{L_\mu(a+it)}{M_\mu(a)}
 =\int e^{itx}\,d(T_a\mu)(x)
\]

is the characteristic function of the tilted probability. Fourier uniqueness reconstructs
`T_a mu`. Multiplication by `M_mu(a)e^{-ax}` reconstructs `mu`. QED.

This proves a joint reconstruction law: character-domain calculus is a lossy formation shadow, but
one admitted phase history retains the full source.

## 4. Finite formation grammar

Fix leaf measures `(mu_l)`. Formation expressions are generated by:

1. `leaf(l)`;
2. positive `mix(E,F)`;
3. independent `fuse(E,F)` by convolution;
4. legal normalized `tilt(a,E)`;
5. `reflect(E)`;
6. clock-preserving `recover(E,K)`.

For every root-to-leaf occurrence `p`, recursively assign an affine transport

\[
 \alpha_p(t)=\varepsilon_p t+b_p,
 \qquad \varepsilon_p\in\{\pm1\}.
 \tag{CD.9}
\]

At a leaf, `(epsilon,b)=(1,0)`. Passing through `tilt(a,-)` replaces
`(epsilon,b)` by `(epsilon,b+epsilon a)`. Passing through reflection replaces it by
`(-epsilon,b)`. Mix, fuse, and recover do not change the path affine map.

### Theorem 4.1 — global affine-leaf legality

For every legal finite formation expression `E`,

\[
 \boxed{
 D(E)=\bigcap_{p\in\operatorname{Leaves}(E)}
 \{t\in\mathbb R:\alpha_p(t)\in D(\mu_{\ell(p)})\}.}
 \tag{CD.10}
\]

### Proof

Induct on `E`. The leaf case is the definition. Mix and fuse both replace two domains by their
intersection by Theorem 2.1. Tilt pulls the child domain back along `t -> t+a`; this changes every
leaf affine map as stated. Reflection pulls it back along `t -> -t`. Recovery leaves it unchanged
by Theorem 3.1. QED.

### Consequences

1. A later recovery or phase gauge cannot restore a character lost by any leaf constraint.
2. Parallel positive branches can only shrink the common domain.
3. Different expressions may have the same domain; the domain is a conserved resource shadow, not
   a complete invariant of formation history.
4. Parenthesization of consecutive positive mixtures or fusions does not affect global legality.

## 5. Cofinal global formation/properness theorem

Let `(D_n)` be reflection-stable character domains containing zero. Define their symmetric inradii

\[
 r_n=\sup\{r\ge0:[-r,r]\subseteq D_n\}.
 \tag{CD.11}
\]

Assume `r_n>0` and `r_n -> 0`. Define the relative admission work

\[
 \mathcal W(t)=\sup_n\frac{|t|}{r_n}\in[0,\infty].
 \tag{CD.12}
\]

### Theorem 5.1 — cofinal character properness

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal W(0)=0,
 \qquad
 \mathcal W(t)=\infty\quad(t\ne0).}
 \tag{CD.13}
\]

Consequently every finite-work sublevel is the compact singleton `{0}`, and the only parameter
with uniformly bounded relative admission depth is the neutral character.

### Proof

The first identity is immediate. If `t ne 0` and `C<infinity`, choose `n` with
`r_n<|t|/C`; then `|t|/r_n>C`. Since this holds for every finite `C`, the supremum is infinite.
QED.

### Corollary 5.2 — exact selection interface

Suppose a literal cofinal formation genealogy has root domains `D_n` satisfying Theorem 5.1, and a
physical transverse parameter `t` is required by the source law to have finite global relative
admission work. Then `t=0`.

This corollary is unconditional once those two source premises are proved. It is not itself an RH
proof: constructing the required literal genealogy and its physical admission is the remaining
arithmetic theorem.
