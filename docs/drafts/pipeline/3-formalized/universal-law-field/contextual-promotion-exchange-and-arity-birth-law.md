## 1. Mechanism question

Let a rulebook admit several unary promotion processes.  Performing two promotions in opposite
orders exposes an order residual.  The field question is not which object carries that residual:

> When is a higher schedule failure genuinely new law, and when is it only a transported binary
> promotion failure seen in a later context?

This is the first open law in the Reflexive Law Mechanics constitution.

## 4. Conjecture/failure loop II -- initial pairwise flatness

### Conjecture 1

If three promotions exchange pairwise at the initial state, every length-three schedule has the
same endpoint.

### Countermodel

On (A=\mathbb Z^3), write (x=(1,0,0)) and define additive promotions

\[
 P(a,b,c)=(0,a,b),\qquad
 Q(a,b,c)=(c,a,b),\qquad
 R(a,b,c)=(0,a+c,b).
\tag{1}
\]

All three send (x) to (e_2=(0,1,0)), and all three send (e_2) to
(e_3=(0,0,1)).  Hence every pair exchanges at (x).  But

\[
 PQR(x)=0,\qquad RQP(x)=e_2.
\tag{2}
\]

So initial pairwise flatness does not control a later context.

### Verdict: [FALSIFIED]

The missing datum is not a primitive ternary tensor.  It is the binary exchange law evaluated
after other promotions have changed its input.

## 5. Surviving conjecture -- contextual generation

Let (A) be an abelian group and let (P,Q,R\in\operatorname{End}(A)).  Define

\[
 [P,Q](x)=P(Qx)-Q(Px)
\tag{3}
\]

and the reversal defect

\[
 \Theta(P,Q,R;x)=PQR(x)-RQP(x).
\tag{4}
\]

### Conjecture 2

Every three-promotion reversal defect is generated by contextual binary residuals:

\[
 \boxed{
 \Theta(P,Q,R;x)
 =[P,Q](Rx)+Q([P,R](x))+[Q,R](Px).}
\tag{5}
\]

Consequently:

1. a nonzero reversal defect forces at least one displayed contextual binary term to be nonzero;
2. unary additive promotion mechanics has no irreducible ternary order defect;
3. nested binary residuals satisfy the cyclic conservation law
   \[
   [P,[Q,R]]+[Q,[R,P]]+[R,[P,Q]]=0;
   \tag{6}
   \]
4. checking exchange through any fixed finite context depth cannot certify global exchange.

The final clause is witnessed for every (d\in\mathbb N) by

\[
 G(n)=n+1,\qquad
 F_d(n)=\begin{cases}d+3,&n=d+1,\\n,&n\ne d+1.\end{cases}
\tag{7}
\]

Then (F_dG(k)=GF_d(k)) for every (k<d), but they differ at (k=d).

## 6. Paper proof

Expanding the right side of (5) and using additivity of (Q) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
 &[P,Q](Rx)+Q([P,R](x))+[Q,R](Px)\\
 &=PQRx-QPRx+Q(PRx-RPx)+QRPx-RQPx\\
 &=PQRx-QPRx+QPRx-QRPx+QRPx-RQPx\\
 &=PQRx-RQPx.
\end{aligned}
\]

This proves (5).  If all three terms on its right were zero, the left side would be zero, proving
the arity certificate by contraposition.

For (6), expand all six compositions.  Each word occurs exactly once with positive sign and once
with negative sign:

\[
 (PQR-PRQ-QRP+RQP)
 +(QRP-QPR-RPQ+PRQ)
 +(RPQ-RQP-PQR+QPR)=0,
\]

after consistently expanding the three nested commutators; equivalently this is the Jacobi
identity in the associative endomorphism ring.

Equations (1)--(2) prove failure of initial pairwise flatness.  For (7), if (k<d), then neither
(k) nor (k+1) equals (d+1), so both composites equal (k+1).  At (k=d),

\[
 F_d(G(d))=F_d(d+1)=d+3,
 \qquad
 G(F_d(d))=G(d)=d+1.
\]

Thus no uniform finite contextual cutoff exists.
