## 2. Group-level formation theorem

Let \(G\) be a group and let

\[
 \theta:G\longrightarrow G,
 \qquad \theta^2=\operatorname{id},
 \tag{CRS.2}
\]

be an involutive group automorphism. Define the twisted reversal

\[
 \rho(g)=\theta(g^{-1})
 \tag{CRS.3}
\]

and its fixed response space

\[
 Q_\theta=\{g\in G:\rho(g)=g\}
          =\{g\in G:\theta(g)=g^{-1}\}.
 \tag{CRS.4}
\]

For \(x,y\in Q_\theta\), define reflection fusion

\[
 x\triangleright y=xy^{-1}x.
 \tag{CRS.5}
\]

### Theorem 2.1 -- functional-equation fixed responses form a reflection space

The operation `(CRS.5)` is closed on \(Q_\theta\) and satisfies

\[
 x\triangleright x=x,
 \qquad
 x\triangleright(x\triangleright y)=y,
 \tag{CRS.6}
\]

and

\[
 x\triangleright(y\triangleright z)
 =(x\triangleright y)\triangleright(x\triangleright z).
 \tag{CRS.7}
\]

Thus every \(s_x:y\mapsto x\triangleright y\) is an involutive automorphism of the
reflection-fusion law.

By contrast, ordinary formation obeys

\[
 \boxed{\rho(xy)=yx}
 \tag{CRS.8}
\]

for \(x,y\in Q_\theta\), and therefore

\[
 \boxed{xy\in Q_\theta\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad xy=yx.}
 \tag{CRS.9}
\]

The exact ordinary-formation exchange defect is

\[
 \Xi(x,y)=(xy)^{-1}\rho(xy)=(xy)^{-1}yx,
 \tag{CRS.10}
\]

and \(\Xi(x,y)=1\) exactly when the two formed histories commute.

### Proof

For closure,

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \theta(xy^{-1}x)
 &=\theta(x)\theta(y)^{-1}\theta(x)\\
 &=x^{-1}yx^{-1}
 =(xy^{-1}x)^{-1}.
 \end{aligned}
\]

The first two identities in `(CRS.6)` follow by cancellation. For `(CRS.7)`, both sides are

\[
 xy^{-1}zy^{-1}x.
\]

Since \(\rho\) is anti-multiplicative,

\[
 \rho(xy)=\rho(y)\rho(x)=yx,
\]

which proves `(CRS.8)`. Hence \(xy\) is fixed precisely when \(xy=yx\), proving
`(CRS.9)` and `(CRS.10)`. QED.

## 3. Native transvections and open history

Let \(e\) denote the identity. Since \(s_e(y)=y^{-1}\), define the transvection

\[
 \tau_x=s_x\circ s_e,
 \qquad
 \tau_x(y)=xyx.
 \tag{CRS.11}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 -- exact transvection history

For every \(n\in\mathbb Z\),

\[
 \boxed{\tau_x^n(e)=x^{2n}.}
 \tag{CRS.12}
\]

Consequently, if \(G\) has a marked length \(L\) satisfying

\[
 L(x^n)=|n|L(x)
 \tag{CRS.13}
\]

on the one-parameter subgroup generated by \(x\), then

\[
 \sup_{n\in\mathbb Z}L(\tau_x^n(e))<\infty
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad L(x)=0.
 \tag{CRS.14}
\]

If \(L\) separates the identity on \(Q_\theta\), bounded full transvection history selects
\(x=e\).

### Proof

Equation `(CRS.11)` gives \(\tau_x(e)=x^2\). If \(\tau_x^n(e)=x^{2n}\), then

\[
 \tau_x^{n+1}(e)=x x^{2n}x=x^{2n+2}.
\]

The inverse transvection is \(s_e\circ s_x:y\mapsto x^{-1}yx^{-1}\), which proves negative
\(n\). Equation `(CRS.14)` follows from

\[
 L(\tau_x^n(e))=2|n|L(x).
\]

QED.

This is the nonlinear replacement for the killed affine-cocycle selector. The open orbit is not an
added cover. It is generated internally by composing two native reflections.

## 4. Complete Hopf response signatures

Let \(k\) be a characteristic-zero field and let

\[
 H=\prod_{n\ge0}H_n,
 \qquad H_0=k,
 \tag{CRS.15}
\]

be a complete connected graded Hopf algebra. Multiplication and coproduct preserve total grade,
and all structure maps are continuous for the product filtration. Put

\[
 H_+=\prod_{n\ge1}H_n.
\]

Let \(\mathcal G(H)\) be the group-like elements in \(1+H_+\), and let
\(\mathcal P(H)\) be the primitive elements in \(H_+\). The degreewise convergent series

\[
 \exp X=\sum_{m\ge0}\frac{X^m}{m!},
 \qquad
 \log(1+Y)=\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{(-1)^{m+1}}mY^m
 \tag{CRS.16}
\]

give mutually inverse bijections

\[
 \exp:\mathcal P(H)\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal G(H),
 \qquad \log=\exp^{-1}.
 \tag{CRS.17}
\]

Let \(\theta\) be a continuous graded involutive Hopf automorphism. It commutes with the antipode
by uniqueness of the antipode. Write

\[
 \mathcal P^\pm
 =\{X\in\mathcal P(H):\theta X=\pm X\}.
 \tag{CRS.18}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 -- logarithmic classification of fixed response signatures

The twisted-antipode fixed signatures are exactly

\[
 \boxed{Q_\theta(\mathcal G(H))=\exp(\mathcal P^-).}
 \tag{CRS.19}
\]

In particular, pure reversal \(\theta=\operatorname{id}\) forces the identity signature. A
nontrivial sheet involution does not: every nonzero anti-invariant primitive \(X\) produces the
nontrivial fixed signature \(\exp X\).

### Proof

If \(g\) is group-like, the antipode identity gives \(S(g)=g^{-1}\). Also

\[
 \Delta(\log g)=\log(\Delta g)
 =\log((g\otimes1)(1\otimes g))
 =\log(g\otimes1)+\log(1\otimes g),
\]

because the two tensor factors commute. Thus \(\log g\) is primitive. The converse follows from
the same calculation with `exp`.

Now

\[
 \theta(g)=g^{-1}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \theta(\log g)=-\log g.
\]

This is `(CRS.19)`. QED.

### Corollary 4.2 -- Lie triple law

The primitive Lie algebra has the parity rules

\[
 [\mathcal P^+,\mathcal P^+]\subseteq\mathcal P^+,
 \qquad
 [\mathcal P^+,\mathcal P^-]\subseteq\mathcal P^-,
 \qquad
 [\mathcal P^-,\mathcal P^-]\subseteq\mathcal P^+.
 \tag{CRS.20}
\]

Hence \(\mathcal P^-\) is closed under the ternary law

\[
 (X,Y,Z)\longmapsto[X,[Y,Z]],
 \tag{CRS.21}
\]

but generally not under the binary Lie bracket. Functional-equation-fixed response histories are
therefore intrinsically Lie-triple, not Lie-subalgebraic.

### Proof

Apply \(\theta\) to each bracket and multiply the two parity signs. QED.

## 5. Exact all-grade conservation and reconstruction

Write a fixed group-like response as

\[
 g=1+\sum_{n\ge1}g_n,
 \qquad g_n\in H_n.
 \tag{CRS.22}
\]

The fixed equation is equivalent to

\[
 g\theta(g)=1.
 \tag{CRS.23}
\]

### Theorem 5.1 -- antipodal grade reconstruction

For every \(n\ge1\),

\[
 \boxed{
 g_n+\theta(g_n)
 +\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}g_i\theta(g_{n-i})=0.}
 \tag{CRS.24}
\]

Equivalently, the reflection-even part of grade \(n\) is forced by lower grades:

\[
 \boxed{
 g_n^+
 =-\frac12\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}g_i\theta(g_{n-i}),
 \qquad
 g_n^+=\frac{g_n+\theta(g_n)}2.}
 \tag{CRS.25}
\]

In particular,

\[
 g_1^+=0,
 \qquad
 g_2^+=\frac12g_1^2.
 \tag{CRS.26}
\]

Thus functional equation does not annihilate the signature. It recursively reconstructs every
even grade from the free odd log-signature sector.

### Proof

Take the degree-\(n\) component of `(CRS.23)`. The terms with a degree-zero factor are
\(g_n+\theta(g_n)\); all remaining decompositions are the displayed sum. This proves
`(CRS.24)` and `(CRS.25)`. At grade one, the sum is empty. Then
\(\theta(g_1)=-g_1\), so `(CRS.25)` at grade two gives

\[
 g_2^+=-\frac12g_1\theta(g_1)=\frac12g_1^2.
\]

QED.

Equation `(CRS.24)` is the requested route-independent conservation identity. It can be tested on
any claimed response tower without knowing its target theorem.

## 6. The first ordinary-fusion escape

Let \(g=\exp X\), \(h=\exp Y\), with \(X,Y\in\mathcal P^-\). The ordinary product has

\[
 \log(gh)=\operatorname{BCH}(X,Y).
 \tag{CRS.27}
\]

### Theorem 6.1 -- even commutator is the first fusion obstruction

The reflection-even part of the product logarithm is

\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{BCH}(X,Y)^+
 =\frac12\bigl(\operatorname{BCH}(X,Y)
                -\operatorname{BCH}(Y,X)\bigr).}
 \tag{CRS.28}
\]

If \(X,Y\) begin in filtration degree one, then

\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{BCH}(X,Y)^+
 =\frac12[X,Y]\pmod{F^3}.}
 \tag{CRS.29}
\]

Thus the first escaped response of two individually functional-equation-fixed histories is the
reflection-even commutator. It vanishes to every order exactly when \(g\) and \(h\) commute.

### Proof

Since \(\theta X=-X\) and \(\theta Y=-Y\),

\[
 \theta\operatorname{BCH}(X,Y)
 =\operatorname{BCH}(-X,-Y)
 =-\operatorname{BCH}(Y,X).
\]

Taking the even projection gives `(CRS.28)`. The BCH expansion modulo filtration three is

\[
 \operatorname{BCH}(X,Y)=X+Y+\tfrac12[X,Y],
\]

whereas swapping \(X,Y\) changes the sign of the bracket. This proves `(CRS.29)`.
The final statement is Theorem 2.1 transported through the injective logarithm. QED.

## 7. Several reversals and the generated selection Gramian

One involution leaves the whole space \(\mathcal P^-\). The exact higher selector uses a source-
marked family of reversals.

Let \(V\) be a real or complex Hilbert space and let

\[
 \Theta_j:V\longrightarrow V,
 \qquad j\in J,
 \tag{CRS.30}
\]

be a finite family of unitary involutions. Each \(\Theta_j\) is self-adjoint. Define the joint
reversal response

\[
 \mathcal A:V\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{j\in J}V,
 \qquad
 \mathcal A X=((I+\Theta_j)X)_{j\in J},
 \tag{CRS.31}
\]

and its positive formation Gramian

\[
 \mathcal Q=\mathcal A^*\mathcal A.
 \tag{CRS.32}
\]

### Theorem 7.1 -- joint reversal properness and selection

The following identities are exact:

\[
 \boxed{
 \|\mathcal AX\|^2
 =\sum_{j\in J}\|X+\Theta_jX\|^2,}
 \tag{CRS.33}
\]

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal Q=2\sum_{j\in J}(I+\Theta_j),}
 \tag{CRS.34}
\]

and

\[
 \boxed{
 \ker\mathcal A=\ker\mathcal Q
 =\bigcap_{j\in J}\ker(I+\Theta_j).}
 \tag{CRS.35}
\]

Consequently a group-like response signature fixed by all twisted antipodes
\(\Theta_j\circ S\) is neutral whenever the common odd primitive sector in `(CRS.35)` is zero.

Stable selection is equivalent to the properness estimate

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{j\in J}\|X+\Theta_jX\|^2
 \ge \alpha\|X\|^2}
 \tag{CRS.36}
\]

for some \(\alpha>0\). In finite dimension, `(CRS.36)` holds exactly when the common odd sector
is zero. In infinite dimension, zero common odd sector is not sufficient; `(CRS.36)` is precisely
the additional closed-range/coercivity gate.

### Proof

Equation `(CRS.33)` is the direct-sum norm. Since \(\Theta_j^*=\Theta_j\) and
\(\Theta_j^2=I\),

\[
 (I+\Theta_j)^*(I+\Theta_j)=2(I+\Theta_j),
\]

giving `(CRS.34)`. Equation `(CRS.35)` follows from
\(\langle\mathcal QX,X\rangle=\|\mathcal AX\|^2\).

If a complete response signature \(g=\exp X\) is fixed by every twisted antipode, Theorem 4.1
gives \(X\in\ker\mathcal A\). Trivial kernel forces \(X=0\), hence \(g=1\).

Estimate `(CRS.36)` says exactly that \(\mathcal A\) is bounded below. In finite dimension an
injective linear map is bounded below. In infinite dimension bounded below is stronger than
injectivity and is equivalent to injectivity plus closed range. QED.

### Corollary 7.2 -- approximate multi-reversal selection

Under `(CRS.36)`,

\[
 \|X\|^2
 \le\alpha^{-1}\sum_j\|X+\Theta_jX\|^2.
 \tag{CRS.37}
\]

Therefore a sequence of response signatures whose complete logarithmic defects tend to zero for
every source reversal must converge to the neutral signature in the response norm. This is the
approximate selection theorem absent from scalar endpoint symmetry.

No commutativity of the involutions is needed for `(CRS.33)`--`(CRS.37)`. If they commute, the
existing simultaneous-parity decomposition identifies `(CRS.35)` with the all-odd parity piece.

## 8. Common recovery is exactly information-neutral

Let \(\mu,\nu\) be probability laws and let \(\kappa\) be a common probability kernel independent
of the observed law. Split packet formation is

\[
 \operatorname{Form}_\kappa(\mu)=\mu\otimes\kappa.
 \tag{CRS.38}
\]

Let a functional-equation action \(F\) lift by acting on the observed law and regenerating the same
kernel:

\[
 \widetilde F(\mu\otimes\kappa)=F(\mu)\otimes\kappa.
 \tag{CRS.39}
\]

### Theorem 8.1 -- the recovery lift adds no selection law

Marginalization recovers \(\mu\), the square with \(F\) commutes, and

\[
 \boxed{
 D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mu\otimes\kappa\Vert\nu\otimes\kappa)
 =D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mu\Vert\nu),}
 \tag{CRS.40}
\]

with the same identity for Jeffreys divergence. Fixed packets correspond exactly to fixed observed
laws. Hence common recovery neither removes the anti-invariant primitive sector nor repairs a
failure of joint properness.

### Proof

The first marginal is a left inverse of split formation. The Radon--Nikodym derivative of the two
packets is the derivative of \(\mu\) with respect to \(\nu\), independent of the recovery
coordinate. Fubini gives `(CRS.40)`. The lifted-action and fixed-point statements follow from the
left inverse. QED.

## 10. Hostile countermodels and exact limits

### 10.1 Sheet twist kills naive antipode rigidity

Let the complete tensor Hopf algebra have primitive letters \(a,b\), and let \(\theta\) exchange
them. Then

\[
 X=a-b\in\mathcal P^-,
 \qquad
 g=\exp(a-b)\ne1,
 \qquad
 \theta(g)=g^{-1}.
\]

Thus a twisted-antipode fixed signature need not be neutral.

### 10.2 Fixed responses need not form a group

Take two anti-invariant primitive letters \(X,Y\) with \([X,Y]\ne0\). Then \(\exp X\) and
\(\exp Y\) are fixed, but their product is not. The lost closure is exactly Theorems 2.1 and 6.1,
not a defect of the example.

### 10.3 Characteristic two is excluded

The even/odd projections and grade reconstruction divide by two. Over characteristic two,
nontrivial elements \(1+x\) with \(x^2=0\) can satisfy \((1+x)^2=1\). Characteristic zero is
load-bearing.

### 10.4 Tree-like histories survive pure reversal

For a bounded-variation path, pure reversal-fixed signature is trivial and hence the path is
tree-like, not necessarily pointwise constant. A reduced-path or source-faithfulness condition is
needed to identify trivial signature with neutral source history.

### 10.5 Existing Dyson coefficients are not automatically a group-like path signature

The corpus Dyson tower has operator-valued coefficients and a nontrivial degree-zero semigroup.
To apply this theorem one must pass to an interaction-picture response with constant term one and
prove the full Chen/shuffle or appropriate operator-Hopf group-like law. Full contractions alone
may not close under Chen composition; partial contractions or an enlarged Hopf monoid can be
required.

### 10.6 Existing functional-equation covariance is not yet the twisted antipode

The corpus proves letter permutation

\[
 J U_wJ^*=U_{\sigma(w)}.
\]

This is provably different from every twisted antipode that has the same action on first-order
responses.

Let \(V\) be the legal-letter space, let \(T(V)\) be its tensor algebra over a field of
characteristic different from two, and let \(\sigma:V\to V\) be an involution. The existing
letterwise covariance extends to the algebra automorphism

\[
 C_\sigma(v_1\cdots v_n)=\sigma(v_1)\cdots\sigma(v_n).
 \tag{CRS.41}
\]

The tensor-Hopf antipode is

\[
 S(v_1\cdots v_n)=(-1)^n v_n\cdots v_1.
 \tag{CRS.42}
\]

There are two relevant scalar sign conventions for extending the observed first-grade relabelling.

1. If \(\theta_+(v)=\sigma(v)\), then \(\theta_+S(v)=-\sigma(v)\), so
   \(C_\sigma\ne\theta_+S\) on every nonzero first-grade response.
2. If \(\theta_-(v)=-\sigma(v)\), then the two signs cancel and

   \[
   (\theta_-S)(v_1\cdots v_n)
   =\sigma(v_n)\cdots\sigma(v_1).
   \tag{CRS.43}
   \]

   This agrees with \(C_\sigma\) in grade one, but in grade two agreement for every \(u,v\)
   is equivalent to

   \[
   \sigma(u)\sigma(v)=\sigma(v)\sigma(u).
   \tag{CRS.44}
   \]

   Since \(\sigma\) is onto, `(CRS.44)` says the generated response algebra is commutative.

Hence:

### Theorem 10.1 -- order-preserving covariance cannot supply response reversal

On a free ordered response algebra with two noncommuting intervention directions, no sign choice
turns the current letterwise functional-equation covariance into a twisted antipode. Any quotient
on which they agree has killed every grade-two word commutator and therefore erased the ordered
memory that the complete response signature was introduced to retain.

### Proof

Equations `(CRS.41)`--`(CRS.43)` are the definitions of the tensor-algebra extension and antipode.
Under the positive sign convention the maps disagree in grade one. Under the negative sign
convention, comparison on \(uv\) gives exactly `(CRS.44)`. Surjectivity of \(\sigma\) converts it
to \(ab=ba\) for every two generators. QED.

A new source operation must therefore reverse the intervention schedule. It must act on the
normalized complete response by either

\[
 w=a_1\cdots a_n
 \longmapsto
 (-1)^n\sigma(a_n)\cdots\sigma(a_1)
 \tag{CRS.45}
\]

or by the sign-compensated variant `(CRS.43)`, or by the correct operator-Hopf analogue. Without
actual order reversal, invoking the reflection-space selector is theatre.

### 10.7 Common recovery cannot supply the missing reversal

The recovery lift is information-neutral after an action exists. It neither upgrades the
order-preserving map `(CRS.41)` to the order-reversing map `(CRS.43)` nor shrinks the common odd
sector.
