- **Statement:** In the abstract pole-purchase frame (single-\(\sigma\), log-type global inputs,

## 1. The frame

An **abstract pole-purchase engine** consists of:

- a discrete abelian **amplifier group** \(\Gamma\) (the character/phase group generated by the
  amplifier family; \(\Gamma=\mathbb Z\) for pure frequency coupling \(n^{ikt}\),
  \(\Gamma=\mathbb Z/2\) for a real character, \(\Gamma=\mathbb Z/2\times\mathbb Z\) for Page's
  situation, \(\Gamma=\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z\) truncated for symmetric powers);
- **channels** \(h_g(\sigma)\) for \(g\) in a finite subset \(S\subset\Gamma\) with \(1\in S\)
  (in the concrete case \(h_g(\sigma)=\mathrm{Re}\,(-L'/L)(\sigma+i\tau_g,\pi_g)\));
- a height/conductor scale \(\mathfrak L\ge1\), and constants \(A,m\ge0\), \(\beta<1+\ldots\)

subject to three axioms, abstracted from the classical derivation:

- **(P1) source cone.** For every pattern \(Q=\sum_{g\in S}c_g\,g\) with real coefficients that
  is pointwise nonnegative on \(\widehat\Gamma\): \(\ \sum_g c_g\,h_g(\sigma)\ \ge\ 0\) for
  \(\sigma>1\). (Concretely: \(\sum_n\Lambda(n)n^{-\sigma}\,Q(\text{phase}(n))\ge0\).)
- **(P2) pole.** \(h_1(\sigma)\le\frac1{\sigma-1}+A\mathfrak L\) for \(1<\sigma\le2\).
- **(P3) zero and global input.** If the channel \(g_0\in S\) carries a zero \(\beta\le1\) (real
  coordinate; height inside the scale), then
  \(h_{g_0}(\sigma)\le-\frac{m}{\sigma-\beta}+A\mathfrak L\), and every other channel satisfies
  \(h_g(\sigma)\le A\mathfrak L\), for \(1<\sigma\le2\).

A **derivation** is the standard positive-combination step: choose a nonnegative pattern \(Q\),
apply (P1), substitute (P2)–(P3), and conclude a constraint on \(\beta\). This frame captures
the de la Vallée Poussin/Landau/Page family exactly; it deliberately does **not** capture
engines that improve the inputs themselves (Vinogradov-type bounds shrink the \(A\mathfrak L\)
terms; Deuring–Heilbronn modifies (P3) by repulsion). That exclusion is the point — see §5.

## 2. Theorem 1 — width cap

For every finite \(S\) and every derivation in the frame with \(m\ge1\): if the conclusion is
\(\beta\le1-\delta\), then

\[
\delta\;\le\;\frac{C(Q)}{\mathfrak L},
\qquad
C(Q)\ \text{depending only on}\ \bigl(c_g\bigr),A,m .
\tag{INV7.1}
\]

#### Proof

By (P1) and the substitutions, for all \(\sigma\in(1,2]\):

\[
0\ \le\ c_1\Bigl(\frac1{\sigma-1}+A\mathfrak L\Bigr)
-\;c_{g_0}\,\frac{m}{\sigma-\beta}\;+\;\Bigl(\sum_{g\ne1,g_0}|c_g|\Bigr)A\mathfrak L .
\]

(The pattern's nonnegativity forces \(c_1>0\); the derivation uses \(c_{g_0}>0\).) Set
\(\sigma=1+a/\mathfrak L\) with \(a>0\) at our disposal. If \(\beta\ge1-\delta\) then
\(\sigma-\beta\le(a+\delta\mathfrak L)/\mathfrak L\), so

\[
c_{g_0}m\,\frac{\mathfrak L}{a+\delta\mathfrak L}
\ \le\ c_1\frac{\mathfrak L}{a}+\widetilde CA\mathfrak L,
\qquad
\widetilde C=\textstyle\sum_g|c_g| .
\]

Dividing by \(\mathfrak L\): \(\ \frac{c_{g_0}m}{a+\delta\mathfrak L}\le\frac{c_1}{a}+\widetilde CA\).
If \(\delta\mathfrak L\ge K\) for large \(K\) the left side is \(\le c_{g_0}m/K\), which is
consistent — i.e., the derivation yields **no** constraint unless \(\delta\mathfrak L\) is
bounded: the exclusion \(\beta\le1-\delta\) is derivable only when the displayed inequality
*fails* for the actual zero, which requires
\(\frac{c_{g_0}m}{a+\delta\mathfrak L}>\frac{c_1}{a}+\widetilde CA\), hence

\[
\delta\mathfrak L\ <\ \frac{c_{g_0}m}{c_1/a+\widetilde CA}\;-\;a
\ \le\ C(Q),
\]

a bound depending only on the pattern data. QED.

## 3. Theorem 2 — constant cap by the Fejér ratio, and the exclusion threshold

Define the **Fejér ratio** of the support:

\[
\rho(\Gamma,S,g_0)
=\sup\Bigl\{\tfrac{c_{g_0}}{c_1}\ :\ Q=\sum_{g\in S}c_g\,g\ \ \text{nonnegative on }\widehat\Gamma,
\ c_1>0\Bigr\}.
\tag{INV7.2}
\]

1. \(\rho\) is finite for every finite \(S\) (nonnegativity plus normalization \(c_1=1\) is a
   compact constraint set; the coefficient functional is bounded on it).
2. In the limit \(a\to\infty\), \(\mathfrak L\to\infty\) of Theorem 1's inequality, a derivation
   excluding any \(\delta>0\) requires \(c_{g_0}m>c_1\), i.e. (with \(m=1\), the generic simple
   zero) the **threshold**

   \[
   \rho(\Gamma,S,g_0)\ >\ 1 .
   \tag{INV7.3}
   \]

3. The optimal width constant is controlled by \(\rho\): maximizing
   \(\delta\mathfrak L\) over \(a\) and admissible \(Q\) gives
   \(\sup\delta\mathfrak L\le F(\rho,A)\) with \(F\) explicit and increasing in \(\rho\).

#### Proof

1: compactness. 2: divide the exclusion requirement by \(c_1\) and let the input terms decouple.
3: substitute the optimal \(c_{g_0}/c_1\le\rho\) into Theorem 1's bound. QED.

## 4. Theorem 3 — the three anchors are one ratio

1. **VP/Landau (\(\Gamma=\mathbb Z\), \(S=\{0,\pm1,\dots,\pm m\}\), \(g_0=1\)):** nonnegative
   patterns are nonnegative cosine polynomials; Fejér's classical extremal gives
   \(\rho=2\cos\bigl(\pi/(m+2)\bigr)<2\): the threshold \(\rho>1\) is met (VP works), the
   constant is capped for every degree \(m\), and the cap's numeric optimization is exactly the
   occupied Landau/Mossinghoff–Trudgian extremal problem. Nothing new is claimed about the
   values; the frame explains their existence.
2. **Page (\(\Gamma=\mathbb Z/2\), \(S=\Gamma\), \(g_0=\chi\), real zero):** nonnegative
   \(Q=a+b\chi\) on \(\{\pm1\}\) forces \(|b|\le a\), so \(\rho=1\): the threshold (INV7.3)
   **fails**. The classical Page barrier — one real zero of one real character survives every
   positive-pattern purchase — is the computation \(\rho(\mathbb Z/2)=1\), one line.
3. **Symmetric powers (\(\Gamma\) generated by the sym-spectrum, \(S\) its truncation at
   degree \(d\)):** the engine's strength is the ratio problem on the truncated spectrum; the
   Kim–Sarnak-type partial bounds correspond to \(\rho>1\) but bounded, with the bound improving
   in \(d\) — the frame types this; the exact \(\rho\)-values there are not computed here.

## 5. Corollary — the split of ULF-INV-4, and where all remaining hope lives

Within the frame, **no finite amplifier family of any size and any spectral support reaches a
region wider than \(c/\mathfrak L\)** (Theorem 1); the family/pattern lever is capped by
\(\rho\) (Theorem 2), and its degeneracies are computable (Theorem 3.2). Therefore every route
to a corridor-grade region (\(\kappa<1\), width \(\gg1/\mathfrak L\)) must improve the **input
axis** — the \(A\mathfrak L\) terms of (P2)/(P3) — which is exactly the
Vinogradov/short-interval/mean-value territory the corpus has independently identified as the
MR wall (`zero-free-region-exponent-is-the-mr-wall`; `matomaki-radziwill-is-the-wall`). The
ULF-INV-4 conjecture thus splits: its pattern half is **proved** (this card); its input half is
**identified with a known wall** rather than open free space. The barrier's practical leverage:
the entire "cleverer finite amplifier/polynomial" search space is closed by theorem — spend
nothing further there.

## 6. Hostile scope

- The frame captures single-\(\sigma\) positive-combination derivations with log-type inputs —
  the VP/Landau/Page class exactly. Engines improving the inputs (Vinogradov; density methods;
  repulsion) are outside **by design**; the corollary's content is precisely that they are the
  only thing left. Multi-\(\sigma\), mollified, or smoothed-explicit-formula derivations are not
  formally captured; extending the frame to them is the named residual work item, and no claim
  is made that they evade the cap (the classical ones do not).
- Fejér's extremal and its refinements are classical and occupied (Fejér; Landau; the
  Mossinghoff–Trudgian school). Page's theorem is textbook (Davenport). The contribution is the
  frame, the width/threshold/ratio split, the one-line identification of Page's barrier with
  \(\rho(\mathbb Z/2)=1\), and the proved closure of the finite-amplifier search space.
- If the abstract frame or an equivalent meta-theorem exists in the literature (survey chapters
  on the method's limits), this card demotes to BRIDGE; targeted searches found method-step
  optimality results and extremal-constant computations, not the frame-level cap. Flagged for
  one further literature pass before any promotion.

## 7. Theorem 1′ — stability of the width cap under multi-σ and averaged derivations

The §6 residual is smaller than stated. Extend the derivation class to:

- **finite multi-σ derivations:** finitely many evaluation points sigma_j = 1 + a_j/L with a
  nonnegative pattern Q_j at each, conclusions added;
- **averaged derivations:** positive-kernel averages (integrals over t- or sigma-parameters with
  nonnegative weight) of admissible derivations.

The width cap survives verbatim. For the multi-σ case: exclusion of beta >= 1 - delta requires

    sum_j c_{g0,j} m / (a_j + delta*L)  >  sum_j c_{1,j}/a_j + A * sum_j C_j ,

and the left side is at most (m * sum_j c_{g0,j}) / (delta*L), which tends to 0 as delta*L grows
while the right side is a positive constant; hence delta*L <= C(patterns). Positive averaging is
a limit of positive combinations of valid inequalities, and the bound is uniform in the
parameters on compacts, so it passes to the average. QED.

Consequently the residual class genuinely outside the frame is not "mollified or multi-sigma
versions of the classical derivation" — those are now capped — but derivations whose test
objects carry **signed** weights justified by structure the cone (P1) does not supply: the
explicit-formula/Weil-functional engines, i.e., exactly the group-3 positivity criteria that are
equivalent to the conclusion. The frame's boundary and the corpus's criterion trichotomy meet:
below the boundary, capped; on the boundary, exactly-RH.
