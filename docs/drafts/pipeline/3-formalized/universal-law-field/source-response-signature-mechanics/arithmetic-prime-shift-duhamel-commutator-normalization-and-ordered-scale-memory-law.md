## 2. Exact one-letter response

For `t>=0`, define the raw and commutator-normalized Duhamel operators

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 D_m(t)&=-\int_0^tS_{t-s}V_mS_s\,ds,\\
 \widetilde D_m(t)&=-\int_0^tS_{t-s}A_mS_s\,ds.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{APS.6}
\]

### Theorem 2.1 — exact logarithmic cancellation

For every `m>=2` and `t>=0`,

\[
 \boxed{
 S_tV_m=m^{-t}V_mS_t,}
 \tag{APS.7}
\]

and

\[
 \boxed{
 D_m(t)=-\frac{1-m^{-t}}{\log m}V_mS_t,
 \qquad
 \widetilde D_m(t)=-(1-m^{-t})V_mS_t.}
 \tag{APS.8}
\]

Thus the raw source response loses one logarithmic derivative.  The source commutator restores it
exactly.

### Proof

Both sides of `(APS.7)` send `e_n` to `(mn)^{-t}e_{mn}`.  Hence

\[
 S_{t-s}V_mS_s=m^{-(t-s)}V_mS_t.
\]

Integrating the scalar factor gives

\[
 \int_0^tm^{-(t-s)}ds
 =\frac{1-m^{-t}}{\log m}.
\]

Multiplication by `log m` proves the normalized formula.  QED.

## 3. All-prime response and global properness

Let `mathbb P` denote the primes and let `H_P` be the closed span of `(e_p)_(p in mathbb P)`.
Use the vacuum source seed `e_1`.  For finite prime controls `c=(c_p)`, define

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 (\mathcal R_T^{\rm raw}c)(t)
   &=\sum_pc_pD_p(t)e_1,\\
 (\mathcal R_T^{\rm com}c)(t)
   &=\sum_pc_p\widetilde D_p(t)e_1,
 \end{aligned}
 \qquad 0\le t\le T.
 \tag{APS.9}
\]

The response values lie in `H_P`.  Put

\[
b_p(T)^2=\int_0^T(1-p^{-t})^2dt
 =T-2\frac{1-p^{-T}}{\log p}
   +\frac{1-p^{-2T}}{2\log p}.
\tag{APS.10}
\]

Define

\[
 \psi_p^T(t)=b_p(T)^{-1}(1-p^{-t})e_p

and let `mathcal Y_T` be their closed span in `L^2([0,T];H_P)`.  The family
`(psi_p^T)` is orthonormal.  This is the common raw/normalized response carrier.

### Theorem 3.1 — source-generated closed-range transduction

For every `T>0`, both response maps extend boundedly from finite controls to

\[
 \ell^2(\mathbb P)\longrightarrow L^2([0,T];H_P).
\]

Their exact energies are

\[
 \boxed{
 \|\mathcal R_T^{\rm raw}c\|^2
 =\sum_p|c_p|^2\frac{b_p(T)^2}{(\log p)^2},
 \qquad

 =\sum_p|c_p|^2b_p(T)^2.}
 \tag{APS.11}

They lie in opposite global phases.

#### Raw phase

`mathcal R_T^raw` is injective and compact, with dense nonclosed range in `mathcal Y_T`.
Its singular values satisfy

\[

 \tag{APS.12}

\[
 \boxed{

 \tag{APS.14}
\]

where `y=sum_p y_p psi_p^T` in `mathcal Y_T`.  Its inverse norm is at most
`b_2(T)^(-1)`.

### Proof

Equation `(APS.8)` gives

\[
 D_p(t)e_1=-\frac{1-p^{-t}}{\log p}e_p,
 \qquad
 \widetilde D_p(t)e_1=-(1-p^{-t})e_p.

The prime basis is orthonormal, so integration proves `(APS.11)`.  For fixed `T>0`,
`b_p(T)^2` increases with `p` because `1-p^{-t}` does, and

\[

This proves `(APS.13)`--`(APS.14)`.  In the raw phase the diagonal singular values are positive and
tend to zero.  Finite-support response histories lie in the range and are dense in the response
carrier, while the map is not bounded below; its range is therefore nonclosed.  A diagonal map
whose entries tend to zero is compact.  QED.

## 4. Ordered two-letter memory

For `m,n>=2`, define the ordered raw second responses

 \tag{APS.16}
\]

Put `a=log m`, `b=log n`, and

\[
 B_{a,b}(t)=

 \tag{APS.17}
\]

For every `m,n>=2`,

\[

 \tag{APS.18}

\[
 \boxed{

 \tag{APS.19}
\]

where

\[
 \boxed{
 \omega_t(a,b)=
 \int_{\substack{u,v\ge0\\u+v\le t}}
 e^{-(a+b)u}\bigl(e^{-bv}-e^{-av}\bigr)\,du\,dv.}
 \tag{APS.20}

\[
 \boxed{

 \tag{APS.21}

Thus source evolution generates an arithmetic orientation before any determinant, positive norm,
or target divisor is introduced.  Positive work retains its magnitude and erases its sign.

### Proof

\[

This proves `(APS.18)`.  Exchanging `m,n` gives `(APS.19)`--`(APS.20)`.  If `a>b`, then for every
`v>0`,

\[

the integration region has positive measure, so `omega_t(a,b)>0`.  The cases `a<b` and `a=b`
follow by antisymmetry.  Multiplying the two intervention letters by `a` and `b` proves
