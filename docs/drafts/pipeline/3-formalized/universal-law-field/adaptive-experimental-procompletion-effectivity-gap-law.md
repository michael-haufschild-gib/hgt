## Goal

Separate two notions that earlier field candidates repeatedly conflated:

1. **experimental coherence:** every finite experiment has an effective realization;
2. **global effectivity:** one law realizes the complete response profile.

The gap is not a route-specific obstruction and not curvature. It is a basic geometry of the
experimental completion of a law space.

## 1. Effective laws and virtual profiles

Let

\[
  \mathcal L_{\mathrm{fin}}=\{F\subseteq\mathbb N:F\text{ finite}\}
\]

be the effective law space. Its complete Boolean response profile is

\[
  \chi_F(n)=
  \begin{cases}
    1,&n\in F,\\
    0,&n\notin F.
  \end{cases}
\tag{AP.1}
\]

The experimental procompletion is the full profile space

\[
  \widehat{\mathcal L}=\{0,1\}^{\mathbb N}.
\tag{AP.2}
\]

A profile (b\in\widehat{\mathcal L}) is **effective** when (b=\chi_F) for some finite
(F\). It is **finitely approximable** when, for every finite query set (Q\subseteq\mathbb N),
there is finite (F) satisfying

\[
  \chi_F|_Q=b|_Q.
\tag{AP.3}
\]

### Theorem 1.1 — finite experimental density

The profile map (F\mapsto\chi_F) is injective, and every
(b\in\widehat{\mathcal L}) is finitely approximable.

### Proof

Injectivity follows because

\[
  n\in F\Longleftrightarrow\chi_F(n)=1.
\]

For a finite query set (Q), take

\[
  F_{Q,b}=\{n\in Q:b(n)=1\}.
\tag{AP.4}
\]

Then (F_{Q,b}) is finite and its characteristic profile agrees with (b) at every point of
(Q). `QED`

### Theorem 1.2 — coherent non-effective boundary

The constant-one profile

\[
  \mathbf 1(n)=1
\tag{AP.5}
\]

is finitely approximable but not effective.

### Proof

Finite approximability is Theorem 1.1. If (mathbf 1=\chi_F) for finite (F), choose
(n\notin F). Then (chi_F(n)=0\ne1), a contradiction. `QED`

Thus experimental coherence does not imply effectivity.

## 2. Finite adaptive experiments

A finite adaptive Boolean experiment with result type (A) is a finite binary tree generated by

\[
  T=\operatorname{out}(a)
  \quad\text{or}\quad
  T=\operatorname{query}(n,T_0,T_1),
\tag{AP.6}
\]

where a query reads (b(n)) and continues through (T_{b(n)}). Write
(\operatorname{run}(T,b)\in A) for its output.

For fixed (T,b), let (Q(T,b)) be the finite set of coordinates encountered along the run of
(T) against (b). Define the **adaptive mimicking support**

\[
  F(T,b)=\{n\in Q(T,b):b(n)=1\}.
\tag{AP.7}
\]

### Theorem 2.1 — adaptive mimicry

For every result type (A), finite adaptive experiment (T), and arbitrary virtual profile
(b\in\widehat{\mathcal L}),

\[
  \boxed{\operatorname{run}(T,\chi_{F(T,b)})
  =\operatorname{run}(T,b).}
\tag{AP.8}
\]

In particular, every single finite adaptive experiment on every virtual profile is exactly
mimicked by an effective finite-support law.

### Proof

By construction, (chi_{F(T,b)}(n)=b(n)) for every (n\in Q(T,b)). Prove `(AP.8)` by induction
on (T). It is immediate for an output leaf. At a query node (n), the two profiles agree at
(n), so they enter the same branch. The coordinates queried in that branch form a subset of
(Q(T,b)), and the induction hypothesis applies. `QED`

The theorem is stronger than fixed-cylinder density. Later queries may depend on all earlier
answers; the mimicking law still exists.

## 3. Effectivity cannot be decided by a finite adaptive experiment

Call a Boolean-output experiment (T) an **effectivity classifier** if

\[
  \operatorname{run}(T,\chi_F)=1
  \quad\text{for every finite }F,
\tag{AP.9}
\]

and

\[
  \operatorname{run}(T,b)=0
  \quad\text{for every non-effective }b.
\tag{AP.10}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 — finite adaptive effectivity no-go

No finite adaptive effectivity classifier exists.

### Proof

Apply adaptive mimicry to (T) and the non-effective constant-one profile. There is finite
(F) such that

\[
  \operatorname{run}(T,\chi_F)=\operatorname{run}(T,\mathbf1).
\]

The left side must be (1) by `(AP.9)` and the right side (0) by `(AP.10)`, contradiction.
`QED`

## 4. Field interpretation

The exact primitive exposed by these theorems is not a scalar obstruction. It is the pair

\[
  \mathcal L_{\mathrm{eff}}
  \longrightarrow
  \widehat{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{exp}}
\tag{AP.11}
\]

of effective laws and their experimental procompletion, together with a declared class of legal
experiments. Three layers must be retained:

1. **finite response:** what one experiment can read;
2. **coherent virtual law:** a compatible point of the experimental completion;
3. **effective law:** a source object whose complete profile realizes that point.

This reframes recurring corpus phenomena without identifying them:

- finite jets or finite observers can be complete at each aperture yet miss global source
  effectivity;
- recurrence may close every reported finite projection while lifted history remains outside the
  effective source image;
- positivity may preserve every selected finite scalar response while erasing the lift needed for
  effectivity;
- a no-go may assert that an observer lives entirely in the procompletion and therefore cannot
  certify whether its coherent point is source-realizable.

The field-level prediction is precise:

> Any proposed universal law geometry that identifies coherent finite satisfiability with global
> effectivity will admit false reconstructions. Any finite adaptive repair remains blind; resolving
> the gap requires an unbounded experiment, an effectivity witness, or a constitutive compactness
> theorem specific to the source.

## 5. Coordinate-free successor

For a source category (mathcal C), experiment category (mathcal E), and response functor

\[
  R:\mathcal C\longrightarrow \widehat{\mathcal R}_{\mathcal E},
\]

define the **effectivity locus** as the essential image of (R) and the **experimental boundary**
as its closure minus its image. The present theorem is the Boolean finite-support chart where the
boundary is nonempty and no compact object of the finite decision-tree doctrine separates it from
the image.

This suggests **experimental effectivity geometry** as one organ of Constitutive Process
Mathematics: study how effectivity loci change under interventions, doctrine changes, completion,
and history refinement. Theorem 3.1 is an anti-theatre gate: a field cannot claim reconstruction
from finite probes without proving an independent compactness/effectivity principle.

## 6. Exact boundaries and hostile tests

1. If every coherent profile is effective, the gap vanishes. The theorem does not manufacture a
   boundary for a compact/effective source.
2. An infinite experiment can distinguish the constant-one profile from every finite support by
   eventually querying outside that support. Finiteness is load-bearing.
3. A supplied effectivity certificate can decide effectivity without reading the profile alone.
   The no-go concerns experiments factoring only through finitely many adaptive Boolean queries.
4. A finite-dimensional profile space has no analogous boundary: every profile has finite
   support.
5. The result does not say hypertranscendence causes noncompactness. It says finite experimental
   coherence never by itself proves source effectivity.
6. The result is a classical compactness/Stone-profinite mechanism. Field content is its role as a
   typed constitutive distinction, not a priority claim for the mechanism.
7. No RH endpoint, route comparison, curvature, physics identity, or source selector follows.

Primary categorical parents include Leinster's codensity description of the ultrafilter monad
([arXiv:1209.3606](https://arxiv.org/abs/1209.3606)) and Di Liberti's relation between codensity,
pro-objects, and compactness ([arXiv:1910.01014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01014)). They supply
the completion machinery; the present theorem isolates adaptive experimental effectivity as the
constitutive invariant required by this program.
