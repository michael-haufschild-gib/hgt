## 1. Setup

Let `I` be a nonempty organ index set and `G` an additive abelian gauge group.  A local gauge packet
is `t=(t_i) in G^I`.  One source intervention `u in G` acts by the diagonal packet

\[
  \Delta u=(u)_i.                                      \tag{SCIS.1}
\]

Define the complete relative response

\[
  \delta t(i,j)=t_j-t_i.                              \tag{SCIS.2}
\]

For the selection clause, let `V,W` be modules over a commutative ring and let

\[
  \rho_i:V\to W

\[
  \Phi_\rho(x)=(\rho_i x)_i.                          \tag{SCIS.3}

## 2. Theorem

### Theorem 2.1 — source-coherent relative reconstruction

For every local packet `t`, global intervention `u`, and indices `i,j,k`:

\[
 \delta(t+\Delta u)(i,j)=\delta t(i,j),               \tag{SCIS.5}
\]

\[
 \delta t(i,j)+\delta t(j,k)=\delta t(i,k),           \tag{SCIS.6}
\]

and

\[
 t_i=t_{i_0}+\delta t(i_0,i).                         \tag{SCIS.7}
\]

For any two packets `s,t`, the following are equivalent:

\[
 \forall i,j,\quad \delta s(i,j)=\delta t(i,j),       \tag{SCIS.8}
\]

\[
 \exists u\in G,\quad t=s+\Delta u.                  \tag{SCIS.9}
\]

Equivalently, `delta` is a complete invariant of local gauge packets modulo the global source
action.  In particular,

\[
 \delta t=0\iff t=\Delta(t_{i_0}).                    \tag{SCIS.10}
\]

### Theorem 2.2 — source formation and exact selection

For every source displacement `x`,

\[
 \delta\Phi_\rho(x)(i,j)=(\rho_j-\rho_i)x.            \tag{SCIS.11}
\]

Moreover,

\[
 x\in K_\rho
 \iff
 \forall i,\quad \rho_i x=\rho_{i_0}x.               \tag{SCIS.12}
\]

Therefore, if `K_rho=0`, every globally synchronized source response selects the zero source
displacement:

\[
 \bigl(\forall i,\rho_i x=\rho_{i_0}x\bigr)
 \Longrightarrow x=0.                                \tag{SCIS.13}
\]

## 3. Proof

Equation `(SCIS.5)` is cancellation in the additive group:

\[
 (t_j+u)-(t_i+u)=t_j-t_i.
\]

Equation `(SCIS.6)` is telescoping.  Equation `(SCIS.7)` is the definition of the relative
response rearranged.

If `(SCIS.8)` holds, set

\[
 u=t_{i_0}-s_{i_0}.
\]

Applying `(SCIS.8)` to `(i0,i)` yields

\[
 s_i-s_{i_0}=t_i-t_{i_0},
\]

hence `t_i=s_i+u` for every `i`, proving `(SCIS.9)`.  Conversely, `(SCIS.9)` and global
cancellation prove `(SCIS.8)`.  Taking `s=0` gives `(SCIS.10)`.

Equation `(SCIS.11)` follows by expanding `(SCIS.2)` on `(SCIS.3)`.  Membership in the
intersection `(SCIS.4)` is exactly the family of equations

\[
 (\rho_i-\rho_{i_0})x=0,
\]

which is `(SCIS.12)`.  If the intersection is the zero submodule, `(SCIS.13)` follows. ∎
