## 1. Exact role after hostile audit

The leading-curvature theorem already handles the first surviving curvature component in every
finite-length `L_infinity` law.  Producing another closed form from a first nonzero component would
only restate that theorem.

The first unclosed mechanism is later and choice-sensitive.  A law can be flat through a declared
order, admit several partial flattenings, and acquire different higher curvature after those
choices.  The local invariant is then an obstruction **spectrum**, not one class.  What had not yet
been constructed was a global history law for that spectrum.

This card closes an exact calibration.  One three-storey graded bracket produces all of:

\[
 \boxed{
 \text{partial-flattening parameter}
 \longleftrightarrow
 \text{later curvature class}
 \longleftrightarrow
 \text{lifted higher period}
 \longrightarrow
 \text{compact recurrent holonomy}.}
 \tag{LPH.1}
\]

The first three arrows are exact reconstructions.  The last arrow is a lattice quotient and loses
the integer lift.  Consequently compact observer recurrence coexists with a proper, linearly
escaping law history generated by the same later obstruction.

This is not the full graded Ambrose--Singer--Spencer conjecture, and it is not a new later-layer
mechanism.  The underlying `S^2 times S^3` obstruction family is already Counterexample 5.2 of the
formalized secondary-curvature card.  The new statements here are its marked one-dimensional
period lift and elementary lattice-phase/properness consequences.  Those statements are correct,
but they are a `[BRIDGE]`, not a founding theorem of the field.

## 2. Three-storey graded law

Work over `R`.  Let `L` be the finite-dimensional graded Lie algebra with homogeneous generators

\[
 x\in L_{-1},\qquad y\in L_{-2},\qquad w\in L_{-3},
 \tag{LPH.2}
\]

and bracket

\[
 [x,y]=w.
 \tag{LPH.3}
\]

All brackets involving `w` vanish, and also

\[
 [x,x]=[y,y]=0.
 \tag{LPH.4}
\]

The unary differential is zero.  These formulas define a graded Lie algebra.  Indeed, every
possibly nonzero nested bracket contains `w` after its inner bracket and therefore vanishes.  Thus
the graded Jacobi identity holds.  The lower-central filtration

\[
 F^1L=L,\qquad F^2L=\mathbb Rw,\qquad F^3L=0
 \tag{LPH.5}
\]

makes `L` finite, complete, and nilpotent.

For differential forms, use the standard tensor-product graded bracket

\[
 [\alpha\otimes u,\beta\otimes v]
 =(-1)^{|u|\deg\beta}\alpha\wedge\beta\otimes[u,v].
 \tag{LPH.6}
\]

Total degree is form degree plus internal degree.

### Lemma 2.1 — cyclic completion does not change the mechanism

The shifted cotangent double

\[
 \mathbb T^*[d]L=L\ltimes L^*[d]
 \tag{LPH.7}
\]

with the coadjoint semidirect bracket carries its canonical nondegenerate invariant pairing of
degree `d`.  The inclusion `L -> T^*[d]L` is a strict filtered graded-Lie morphism.  Hence every
connection, curvature, period, and observer statement below embeds unchanged in a finite
nilpotent cyclic law algebra.

#### Proof

The coadjoint action is a graded representation because the adjoint action is.  The evaluation
pairing between `L` and its shifted dual is nondegenerate.  Its invariance is the defining identity
of the coadjoint action.  The first summand is a graded-Lie subalgebra, so all calculations internal
to `L` remain unchanged.  Nilpotence of the adjoint action implies nilpotence of the coadjoint
semidirect extension.  `QED`

Thus lack of a cyclic pairing is not hidden in the example; the model belongs to the cyclic sector
after the canonical completion.

## 3. Product law connection

Let `Sigma` be a closed connected oriented surface and `N` a closed connected oriented
three-manifold.  Choose closed forms

\[
 \omega\in\Omega^2(\Sigma),\qquad
 \eta\in\Omega^3(N)
 \tag{LPH.8}
\]

with nonzero periods

\[
 m=\int_\Sigma\omega\ne0,
 \qquad
 n=\int_N\eta\ne0.
 \tag{LPH.9}
\]

On `M=Sigma x N`, suppress pullback notation and define, for `t in R`,

\[
 a_t=\omega x+t\eta y.
 \tag{LPH.10}
\]

Both summands have total degree one.  Its homotopy Maurer--Cartan curvature is

\[
 \operatorname{Curv}(a_t)
 =d a_t+\frac12[a_t,a_t].
 \tag{LPH.11}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 — exact later interaction curvature

The connection `a_t` is flat through form degree four.  Its only curvature component is

\[
 \boxed{
 F_5(t)=-t\,\omega\wedge\eta\,w.}
 \tag{LPH.12}
\]

In particular,

\[
 dF_5(t)=0.
 \tag{LPH.13}
\]

The two proper components are separately flat:

\[
 \operatorname{Curv}(\omega x)=0,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Curv}(t\eta y)=0.
 \tag{LPH.14}
\]

The curvature is born only from their joint graded interaction.

#### Proof

Closedness of `omega` and `eta` gives `d a_t=0`.  The self-brackets vanish by `(LPH.4)`.
For the cross bracket, `(LPH.6)` gives

\[
 [\omega x,\eta y]
 =(-1)^{(-1)3}\omega\wedge\eta[x,y]
 =-\omega\wedge\eta\,w.
 \tag{LPH.15}
\]

Also

\[
 [\eta y,\omega x]
 =(-1)^{(-2)2}\eta\wedge\omega[y,x]
 =-\omega\wedge\eta\,w,
 \tag{LPH.16}
\]

because `[y,x]=-[x,y]` and two- and three-forms commute.  The factor `1/2` in `(LPH.11)`
cancels the two identical cross terms, proving `(LPH.12)`.  There is no coefficient space below
degree `-3`, and `w` is central, so no higher component occurs.  Equation `(LPH.13)` follows from
closedness of the two forms.  Setting either summand to zero proves `(LPH.14)`.  `QED`

### Interpretation as a later obstruction

The base connection

\[
 a_0=\omega x
 \tag{LPH.17}
\]

is flat.  Every correction `t eta y` changes no curvature component through degree four.  It is
therefore an order-four partial flattening in the precise sense of the NHG obstruction-spectrum
theorem.  Its next obstruction is

\[
 \mathfrak o_5(t)=[F_5(t)]
 =-t[\omega\wedge\eta]w.
 \tag{LPH.18}
\]

Thus this is not another first-page example.  It integrates the choice-sensitive next obstruction
over the whole partial-flattening line.

## 4. Period reconstruction

Define the lifted fifth period

\[
 \Pi(t)=\int_{\Sigma\times N}F_5(t)\in\mathbb Rw.
 \tag{LPH.19}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 — partial flattening, obstruction, and period reconstruct one another

One has

\[
 \boxed{
 \Pi(t)=-tmn\,w.}
 \tag{LPH.20}
\]

Consequently the following are equivalent:

1. `t=0`;
2. `F_5(t)=0`;
3. `[F_5(t)]=0` in de Rham cohomology with coefficient `Rw`;
4. `Pi(t)=0`.

Moreover,

\[
 \boxed{
 t=-\frac{\Pi(t)}{mnw},}
 \tag{LPH.21}
\]

where `(LPH.21)` means the unique scalar coordinate of `Pi(t)` in the marked basis `w`.

#### Proof

Fubini's product formula and `(LPH.9)` give

\[
 \int_{\Sigma\times N}\omega\wedge\eta
 =\left(\int_\Sigma\omega\right)
  \left(\int_N\eta\right)=mn.
\]

Substitute `(LPH.12)` to obtain `(LPH.20)`.  Since `m`, `n`, and `w` are nonzero, the period
vanishes exactly when `t=0`.  A cohomologically trivial closed form has zero period on the closed
fundamental cycle, so nonzero period proves nonzero class.  The remaining implications are
immediate, and division by the marked nonzero scalar `mnw` gives `(LPH.21)`.  `QED`

### Corollary 4.2 — exact obstruction and period spectra

As `t` ranges over `R`, both the obstruction spectrum and lifted period spectrum are the full
marked line:

\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{Ob}_5(a_0)supseteq
 \{-t[\omega\wedge\eta]w:t\in\mathbb R\}
 =\mathbb R[\omega\wedge\eta]w,}
 \tag{LPH.22}
\]

\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{Per}_5(a_0)=\{\Pi(t):t\in\mathbb R\}=\mathbb Rw.}
 \tag{LPH.23}
\]

The map from this partial-flattening line to the period line is a linear isomorphism.  This is the
first explicit NHG model in which a Massey-choice-type later obstruction spectrum is globally and
faithfully integrated.

## 5. Proper-face invisibility and joint detection

### Theorem 5.1 — no proper factor sees the interaction

For every `t`, pullback of `F_5(t)` to either factor is zero:

\[
 i_\Sigma^*F_5(t)=0,
 \qquad
 i_N^*F_5(t)=0.
 \tag{LPH.24}
\]

Every observer that retains only one summand of `(LPH.10)` also sees zero curvature.  Nevertheless
the joint product observer sees the nonzero period `(LPH.20)` for every `t != 0`.

#### Proof

A five-form restricts to zero on a two- or three-dimensional factor.  More specifically, the
pullback of `eta` to the surface and the pullback of `omega` to the three-manifold are zero.  The
single-summand statement is `(LPH.14)`.  Joint nonvanishing follows from Theorem 4.1.  `QED`

This is an exact higher interaction law:

\[
 \boxed{
 \text{all proper faces flat}
 \quad\not\Rightarrow\quad
 \text{joint law flat}.}
 \tag{LPH.25}
\]

The missing datum is not another marginal.  It is the graded bracket joining the two marginals.

### Theorem 5.2 — observer naturality and sharp erasure criterion

Let `q:L->L'` be a strict graded-Lie observer.  Then

\[
 \operatorname{Curv}(q a_t)=qF_5(t),
 \qquad
 \int_M\operatorname{Curv}(q a_t)=q\Pi(t).
 \tag{LPH.26}
\]

The observer erases the entire later interaction history exactly when

\[
 q(w)=0.
 \tag{LPH.27}
\]

For a family `(q_i)`, the joint observer reconstructs `t` exactly when the family separates the
marked line `Rw`, equivalently when some `q_i(w)` is nonzero.

#### Proof

Strictness gives `q[u,v]=[qu,qv]` and commutation with the zero differential, hence curvature
naturality.  Integration is linear.  Equation `(LPH.20)` then gives `(LPH.27)` and the joint
separation criterion.  `QED`

Serial observation cannot repair erasure of `w`; a later observer receives zero.  Parallel
observers repair it precisely by jointly separating the interaction line.

## 6. Recurrent compact period phase with proper lifted history

Fix a lattice

\[
 \Lambda=\ell\mathbb Zw\subset\mathbb Rw,
 \qquad \ell>0,
 \tag{LPH.28}
\]

and let

\[
 h_\ell(t)=\Pi(t)\bmod\Lambda\in\mathbb Rw/\Lambda.
 \tag{LPH.29}
\]

This is a compact **period-phase observer**; the lifted period `Pi(t)` is retained separately.  If
the curvature satisfies the relevant integral-period condition, the same quotient is the phase of
a differential character.  No such integrality assumption, differential character, or higher
transport is needed or claimed below.

### Theorem 6.1 — exact recurrence/open-lift/properness trinity

Put

\[
 T={\ell\over|mn|}>0.
 \tag{LPH.30}
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{h_\ell(t+T)=h_\ell(t)}
 \tag{LPH.31}
\]

exactly; the sign of `mn` merely decides which lattice generator is traversed.  In particular the
compact observer is periodic.
Along the recurrent sequence `t_k=kT`,

\[
 h_\ell(t_k)=h_\ell(0),
 \qquad
 \|\Pi(t_k)\|=k\ell\|w\|\longrightarrow\infty.
 \tag{LPH.32}
\]

For any fixed positive norm on the marked line,

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal A(t)=\|\Pi(t)\|^2
 =m^2n^2\|w\|^2t^2}
 \tag{LPH.33}
\]

is proper.  Therefore bounded lifted action forces bounded partial-flattening parameter, and

\[
 \mathcal A(t)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad t=0.
 \tag{LPH.34}
\]

#### Proof

By `(LPH.20)`, changing `t` by `T` changes the lifted period by one signed lattice generator.
This proves `(LPH.31)`.  At `t_k=kT`, the lift is `-k sign(mn) ell w`, giving `(LPH.32)`.
Equation `(LPH.33)` follows directly from `(LPH.20)` and is a positive quadratic with nonzero
coefficient, hence proper and zero only at the origin.  `QED`

The mechanism is now one source-generated chain:

```text
graded bracket
  -> later interaction curvature
  -> factorized higher period
  -> compact period quotient
  -> recurrent observer + proper open law history.
```

Recurrence and properness are not independently attached doctrines in this model.  Both come from
the same marked period map.

## 7. Exact coupling and its significance boundary

The construction couples four NHG shadows, but hostile audit shows that they are not four
independently source-generated organs: the lattice and norm are externally chosen after the period
map is known.

| organ | exact realization |
|---|---|
| law formation | order-four partial flattening `t eta y` |
| nonlinear interaction | graded bracket `[x,y]=w` |
| higher topology | fifth obstruction class and lifted period |
| dynamics/properness | compact recurrent quotient with proper period lift |

The fourth organ is not an analogy: its recurrence period and escape slope were forced by the
period calculation:

\[
 \text{recurrence period}={\ell\over|mn|},
 \qquad
 \text{lift escape slope}=|mn|\|w\|.
 \tag{LPH.35}
\]

Their product is the lattice length `ell ||w||`.  This identity is an exact consequence once the
external lattice and norm are supplied.  It is not a withheld prediction of the graded law alone.

## 8. Conjecture--proof/failure loops

### Loop A — leading curvature is enough

**False.**  The base law `a_0` is flat.  The nonzero class appears only after choosing a legal
order-four partial flattening.

**Survivor.**  The whole partial-flattening obstruction spectrum must be retained.

### Loop B — a later obstruction spectrum has no global geometry

**False in this sector.**  The period map `(LPH.20)` integrates the whole spectrum and is an
isomorphism on the marked line.

**Boundary.**  General later spectra are multivalued and need not be affine lines.

### Loop C — flat proper faces force a flat joint law

**False.**  Equations `(LPH.14)` and `(LPH.24)` coexist with nonzero `(LPH.20)`.

**Survivor.**  The cross bracket, not the marginal curvature, is the joint interaction carrier.

### Loop D — compact higher holonomy closes law history

**False.**  Equation `(LPH.32)` gives exact periodic recurrence downstairs and linear escape
upstairs.

**Survivor.**  Retain the lattice lift or the proper period action.

### Loop E — cyclicity must be postulated independently

**False as an obstruction to this model.**  The cotangent double supplies a canonical cyclic
completion without changing the interaction sector.

**Boundary.**  A positive real polarization of that cyclic completion is additional structure.

### Loop F — this proves the full Ambrose--Singer--Spencer conjecture

**False.**  The theorem integrates one explicit later layer.  It does not prove that every
restricted higher holonomy tangent is generated by an arbitrary full curvature Postnikov tower.

**Survivor.**  The general conjecture now has a nontrivial length-three test case with exact
interaction, period, observer, recurrence, and properness laws.

## 10. Scope and next gate

The theorem is finite-dimensional, nilpotent, and unconditional.  It uses no zeta function, proof
route, target zero, RH hypothesis, or fitted arithmetic parameter.

It does **not** prove:

1. full higher transport for every filtered `L_infinity` algebroid;
2. generation of all restricted higher holonomy by every later obstruction spectrum;
3. source selection of a positive polarization;
4. compact-resolvent index theory;
5. any RH statement.

The next exact field target is no longer “find a length-three example.”  It is:

> Prove that for a finite nilpotent three-storey graded law whose later obstruction spectrum is an
> affine subspace of a flat coefficient local system, the lifted higher-period map is affine and
> its derivative is generated by the transported cross-curvature bracket; then classify when a
> lattice quotient is proper relative to the marked lift.

That statement would extend `(LPH.20)` from one product model to a genuine length-three
Ambrose--Singer theorem for an affine family of partial flattenings.
