## 1. Frozen conjecture and hostile attack

### 1.1 Strongest truth-hunch

Fix a field `k`, a type `I` of primitive interventions, a type `R` of legal rewrites, and an
observer module `O`. A **linear intervention grammar** consists of

\[
 \rho:R\longrightarrow k^{(I)},
 \qquad o:I\longrightarrow O,                              \tag{CFIP.1}
\]

where `k^(I)` denotes finitely supported formal histories, such that every declared relation is
observer-neutral:

\[
 \sum_{i\in I}\rho(r)_i o(i)=0\qquad(r\in R).             \tag{CFIP.2}
\]

The grammar should generate one canonical source object with this property:

\[
 \mathcal H_G:=k^{(I)}/\operatorname{span}\{\rho(r):r\in R\}. \tag{CFIP.3}
\]

All flat linear readouts of the grammar should be representations of `H_G`, not competing
candidates for its identity.

### 1.2 Killed stronger claims

Four stronger readings are false.

1. **Every flat readout assignment factors automatically.** False: a generator assignment that sends one
   legal relation to a nonzero vector cannot descend to the quotient.
2. **Every factoring readout is faithful.** False: a representation may have a larger kernel than the
   legal relation span and erase source history.
3. **The algebraic quotient already supplies RH selection.** False: it records whether a history is
   exactly fillable but not the cost of a filling. Rescaling a relation generator leaves the
   quotient unchanged while changing every quantitative filling budget.
4. **Every route is a flat readout.** False: a chain-level route may carry a source rewrite boundary
   to a nonzero target rewrite boundary. Quotienting first erases exactly the curvature data that
   such a route retains.

Thus the quotient is the flat observer/readout layer, not the general route-classification or
metric-selection layer. ULF-TAFM-12's normed completion and curvature--filling action remain
load-bearing.

### 1.3 Positive survivor

There is nevertheless an exact universal law: annihilating the primitive rewrite list is both
necessary and sufficient for a unique flat readout representation. It gives a complete failure
certificate for every boundary-annihilating observer. The chain-level universal route law is a
strict successor, not a corollary smuggled into this theorem.

## 2. Construction

Let

\[
 C_1(G)=k^{(I)},\qquad C_2(G)=k^{(R)}.                     \tag{CFIP.4}
\]

Extend `rho` linearly to the boundary

\[
 \partial_G:C_2(G)\longrightarrow C_1(G),
 \qquad
 \partial_G\!\left(\sum_r a_r[r]\right)=\sum_r a_r\rho(r). \tag{CFIP.5}
\]

Extend `o` linearly to the observer

\[
 q_G:C_1(G)\longrightarrow O,
 \qquad
 q_G\!\left(\sum_i c_i[i]\right)=\sum_i c_i o(i).        \tag{CFIP.6}
\]

Equation `(CFIP.2)` gives `q_G partial_G=0`. Define the **presented history module**

\[
 \overline C_1(G):=C_1(G)/\operatorname{im}\partial_G,   \tag{CFIP.7}
\]

write `pi_G:C_1(G)→overline C_1(G)` for the quotient map, and let

\[
 \iota_G(i):=\pi_G([i]).                                  \tag{CFIP.8}
\]

For a `k`-module `W` and an assignment `phi:I→W`, let

\[
 L_\phi:C_1(G)\longrightarrow W,
 \qquad L_\phi\!\left(\sum_i c_i[i]\right)=\sum_i c_i\phi(i). \tag{CFIP.9}
\]

Call `phi` **relation-admissible** when

\[
 L_\phi(\rho(r))=0\qquad(r\in R).                         \tag{CFIP.10}
\]

## 3. Main theorem

### Theorem ULF-TAFM-13 — canonical free intervention presentation

For every linear intervention grammar `G`, module `W`, and generator assignment `phi:I→W`, the
following are equivalent.

1. There exists a unique linear map

   \[
   \overline L_\phi:\overline C_1(G)\longrightarrow W     \tag{CFIP.11}
   \]

   satisfying `overline L_phi(iota_G(i))=phi(i)` for every `i`.
2. `phi` is relation-admissible: it annihilates every declared rewrite `(CFIP.10)`.

When these conditions hold,

\[
 \overline L_\phi\pi_G=L_\phi.                            \tag{CFIP.12}
\]

Moreover, the grammar observer assignment `o` is relation-admissible by `(CFIP.2)`. Hence it has a
unique presented observer

\[
 \overline q_G:\overline C_1(G)\longrightarrow O,
 \qquad \overline q_G\pi_G=q_G.                           \tag{CFIP.13}
\]

### Proof

Assume first that `phi` is relation-admissible. For a rewrite chain
`a=sum_r a_r[r]`, linearity and `(CFIP.10)` give

\[
 L_\phi(\partial_Ga)
 =\sum_r a_rL_\phi(\rho(r))=0.                            \tag{CFIP.14}
\]

Therefore `im partial_G` is contained in `ker L_phi`. The quotient lifting theorem supplies a
linear map `overline L_phi` satisfying `(CFIP.12)`. On the generator class,

\[
 \overline L_\phi(\iota_G(i))
 =L_\phi([i])=\phi(i).                                    \tag{CFIP.15}
\]

For uniqueness, suppose `F:overline C_1(G)→W` has the same values on all `iota_G(i)`. Every
finitely supported history has the form `x=sum_i c_i[i]`; hence

\[
 F(\pi_Gx)=\sum_i c_iF(\iota_G(i))
           =\sum_i c_i\phi(i)
           =L_\phi(x)
           =\overline L_\phi(\pi_Gx).                    \tag{CFIP.16}
\]

Every quotient class is `pi_G(x)` for some `x`, so `F=overline L_phi`.

Conversely, suppose a flat representation `F` with the prescribed generator values exists. By the same
finite-support expansion,

\[
 F(\pi_Gx)=L_\phi(x)                                      \tag{CFIP.17}
\]

for every history `x`. Each primitive relation lies in `im partial_G`, since
`rho(r)=partial_G([r])`, so `pi_G(rho(r))=0`. Thus

\[
 L_\phi(\rho(r))=F(\pi_G\rho(r))=F(0)=0.                 \tag{CFIP.18}
\]

This proves necessity, sufficiency, and uniqueness. Taking `phi=o` and using `(CFIP.2)` proves
`(CFIP.13)`. ∎

## 4. Intrinsic diagnostics

The theorem splits every proposed flat observer or scalar readout into three independent questions.

1. **Flatness:** does its generator assignment annihilate every source rewrite? If not, the organ
   is not a flat readout. The nonzero image of one relation is an explicit curvature witness, not
   necessarily a defect in a chain-level route.
2. **Resolution:** if it factors, what is the kernel of the induced readout? A kernel larger than
   zero is information discarded by that observer.
3. **Selection:** after factorization, does a source-fixed normed completion make complete
   curvature-plus-residual action sublinear? This is not decided by the algebraic quotient.

This is coordinate-independent bookkeeping with mathematical force, but only at the flat readout
layer. A general route must instead exhibit target rewrite witnesses and boundary naturality.

## 6. New prediction

Consider a fourth flat readout not used to define a proposed source grammar. Once its primitive
generator images are specified, exactly one of the following occurs.

1. A source relation has nonzero image; then the readout cannot descend to the flat quotient.
2. Every source relation vanishes; then the flat readout representation exists uniquely.
3. In case 2, a nonzero presented history enters the readout kernel; then the observer is provably
   information-losing on that history.
4. In case 2, the representation is faithful on the target history; then every later failure of
   selection belongs to quantitative completion/filling action, not algebraic compatibility.

This predicts a reusable audit theorem for every proposed scalar/observer endpoint. It does not
classify chain-level routes carrying nonzero relation images.

## 9. Boundary of theorem

- It classifies flat readouts only, not general chain-level route morphisms.
- It does not assert that current corpus observers already factor through one literal grammar.
- It does not manufacture or validate primitive arithmetic relations.
- It does not prove induced readout maps are injective.
- It does not provide a canonical norm or completion.
- It does not turn functional-equation neutrality into selection.
- It does not prove RH.

These boundaries are decisive. In particular, quotienting cannot be used to declare nonzero
