## Goal and concrete input

For a finite real divisor weight `c`, let `s(k)=∑(d≤N,d∣k)c(d)`, `H=∑(d≤N)c(d)/d`, and let `Y(e)=∑(d≤N,e∣d)c(d)/d` be its existing Selberg coordinates. Use a literal common-period average of `s(k)s(k+h)`, with canonical period `N!`, to define the lag covariance. Prove its arithmetic density formula, not merely a renamed abstract diagonal.

The existing one-lag theory sees every squareful mode through the same zero Möbius eigenvalue. The lag-two coefficient is `r₂(e)=μ(e)+2·1_(2∣e)μ(e/2)`, exposing mode 4 while retaining the full signed lag-one information. This addresses arithmetic node resolution; it does not establish Wall 11 closure.

## Theorem and symbolic mechanism

Write `V=∑(e≥2)φ(e)Y(e)²`, `C₁=∑(e≥2)μ(e)Y(e)²`, and `C₂=∑(e≥2)r₂(e)Y(e)²`. The literal period bridge is proved below and formalized by `periodCovariance_zero`, `periodCovariance_one`, and `periodCovariance_two`.

Exact region, at every cutoff at least 12:

1. `2V+3C₁+C₂ ≥ 0`.
2. `V+C₁+C₂ ≥ 0`.
3. `V−C₁+C₂ ≥ 0`.
4. `V−4C₁−2C₂ ≥ 0`.
5. `V−C₁−2C₂ ≥ 0`.

Normalized rays are `(-1,1)`, `(-1/2,-1/2)`, `(0,-1)`, `(1/2,-1/2)`, `(0,1/2)`, from modes 2, 3, 4, 6, 12 respectively. General modes reduce using the 2-adic valuation and Möbius sign. The facets need `φ(q)≥6` for odd `q>1` with `μ(q)=1`; two distinct odd prime factors give the stronger `φ(q)≥8`, formalized as `odd_totient_ge_eight_of_moebius_one`.

An explicit three-ray triangulation realizes every point satisfying the five inequalities. Put `x=C₁`, `y=C₂` and assign nonnegative variance masses as follows:

- If `x+y≥0`, use modes 2, 6, 12 with masses `(V−4x−2y)/3`, `2(V−x−2y)/3`, `2(x+y)`.
- If `x+y<0` and `V+2x+y≥0`, use modes 2, 4, 6 with masses `(V−x+y)/3`, `−x−y`, `2(V+2x+y)/3`.
- Otherwise use modes 2, 3, 4 with masses `V+x+y`, `−2(V+2x+y)`, `2V+3x+y`.

Set each nonconstant coordinate to `Y(q)=√(mass_q/φ(q))`, add an arbitrary mean coordinate at 1, and use the existing finite Ramanujan multiples inverse. These are symbolic formulas; no numerical experiment is proposed.

The dual classification is: `αV+βC₁+γC₂≥0` for all finite real weights iff all five expressions `α−β+γ`, `2α−β−γ`, `α−γ`, `2α+β−γ`, `2α+γ` are nonnegative. These claims have a complete paper proof below and checked Lean certificates for actual literal period moments and finite-weight realizers.

## Dependency-closed paper proof [PROVED HERE]

1. **Literal complete-period density.** Let `P=N!`. For positive `a,b≤N`, put `g=gcd(a,b)`, `a=gA`, `b=gB`. A solution of `a∣k` and `b∣k+h` forces `g∣h`. If `h=gH`, every solution has `k=gm`, and its conditions reduce to `A∣m`, `B∣m+H`, where `gcd(A,B)=1`. The Chinese remainder theorem gives one residue class modulo `AB`. Since `gAB=lcm(a,b)` divides `P`, exactly `P/(gAB)` integers in `1..P` satisfy the pair. Thus its normalized density is `g·1_(g∣h)/(ab)`. Single-divisor counts give mean `H=∑c(d)/d`, also after every additive shift. Expanding the finite sums therefore identifies the centered literal lag covariance with the corresponding density quadratic form minus `H²`.

2. **Diagonal and mean removal.** For every positive `g`, `∑(e∣g)μ(e)=1_(g=1)` and, by the bijection `e=2d`, `∑(e∣g,2∣e)μ(e/2)=1_(g=2)`. Consequently `∑(e∣g)r₂(e)=1_(g=1)+2·1_(g=2)=g·1_(g∣2)`. Interchanging finite divisor and pair sums gives the lag-two diagonal `∑r₂(e)Y(e)²`. The lag-one diagonal uses the first Möbius identity; the zero-lag diagonal uses `∑(e∣g)φ(e)=g`. In each case mode 1 is exactly `H²`. Removing it gives the stated `V,C₁,C₂`. The empty cutoff is identically zero.

3. **Odd arithmetic bound.** If odd `q>1` has `μ(q)=1`, it is squarefree and not a prime power. Hence it has distinct odd prime divisors `p,r`. One is at least 3 and the other at least 5. Their product divides `q`, so `8≤(p−1)(r−1)=φ(pr)≤φ(q)`; the last inequality follows from totient divisibility. For other Möbius signs, `μ(q)∈{-1,0,1}` and `φ(q)≥2` suffice. Thus every odd `q>1` satisfies `φ(q)≥6μ(q)`, `φ(q)+2μ(q)≥0`, and `φ(q)≥0`.

4. **All arithmetic rays satisfy the five facets.** For odd `e>1`, the ray is `(φ(e),μ(e),μ(e))`; substitute the bounds in step 3. If `e=2r` with odd `r`, multiplicativity gives `(φ(r),−μ(r),μ(r))`. If `e=4r` with odd `r`, the ray is `(2φ(r),0,−2μ(r))`. Both cases use `φ(r)≥μ(r)` and `φ(r)+2μ(r)≥0`; at `r=1` these follow from `φ(1)=μ(1)=1`, and at `r>1` from step 3. If `8∣e`, both Möbius terms vanish and the ray is `(φ(e),0,0)`. These cases exhaust every `e≥2`, and direct substitution proves all five inequalities. Multiplying each inequality by `Y(e)²≥0` and summing proves necessity for every finite real weight.

5. **Explicit realization proves sufficiency.** In each of the three branches above, the displayed masses are nonnegative by the five facets and the branch tests. Algebra gives total mass `V` and the two weighted ray coordinates `x,y`. Dividing each mass by its positive totient and taking its real square root produces actual coordinates at the three selected modes. Put any prescribed mean at mode 1 and zero at all remaining modes, then apply the existing finite multiples inverse at level `N+12`. Its exact coordinate theorem gives all four requested moments. The argument needs no prior assumption `V≥0`: the nonnegative masses themselves imply it.

6. **Dual sharpness and strict extra information.** A linear functional is nonnegative on the cone exactly when it is nonnegative on the five displayed rays. Pure-mode inverse weights realize each ray, proving necessity as well as sufficiency of the five dual conditions. For every prescribed mean `H` and every cutoff `N+12`, normalized modes 4 and 12 have that same mean, variance 1, and lag-one covariance 0, but lag-two covariances `−1` and `1/2`. Thus the second lag supplies arithmetic information unavailable from mean, variance, and lag one alone.
