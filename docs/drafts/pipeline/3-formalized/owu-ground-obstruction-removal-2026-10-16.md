## Dependency-closed paper proof

Fix a positive aperture. Write J for the physical embedding, Q for the literal
Weil form, m for the attained blind minimum, and A for its exact arithmetic
correction. The already-proved dual theorem says
`q(u) = Re <(Q-A)u,u> - m ||Ju||^2 >= 0` for every odd form vector.
Its null vectors constitute the odd part S of the actual finite-dimensional
ground eigenspace. S contains every blind minimizer and is nonzero.

Let F_u be the entire Fourier--Laplace transform of the compact time
representative. Existing corpus division produces a same-aperture form vector
at each transform zero. If F_u(z)=0 and Im(z^2) is nonzero, oddness also gives
F_u(-z)=0. Two divisions give F_u(w)=(w^2-c)F_v(w), c=z^2. Transform injectivity
shows v is odd. Set h=u+i Im(c)v and k=Im(c)v. The polynomial identity
`(x-conj c)(x-c)=(x-Re c)^2+(Im c)^2`
splits the entire Hermitian transform product, with x=w^2. It therefore splits
every frequency-diagonal integral, all arithmetic traces, physical mass, and
the cross-evaluation pole term. Consequently q(u)=q(h)+q(k). Both summands are
nonnegative, so q(v)=0. Thus S is closed under paired division at off-axis zeros.

Let d=dim S>0. Evaluation at d-1 distinct off-axis points has a nonzero kernel.
Choose u in that kernel and divide successively by w^2-c_j, obtaining v_0=u,
...,v_(d-1)=G in S. They are independent: after factoring out F_G, their
polynomial factors have distinct degrees 0,...,d-1 in w^2. Any polynomial
relation vanishes at infinitely many real w where F_G(w) is nonzero, hence is
identically zero. These d vectors form a basis. It follows that
`F(S)={P(w^2)F_G(w): deg P<d}` exactly, with unique P.

If F_G had a zero z off both axes, paired division would give G' in S. The
basis representation F_G'=P(w^2)F_G would imply
`(X-z^2)P(X)=1`, impossible by polynomial degree. Hence all zeros of F_G lie
on the real or imaginary axis. Any other cyclic generator differs by a nonzero
scalar, since the two generator polynomials multiply to 1. All original blind
minimizers lie in S, so their entire transforms have this common factor. No
claim is made that G itself is blind or that m is nonnegative.
