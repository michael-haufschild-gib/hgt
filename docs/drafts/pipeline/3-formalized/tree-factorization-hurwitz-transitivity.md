## Why this is needed

The actual critical-value map now has properness, local inverses, a connected regular covering, finite collision fibers and exact root transport through avoiding deformation squares. Those facts do not realize an arbitrary prescribed planar monodromy. The next combinatorial obligation is to connect every eligible transposition factorization to one actually realized factorization. A separate geometric proof must then identify the adjacent moves with actual critical-value exchanges and identify simple polynomial branch inertia and exterior monodromy. This card proves only the combinatorial part; it is not an assumed realization theorem.

## Complete paper proof [PROVED HERE]

Write multiplication so the rightmost permutation acts first. A Hurwitz move replaces adjacent factors (a,b) by (b,b⁻¹ab). Their product is unchanged, and conjugation preserves transpositions. Inverse moves are allowed. We prove transitivity by induction on d, with the d=1 empty factorization immediate.

Given a length-(d−1) transposition factorization of a transitive permutation σ with d>1, count occurrences of each sheet among the supports of the factors. Each transposition contributes exactly two, by the inspected support-cardinality theorem, so the sum of all occurrence counts is 2(d−1). Every sheet occurs at least once: a sheet fixed by every factor would be fixed by σ and every power of σ, contradicting transitivity on more than one sheet. If every sheet occurred at least twice, their total count would be at least 2d, impossible. Thus some sheet a occurs exactly once. This is the only leaf property required below; no graph construction, edge distinctness or tree-cardinality argument is needed.

We can turn that leaf into any fixed chosen sheet a₀ using only Hurwitz moves. Move the first factor past every subsequent factor. If the original product is σ, the resulting list is (t₂,…,tₖ,σ⁻¹t₁σ): successive conjugations by the tail give precisely the final term. Repeating this cycling operation k=d−1 times conjugates every factor by σ⁻¹, while retaining product σ. Repetition therefore translates the support graph by powers of σ⁻¹. Since σ acts transitively, some such power sends a to a₀. The transformed factorization thus has a₀ as a leaf. This step avoids the unjustified assumption that an arbitrary chosen sheet was already a leaf.

Move the unique factor containing a₀ to the last position. At each move, the next factor fixes a₀. Its conjugation of the leaf factor remains a transposition containing a₀, while all earlier factors still fix a₀. At the end, the list is (u₁,…,uₖ₋₁,(a₀,b)), and its prefix U fixes a₀. Since σ=U(a₀,b), evaluating at b yields σ(b)=a₀. Thus b=σ⁻¹(a₀), determined solely by σ and the selected sheet.

The prefix product τ=σ(a₀,σ⁻¹(a₀)) fixes a₀ and induces a transitive permutation on the remaining d−1 sheets: its orbit follows the σ-cycle with a₀ deleted. Explicitly, every remaining sheet except σ⁻¹(a₀) still maps to its σ-successor, while σ⁻¹(a₀) maps directly to σ(a₀). The prefix consists of d−2 transpositions supported on those sheets. Apply the induction hypothesis to the prefixes obtained from any two original factorizations. Extend each smaller-set Hurwitz move by fixing a₀, and append the common final factor. This connects the normalized factorizations, hence the originals.

## Stronger counting survivor [PROVED HERE on paper]

Transitivity is unnecessary for the counting lemma: every length-(d−1) transposition word whose product has no fixed points contains a sheet moved by exactly one factor. A sheet with zero occurrences would be fixed by the product; all remaining counts are positive, and their total 2(d−1) prevents every count from being at least two. This exact weaker-hypothesis statement and the support-incidence sum are formalized and fully audited in `TranspositionOccurrenceLeaf.lean`.
