# The growth boundary for ordinary-prime quotient profiles

For a fixed real profile $F(n)=O(n^a)$ with $0\le a<1$, the uncentered ordinary-prime sum $\sum_{p\le X}F(\lfloor X/p\rfloor)$ is $O(X^b)$ for some $0\le b<1$ exactly when $F(n)=0$ at every positive integer. Its jump series factors as $P(s)D(s)$, where $P(s)=\sum_p p^{-s}$ and $D$ has holomorphic continuation to $\Re s>a$ by summation by parts. Since $P(s)=-\log(s-1)+O(1)$ on the real ray, no nonzero holomorphic factor can remove its branch at one. Uniqueness of the jump series forces $F(n)=0$ for every $n\ge1$.

At the linear boundary put $w(n)=(-1)^{\Omega(n)}/\prod_p v_p(n)!$, $d(n)=w(n)/(1+\Omega(n))$ and $F(X)=\sum_{n\le X}d(n)$. At $n>1$ the identity $\sum_{p\mid n}v_p(n)=\Omega(n)$ gives $\sum_{p\mid n}d(n/p)=-w(n)$. Thus $\sum_{p\le X}F(\lfloor X/p\rfloor)=1-\sum_{n\le X}w(n)$ for $X\ge1$.

Write $w=\mu*g$. The local series of $g$ is $e^{-z}/(1-z)$, and that of $g^{-1}$ is $(1-z)e^z$. Their coefficients at $p$ vanish, and those at $p^k$, $k\ge2$, have modulus at most one. Both corrections are supported on powerful numbers and have absolutely convergent Dirichlet series in $\Re s>1/2$. Consequently $\sum_{n\le X}w(n)=O(X^b)$ is equivalent to $M(X)=O(X^b)$ for every $b>1/2$. The seven-eighths strip gives every $7/8<b<1$ for this explicit nonzero $F$, which itself is bounded by $X$ and admits no sublinear power bound. An independent arithmetic estimate for the factorial-weighted parity sum at every $b>1/2$ would give RH.
