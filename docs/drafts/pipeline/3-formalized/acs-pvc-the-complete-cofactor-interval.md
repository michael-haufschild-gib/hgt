# The complete cofactor interval above a linear first-prime cut

Let \(B_{>}(N)\) be the original seventeen-ninths prefix restricted to \(p>n\). On every fragment ending at \(M\), the removed price is \(O(M^3(1+\log(2M)))\). Thus the full one-sided \(11/3\) and \(3+\eta\) targets can both be tested using \(B_{>}\). Each remaining product \(q=pv\) has exactly one prime divisor above \(n\), since \(v\le n<p\). Bounding \(B_{>}\) remains open.

**Proof.** Write \(L\) for the spline horizon, \(f\) for the dyadic spline and \(U=\lfloor n^{17/9}\rfloor\). Eventually \(U\ge\mathrm{productLower}(1,L)\), so a product \(q>U\) first enters at \(k=q\) and remains through \(L\). For \(4<p\le n\) all \(v\le n\) have \(pv\le n^2\le L\). Summing in \(k\) first gives the exact weight
\[
  n^{-1}\{V_{L+1}(pv/n^2)+f(pv/n^2)\}.
\]
The second summand is the returned first endpoint. Since \(W(0)=1\) and \(f(0)=0\), the one-cell mesh is \(V_1=-f\). The numerator is therefore \(V_{L+1}-V_1\), with the same uniform variation estimate on both terms. The cofactor interval is exactly \((\lfloor U/p\rfloor,n]\).

The signed cofactor coefficients \(a_p(v)=\mathbf1_{p\nmid v}\mathrm{innerJump}(1,v)\) have uniformly bounded prefixes. Subtract the multiples of \(p\) from a full prefix and apply the large-prime dilation identity. For \(d=p/n^2\), the mesh \(V_b(vd)\) is bounded and its adjacent differences are \(O(1/v)\) uniformly in \(b\) and \(d\). This follows by comparing \(xV_b((v+1)d/x^2)\) at \(x=1\) and \(x=\sqrt{1+1/v}\) using its bounded conductor derivative. Abel summation on the cofactor interval therefore costs \(O(1+\log(2n))\), including the sample at each end. The ordinary prime mass is at most \(\sum_{q\le n}\Lambda(q)\le7n\). After the \(n^{-1}\) normalization and the exterior \(n^2\), summation proves the fragment bound. The finitely many initial conductors have bounded total cost.

**Removing the entire cofactor cap.** Put \(F_n=\max(n,\lfloor L_n/(n+1)\rfloor,2\lfloor\sqrt{L_n}\rfloor)\). The fixed-prime argument charges all \(p\le F_n\) at most \(O(nF_n(1+\log(2n)))\) in the weighted row, because the cofactor interval is always clipped at \(\min(n,\lfloor L_n/p\rfloor)\). Since \(L_n\ll n^2\), this still costs \(O(M^3(1+\log(2M)))\) on every terminal fragment. For every remaining \(p>F_n\) and every actual horizon \(k\le L_n\), one has \(\lfloor k/p\rfloor\le n<p\). Also \(\mathrm{productLower}(1,k)\le 2\lfloor\sqrt{L_n}\rfloor<p\), so dividing the original lower endpoint by \(p\) gives exactly \(\lfloor U/p\rfloor\). The entire inner sum runs over \(\lfloor U/p\rfloor<v\le\lfloor k/p\rfloor\) with the original signed weights. The upper cofactor cap and coprimality both disappear at the full RH exponent.

**Lean verification.** For each \(\eta>0\), the one-sided \(M^{3+\eta}\) estimate for the original prefix is equivalent to the same estimate for primes \(p>F_n\) with the cofactor interval above. The eleven axiom checks, including the signed fragment estimate, use only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`. **Four adjacent cofactor caps.** Set \(G_V(s)=\sum_{1\le v\le V}g(v)v^{-s}\), where \(g(v)=\mathrm{innerJump}(1,v)\) is the actual coefficient for \(p>n\). The unit stencil has coefficients \((1,c-2,-2c)\) at \((1,2,4)\). Indeed its unit coefficient is one and its reciprocal moment vanishes. For \(V\ge1\),
\[
 G_V(s)=1-A_V(s)-c2^{-s}A_{\lfloor V/2\rfloor}(s),\qquad
 A_V(s)=\sum_{v=1}^V(-1)^{v+1}v^{-s}.
\]
The unit correction accounts for \(g(1)=0\). For \(\Re s>0\), \(s\ne1\), the limiting response is \(1-(1+c2^{-s})(1-2^{1-s})\zeta(s)\). At every right zero \(\rho\), it equals one. Averaging the four sharp caps \(4K,\ldots,4K+3\) gives \(1+O_\rho(K^{-1-\Re\rho})\). To see the gain, put \(P_j=(A_j+A_{j+1})/2\). The four-cap average equals \(1-(P_{4K}+P_{4K+2})/2-c2^{-\rho}P_{2K}\). At an eta zero, the paired-tail estimate gives \(|P_j|\le\tfrac34|\rho||\rho+1|(j+1)^{-1-\Re\rho}\). The error constant is at most \(\tfrac34(1+|c|)|\rho||\rho+1|\), uniformly over zeros. The average thus cancels the leading cofactor endpoint on each zero probe. The sum over ordinary primes at the original moving cut remains open. For a fixed right zero and tolerance \(\varepsilon>0\), choose \(d\ge2\) so that \(|G_j(\rho)-1|<\varepsilon/2\) for all \(j\ge d\). At an actual horizon \(k\), put \(Y=\max(\mathrm{productLower}(1,k),U)\). For \(p>n\), \(d\le n\) and \(dp\le k\), the coprime probe on \(Y<pv\le k,\ v\le n\) has magnitude below \(\varepsilon\) if \(dp\le Y\), and lies within \(\varepsilon\) of one if \(Y<2p\). Indeed its value is \(G_m-G_{\min(m,\lfloor Y/p\rfloor)}\) for \(m=\min(n,\lfloor k/p\rfloor)\), and \(G_0=G_1=0\). The same \(d\) works at every prime and horizon. **Seventh cofactor difference.** For \(K_b(x)=V_b(x)-V_1(x)\), \(h>0\) and \(v\ge1\), the forward difference in \(v\) satisfies \(|\Delta^7 K_b(vh)|\ll h^{-1}v^{-8}\), uniformly in \(b\). Only \(O(1/(vh))\) quotient samples contribute, each with step \(jh\ll v^{-1}\). Global Lipschitz continuity of the sixth spline derivative bounds each seventh difference by \(O((jh)^7)\). Summing above \(D\ge1\) gives \(O(h^{-1}D^{-7})\) at every cofactor endpoint. **First-prime cut on all products.** The full signed fragment on \(J<n\le M\) differs from the reading restricted to \(p>\lfloor n^{19/10}\rfloor\) by \(O(M^{73/20})\). In the latter reading the product cut is zero, the cofactor cap is the full natural endpoint and \(v\ll n^{1/10}\). The two one-sided \(11/3\) upper bounds are equivalent. At \(p\le n^{12/7}\) the discarded fragment costs \(O(M^3(1+\log(2M)))\), keeping the original product cut on the remainder. These follow by summing the signed fine-mesh estimate against the positive first-prime weights; the cofactor endpoint and the finite initial conductors are included. **Exact first-prime mesh.** For every cut \(P\), the signed product coefficient is bounded by \(C\log q\), uniformly in \(P,q\), by the ordinary divisor identity. Transposing back to primes gives the exact mesh on \(\lfloor U/p\rfloor <v\le\lfloor L_n/p\rfloor\), including the first positive quotient sample. Taking a difference of two prime cuts gives the corresponding sharp first-prime band. All six declarations passed the axiom audit. **Two adjacent quotient cells (§2.18).** Let \(R=4e^8\), \(x=pv/n^2\), and \(f\) be the dyadic spline. For \(3x>R\) and quotient horizon at least three, the first two positive quotients have exact sum \(W(1)[f(x)-f(2x)]+f(2x)\). Here \(W(1)<0\) by the square-root moment and \(W(2)=1\) by parity. Immediately below \(R/2\), the first cell is positive, the second is negative, and their sum is uniformly bounded below by a positive constant. The common sample has coefficient \(1-W(1)\). At first-prime spacing \(h=p/n^2>R/3\), with both horizons at least two, only cofactor two can survive, giving \((1-c)W(1)f(2h)\) with \(1-c>0\). All four axiom checks used standard Lean axioms. **Quartic compensation on actual prime bands.** Put \(a=3R/8, b=2R/5\). At all sufficiently large conductors the original first-prime sum on \(an^2<p\le bn^2\) is exactly \(n(1-c)W(1)\sum_{an^2<p\le bn^2}(\log p)f(2p/n^2)\). Its price is \(Cn^3+O(n^{25/9})\), with \(C=(1-c)W(1)\int_a^b f(2t)\,dt>0\). Every sufficiently late fragment \((N,M]\) therefore contributes at least \(c_1(M-N)N^3\). The existing \(|B(N)|\ll N^{15/4}\) upper bound forces the contribution from all complementary first primes on \((N,2N]\) below \(-c_2N^4\) for some \(c_2>0\). These six declarations passed a source check with only standard axioms. **Mellin transform on the same band.** Write \(h=ae^u\), with \(0\le u\le L=\log(16/15)\). Up to a positive constant, the tilted density is \(A(u)=e^{(\beta-1/2)u}(\log(4/3)-u)^7\). Integrating the eight box convolutions from their right endpoint gives the seventh power. For \(\beta\le1\), \(A>0\) and \(-A'\) has a positive minimum on this interval. Integration by parts against \(1-\cos(\gamma u)\), retaining \(A(L)(1-\cos(\gamma L))\), gives \(\int_0^L A(u)\sin(\gamma u)\,du>0\) for every \(\gamma>0\). Thus the complex integral of the actual sharp-band profile is nonzero at every nonreal exponent with real part at most one. All four declarations passed the source and axiom checks. **Fixed-product inversion on odd integers.** For odd \(u\), \(c_P(u)+(c_P(2u)+c_P(4u))/2\) recovers exactly the prime singleton above \(\max(4,P)\). The same identity holds on weighted sharp intervals. On odd composites \(c_P(u)\) is the negative sum of the eligible divisor logarithms. For every \(x\le H\), the cumulative omitted divisor logarithms differ from \(xD_P(H)/2\) by \(O(\max(4,P)+\sqrt H)\), where \(D_P(H)\) is their odd reciprocal Mangoldt density. The estimate also holds against finite tests with the terminal and variation factors. **Prime error from positive odd composites.** At \(P=\lfloor\sqrt H\rfloor\), let \(C_H\) sum eligible prime-divisor logarithms on odd composites. The corrected baseline \(T_H\) consists of the odd factorial logarithm, minus \(HD_P(H)/2\), plus the omitted Mangoldt mass, minus \(H\). The preceding endpoint estimate and the exact split of \(\psi(H)\) give \(T_H-C_H=\psi(H)-H+O(\sqrt H)\). A right zero forces both signs at its exponent on every late square-root window. A uniform lower bound \(C_h\ge T_h-Ah^{5/6}\) on arbitrarily late such windows implies \(|B(N)|\ll N^{11/3}\) at every terminal. The family at all exponents above one half is equivalent to RH. The same comparison transfers the single-quartet wave in both directions with error \(O(\sqrt x\log^2x)\). The lower bound and exclusion of that wave remain open. **Gcd product on a prime-power octave.** For \(L_j=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,j)\) and every endpoint \(X\), the logarithm of \(\prod_{u\le X}\gcd(u,L_{2M})/\gcd(u,L_M)\) is exactly \(\sum_{M<d\le2M}\Lambda(d)\lfloor X/d\rfloor\). Replacing the floors by \(X/d\) increases the sum by at most \(\psi(2M)-\psi(M)\). Removing proper powers costs at most \((X/M)(\psi(2M)-\theta(2M))\). These bounds follow by counting multiples of each prime power retained by the lcm.
