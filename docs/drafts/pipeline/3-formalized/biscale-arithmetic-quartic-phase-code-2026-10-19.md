## Goal and actual objects

Take a finite vertex set with pairwise coprime odd integers b_v>1. For each unordered pair of distinct vertices e={u,v}, assign an arbitrary real coefficient a_e, including zero. Realize the coordinate profile at order 2*b_u*b_v by the existing Ramanujan inverse. The formal family uses the explicit cofinal cutoffs `N_n=2+∑_u∑_v 2*b_u*b_v+n`, with arbitrary n≥0; these admit every edge order, including the empty vertex case. No abstract replacement signal is permitted. The active edges are exactly those with a_e≠0.

Exact theorem: equality of all labelled affine moments and all translated moments through degree four (equivalently through five) holds iff:

1. Every edge coordinate has the same square.
2. For each triangle, the three monomials consisting of two edge coordinates times the square of the third agree.
3. For each simple four-cycle, the product of its four edge coordinates agrees.

Thus active triangles force their edge signs to agree, while active four-cycles impose an even sign constraint. An explicit binary matrix has one row for each pairwise sign comparison in an active triangle and one row for each active four-cycle. The proposed least additional translated degree, with affine data supplied, is zero for at most one active edge, four when this matrix has kernel consisting only of constant signs, and six otherwise. The matrix entries must be specified solely from arithmetic support; defining them through unknown Fourier resonances would not compute the answer.

The accepted advance is a complete native ambiguity classification for arbitrary finite edge supports, including all vanishing coordinates and actual witnesses. Generic graph gauge theory, binary linear algebra, inverse realization, and the already-proved triangle special case receive no separate novelty credit. This entire package counts once, bringing the programme to 4/100.

## Paper proof

Write `o({u,v})=2*b_u*b_v`, and choose N≥2 admitting every such order. Let P=N!. Distinct edges have incomparable orders: if `o(e)|o(f)` and a vertex u of e is absent from f, then `b_u` divides `2*b_x*b_y` for the endpoints x,y of f, despite being coprime to that product. This forces `b_u=1`, contrary to the domain. Thus e⊆f, and two-element edges must coincide. The Ramanujan inverse realizes exactly the assigned coordinate profile. Existing comparable-product classification therefore says that all affine observations are equivalent to equality of coordinate squares. The paper argument below works for every such N. The full family-level Lean certificate uses the explicit `N_n` above; its extension to smaller admissible cutoffs remains paper-only. The central fourth-array equivalence in `ArithmeticEdgeQuartic.lean` is already formal for arbitrary admissible N.

**No singleton vertex.** Suppose four frequencies sum to zero and their orders are the four edge orders. If vertex u occurs in only edge occurrence i, set M to the product of the other three orders. Each other frequency is annihilated by M, so multiplying the zero-sum equation by M gives `M*r_i=0`. Hence `o(e_i)|M`, and therefore `b_u|M`. But `b_u` is coprime to each factor of M, including its powers of two. Contradiction. This argument also covers composite blocks and repeated edges.

**Complete four-edge classification.** Count distinct edges in the multiset. One distinct edge has multiplicity four. Two distinct edges must have multiplicities two and two: a 3+1 pattern has an endpoint belonging only to the singleton edge. With three distinct edges, write the doubled edge as {u,v}. A singleton edge must contain an endpoint w outside {u,v}. The other singleton must contain w. It cannot introduce a second new vertex, since that vertex would occur only once; nor can both endpoints of the first singleton be outside {u,v}, since then both singleton edges would coincide. Thus the two singleton edges are {u,w},{v,w}, giving a triangle with one doubled edge. With four distinct edges, all nonisolated vertex degrees are at least two and their sum is eight, so there are at most four vertices. Three vertices support at most three distinct edges. There are therefore exactly four vertices, each of degree two, and the simple graph is a four-cycle: following successive distinct neighbors cannot close a triangle without leaving the fourth vertex unable to have degree two. These cases exhaust disconnected graphs too.

**Every needed relation is realized.** Set δ=P/2 and α_v=P/b_v in Z/PZ. Their exact additive orders are two and b_v. The existing triangle construction realizes the three triangle monomials. Around a four-cycle u,v,w,z, use `δ+α_u−α_v`, `δ+α_v−α_w`, `δ+α_w−α_z`, `δ+α_z−α_u`. Their sum is 4δ=0. Coprimality of the odd blocks and two gives their exact orders `o(uv),o(vw),o(wz),o(zu)`. The full fourth moment Fourier coefficient is P^4 times the corresponding coordinate product, so equality of fourth arrays forces every stated triangle and cycle equation.

**Sufficiency and zeros.** Conversely, take any zero-sum frequency quartet. A coordinate outside the allowed orders vanishes in both profiles. For a quartet entirely in the allowed orders, the complete classification above applies. The one-edge and paired-edge products agree by square equality; triangle and four-cycle products agree by the corresponding equation. Thus every Fourier coefficient of the fourth lag array agrees. The already-proved finite moment Fourier inverse gives equality of the complete arrays. Zero coefficients cause no exception: square equality forces exactly the same zero set, and every monomial using a missing edge vanishes on both sides.

**Actual competitors and translations.** In fact fourth arrays alone force all coordinate squares: at a frequency of any admitted order q, the resonant tuple `(r,−r,r,−r)` reads `P^4*Y_q^4`, and real fourth-power equality implies square equality. Frequencies outside the cutoff have zero coordinates by definition. Hence any arbitrary real sieve competitor with the same fourth array has the same active edge support and is realized, as an actual signal, by one of these profiles. Likewise the tuple `(r,−r)` shows that complete quadratic arrays determine all coordinate squares. The common sign is the actual shift H=∏_v b_v: H is odd and divisible by each odd edge order, so every active Ramanujan mode changes sign under this shift. Every odd translated moment vanishes. The existing sixth valuation theorem proves that two family signals are translates iff their coordinates differ by one common sign; it also supplies the degree-six upper bound.

**Explicit phase code and sharpness.** On the active edge set E, write each competing coordinate as `(-1)^x_e*a_e`, with `x_e∈F₂`. Make a binary matrix M_E with rows `1_e+1_f` for each pair of edges in an active triangle, and rows `∑_{e∈C}1_e` for each active simple four-cycle C. The preceding equivalence identifies the entire fibre of observations through degree four or five with `ker M_E`; it does not define M_E through unknown frequency relations. Every row has even weight, so the constant vector belongs to this kernel. For nonempty E, fourth-order recovery modulo actual translation holds exactly when `rank M_E=|E|−1`. Otherwise a nonconstant kernel vector gives an actual nontranslation witness with every observation through five unchanged. For |E|≥2, a single-coordinate sign flip preserves all data through three and is not a translate, proving the degree-four lower bound. For |E|≤1, affine squares already give exactly one translation orbit. The ambiguity has `2^(|E|−rank M_E)` distinct cutoff sieve signals, or half that many translation orbits when E is nonempty. Each profile has its canonical Ramanujan-inverse weights, and coordinate injectivity identifies it with one cutoff signal. Coefficients beyond N are invisible to that signal, so arbitrary infinite weight functions are counted through their cutoff signals.

**Without affine data.** Choose N≥2 as above. The same argument gives least translated degree two for |E|≤1, four for |E|≥2 and rank `|E|−1`, and six otherwise. Quadratic arrays supply the required squares; all smaller odd data vanish on the family. For |E|=1, scaling a nonzero signal changes its magnitude while preserving its zero mean. For E empty, the order-two Ramanujan mode is an actual nonzero mean-zero competitor at N≥2. Hence degrees zero and one cannot recover even the zero signal against arbitrary competitors. This strengthens the original candidate and is part of the same package, not a separate count.
