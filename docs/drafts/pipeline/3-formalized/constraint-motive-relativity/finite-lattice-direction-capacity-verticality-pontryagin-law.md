- **Statement:** If a nested finite-port Gram family gains unbounded capacity along only finitely
  many marked lattice directions, its limiting vertical fibre is exactly the real span of the

## 1. Setup

Let `Y` be a finite-dimensional real inner-product space and let

\[
 \iota:\Lambda\hookrightarrow Y
\tag{FLD.1}
\]

be a full lattice embedding. Identify `Lambda` with its image. Fix a positive-definite
self-adjoint baseline operator `A_0` on `Y`, nonzero lattice directions

\[
 a_1,\ldots,a_r\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\},
\]

and nonnegative nondecreasing sequences `c_i(n)`. Define

\[
 G_n=A_0+\sum_{i=1}^r c_i(n)\,a_i\otimes a_i,
 \qquad Q_n=G_n^{-1},
\tag{FLD.2}
\]

where `(a tensor a)y=<a,y>a`. Put

\[
 I_\infty=\{i:c_i(n)\longrightarrow+\infty\},
 \qquad
 V=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{a_i:i\in I_\infty\}.
\tag{FLD.3}
\]

The empty-span convention is `V=0`.

## 2. Main theorem

### Theorem 2.1 — finite lattice-direction rationality

The inverse Gram operators converge in operator norm to a positive-semidefinite operator
`Q_infinity`, and

\[
 \boxed{\ker Q_\infty=V.}
\tag{FLD.4}
\]

Consequently, if `(G_n)` is used as the Gram family of the parent cofinal-capacity theorem, then

\[
 \boxed{\operatorname{mul}\overline{\Gamma(T)}=V.}
\tag{FLD.5}
\]

Let

\[
 \Lambda_V=\Lambda\cap V.
\tag{FLD.6}
\]

Then `Lambda_V` is a primitive sublattice of rank `dim V`, and Pontryagin duality gives the
canonical short exact sequence

\[
 0\longrightarrow\widehat{\Lambda/\Lambda_V}
 \longrightarrow\widehat\Lambda
 \longrightarrow\widehat{\Lambda_V}
 \longrightarrow0.
\tag{FLD.7}
\]

Thus, under finite marked lattice-direction provenance, capacity divergence, relation-valued
verticality, loss of integral directions, and the resulting compact phase factor are four exact
shadows of the same subspace `V`.

### Proposition 2.2 — exact nested Hilbert realization

Every family `(FLD.2)` is a Gram family of nested surjective finite-dimensional port maps. After
replacing `c_i(n)` by `c_i(n)-c_i(0)` and absorbing the finite initial term into `A_0`, assume
`c_i(0)=0`. Put

\[
 \Delta_i(k)=c_i(k)-c_i(k-1)\geq0,
 \qquad H=Y\oplus\ell^2(\{1,\ldots,r\}\times\mathbb N),
 \qquad H_n=Y\oplus\mathbb R^{\{1,\ldots,r\}\times\{1,\ldots,n\}},
\]

where `H_n` embeds into `H` by zero extension. Put `D=union_n H_n`; then `D` is dense in `H`.

and define

\[
 T_n\bigl(x,(t_{ik})\bigr)
 =A_0^{1/2}x+
 \sum_{i=1}^r\sum_{k=1}^n\sqrt{\Delta_i(k)}\,t_{ik}a_i.
\tag{FLD.8}
\]

Under the standard inclusions `H_n subset H_(n+1)`, one has

\[
 T_{n+1}|_{H_n}=T_n,
 \qquad T_nT_n^*=G_n.
\tag{FLD.9}
\]

Every `T_n` is surjective because `A_0^(1/2)` is invertible. Compatibility defines
`T:D->Y` with `T|H_n=T_n`. Hence `(FLD.5)` is a literal application of the parent theorem, not a
formal analogy.

## 3. Proof of Theorem 2.1

For every index outside `I_infinity`, monotonicity and boundedness imply
`c_i(n)->c_i(infinity)<infinity`. Define

\[
 B_n=A_0+\sum_{i\notin I_\infty}c_i(n)a_i\otimes a_i.
\]

Then `B_n->B_infinity` in operator norm and `B_infinity` is positive definite. The remaining
operator

\[
 D_n=\sum_{i\in I_\infty}c_i(n)a_i\otimes a_i
\]

has image in `V`. If `V!=0`, let

\[
 m_n=\min_{i\in I_\infty}c_i(n)\longrightarrow+\infty,
 \qquad
 S=\sum_{i\in I_\infty}a_i\otimes a_i.
\]

The restriction of `S` to `V` is positive definite because its listed vectors span `V`.
Therefore, for some `s>0`,

\[
 D_n|_V\succeq m_n S|_V\succeq m_ns\,1_V.
\tag{FLD.10}
\]

Use the orthogonal decomposition `Y=V direct-sum V^perp`. In block form,

\[
 G_n=
 \begin{pmatrix}
  R_n&E_n\\ E_n^*&C_n
 \end{pmatrix}.
\tag{FLD.11}
\]

The blocks contributed by `D_n` occur only in `R_n`. Equation `(FLD.10)` gives
`norm(R_n^(-1))->0`, while

\[
 E_n\longrightarrow E_\infty,
 \qquad C_n\longrightarrow C_\infty,
\]

and `C_infinity`, the compression of the positive-definite `B_infinity` to `V^perp`, is positive
definite. The Schur complements satisfy

\[
 S_n=C_n-E_n^*R_n^{-1}E_n\longrightarrow C_\infty.
\tag{FLD.12}
\]

They are positive definite because `G_n` is. The block inverse formula gives

\[
 Q_n=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 R_n^{-1}+R_n^{-1}E_nS_n^{-1}E_n^*R_n^{-1}
   &-R_n^{-1}E_nS_n^{-1}\\
 -S_n^{-1}E_n^*R_n^{-1}&S_n^{-1}
 \end{pmatrix}
 \longrightarrow
 \begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&C_\infty^{-1}\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{FLD.13}
\]

The limiting kernel is exactly `V`, proving `(FLD.4)`. When `V=0`, every coefficient is bounded,
`G_n` converges to a positive-definite operator, and the same conclusion holds with zero kernel.

For the integral statement, the vectors with diverging weights lie in `Lambda` and span `V`, so
`Lambda_V` spans `V` and has rank `dim V`. It is primitive: if `m lambda` lies in `Lambda_V` for
a nonzero integer `m` and `lambda in Lambda`, then `lambda=(m lambda)/m` lies in the real subspace
`V`. Thus `Lambda/Lambda_V` is torsion-free and hence free. Applying `Hom(-,T)` to the lattice
sequence gives `(FLD.7)`; restriction of characters is surjective because a primitive sublattice
extends to a lattice basis. This completes the proof.

## 4. Cofinal-direction countertheorem

### Theorem 4.1 — finite-cutoff lattice provenance is insufficient

There is a nested rational Gram family on `Y=R^2` such that every increment is a positive multiple
of `a_n tensor a_n` for some `a_n in Z^2`, but the limiting inverse-capacity kernel is an
irrational line.

### Proof

Fix irrational `alpha` and let `v=(1,alpha)/sqrt(1+alpha^2)`. Choose rationals
`r_n=p_n/q_n` satisfying

\[
 |r_n-\alpha|\leq2^{-n},
\]

and put

\[
 a_n=(q_n,p_n)\in\mathbb Z^2,
 \qquad u_n=\frac{a_n}{\|a_n\|},
 \qquad G_N=1+\sum_{n=1}^Nu_n\otimes u_n.
\tag{FLD.14}
\]

Each increment is
`u_n tensor u_n=norm(a_n)^(-2) a_n tensor a_n`, so every finite stage has marked lattice
provenance. This is again a literal nested port family: take
`H_N=R^2 direct-sum R^N` and

\[
 T_N(x,t)=x+\sum_{n=1}^Nt_nu_n.
\]

Then `T_(N+1)|H_N=T_N`, every `T_N` is surjective, and `T_NT_N^*=G_N`. Let `w` be a unit vector
perpendicular to `v`. In the basis `(v,w)`, write

\[
 G_N=\begin{pmatrix}A_N&B_N\\B_N&C_N\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The normalization map `r mapsto (1,r)/sqrt(1+r^2)` has bounded derivative on a compact
neighborhood of `alpha`, so `norm(u_n-v)<=C 2^(-n)`. Hence `v dot u_n->1`, giving
`A_N->infinity`; the series defining `B_N` is absolutely convergent because
`|w dot u_n|<=C 2^(-n)`; and `C_N=1+sum_(n<=N)|w dot u_n|^2` converges to a finite positive
limit. The two-by-two inverse formula therefore gives

\[
 G_N^{-1}\longrightarrow
 \begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&C_\infty^{-1}\end{pmatrix}
\]

in the `(v,w)` decomposition. Its kernel is `R v`, while
`R v intersect Z^2=0`. Thus a cutoff-by-cutoff lattice label does not force rational verticality;
the finite set of direction types in Theorem 2.1 is load-bearing.

\[
 G_k=1+\sum_e\ell_e t_e(k)u_eu_e^T,

### 5.2 Rooted prime-support HT Hodge sector

For the rooted path on a prime support of size `m`, let `D_r` be the root-deleted incidence

\[
 B=D_r^{-1}\in\operatorname{GL}_{m-1}(\mathbb Z).

For `t_k<1` with `t_k increase 1`, the positive rooted Hodge cometric and its inverse are

\[
 Q_k=(1-t_k)D_r^TD_r,
 \qquad
 G_k=Q_k^{-1}=s_kBB^T,
 \qquad s_k=(1-t_k)^{-1}\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{FLD.17}
\]

Write `b_1,...,b_(m-1)` for the columns of `B` and set `s_*:=s_0=(1-t_0)^(-1)`. Then
`s_k-s_*>=0`, and

\[
 G_k=s_*BB^T+\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}(s_k-s_*)b_jb_j^T.
\tag{FLD.18}
\]

Theorem 2.1 applies with `Lambda=Z^(m-1)` and gives full verticality

\[
 \ker\lim_kQ_k=\mathbb R^{m-1}.
\tag{FLD.19}

### 5.3 Weighted prime port

For a nested nonempty exhaustion `F_n` of the support of a weighted prime port, fix time `t` and
realify

\[
 \lambda_{t,F_n}:H_{w,F_n}\longrightarrow\mathbb C

as a map to `Y=C_R`. Its exact Gram operator is

\[
 G_n=C_{F_n,0}\,1_Y,
 \qquad
 C_{F_n,0}=\sum_{p\in F_n}\frac{|a_p|^2}{w_p}.
\tag{FLD.20}
\]

Take the Gaussian lattice `Lambda=Z[i]`, directions `a_1=1`, `a_2=i`, and a nonempty initial
aperture with capacity `C_*:=C_{F_0,0}`. Then

\[
 G_n=C_*1_Y+(C_{F_n,0}-C_*)(a_1a_1^T+a_2a_2^T),
\tag{FLD.21}
\]

an exact instance of `(FLD.2)`. If total capacity diverges, both weights diverge, so `V=Y` and
`Q_n=C_(F_n,0)^(-1)1_Y->0`; this is exactly the parent card's dense-kernel/full-vertical phase. If
total capacity is finite, no direction diverges and the port remains continuous.

## 6. Conjecture/proof/failure loops

### Loop A — every cofinal capacity kernel is lattice-rational

**Failed.** The irrational rank-one family `G_n=1+n vv^*`, with
`v=(1,sqrt(2))`, has limiting kernel `R v`.

**Survivor.** Rationality requires compatibility between capacity increments and source lattice
provenance.

### Loop B — lattice provenance at every finite cutoff suffices

**Failed.** Theorem 4.1 uses only lattice-generated increments but changes their direction
cofinally; the limiting line is irrational.

**Survivor.** Uniform finite-direction provenance is sufficient.

### Loop C — finite marked lattice directions suffice

**Proved.** The block inverse law `(FLD.13)` identifies the kernel exactly, while `(FLD.7)` turns
it into a compact phase exact sequence.

### Loop D — the theorem has no literal corpus realization

**Failed.** Sections 5.1--5.2 give two independently constructed realizations: the positive
multi-Rees Stokes sector and rooted prime-support HT Hodge sector.

**Survivor.** The theorem now has real cross-corpus leverage, but neither realization includes a
same-lattice recurrence flow, the Stokes negative square, or an RH selector. Those remain separate
doctrine data.

## 7. Predictions and falsifiers

1. Any literal source chart satisfying `(FLD.2)` has a lattice-rational vertical fibre; an
   irrational observed fibre falsifies its finite-direction provenance.
2. The vertical rank is exactly the rank of `Lambda_V`, not the number of divergent weights;
   linearly dependent marked directions do not add rank.
3. Under `(FLD.2)`, the compact phase factor lost or retained is forced by `(FLD.7)`; it cannot be
   chosen independently of the capacity kernel.
4. Cofinal creation of new lattice direction types can destroy rationality without violating any
   finite-cutoff integrality test.
5. No RH consequence follows until a literal route comparison proves the hypotheses before
   Xi-zero specialization.
