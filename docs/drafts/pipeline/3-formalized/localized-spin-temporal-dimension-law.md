# Localized-spin temporal-dimension law

Status: formalized  
Label: `[LEAN-PROVED]`

## Question

Can one strictly local flip observable in an exactly configuration-localized
commuting spin system carry exponentially many independent recurrence phases?

## Candidate law

For a distinguished two-level degree of freedom and a finite environment
`Y`, write its conditional flip-frequency packet in the complete Walsh basis

\[
\Omega_J(y)=2\sum_{S\subseteq Y}J_S\chi_S(y).
\]

Theorem:

\[
\dim_{\mathbb Q}\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Q}
  \{\Omega_J(y):y\in\{0,1\}^{Y}\}
=
\dim_{\mathbb Q}\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Q}
  \{J_S:S\subseteq Y\}.
\]

Thus one local flip has temporal dimension at most \(2^{|Y|}\), with equality
exactly when its incident interaction coefficients are
\(\mathbb Q\)-independent. The noninteracting endpoint has dimension at most
one. No configuration transition is generated by the Hamiltonian.

## Physical boundary

This is an exactly diagonal commuting-spin/l-bit law. It does not by itself
prove Anderson localization or microscopic many-body localization. A local
l-bit flip is a physical-site-local probe only when the l-bit basis is the
physical product basis; after quasi-local dressing it becomes a quasi-local
observable.

## Proof

1. Every \(\Omega_J(y)\) is a rational linear combination of the \(J_S\).
2. Walsh inversion gives
   \[
   \sum_y\chi_S(y)\Omega_J(y)=2^{|Y|+1}J_S,
   \]
   so every coefficient lies in the frequency span.
3. Equality of subspaces gives exact rank equality.
4. Add coefficient-support and independence corollaries.
5. Connect the packet to a diagonal two-level conditional energy and a local
   flip observer.

## Kill tests

- Existing literature already states the exact local-observer rank identity.
- The observable requires a global signed energy functional rather than one
  local flip.
- The result cannot be connected to finite-time recurrence or finite
  resolution without exponential-in-system resources.
- A microscopic Anderson claim enters without a proved transfer theorem.
