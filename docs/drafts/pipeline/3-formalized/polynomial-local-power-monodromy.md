## Exact local coordinate conjecture — complete paper proof [PROVED HERE]

Let f be analytic at a, with analytic order m a positive finite integer. Then there is an open partial homeomorphism φ between neighborhoods of a and 0, analytic with analytic inverse, with φ(a)=0 and f(z)=φ(z)^m throughout its source. Moreover its source can be restricted inside any prescribed neighborhood of a.

Proof. The inspected finite-order factorization gives f(z)=(z−a)^m u(z) near a, with u analytic and u(a)≠0. The analytic order of u is zero, divisible by m. The inspected root theorem gives analytic v with v^m=u near a. Evaluation at a shows v(a)≠0. Put s(z)=(z−a)v(z). Then s(a)=0 and s′(a)=v(a)≠0, and s^m=f near a. The strict analytic inverse theorem produces an open partial homeomorphism whose forward function is s. Shrink its source inside the prescribed neighborhood and a neighborhood where s is analytic, s′≠0, and s^m=f. Its inverse is analytic at every target point by the analytic inverse theorem and local inverse uniqueness. This proves all claimed properties without assuming a polynomial realization or any branch permutation.

For a nonzero polynomial F vanishing at a, polynomial root factorization gives the same statement with m=F.rootMultiplicity(a)>0. Applied to F=P−P(a), this constructs the actual map's local power coordinate. The ordinary nonzero/finite-order input excludes a locally constant map; it is not an unresolved realization premise.

## Actual regular critical-fiber multiplicities — complete paper proof [PROVED HERE]

For the actual additive family P with distinct critical positions a_j, the inspected derivative identity is P′=(n+2)∏(X−a_j). The product is separable by `Polynomial.separable_prod_X_sub_C_iff`; multiplication by a nonzero constant preserves separability. At each a_j the derivative therefore has root multiplicity exactly one (positive because it is a root, at most one by separability). F=P−P(a_j) is monic of degree n+2 and vanishes at a_j. The inspected characteristic-zero derivative multiplicity law gives mult(F′,a_j)=mult(F,a_j)−1, and mult(F,a_j)>0. Hence mult(F,a_j)=2. The coordinate theorem above thus gives an actual quadratic local model there.

Choose r>0 with r<η and r²<η,δ and that last disk radius; such an r exists by continuity of squaring at zero. Define the positively oriented target circle γ(t)=c+r² exp(2πit), 0≤t≤1. It avoids all critical values, so the proved unique root transport applies.
