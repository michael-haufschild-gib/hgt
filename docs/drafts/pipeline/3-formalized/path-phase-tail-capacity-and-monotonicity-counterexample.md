# Path phase-tail capacity and literal-monotonicity counterexample

- **ID:** `path-phase-tail-capacity-and-monotonicity-counterexample`
- **Stage:** 3-formalized
- **Type:** theorem / sharp circuit obstruction / counterexample / positive no-go survivor
- **Source:** failure analysis of all-pairs monotonicity for the nested inverse-power path
- **Labels:** `[FORMALIZED] [NOVELTY AUDITED--NO CLOSE PRECEDENT FOUND]`

## Exact statement

Let `P_n` be a path on `n+1` qubits, with edge set `E`, and put

\[
 U(\theta)=\exp\!\left(-i\sum_{e=\{u,v\}\in E}\theta_e
 |11\rangle\!\langle 11|_{uv}\right).
\]

Circuits are ancilla-free products of arbitrary support-certified one- and two-qubit unitary
gates.  One-qubit and scalar gates are free; the cost is the number of two-qubit gates; error is
global operator norm.  For every edge choose the principal signed lift
`beta_e in [-pi,pi]` of `theta_e modulo 2pi`, and set

\[
 a_e=|\beta_e|,\qquad
 M=\sum_{e\in E}a_e,\qquad
 \tau_G=\min_{S\subseteq E,\ |S|\le G}\sum_{e\notin S}a_e.
\]

### Theorem A -- path phase-tail capacity law

For every `G in N` and `epsilon >= 0`:

1. If `tau_G <= 4 epsilon`, then
   \[
   C^{\rm op}_\epsilon(U(\theta))\le G.
   \]
2. If
   \[
   |e^{-i\phi}-1|>4\epsilon
   \quad\hbox{for every }\phi\in[\tau_G,M],
   \]
   then
   \[
   C^{\rm op}_\epsilon(U(\theta))>G.
   \]

The result permits arbitrary two-qubit supports and arbitrary non-diagonal gates.  The path is a
property of the target, not a hardware-connectivity restriction on the approximating circuit.

### Theorem B -- all-pairs laboratory monotonicity is false

For the nested inverse-power path

\[
 H_L=\sum_{j=1}^{L}j^{-2}P_{2j-1,2j}
     +\sum_{k=2}^{L}k^{-4}P_{2k-2,2k-1},
 \qquad U_L(t)=e^{-itH_L},
\]

take

\[
 L=16384,\qquad \epsilon={1\over1024},\qquad
 t=2\pi,\qquad s=2\pi-{1\over10^6}.
\]

Then both times lie in the claimed laboratory window

\[
 1024\pi\epsilon\le s<t\le {\epsilon L\over2},
\]

but

\[
 C^{\rm op}_\epsilon(U_L(s))\ge1514
 \quad\hbox{and}\quad
 C^{\rm op}_\epsilon(U_L(t))\le1513.
\]

Consequently all-pairs monotonicity throughout the laboratory window is false.

## Proof of Theorem A

### 1. Free centering gives the upper law

Fix a set `S` attaining `tau_G`, enlarging it by zero-weight edges if necessary so that
`|S| <= G`.  Implement every edge in `S` by its exact controlled-phase gate.  This costs at most
`G` two-qubit gates.

For an omitted edge `e={u,v}`, write `Z_u,Z_v` for the diagonal signs `+1,-1` on that qubit.  The
identity

\[
 |11\rangle\!\langle11|_{uv}
 ={1-Z_u-Z_v+Z_uZ_v\over4}
\]

splits the omitted generator as

\[
 \sum_{e\notin S}\beta_eP_e=A+B+R,
 \qquad
 R={1\over4}\sum_{e\notin S}\beta_e Z_uZ_v,
\]

where `A` is scalar and `B` is a sum of one-qubit diagonal operators.  All terms commute.  The
unitary generated by `A+B` is therefore free.  Every eigenvalue of `R` has absolute value at most

\[
 {1\over4}\sum_{e\notin S}|\beta_e|={\tau_G\over4}.
\]

For a real number `x`, `|e^{-ix}-1| <= |x|`.  Applying this entrywise gives

\[
 \|e^{-iR}-I\|\le\|R\|\le{\tau_G\over4}\le\epsilon.
\]

The kept controlled phases followed by the free centering unitary hence give a circuit of cost at
most `G` and error at most `epsilon`.

### 2. One arbitrary two-qubit gate can hide at most one path edge

Let a circuit contain at most `G` two-qubit gates.  Form the undirected graph `F` whose edges are
their two-qubit supports, discarding repetitions, and choose a spanning forest `F_0` of `F`.  Write
`r=|E(F_0)|`; then `r<=G`, and `F_0` has the same connected components as `F`.

Because the target path connects all vertices, `F_0` extends to a spanning tree `T` using only
target-path edges.  Exactly `n-r` of the `n` path edges belong to `T`.  Give every edge of `F_0`
the constraint "equal signs" and every path edge of `T` the constraint "opposite signs".  A signed
tree has no consistency obstruction, so there is a sign `sigma_v in {+1,-1}` satisfying all these
constraints.  Thus:

- every two-qubit support lies wholly within one sign class;
- all but at most `r<=G` target-path edges cross the sign cut.

This is the sharp path-cut certificate.  Nonlocal circuit edges and non-diagonal gate matrices do
not evade it.

### 3. The cut supplies a phase invariant

Let `A={v:sigma_v=+1}`.  Every circuit gate acts wholly inside `A` or wholly outside it, so the full
circuit unitary factors across this cut.

Choose signs `d_v in {+1,-1}` recursively along the target path so that

\[
 d_ud_v\beta_e=|\beta_e|=a_e
\]

on every edge.  On each side of the cut choose two bit strings whose coordinatewise differences
are the corresponding `d_v`.  Combining the two choices gives four computational-basis strings
`x_00,x_01,x_10,x_11`.  The mixed second difference of the target phase is

\[
 \Phi=\sum_{e\text{ crossing }A}a_e.
\]

At most `G` path edges fail to cross.  By the definition of `tau_G`,

\[
 \tau_G\le\Phi\le M.                                           \tag{1}
\]

If `W` is the circuit matrix, factorization across `A` gives the exact diagonal-entry identity

\[
 W_{00}W_{11}=W_{01}W_{10}.                                   \tag{2}
\]

Operator-norm error at most `epsilon` bounds each of the four target/circuit diagonal-entry errors
by `epsilon`.  Circuit unitarity bounds every diagonal entry of `W` by one.  Inserting the four
approximations into (2), twice using the triangle inequality, yields

\[
 |e^{-i\Phi}-1|\le4\epsilon.                                  \tag{3}
\]

The lower hypothesis and (1) contradict (3).  Hence no circuit with at most `G` two-qubit gates
approximates the target.

## Proof of Theorem B

Put

\[
 \delta=10^{-6},\quad K=1477,\quad B=38,\quad G=(K-1)+(B-1)=1513,
\]

and abbreviate

\[
 W=\sum_{j=1478}^{L}{1\over j^2}+\sum_{k=39}^{L}{1\over k^4}. \tag{4}
\]

At `t=2pi`, the first matching phase is zero modulo `2pi`.  Keep matching bonds
`j=2,...,1477` and bridge bonds `k=2,...,38`.  These are exactly `1513` bonds.  All omitted folded
phase mass equals `2pi W`.

At `s=2pi-delta`, the first matching bond has folded absolute phase `delta`; every other matching
bond has phase `s/j^2` and every bridge has phase `s/k^4`.  The same `1513` kept bonds are the
strictly largest folded phases.  Indeed their smallest phase is `s/1477^2`, while

\[
 \delta<{s\over1477^2},\qquad
 {1\over1478^2}<{1\over1477^2},\qquad
 {1\over39^4}<{1\over1477^2},\qquad
 {1\over1477^2}<{1\over38^4}.                                \tag{5}
\]

Thus

\[
 \tau_G(t)=2\pi W,
 \qquad
 \tau_G(s)=\delta+sW.                                         \tag{6}
\]

### Exact tail bounds

For every positive integer `n`, direct subtraction gives

\[
 {1\over n^2}\le {2\over2n-1}-{2\over2n+1},
\]

the difference being `1/[n^2(4n^2-1)]`, and

\[
 {1\over n^4}\le {8\over3}
 \left({1\over(2n-1)^3}-{1\over(2n+1)^3}\right),
\]

the difference having positive numerator `160n^4-36n^2+3`.  Summation gives

\[
 W\le W_+:=
 {2\over2955}-{2\over32769}
 +{8\over3}\left({1\over77^3}-{1\over32769^3}\right).       \tag{7}
\]

The reverse one-step inequalities

\[
 {1\over n^2}\ge
 \left({1\over n}-{1\over n+1}\right)
 +{1\over2}\left({1\over n^2}-{1\over(n+1)^2}\right),
\]

\[
 {1\over n^4}\ge
 {1\over3}\left({1\over n^3}-{1\over(n+1)^3}\right)
 +{1\over2}\left({1\over n^4}-{1\over(n+1)^4}\right)
\]

have respective positive difference numerators `1` and `10n^2+10n+3`.  Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
 W\ge W_-:=&
 {1\over1478}-{1\over16385}
 +{1\over2}\left({1\over1478^2}-{1\over16385^2}\right)\\
 &+{1\over3\cdot39^3}-{1\over3\cdot16385^3}
 +{1\over2}\left({1\over39^4}-{1\over16385^4}\right).       \tag{8}
\end{aligned}
\]

Use the certified bounds

\[
 {3141592\over10^6}<\pi<{3141593\over10^6}.                  \tag{9}
\]

Clearing the positive denominators in (7)--(9) gives

\[
 {1\over256}-2{3141593\over10^6}W_+
 ={9238233995087822867\over20379950352714880620000000}>0.    \tag{10}
\]

Therefore `tau_G(t)<4epsilon`, and Theorem A(1) gives

\[
 C^{\rm op}_\epsilon(U_L(t))\le1513.                          \tag{11}
\]

Set

\[
 q=\delta+\left(2{3141592\over10^6}-\delta\right)W_-.
\]

Again by exact integer arithmetic,

\[
 q-{q^3\over24}-{1\over256}
 ={8839366133908167534680919933239426706180981559018725681339782102893485527230633179925206494328473
 \over
 17459242990948094248260351429563509973790883779393402829907652450321218283000000000000000000000000000000}
 >0.                                                               \tag{12}
\]

Equations (8), (9), and (12) imply

\[
 \tau_G(s)-{\tau_G(s)^3\over24}>4\epsilon,\qquad \tau_G(s)<1. \tag{13}
\]

For completeness, the total folded mass `M(s)` is below `3pi/2`.  Peel the finite sums at `10` and
`5` and apply the elementary cubic tail majorants:

\[
 Q=\sum_{j=2}^{10}{1\over j^2}+\left({1\over10}-{1\over L}\right)
  +\sum_{k=2}^{5}{1\over k^4}+\left({1\over5^3}-{1\over L^3}\right).
\]

Then `M(s)<=delta+sQ`, and clearing denominators using (9) gives

\[
 {3\over2}{3141592\over10^6}
 -\left[\delta+left(2{3141593\over10^6}-\delta\right)Q\right]
 ={54569971712775025116751\over727326941773824000000000}>0.   \tag{14}
\]

Now let `phi in [tau_G(s),M(s)]`.  If `phi<=1`,
`2 sin(phi/2)>phi-phi^3/24>=tau_G(s)-tau_G(s)^3/24>4epsilon`; the middle inequality follows by
factoring the difference and using `0<=tau_G(s)<=phi<=1`.  If `1<=phi<=pi`, Jordan's inequality
gives `2 sin(phi/2)>=2phi/pi>=2/pi>4epsilon`.  If `pi<=phi<3pi/2`, put
`psi=2pi-phi in (pi/2,pi]`; periodicity and Jordan's inequality give
`|e^{-i phi}-1|=2sin(psi/2)>=2psi/pi>=1>4epsilon`.  Thus Theorem A(2) yields

\[
 C^{\rm op}_\epsilon(U_L(s))>1513.                            \tag{15}
\]

Since the complexity is integer-valued, (15) is the stated lower bound `1514`.

Finally, (9) gives

\[
 \pi=1024\pi\epsilon<s<2\pi<8={\epsilon L\over2},
\]

so the counterexample lies strictly inside the same laboratory window as the ramp theorem.

## Hostile audit

1. **Arbitrary nonlocal gates:** covered.  The spanning-forest cut uses the actual support of every
   two-qubit gate and charges at most one lost path edge per independent support edge.
2. **Non-diagonal gates:** covered.  Only support factorization, unitarity, and four diagonal matrix
   entries are used.
3. **Phase wrapping:** exact.  Every edge is replaced by a principal signed lift; the four-config
   signs turn all crossing contributions into absolute folded phases.
4. **Approximation rather than exact synthesis:** covered by the `4 epsilon` four-entry stability
   estimate and the free-centering construction.
5. **Numerical evidence:** none.  Equations (10), (12), and (14) are exact positive rational
   identities, not floating-point tests.
6. **Window membership:** exact and strict.
7. **Claim strength:** this disproves literal all-pairs monotonicity only.  It does not weaken the
   existing two-sided linear ramp envelope or the scale-separated comparison theorem.

## Positive survivor and representation lesson

The failed monotonicity representation used elapsed time as the ordering variable.  The correct
finite-resolution variable is the **folded phase-tail mass** `tau_G`: phase accumulation raises it,
while a local `2pi` return can move a bond from the kept head into the free tail.  Theorem A is the
strong positive survivor.  It both constructs circuits and obstructs arbitrary circuits using the
same ordered phase statistic.

The next exact conjecture forced by this theorem is a phase-return exchange law bounding every
downward jump by the number and phase mass of bonds crossing their principal-lift cut locus.  This
claim remains Stage 1 and is not used above.
