## Literal atom and its Mellin character

    φ_λ(x)=2λ u^(5/4)(2λu−3)exp(−λu),  x∈ℝ.               (1)

    M_a(λ):=∫_ℝ exp(a x)φ_λ(x)dx
          =(a−1/2)Γ(a/2+5/4) λ^(−a/2−1/4).              (2)

Indeed dx=du/(2u), reducing the integral to λ∫_0^∞u^(a/2+1/4)(2λu−3)exp(−λu)du. The same calculation is holomorphic in complex a whenever Re a>−5/2. In particular,

    M_(1/2)(λ)=0,             M_(-1/2)(λ)=−1.           (3)

The zero is an exact flux law:

    exp(x/2)φ_λ(x)
      =−2λ d/dx [exp(3x)exp(−λexp(2x))].                 (4)

The primitive vanishes at both ends. The opposite weight assigns the same charge −1 to every atom and cannot be summed termwise through the complete Euler source.

More explicitly, for any index set A for which the pointwise sum converges, define its nonnegative flux potential

    H_A(x)=Σ_(n∈A) 2πn² exp(3x)exp(−πn²exp(2x)).

Then exp(x/2)Σ_(n∈A)φ_(πn²)(x)=−H_A'(x) wherever termwise differentiation is justified. For a finite prime set, H_A tends to zero at both physical ends; for all integers, its positive-end limit is zero and its negative-end limit is 1/2. Thus Euler completion changes the left boundary condition of the **same exact differential law**. It does not change the local law.
For the finite-prime assertion, write each summand as n⁻¹ times 2π(n exp x)³exp(−π(n exp x)²). The bracket is bounded and tends to zero at either end for each fixed n, while Σ_(smooth n)n⁻¹ is finite. Dominated convergence proves both endpoint limits and justifies the termwise flux law. For the all-integer source, integrate the flux law from x to +∞ and use (5); its negative-end limit is 1/2.

## Finite-prime versus completed source

Let S_P be the finite-prime theta source, summing (1) over positive integers whose prime factors are below a fixed cutoff P. Let k=thetaCuspFullSource, the sum over all positive integers. Then for every finite P,

    ∫_ℝ exp(x/2)S_P(x)dx=0,
    ∫_ℝ exp(x/2)k(x)dx=1/2.                              (5)

Both integrals are absolutely defined. The weighted L¹ norm of atom n is C/n for a finite seed constant C. The reciprocal sum over a monoid generated by finitely many primes is finite, so integration commutes with its atom sum; (3) makes every term zero. The second equality is the unconditional Lean theorem integral_exp_half_mul_thetaCuspFullSource_eq_half, derived from XiC(i/2)=1/2. The finite-prime source converges to k pointwise and compact locally, but cannot converge in L¹(exp(x/2)dx).

For each fixed R≥0, compact-local convergence and positivity of all atoms on x≥R give the sharper escaped-charge law

    lim_(P→∞) ∫_(-∞,−R) exp(x/2)(k(x)−S_P(x))dx=1/2.     (6)

The compact and right-tail residual integrals tend to zero; the total residual moment is 1/2 at every finite stage. The defect accumulates at the negative cusp, which also makes the theta one-port metric domain singular.

There is a canonical boundary-sensitive limit, stronger than (5) and (6). Let ψ be any bounded continuously differentiable real or complex test on ℝ with ψ' integrable; its finite limit ψ(−∞) exists. Then

    lim_(P→∞) ∫_ℝ ψ(x)exp(x/2)S_P(x)dx
      =∫_ℝ ψ(x)exp(x/2)k(x)dx − (1/2)ψ(−∞).      (6a)

To prove it, put H_P for the finite-prime flux potential above and H for the all-integer one. Each H_P vanishes at both ends, 0≤H_P≤H≤1/2 pointwise, and H_P(x)→H(x) for every x. Integration by parts gives ∫ψ exp(x/2)S_P=∫ψ'H_P. Dominated convergence applies because ψ'∈L¹ and H_P≤1/2. Finally H'=−exp(x/2)k, H(+∞)=0, and H(−∞)=1/2, so integration by parts for H produces the stated boundary term. This is convergence in a specified space of observations to the **completed bulk plus a negative half-unit boundary functional**. No total-variation or weak-measure convergence on a compactification is claimed.

The same limit holds for **any** sequence of index sets A_j⊆ℕ≥1 with Σ_(n∈A_j)1/n<∞ at every stage and 1_(A_j)(n)→1 for each fixed n; nesting and a prime ordering are unnecessary. Define S_j by summing the corresponding atoms. The reciprocal condition makes each potential H_(A_j) vanish at both ends. Pointwise convergence of its positive series follows by dominated convergence in n, and 0≤H_(A_j)≤H≤1/2 gives the same test-space limit (6a). Thus the critical boundary charge is independent of how admissible Euler atoms are chronologically exhausted. Different orderings can affect other signed source observables, but they cannot remove this one.

## Exact escaping packet for integer cutoff

Let K_N(x)=Σ_(1≤n≤N)φ_(πn²)(x). Every K_N has zero critical moment. In moving coordinate x=y−log N, its weighted density is

    B_N(y):=exp((y−log N)/2)K_N(y−log N)
           =(exp y/N)Σ_(n=1)^N thetaCuspSeed((n/N)exp y).

On every compact y interval this Riemann sum converges uniformly to

    B_∞(y)=∫_0^(exp y)thetaCuspSeed(t)dt
          =−2π exp(3y)exp(−πexp(2y))<0.                (10)

The second equality follows from thetaCuspSeed(t)=−2π d/dt[t³exp(−πt²)]. Its total signed mass is

    ∫_ℝ B_∞(y)dy
      =−2π∫_0^∞t²exp(−πt²)dt=−1/2,                    (11)

using the Gaussian half-moment formalized in ThetaCuspSeedZeroMean. Thus integer cutoffs contain a physical packet tending locally to k with critical mass +1/2 and a compensating negative packet moving to x=−∞ with mass −1/2. Formula (10) is a local moving-frame limit. Global L¹ convergence of B_N to B_∞ is false: the physical positive packet moves to y=+∞ in this frame.

The moving flux potential itself has the local limit

It rises from zero to 1/2 across the moving left wall. At fixed physical x, the completed potential falls from 1/2 to zero. This two-wall picture is a precise interpretation of (5), not a proposed zero-location theorem.

## Continuous dilation erases the bounded arithmetic selector

The [literal source vacuum](theta-euler-logcell-residue-highpass.md) is separated from discrete atoms n≥2 by a bounded functional on the positive half-line. This property disappears when arithmetic rates πn² are replaced by any open interval J of continuous rates:

    closed span {φ_λ : λ∈J}=L²(ℝ,dx).                   (12)

Suppose g∈L²(ℝ) annihilates those atoms. Define

    q(u)=(1/2)u^(1/4)conj(g((log u)/2)),

    ⟨g,φ_λ⟩=2λ[−2λF'(λ)−3F(λ)].                         (13)
