# Rational sections on prime-centred windows

For each β < 1 and K ≥ 1 there are arbitrarily large primes p with the following property. Put mass c > 0 at conductor 1 and signed coefficients of net mass M > 0 and total variation V on integer conductors in [p, p + p^β]. Whenever max(c,V) ≤ K min(c,M), the full rational contact generator is a small section at the original radius on every sufficiently large multiple of this divisor. Its ordinary logarithmic section count divided by m² tends to the signed Weil form. For the two-atom divisors e₁ + eⱼ, all j in the window share one multiplicity threshold. For 7/12 < β < 1 this includes at least p^β/(4 log p) consecutive primes strictly beyond p.

**Proof.** Under RH, every nonzero integer probe has strictly positive degree. Otherwise let w > 0 be the zero width, T(x) = 2 Re S(log x) and a = max(0,w+β−1) < w. The prime upper-growth theorem makes T(p)/p^a unbounded after removing any finite set of primes. The width-w derivative bound and log(x/p) ≤ (x−p)/p give |T(x)−T(p)| ≤ E p^a throughout the window, with E independent of p.

The anchor–cluster pairing is at least cM T(p) − cV E p^a. All cluster–cluster log ratios lie in [−log 2,log 2], where |Re S| has some fixed bound D. The cluster self-pairing costs at most DV². Since c² ≤ KcM, V² ≤ K²cM and cV ≤ KcM, strict positivity follows at any peak with T(p) > K|Re S(0)| + DK² + KE p^a. The Tate limit of radius minus log generator places the generator inside the ball, and strict positivity identifies the ordinary count limit with the full degree. Finite intersection of the multiplicity tails gives the two-atom assertion. For β > 7/12 the short-interval prime number theorem gives logarithmic prime mass greater than p^β/2 for all sufficiently large p. Each prime in the window contributes at most 2 log p.
