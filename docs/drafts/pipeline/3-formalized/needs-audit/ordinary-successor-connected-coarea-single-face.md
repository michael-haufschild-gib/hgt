## Definitions

Let `S` be a finite set of distinct ordinary primes, let `q>=1` and `H` be natural numbers, and
let `x` be an observation point.  Put

```text
chi_T(x)=product_(p in T)chi_p(x),
theta_T=product_(p in T)theta_p,

C_S^H(q)=sum_(T subset S, q product(T)<=H)(-1)^|T|theta_T,

L_S^H(q;x)=-sum_(T subset S, H<q product(T))(-1)^|T|chi_T(x).
```

For an observation functional `beta` with `beta(1)=J`, define the joined trace and its connected
prime-adjunction forcing by

```text
tau_S^H(q)=J C_S^H(q)-beta(L_S^H(q)),

F_(p,S)^H(q)=beta((chi_p-theta_p)L_S^H(qp)),   p notin S.       (OS1)
```

The ordinary-successor shells are

```text
Sigma_(S,H)^chi(q;x)
 =sum_(T subset S, q product(T)=H+1)(-1)^|T|chi_T(x),

Sigma_(S,H)^theta(q)
 =sum_(T subset S, q product(T)=H+1)(-1)^|T|theta_T.             (OS2)
```




## The stationary survival mode

At the reference incidence `chi_p=theta_p`, the low and high cutoff predicates partition the full
Boolean cube.  Therefore

```text
C_S^H(q)+H_S^H(q;theta)=V_S,
V_S=product_(p in S)(1-theta_p),
L_S^H(q;theta)-C_S^H(q)=-V_S.                                  (OS6)
```

The right side is independent of `H`, and `(OS3)` gives

```text
Delta_H(L_S^H(q;theta)-C_S^H(q))=0.                             (OS7)
```

More sharply, inside the affine span of `(L,C)`, the stationary line is unique.  For scalars
`alpha,beta`,

```text
Delta_H(alpha L_S^H(q;theta)+beta C_S^H(q))
 =(alpha+beta)Sigma_(S,H)^theta(q).                              (OS8)
```

The shell at `H=q-1` contains the empty subset and is nonzero.  Hence `(OS8)` vanishes for every
cutoff exactly when `beta=-alpha`; that line is generated by `L-C=-V_S`.

For the quadratic-root density `theta_p=nu_h(p)/p`, the prime-local classification gives, when
`2 in S`,

```text
V_S(h)=0    exactly when h is odd,
V_S(h)>0   when h is even.                                       (OS9)
```

Thus the even-gap survival ramp is not successor curvature.  It is the nonzero stationary mode
left in the kernel of the ordinary-cutoff derivative.  If a proposed primitive `Psi_H` satisfies

```text

```

on `H_0<=H<H_1`, then necessarily

```text
Psi_(H_1)-Psi_(H_0)=-(H_1-H_0)V_S.                              (OS10)
```

No uniformly bounded local successor primitive can absorb a nonzero ramp over a cofinal aperture.

## Dependency-closed paper proof

When the cutoff changes from `H` to `H+1`, a subset term enters the low sum exactly when
`q product(T)=H+1`.  The same term leaves the unsigned high sum, so it enters `L=-Hhigh` with the
same sign.  This proves the first two identities in `(OS3)`; the trace identity follows by
linearity.  Inserting the second identity into `(OS1)` proves `(OS4)`.

If two subsets `T,U subset S` satisfy `q product(T)=q product(U)`, cancellation of the positive
integer `q` and uniqueness of ordinary prime factorization give `T=U`.  Hence `(OS2)` has at most
one term.  Its sign is `(-1)^|T|=mu(product(T))`, proving `(OS5)`.

At reference incidence, the low and high predicates are complementary on every subset.  Finite
product expansion gives their sum `V_S`; using `L=-Hhigh` proves `(OS6)` and `(OS7)`.  Equation
`(OS8)` follows from the common shell increment in `(OS3)`.  The empty-subset shell at `H=q-1`
proves the uniqueness claim.  The local quadratic root formula

```text

1-theta_2(h)=1/2   for even h
```

together with strict positivity of all other prime factors proves `(OS9)`.  Finally `(OS10)` is
finite telescoping.  Every dependency is closed.
