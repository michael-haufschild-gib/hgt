  ```text
  B_12(x) B_23(x+y) B_12(y)
  ```

## Exact braid-to-quantum conversion

Put the algebraically convenient left flip

```text
L(x)=P B(x).                                                   (2)
```

Using `B_ij=P_ij L_ij`, conjugation of two-leg operators by tensor permutations, and
`P_12 P_23 P_12=P_23 P_12 P_23`, equation `(1)` is equivalent to

```text
L_12(y) L_13(x+y) L_23(x)
  = L_23(x) L_13(x+y) L_12(y).                                (3)
```

The flip anchor becomes

```text
L(0)=c I.                                                      (4)
```

Setting `y=0` and `x=0` in `(3)`, respectively, and cancelling the nonzero scalar `c`
gives the two pointwise commutation laws

```text
[L_13(x),L_23(x)]=0,
[L_12(x),L_13(x)]=0.                                          (5)
```

These identities are a direct theorem of the zero anchor; no analyticity, classification,
or dimension-two assumption is used.

## Positive unitarity forces local product diagonality

Fix `x` and take a minimal operator-Schmidt decomposition

```text
L(x)=sum_(a=1)^r A_a tensor B_a,                               (6)
```

with both `{A_a}` and `{B_a}` linearly independent.  Expanding `(5)` gives

```text
sum_(a,b) [A_a,A_b] tensor B_a tensor B_b = 0,
sum_(a,b) A_a tensor A_b tensor [B_a,B_b] = 0.                 (7)
```

The tensor families `{B_a tensor B_b}` and `{A_a tensor A_b}` are linearly independent.
Consequently

```text
[A_a,A_b]=0,
[B_a,B_b]=0                                                    (8)
```

for every pair `a,b`.

Let `A` and `B` be the unital finite-dimensional algebras generated by the left and right
Schmidt families.  They are commutative by `(8)`, and `L` belongs to `A tensor B`.  Since
`L` is unitary, `L^*=L^{-1}` is a polynomial in `L` by the finite-dimensional minimal
polynomial, hence also belongs to `A tensor B`.  Applying dual coefficient functionals to
the minimal decomposition of `L^*` shows `A_a^* in A` and `B_a^* in B`.  Thus `A` and `B`
are commutative finite-dimensional `C^*`-algebras.

There are unitaries `U_x,V_x` that simultaneously diagonalize `A` and `B`.  Therefore

```text
(U_x tensor V_x) L(x) (U_x tensor V_x)^*
```

is diagonal.  This proves the statement.  The theorem is pointwise: it does not yet give
one rapidity-independent basis or force `U_x=V_x`.

## The published `O(N)` benchmark separates the conventions

Alazzawi--Lechner define their S-matrix by the adjacent equation `(1)` and later display
the standard `O(N)` formula

```text
R_N(theta)=sigma_1(theta) K
          +sigma_2(theta) I
          +sigma_3(theta) P,                                  (9)
```

with `N>=3` and `R_N(0)=-P`.  This displayed family does not satisfy `(1)` in that
operator convention.

Indeed, choose three distinct internal labels.  On the six-dimensional subspace spanned
by their permutations, every contraction operator `K_ij` vanishes and

```text
R_ij(x)=a(x) I+b(x)P_ij,
a(x)=sigma_2(x).                                               (10)
```

Insert this displayed matrix into `(1)`, set `y=0`, and use `R_N(0)=-P`.  After
cancelling the common sign, the difference
between the two sides on this subspace is

```text
a(x)^2 (P_12-P_23).                                            (11)
```

For real `x != 0`, the published Gamma quotient `sigma_2(x)` is nonzero: the Gamma
function has no zeros, and none of its displayed arguments is a pole.  Since
`P_12 != P_23` on three distinct labels, `(11)` is nonzero.

The convention-correct statement is:

1. the physical `O(N)` matrix `(9)` solves the standard quantum equation `(3)` and has
   `R_N(0)=-P`;
2. its operationally correct right-flip braid conversion

   ```text
   B_N=R_N P=sigma_1 K+sigma_2 P+sigma_3 I                    (12)
   ```

   solves the adjacent equation `(1)` and has `B_N(0)=-I`.

Thus the physical fermionic flip anchor and the braid-converted identity anchor cannot be
simultaneously assigned to the same operator.  The diagonal examples in the paper do not
expose the mismatch because their flip-removed amplitudes already commute.
