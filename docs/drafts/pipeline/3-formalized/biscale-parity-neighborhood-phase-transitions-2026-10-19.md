## Theorem

The optimal exponent is 1 at N=2, 1/3 for 3≤N≤11, 1/5 for 12≤N≤19, and 1/6 for N≥20. The neighborhood contains arbitrary real perturbations in every admitted coordinate, including changing support. This is a pairwise neighborhood modulus, not a point-to-center estimate. Constants may depend on N and the chosen nonzero parity amplitude. No numerical experiment is used.

The contribution is the complete cutoff-dependent transition and its actual-source sharpness. Generic finite-group invariant mechanisms, scalar root estimates, the existing global sixth-root estimate, and package 6's N=3 cubic obstruction receive no separate credit.

## Proof structure

1. A nonzero parity coordinate supplies all squared magnitudes through the resonant products `a_q² a_2⁴`. On a bounded parity neighborhood these yield square-root magnitude control. Lower or equal dyadic-depth modes have cubic sign readouts using the existing mixed quintic and same-block sixth constructions.
2. For N≤11 the only admitted orders above parity's two-adic depth are 4 and 8. The resonance with orders `(4,8,8,2,2)` yields `a_4 a_8² a_2²`, of perturbation degree three. Thresholding small modes reduces the surviving signs to the canonical actual translation.
3. For N≤19 the only additional higher-depth orders are 12 and 16. The same-depth pair 4,12 has a quartic sign readout, padded by a parity pair to degree six. Mixed higher depths have the existing quintic readout. These give fifth-root control after thresholding, including every cutoff and zero-support case.
4. At N=12, the sharp pair is `2R_2 + t R_8 ± t R_12`, where R_q is the actual Ramanujan mode. A sign-sensitive monomial needs at least three order-12 factors and two order-8 factors, hence perturbation degree five. Every raw-array error is O(t⁵), while every physical translation leaves error bounded below by a positive multiple of t.
5. At N=20, the sharp pair is `2R_2 + t R_12 ± t R_20`. Coprime odd factors and common higher dyadic depth force perturbation degree six. The existing global bound gives the matching upper exponent. Both the raw-array estimates and separation under every natural translation are formalized.
6. Construct the displayed coordinate profiles directly at every larger admissible cutoff using the exact truncated Möbius inverse. Fourier inversion bounds the literal N! raw averages; normalized Fourier coefficients give physical separation under every natural translation. No period-averaging lift is needed.

## Dependency-closed paper argument

Write `a_q=selbergMultiplesCoord N c q`, `b_q=selbergMultiplesCoord N e q`, and `W_N=∑_(1≤q≤N) φ(q)`. Work first in the box `1≤a_2,b_2≤3` and `|a_q|,|b_q|≤3` for every admitted q. The physical sup-norm ball of radius one around `2R_2` lies in this box: normalized Fourier coefficients have operator norm at most one. ### Upper bounds

The existing norm-one Fourier readout gives error at most ε in every resonant product. Opposite pairs yield `a_q² a_2⁴`, and six parity frequencies yield `a_2⁶`. For A,B≥1, factorization of differences gives `|A−B|≤|A⁶−B⁶|` and `|A⁴−B⁴|≤|A⁶−B⁶|`. Consequently

`|a_q²−b_q²| ≤ ε + b_q² ε ≤ 10ε`.

Since `||a_q|−|b_q||²≤|a_q²−b_q²|`, noise ε≤δᵈ, 0≤δ≤1 and d≥2 gives magnitude error at most 4δ. Retain precisely the coordinates with `|a_q|>4δ`; replace target magnitudes there by `|a_q|`, preserving target signs; set both other coordinates to zero. The source and target rounding errors are at most 4δ and 8δ per coordinate. If δ≥1/4, the trivial physical bound `6W_N≤24W_Nδ` suffices. Otherwise the parity anchor remains active and positive in both rounded profiles.

For any retained sign-sensitive resonant product, opposite product signs force its error to be at least its source magnitude. If it contains at most d factors outside the parity anchor, that magnitude is strictly greater than δᵈ. Such signs therefore agree exactly. Specifically:

- Odd q: the existing mixed quintic `a_q³ a_2²` fixes its sign.
- Depth-one q: the existing sixth product `a_q³ a_2³` fixes its sign.
- For N≤11, higher-depth orders are exactly the admitted members of `{4,8}`. The tuple with frequencies `(-P/4,P/8,P/8,P/2,-P/2)`, where P=N!, gives the fifth product `a_4 a_8² a_2²`. Thus if order 8 survives, order 4's sign is fixed. Otherwise only one higher-depth mode can survive. This proves the needed sign pattern with d=3.
- For N≤19, higher-depth orders are exactly the admitted members of `{4,8,12,16}`. If the greatest active depth is at least three, it has only one mode, and the existing mixed quintics fix every lower sign, with d=5. If the greatest depth is two, the only possible pair is 4,12. The frequencies `(P/12,P/12,P/12,-P/4,P/2,-P/2)` give `a_12³ a_4 a_2²`; their perturbation degree is four, so they also work with d=5. The two top signs must agree relative to the source.

In both cases all signs below the greatest retained depth match, and its signs have one common ratio. The existing canonical half-period theorem realizes exactly that possible top-block reversal as one natural translation. The physical error after rounding is at most `12W_Nδ`; together with the large-δ case this gives `24W_N ε^(1/d)` for d=3 or 5.

At N=2, the fifth tuple `(0,P/2,P/2,P/2,P/2)` controls `a_1 a_2⁴`. The same anchor estimate gives `|a_1−b_1|≤4ε` and `|a_2−b_2|≤ε`, hence a physical Lipschitz bound `5ε`. For N≥20 the existing global sixth-root theorem gives the upper bound. Scaling actual weights and moments transfers this box result to every nonzero parity amplitude; small and large noise are split at a fixed positive scale if needed. No missing analytic estimate is assumed.

### Sharp witnesses and all-array cancellation

For N≥3 use `2R_2 ± tR_3`; for N≥12 use `2R_2+tR_8±tR_12`; for N≥20 use `2R_2+tR_12±tR_20`. Take 0<t≤1. Their coordinate profiles have exactly the displayed support and their distance from `2R_2` is at most `2t`, `8t`, and `12t`, respectively.

In a zero-sum tuple, an order cannot be the only one with a given odd prime factor: multiplying the sum by the product of all other orders contradicts its additive order. More generally, if every other order divides B, then the singled-out order must divide B. A half-period h with every order dividing 2h gives the parity constraint that an even number of the orders fail to divide h. This follows by multiplying the corresponding additive-character values, whose product is one.

- On `{2,3}`, a product changed by the sign flip has an odd number of order-3 factors. There cannot be exactly one, by the prime-3 obstruction. Thus it has at least three small factors.
- On `{2,8,12}`, a changed product has an odd number of order-12 factors, at least three by the prime-3 obstruction. If no order-8 factor occurs, the half-period h=6 forces an even number of order-12 factors, contradiction. Exactly one order-8 factor is also impossible, because all other orders divide 12 whereas 8 does not. Thus at least two order-8 factors occur, giving at least five small factors.
- On `{2,12,20}`, a changed product has an odd number of order-20 factors, at least three by the prime-5 obstruction. The common half-period h=30 forces the total number of order-12 and order-20 factors to be even. The order-12 count is therefore odd and at least three by the prime-3 obstruction. Thus at least six small factors occur.

Fourier inversion of the literal m-lag array expresses it as the sum of character-weighted resonant coordinate products. For m=5 or 6 there are at most P⁶ summands, all characters have norm one, and each differing product is bounded by `128tᵈ`, where d=3,5,6 respectively. Therefore every raw fifth/sixth error is at most `128P⁶tᵈ`. This proof works at every larger cutoff directly; no unproved change of averaging period is needed.

### Separation under every translation

Normalized Fourier coefficients of a physical error are bounded by its uniform norm. For the first family, the order-3 translation phase w satisfies w³=1. Since `|w³−(-1)³|≤3|w+1|`, each translation has uniform error at least `2t/3`.

For the second family let z,w be the order-8 and order-12 translation phases. They satisfy z²=w³ because `2P/8=3P/12`. If D is the uniform physical error, then `t|1−z|≤D` and `t|1+w|≤D`. The triangle inequality gives `2t≤2t|1−z|+3t|1+w|≤5D`. Thus D≥2t/5 for every translation.

For the third family the order-12 and order-20 phases satisfy z³=w⁵. The same argument gives D≥t/4. At N=2, use the constant perturbations `2R_2 ± tR_1`; their zero-frequency Fourier difference is 2t for every translation. Every changed resonant product contains at least one constant factor, so the same raw-array estimate applies with perturbation degree one.

These families belong to every positive-radius neighborhood for sufficiently small t. A larger exponent α>1/d would force `c t≤C(128P⁶tᵈ)^α`, impossible as t tends to zero because dα>1. Weight scaling covers every nonzero parity amplitude: for λ≠0, normalize by s=λ⁻¹, so physical radius scales by |s| and a common raw-error bound ε becomes at most `(|s|⁵+|s|⁶)ε`. Since the local bounds allow every ε≥0, this transfers the exponent exactly in both directions. The complete classification is proved on paper and verified in Lean. The formal unit-ball root bounds use the sufficient constant `12W_N+8`; N=2 uses five.
