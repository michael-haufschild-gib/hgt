# Orbit size is residue degree; the functional equation of the prime traces

- **ID:** gtz-orbit-degree-and-trace-functional-equation
- **Stage:** 3-formalized
- **Type:** THEOREM
- **Source:** programme card `gtz-galois-theory-of-zeros-programme.md`, problems 1 and 2 (review-1411 §2.19).
- **Statement:** `B` the prime trace field, generated by `T_t(m) = Σ_{Im ρ = ±t} n_m^ρ` (with
  multiplicity, `n_m = ∏ p^{m_p}`); `E = ℚ(p^ρ)`, `G = Gal(E/B)` acting on the zeros;
  `F_ρ = B(p^ρ : p)`.
  (A) the stabilizer of `ρ` is `Gal(E/F_ρ)`; `[F_ρ : B] = |G·ρ|`; `F_ρ = B(2^ρ, 3^ρ)` is finite
  over `B`, every orbit is finite;
  (B) `[F_ρ : B]` is even;
  (C) `[F_ρ : B] = 2` iff `p^{2 Re ρ} ∈ B` (any one prime); `= 2` at every zero with rational
  real part;
  (D) `T_t(m) = n_m · T_t(−m)`; `T_{−t}(m) = T_t(m)`; `T_t(0)` is the number of copies of
  ordinate `±t`, twice the number at `t`; `n_m ∈ ℚ_{>0}`;
  (E) `B` is generated by the `T_t(m)` with `t ≥ 0` and `Σ m_p log p ≥ 0`.

## Goal

Problem 1 (presentation of `B`): relations among the generators that come from the functional
equation, not from the definition; controlled finite subfields (`F_ρ` finite of degree the orbit
size). Problem 2: orbits are measured by degrees.

## Proof

(A) `F_ρ` is fixed exactly by the `σ` with `σρ = ρ` (two primes separate exponents). Krull:
`[L : B] = [G : Gal(E/L)]` for every intermediate `L`; the index of a stabilizer is the size of
the orbit. `2^ρ, 3^ρ` are integral over `B` (`E/B` Galois), so `B(2^ρ, 3^ρ)` is finite.
(B) Conjugation lies in `G`, so the orbit is closed under `ρ ↦ ρ̄`, which has no fixed zero. The
orbit is the disjoint union of its points with `Im > 0` and their conjugates.
(C) The orbit contains `ρ ≠ ρ̄`; size two means orbit `= {ρ, ρ̄}`, which is the weight criterion.
(D) `q ↦ 1 − q` on copies is a bijection between the fibres at `t` and `−t`, so
`Σ n^q = Σ n^{1−q} = n Σ n^{−q}`. `n_m = e^{Σ m_p log p}` is a product of prime powers.
(E) For `t < 0` use `T_t = T_{−t}`; for `Σ m_p log p < 0` use `T_t(m) = n_m T_t(−m)` with
`n_m ∈ ℚ`. ∎
