# Fractional-ideal elements are ideal-class unit orbits

- **ID:** fractional-ideal-elements-are-class-unit-orbits
- **Stage:** 3-formalized
- **Type:** THEOREM (Lean)
- **Source:** [Dual Hecke theta unfolding](../1-in-progress/hecke-dual-theta-unfolding-over-fractional-ideal-classes.md)
- **Statement:** For any invertible fractional ideal `J`, its nonzero elements are in explicit bijection with nonzero integral ideals of class `[J]⁻¹` times global units. The chosen generators and all unit-orbit elements have absolute field norm `Norm(I)Norm(J)`.

## Proof

For an integral ideal `I` in class `[J]⁻¹`, the product `IJ` has trivial
class, hence is principal. Choose a generator `g_I`. It is nonzero
because both ideals are invertible. Since `I≤1`, its principal ideal
`IJ` lies in `J`, so `g_I∈J`.

Conversely, a nonzero `a∈J` satisfies `(a)J⁻¹≤1`, hence this quotient
is an actual integral ideal `I_a`. Its product with `J` is `(a)`, so
it is nonzero and has class `[J]⁻¹`. Equality of the principal ideals
generated by `g_(I_a)` and `a` supplies a global unit `u` with
`a=u*g_(I_a)`.

If two pairs produce the same element, their principal ideals agree.
Cancelling `J` gives equality of their integral ideal indices. Cancelling
the common nonzero generator then gives equality of the units. Thus
`(I,u)↦u*g_I` is bijective. The chosen generators also embed the ideal
index in the countable number field, proving countability.

Applying the fractional-ideal norm to `(g_I)=IJ` gives
`|Norm_(K/ℚ)(g_I)|=Norm(I)Norm(J)`. Unit multiples generate the same
principal fractional ideal, so the norm identity holds on every orbit.
No integral generator is assumed for a fractional principal ideal.
