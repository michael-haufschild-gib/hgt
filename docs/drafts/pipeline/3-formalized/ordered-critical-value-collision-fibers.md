## Complete paper proof: compact complex points are finite [PROVED HERE]

Let A be a nonzero finitely generated commutative complex algebra whose complex-point space is compact in the pointwise topology. The inspected normalization theorem gives an injective finite map g:C[T_1,...,T_s]→A. It is integral, so the checked extension theorem makes precomposition on complex points surjective. If s>0, evaluation at g(T_1) is a continuous surjection from the compact space of complex points of A onto C: any desired coordinate value comes from a polynomial evaluation point and extends across the integral inclusion. This would make C compact, contradicting its unbounded norm. Therefore s=0. The polynomial algebra on no variables is C, so finiteness over that algebra makes A finite-dimensional over C. The inspected finite-algebra-homomorphism theorem now makes its complex-point set finite. The zero algebra has no complex points and is immediate. Nilpotents do not affect the argument; no reducedness hypothesis is needed.

A useful equivalent proof only needs boundedness of every point-evaluation range, instead of compactness of the whole point space. The same surjective coordinate contradicts boundedness; the s=0 conclusion is unchanged.

## Complete paper proof: apply to actual critical-value fibers [PROVED HERE]

Use coordinates θ=(b,c_1,...,c_n) on Parameter n=C^n×C. Define universal positions A_0=−∑_{i=1}^n X_i and A_i=X_i in the multivariate polynomial ring R=C[X_0,...,X_n]. Put Q(Z)=∏_{j=0}^n(Z−A_j) in R[Z]. The actual critical-value coordinate B_j is evaluation of the multivariate polynomial

V_j=(n+2)∑_{k=0}^{n+1} Q.coeff(k)·(k+1)^{-1}·A_j^{k+1}+X_0.

This follows directly by applying multivariate evaluation to the finite coefficient formula for the already constructed antiderivative; evaluation commutes with the product defining Q, its coefficients, and finite sums. The coordinate permutation θ↔(c,b) is a linear homeomorphism.

For any fixed value tuple y, let J be the ideal generated by V_j−y_j. Complex points of R/J are exactly coordinate tuples in B^{-1}(y): a homomorphism is determined by the images of the variables, and it factors through J precisely when all these equations vanish. This identification is a homeomorphism. Each coordinate evaluation is continuous, and evaluation of any quotient element is evaluation of a polynomial representative, hence continuous in the tuple; these facts give both directions in the pointwise topology. Properness of B makes the fiber compact. Thus the point space of R/J is compact. The preceding theorem makes it finite, proving finiteness of every actual critical-value fiber. Empty fibers would be immediate, although actual surjectivity already rules them out.

## Collision endpoint mechanism [PROVED HERE on paper]

Let v:(0,1]→Parameter n be continuous and suppose B(v(t)) tends to y as t→0. The value curve stays in a compact set near zero: its extension by y is continuous on a compact interval. Properness traps the corresponding parameter tail in a compact set. The cluster set of v at zero is nonempty and compact; it is connected because it is the nested intersection of closures of connected interval-tail images. Continuity of B puts that cluster set inside the finite fiber B^{-1}(y). A nonempty connected subset of a finite Hausdorff space is a singleton. Compactness then forces v(t) to converge to that sole cluster point. This proves existence and uniqueness of the endpoint of each continuous lift. Different lifted paths may still have different limiting polynomials; no selection of prescribed covering monodromy has been inferred.

## Stronger endpoint mechanism exposed by the corpus [PROVED HERE on paper]

The inspected definition of properness gives a stronger statement with no metric or compact-tail premise. Let f:X→Y be proper, X Hausdorff, and f^{-1}(y) finite. Let F be a nontrivial filter on X with a basis of preconnected sets. If f_*F tends to y, then F tends to one point of f^{-1}(y).

Proof. Choose pairwise disjoint open neighborhoods U_a of the finitely many fiber points; the inspected `exists_pairwise_disjoint_nhds` constructs these. Their union contains the fiber. Closedness of f and the inspected `Bottcher.exists_nhds_preimage_subset` provide a neighborhood W of y with f^{-1}(W) contained in that union. Therefore the union belongs to F. Take a nonempty preconnected basis set S contained in it. Choose one point of S and the neighborhood U_a containing it. The union of the other U_b is open and disjoint from U_a. Preconnectedness forces S entirely into U_a, so U_a belongs to F.

To prove F≤nhds(a), use the inspected ultrafilter criterion. Any ultrafilter V≤F still maps to y. Properness (`IsProperMap.ultrafilter_le_nhds_of_tendsto`) makes V converge to some b in the fiber. Then both U_a and U_b belong to V. Disjointness forces b=a. Thus every ultrafilter below F tends to a, and the filter itself tends to a. This also proves the fiber is nonempty without assuming surjectivity. Hausdorffness makes the limit unique.

For a continuous parameter curve near an endpoint, the image of the one-sided neighborhood filter has a basis of images of intervals. These are preconnected, so the theorem applies directly to the actual proper finite-fiber critical-value map. This avoids an additional theorem about intersections of connected compact sets and strengthens the previous endpoint argument.
