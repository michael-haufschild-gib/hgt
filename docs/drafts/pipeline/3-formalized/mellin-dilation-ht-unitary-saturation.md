## An explicit odd HT phase

Let

```text
h(z)=1/Gamma(z),
q(z)=h(z)-h(-z).                                            (1)
```

The reciprocal gamma function is real entire, so `q` is real entire and odd. It is
hypertranscendental over `C(z)`.

### Proof of hypertranscendence `[PROVED]`

Euler reflection and the recurrence of Gamma give

```text
Gamma(-z) Gamma(1+z)=-pi/sin(pi z),
Gamma(1+z)=z Gamma(z),
```

hence

```text
h(z)h(-z)=-z sin(pi z)/pi.                                  (2)
```

Since `h(-z)=h(z)-q(z)`, equation `(2)` becomes

```text
h(z)^2-q(z)h(z)+z sin(pi z)/pi=0.                           (3)
```

If `q` were DA, then `(3)` would make `h` algebraic over a DA differential field and therefore DA
over `C(z)`. Its reciprocal `Gamma` would be DA, contradicting Holder's theorem. Thus `q` is HT.

For every real `c`, define

```text
H_c(z)=exp(i c q(z)).                                       (4)
```

Then

```text
H_(c+d)=H_c H_d,
H_c(-z)=H_c(z)^(-1),
H_c(-t)=conj(H_c(t)),
|H_c(t)|=1  for t in R.                                    (5)
```

For `c!=0`, `H_c` is HT. Indeed, if it were DA, its logarithmic derivative

```text
H_c'/H_c=i c q'                                             (6)
```

would be DA. Then `q` is DA over the DA field generated by `q'`, by the first-order equation
`Y'=q'`; transitivity of differential algebraicity would make `q` DA over `C(z)`, contradicting
the preceding proof.

Thus `(H_c)_(c in R)` is a continuum group of zero-free entire HT phases, with `H_0=1`.

## Mellin commutant theorem `[PROVED]`

```text

```

```text
(D_a f)(x)=a^(1/2)f(ax)                                     (8)
```

`H_c(t)` on `L2(R,dt)`. Equation `(5)` makes `U_c` unitary, and multiplication operators commute:

```text
U_c M D_a M^(-1)=M D_a M^(-1) U_c                          (9)
```

for every `a>0`.

Let `v` and `(g_j)_(j in J)` be vectors, and let `A_j subset R_+` be arbitrary dilation sets. Put

```text

```

Writing `V_c=M^(-1)U_cM`, equations `(9)` and `(10)` give

```text
V_c C(g)=C(V_c g),                                          (11)
dist(V_c v,C(V_c g))=dist(v,C(g)).                           (12)
```

