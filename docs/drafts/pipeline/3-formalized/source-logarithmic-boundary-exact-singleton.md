## Theorem

Let `C -> K -> L` be a tower of ordinary differential fields of characteristic zero.  Let

```text
tau_i   : K ~= K,
sigma_i : L ~= L
```

be any family of compatible differential-field automorphisms.  They need not commute.  Assume
that they fix `C` and that their common fixed field in `L` is exactly `C`.

Let `x in L^*`, `b_i in K^*`, and suppose

```text
sigma_i(x) = b_i x.                                      (1)
```

Then `x` is differentially algebraic over `K` if and only if there exist a nonzero finitely
supported coefficient family

```text
c : N -> C
```

and one `k in K` such that, simultaneously for every `i`,

```text
tau_i(k)-k = sum_n c_n D^n(D(b_i)/b_i).                  (2)
```

Equivalently, `x` is hypertranscendental exactly when no nonzero finite differential character
of its logarithmic source increments is one simultaneous base coboundary.

The formal statement indexes the coefficient family by `Unit x N`; this is definitionally the
single-observable full jet.

## Formal proof architecture

1. Differential algebraicity is proved closed under `x -> D(x)/x` by placing the logarithmic
   derivative in the differential field generated by `x` and applying the formal transitivity
   theorem.
2. The reverse hypertranscendence implication is obtained from the formal rational first-jet
   transfer theorem.  Hence

   ```text
   HT_K(x) iff HT_K(D(x)/x).                              (3)
   ```

3. Equation (1) is converted in Lean to the additive source law

   ```text
   sigma_i(D(x)/x) = D(x)/x + D(b_i)/b_i.                (4)
   ```

4. Differential algebraicity plus the arbitrary-family additive Ostrowski theorem yields (2).
5. Conversely, (2) makes the corresponding finite jet combination fixed under every `sigma_i`.
   Fixed-field exactness places it in `C`, producing an explicit nonzero differential polynomial
   relation.  Lean proves nonzeroness by separating one nonzero coefficient with two polynomial
   evaluations.

No commutation relation among the source automorphisms occurs anywhere in the proof.
