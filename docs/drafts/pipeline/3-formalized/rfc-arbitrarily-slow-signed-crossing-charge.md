# Arbitrarily slow signed crossing charge on a density-one matching

- **Stage** 3-formalized
- **Target** For every nonnegative \(w(X)\to\infty\), construct one global restricted involution of displacement \(o(n)\) whose unpaid set through \(X\) has size \(o(X)\) and whose signed crossing charge is bounded by \(w(X)\) at every cutoff. Its ordinary fixed-set prefix then differs from \(M(X)\) by at most \(w(X)\) at every cutoff.

**Cofactors.** Fix \(Q\) and \(f(n)=\mu(n)1_{(n,Q)=1}\). For every \(\delta>0\), this row can be approximated by a real sequence with uniformly bounded partial sums and eventual mean absolute error at most \(\delta\). For Liouville's function \(\lambda\), subtract its forward mean over \(h\) consecutive places. The difference has primitive bounded by \(2h\). The fixed-length Matomäki–Radziwiłł mean square, followed by Cauchy–Schwarz and dyadic summation, makes the mean absolute error arbitrarily small. For the coprime Möbius row, truncate the square-divisor Möbius sum at \(B\) and impose coprimality to \(Q\) exactly. The mean absolute truncation error through \(X\) is at most \(X/B+\sqrt X\). Expanding the finite sieve expresses the truncated row as a finite linear combination of sequences \(1_{d\mid n}\lambda(n/d)\), by complete multiplicativity. Inserting zeros at nonmultiples preserves boundedness of partial sums and the mean error bound.

**Keep actual exchanges.** Read the cofactor signs in order. Keep the current sign whenever adding it leaves the retained sum \(T\) inside the integer interval \([-J,J]\). Otherwise discard it. For any approximant \(g\) with primitive \(G\) bounded by \(J\),
\[
   \#\{\text{discarded nonzero signs through }N\}+|T(N)-G(N)|
     \le \sum_{n\le N}|f(n)-g(n)|.
\]
At a rejected positive step, \(T=J\ge G(n)\), so discarding the sign decreases the prospective distance to \(G(n)\) by exactly one. The lower endpoint gives the same identity. At a retained step use the triangle inequality. Thus shrinking the tolerated density only requires increasing the finite cap. With any nondecreasing cap tending to infinity the same purely sequential selection discards a density-zero set. For each fixed approximant the comparison applies after the cap exceeds its bound.

Use this same cofactor selection at every row of a short exchange table of modulus \(Q\). For a squarefree vertex \(n\), switching its local divisor preserves the cofactor \(c=n/\gcd(n,Q)\). Keep both ends of a selected edge and fix both ends otherwise. Each divisor class loses at most the discarded cofactor count. At any sharp cutoff, with arbitrary outer walls, each crossing row is a single interval in \(c\), so its signed charge is bounded by \(2J\). Thus for every tolerance there is a fixed divisor table thinned to eventual unpaid fraction at most that tolerance, with a uniform signed bound at all three endpoints.

**One map.** Use successively more accurate thinned tables on disjoint intervals. Start each block above the onset of its estimates and where \(w\) exceeds its finite crossing bound. Before the first block, fix every vertex. Make successive walls grow by factors tending to infinity. The left-wall losses inject into old crossing endpoints there, displacement stays sublinear, and the signed crossing formula at every cutoff involves only its own block. This is a signed estimate on all moving vertices of a density-one restricted matching. Estimating the sparse fixed vertices remains necessary for the full prefix.
