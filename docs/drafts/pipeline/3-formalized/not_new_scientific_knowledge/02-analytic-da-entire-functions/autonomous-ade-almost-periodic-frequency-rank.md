# Autonomous ADE almost-periodic frequency-rank law

- **ID:** `autonomous-ade-almost-periodic-frequency-rank`
- **Stage:** 3-formalized
- **Type:** THEOREM (paper) / GENERAL HT LANDSCAPE / finite-flow no-go
- **Scientific status:** `[PROVED] [SEARCH] [BRIDGE] [NOT NEW SCIENTIFIC KNOWLEDGE]`
- **Statement:** A holomorphic almost-periodic solution of a regular autonomous complex algebraic
  differential equation of order `n` has Bohr frequency rank strictly less than `2n`. Its
  translation-equivariant divisor distribution has frequency rank no larger. Hence no
  finite-dimensional regular autonomous ADE can generate an infinite rationally independent
  prime-log spectrum.

## Regular autonomous class

Let `n>=1`, let `U subset C^n` be open, and let `R:U->C` be locally Lipschitz and holomorphic. Let
`f` be holomorphic on a horizontal strip

```text
S_eta={z in C: |Im z|<eta}                                 (1)
```

and assume:

1. the real translates `{f(z+t):t in R}` are relatively compact for uniform convergence on every
   closed substrip of `S_eta`;
2. `f` is not identically zero;
3. its jet lies in `U` and satisfies

   ```text
   f^(n)=R(f,f',...,f^(n-1));                               (2)
   ```

4. the first-order system associated with `(2)` has unique solutions through every point of the
   compact jet hull.

Polynomial `R` is the principal algebraic-differential case. The last hypothesis is automatic for
locally Lipschitz `R`; it is stated because uniqueness, not polynomiality, is the structural input.

## Finite frequency rank `[PROVED; SEARCH]`

Put

```text
x(t)=(f(t),f'(t),...,f^(n-1)(t)) in C^n = R^(2n).           (3)
```

Holomorphic almost periodicity and Cauchy estimates imply that every derivative in `(3)` is Bohr
almost periodic. Equation `(2)` turns `(3)` into the autonomous real first-order system

```text
x'=Psi(x) in R^(2n),                                       (4)
```

whose initial-value problem is unique on the orbit hull.

Cartwright's frequency theorem, in the explicit form of O'Brien's Theorem 3, says that an almost
periodic solution of a uniquely solvable autonomous system in `R^p` has a frequency basis with
strictly fewer than `p` elements. Applying it to `(4)` with `p=2n` gives

```text
rank_Q Gamma(x)<2n.                                        (5)
```

Every nonzero Bohr coefficient of `f` is a coordinate coefficient of `x`, so

```text
rank_Q Gamma(f)<=rank_Q Gamma(x)<2n.                        (6)
```

This proves the function-frequency assertion.

## Divisor-frequency corollary `[PROVED]`

Let `X_f` be the translation hull of `f`. It is a compact connected abelian group: real translation
has dense image, and the hull operation is the group law. Its character group is the additive
frequency group generated by the Bohr spectrum of `f`.

The jet-at-zero map

```text
J:X_f->C^n,
J(g)=(g(0),g'(0),...,g^(n-1)(0))                            (7)
```

is continuous. It is injective because two hull solutions with the same initial jet solve the same
autonomous system and uniqueness makes them equal. Thus `X_f` embeds in `R^(2n)`. Equivalently,
Cartwright's dimension argument gives

```text
dim X_f=rank_Q dual(X_f)<2n.                                (8)
```

For a nonzero holomorphic function `g`, its zero divisor is the Poincare--Lelong distribution

```text
Div(g)=(1/(2 pi)) Delta log|g|.                             (9)
```

Local uniform convergence of nonzero holomorphic functions gives convergence of `(9)` in the
distribution topology. The hull of a nonzero uniformly almost-periodic function contains no zero
function, so

```text
g |-> Div(g)                                                (10)
```

is a continuous translation-equivariant map on `X_f`. Its image is therefore a compact abelian
factor of `X_f`. Pontryagin duality embeds the character group of that factor into `dual(X_f)`.
Consequently every pure-point frequency of the divisor distribution belongs to a group of rank
strictly less than `2n`:

```text
rank_Q Gamma(Div(f))<=rank_Q Gamma(f)<2n.                  (11)
```

The same conclusion holds for every continuous translation-equivariant projection of the divisor,
including a height-pushforward whenever that pushforward is locally finite and continuous in the
chosen distribution topology.

## Boundary cases

- For `n=1`, `(5)` gives rank `<2` for a complex scalar equation, consistent with periodic or
  rank-one solenoidal motion.
- The frequency group need not be finitely generated as an abelian group. A finite-rank compact
  connected abelian hull may be a protorus rather than a torus. The theorem asserts finite
  `Q`-rank, exactly the notion needed by the spectral-rank conjecture.
- Nonautonomous almost-periodic coefficients may contribute their own frequency module. O'Brien's
  more general theorem bounds only the additional codimension; the autonomous hypothesis cannot be
  silently dropped.
- A singular implicit algebraic ODE without unique initial-value evolution is outside the theorem.
  That is the genuine remaining nonlinear ADE boundary.
- Infinite-dimensional flows are outside the theorem and can carry infinite frequency rank.

## HT and RH-route consequence

The theorem gives a precise finite-flow exclusion. Any proposal that claims to generate the full
prime-log module `{log p}` by a regular autonomous algebraic differential state of fixed finite
dimension is impossible: the prime logs are linearly independent over `Q` in arbitrarily large
finite subsets, whereas `(5)` has a fixed finite rank bound.

This applies to a concrete Hilbert--Polya, Connes, de Branges, Weil, Li, or Nyman--Beurling
implementation only when its proposed source generator is such a finite-dimensional autonomous ADE
or an equivalent finite jet flow. It does not exclude:

1. an infinite-dimensional self-adjoint operator;
2. an exact trace or explicit formula supplied as a constraint rather than generated by a flow;
3. a singular/nonunique differential relation;
4. the complete RH-equivalent positivity criteria.

The landscape conclusion is stronger and route-independent:

> An infinite-rank prime spectrum cannot be propagated by any regular finite autonomous algebraic
> differential flow. If it is present, it must enter through an infinite-dimensional source or a
> non-flow constraint.

## Scientific novelty verdict

`[SEARCH] [BRIDGE] [NOT NEW SCIENTIFIC KNOWLEDGE]`. The central frequency-rank theorem is already in
the literature. O'Brien's Theorem 3 states that an almost-periodic solution of a uniquely solvable
autonomous system in `R^p` has fewer than `p` rationally independent basis frequencies, explicitly
crediting Cartwright's autonomous-flow work. The complex ADE conversion is mechanical. The divisor
factor corollary uses standard Poincare--Lelong continuity and Pontryagin duality.

Primary sources:

1. M. L. Cartwright, *Almost periodic flows and the solutions of differential equations*, Proc.
   London Math. Soc. (3) 17 (1967), 355--380.
2. G. C. O'Brien, *The frequencies of almost periodic solutions of almost periodic differential
   equations*, J. Austral. Math. Soc.; Theorem 3,
   `https://doi.org/10.1017/S1446788700017092`.
3. S. Takahashi, *Dimension of compact groups and their representations*, Tohoku Math. J. 5
