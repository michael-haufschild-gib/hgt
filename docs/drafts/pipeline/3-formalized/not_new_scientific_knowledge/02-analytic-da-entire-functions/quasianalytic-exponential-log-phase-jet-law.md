- **Statement:** Let `E` be an injective power-Poincare asymptotic field at upper infinity. Adjoin
  finitely many algebraic phase monomials from one recurrent exponential `w(s)=exp(beta s)` and one
  logarithmic phase `u(s)=s^(i theta)`, with `beta,theta in R`. Every finite tuple in `E(w,u)` has,

  ```text
  C[z,exp(beta z),exp(-beta z)].
  ```

## 1. Power-Poincare coefficient fields

An **upper power-Poincare field** is a field `E` of meromorphic functions on an upper half-plane with
an injective complete asymptotic map such that every nonzero `b in E` has:

1. a first generalized power monomial `c s^lambda`, `c!=0`, `lambda in R`;
2. at most polynomial growth together with its reciprocal on upper vertical tubes;
3. for every requested `L` and every local disk `|z|<=R`, a finite translated expansion by
   polynomials in `z` with remainder `O_R(S^(-L))` after its leading power is removed.

Ordinary Laurent Poincare fields are examples. Adjoining finitely many real powers `s^a` preserves
these properties: after selecting the dominant rational-function degree, the remaining expansion is
supported in the locally finite positive semigroup generated by `1` and `|a|`.

Fix real `beta,theta` and put

```text
w(s)=exp(beta s),
u(s)=s^(i theta).                                          (1)
```

If `beta=0`, omit `w`; if `theta=0`, omit `u`. Assume that evaluation embeds the resulting polynomial
ring over `E` into meromorphic functions. On upper vertical tubes,

```text
w(z+iS)=omega_S exp(beta z),  |omega_S|=1,
u(z+iS)=a_S(1+z/(iS))^(i theta),
|a_S|=exp(-pi theta/2).                                    (2)
```

## 2. Finite phase polynomials have a uniform directional jet

Let

```text
P(W,U)=sum_(k,l) b_(k,l)(s) W^k U^l                       (3)
```

be nonzero, with finite support. Among its coefficient asymptotics choose the greatest real growth
exponent. The coefficient at that layer is a nonzero finite Laurent polynomial

```text
P_0(W,U)=sum_(k,l) c_(k,l) W^k U^l.                       (4)
```

For a phase point `(omega,a)` on the compact circles in `(2)`, group `(4)` by the `W` exponent:

```text
P_0(omega exp(beta z),a U)
 =sum_k omega^k exp(k beta z) Q_k(a U).                    (5)
```

Let `d` be the largest number of distinct `U` exponents occurring in any `Q_k`, minus one. The
vectors

```text
((U d/dU)^m Q_k)(a),  0<=m<=d,                             (6)
```

determine all coefficients of `Q_k`: after removing the unit phases `a^l`, the matrix is the
Vandermonde matrix `(l^m)`. Hence there is a constant `c_P>0`, independent of `(omega,a)`, such that
the maximum coefficient norm of the finite family

```text
sum_k omega^k exp(k beta z)
  ((U d/dU)^m Q_k)(a),  0<=m<=d,                           (7)
```

is at least `c_P`.

`[PROVED]` Therefore `P(w(z+iS),u(z+iS))`, including its power-Poincare coefficients, has a
translated exponential-polynomial coefficient of finite algebraic height order, uniformly for all
large `S`. The bound depends on the fixed polynomial and coefficient field elements, not on the
phase point.

### Proof

Distinct functions `exp(k beta z)` are linearly independent when `beta!=0`; when `beta=0` there is
only one group. The Vandermonde matrix in `(6)` is invertible because the integer exponents `l` are
distinct. Multiplication by the phase diagonals `omega^k a^l` preserves coefficient norms up to
fixed finite-dimensional constants. Expanding the second identity in `(2)` shows that
`(U d/dU)^m` first appears multiplied by `S^(-m)` and a nonzero polynomial in `z`. The
power-Poincare expansion of the `b_(k,l)` supplies only finitely many earlier layers. Thus one of the
finite translated coefficients survives at a fixed algebraic power of `S`. This proves the claim.

This is the positive **directional phase-jet law**: a nonzero finite phase polynomial always exposes
a finite jet along the actual translation direction. A multivariate common-zero set cannot hide all
directional jets.

## 3. Finite tuples have exponential-polynomial projective limits

Let `F=(F_1,...,F_r)` be a finite nonzero tuple in `E(w,u)`. Clear one common denominator and obtain a
nonzero polynomial tuple `(P_1,...,P_r)`. Choose one nonzero component and apply Section 2. There are
`C>0`, `M>=0` such that the coefficient norm of a sufficiently long translated asymptotic jet of the
whole tuple is at least `C S^(-M)`.

For an arbitrary sequence `S_n->infinity`, truncate every component with error `O_R(S_n^(-L))`,
where `L>M+1`. The truncated tuple lies in one finite-dimensional space

```text
V=span_C{z^m exp(k beta z):(m,k) in a finite set}.          (8)
```

Normalize by the maximum coefficient norm `rho_n`. Section 2 gives

```text
rho_n>=C S_n^(-M).                                         (9)
```

After a subsequence, compactness of the unit sphere in `V^r` gives a nonzero limit tuple

```text
H in C[z,exp(beta z),exp(-beta z)]^r\{0}.                  (10)
```

The normalized remainder is `O_R(S_n^(M-L))->0`. Restoring the common denominator as a meromorphic
functional normalization proves locally uniform functional-projective convergence to `H`.

## 4. Recession removes the polynomial jet

Write the limit tuple as a finite sum of `z^m exp(k beta z)`. Let `m_0` be its greatest polynomial
degree. Under `z|->z+iT`, division by `(iT)^m_0` leaves a nonzero Laurent polynomial tuple in
`exp(beta z)` after a subsequence on which every phase `exp(i k beta T)` converges. Therefore the
positive carrier chain is

```text
E(w,u)
  -> C[z,exp(beta z),exp(-beta z)]
  -> C[exp(beta z),exp(-beta z)].                          (11)
```

The terminal rational character rank is

```text
delta_beta=rank_Q(beta Z) in {0,1}.                        (12)
```

Polynomial jet degree can be unbounded across the field, but contributes zero to `(12)`.

## 5. Recurrent consequence

Let `f` be holomorphic and vertically Bohr uniformly almost periodic on a strip. If `f` satisfies an
ADE with coefficients in a subfield of `E(w,u)`, Section 3 turns it into an ADE over the finite
exponential-polynomial carrier. Section 4 removes the polynomial jet. The relative implicit-ADE
frequency theorem then gives

```text
rank_Q Gamma_B(f)<=delta_beta+2m(n+1),                     (13)
```

where `n` is the minimal limiting order and `m` is the size of a finite regular-sample atlas.

Thus differential-algebraic dependence positively equips the recurrent output with exactly two
resources: one compact recurrent character direction `beta Z` and a finite differential-state
carrier. Logarithmic phase affects jet depth but adds no terminal character.

## 6. Attacks and sharp boundary

1. **Phase algebraicity is removed first.** Any exact algebraic relation among proposed phase
   variables must be quotiented out before applying the theorem; otherwise the polynomial
   evaluation map is not injective.
2. **The translation direction is load-bearing.** The Vandermonde argument resolves only jets seen
   by the actual logarithmic derivative `U d/dU`. A phase constant under translation belongs in the
   coefficient field.
3. **Finite phase support is load-bearing.** Infinite Fourier support has no uniform finite
   Vandermonde matrix and can carry infinite compact rank.
4. **Quasianalyticity is load-bearing.** A flat coefficient field element could have an inverse of
   superpolynomial growth and defeat the finite-order lower bound.
5. **Arbitrary synchronization is included.** The phase points in `(2)` may approach the common-zero
   locus at any rate; the full finite coefficient-vector normalization captures the first surviving
   directional jet.
6. **Common zeros and poles are projective scalars.** Clearing the common denominator is functional;
   no fixed base-point normalization is assumed.
7. **Several recurrent exponential frequencies generalize finitely.** Replacing `beta Z` by a fixed
   finite-rank lattice gives the same proof and terminal rank equal to that lattice rank.

## 7. Parent and novelty audit

`[BRIDGE]` The proof combines complete Poincare asymptotics, a finite Vandermonde matrix, linear
independence of distinct exponentials, and compactness in a finite-dimensional coefficient space.
The functional-projective carrier formulation was not found in preliminary exact searches, but the
mechanism is a short consequence of these parents. It is therefore classified as a proved bridge,
not new scientific knowledge. Its role is to turn the previously conjectural multivariate
phase-ideal resolution into a finite directional-jet theorem.
