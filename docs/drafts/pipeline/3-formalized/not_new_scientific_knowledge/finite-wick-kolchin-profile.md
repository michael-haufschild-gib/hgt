The negative-control theorem assumes that all sectors outside the designated Gaussian family
vanish.  Erasing zero then makes the full and Gaussian finite jet packets equal, so their
transcendence ranks agree and the natural-number excess is zero.

For ambient-field invariance, an injective base-field algebra homomorphism preserves algebraic
independence.  The algebraic matroid in the target field therefore pulls back to the source
matroid, preserving the rank of every finite packet and hence the Wick-reduced difference.
When the packet is generated from actual iterated derivatives, the existing clock-covariance
theorem transports each jet through a derivation-preserving embedding.  The target packet is
therefore exactly the algebraic image of the source packet, so both finite differential ranks are
source-frame invariant.  Vanishing of a higher connected response also forces all its derivatives
to vanish, giving the differential Gaussian negative control.

The conditional formulation uses the rank of the union of a base packet and a signal packet minus
the base rank.  Matroid submodularity proves that any finite counterterm packet that is algebraic
over the base remains rank-neutral after adjoining an arbitrary signal.  Such a counterterm packet
therefore leaves the finite relative interaction rank unchanged.  When the Gaussian sector is
contained in the full sector window, this conditional rank equals the original Wick-reduced
difference exactly.

If every element of a finite signal packet lies in the ordinary algebra generated by a finite
face/base packet, the signal lies in the closure of that packet in the algebraic matroid.
Adjoining it leaves rank unchanged and its relative rank is zero.  This is the formal receiver for
the paper-level face-complete Gaussian Wick-closure theorem.
