## Statement

`[PROVED IN LEAN]` In the polynomial cutoff-germ model `ℚ[X]`, let the separable finite counterterms be the
submodule generated by `1` and `X`. The quadratic cutoff term `X^2` is not in that submodule, and its class
`ε₁ = [X^2]` in the quotient `ℚ[X] / span{1, X}` is nonzero.

`[PROVED IN LEAN]` More generally, for every cutoff order `n`, the exact term `X^n` is not in the span of
the lower terms `{X^k : k < n}`. Its class in the quotient `ℚ[X] / span{X^k : k < n}` is nonzero.

`[PROVED IN LEAN]` The coefficient-at-order functional descends through both quotients. The quadratic
detector on `ℚ[X] / span{1, X}` evaluates `ε₁` to `1`, and the exact-order detector on
`ℚ[X] / span{X^k : k < n}` evaluates the order-`n` class to `1`.

`[PROVED IN LEAN]` The detector is scheme-invariant under allowed counterterm changes: if two
representatives differ by separable counterterms, their quadratic coefficients agree; if they differ by
lower-order terms, their exact-order coefficients agree. More generally, the class of `a X^n` is detected
as `a` and is nonzero when `a ≠ 0`.

`[PROVED IN LEAN]` The visible half-quadratic Stieltjes seam term has class
`[1/2 · X^2] ∈ ℚ[X] / span{1, X}`. The quadratic detector evaluates this class to `1/2`, hence the class is
nonzero.

## Research reading

This is the formal toy obstruction extracted from the failed `γ_1 = B_1·γ + C_1` split. It records the
order mismatch behind the seam residue: constants and order-1 cutoff data cannot absorb an order-2 log
counterterm. The hierarchy upgrade records the general principle: no exact cutoff order is killed by any
combination of lower-order counterterms. The detector upgrade records the invariant explicitly: the top
coefficient is well-defined modulo all lower counterterms and reads each obstruction class by its leading
coefficient. This makes the toy quotient match the visible `1/2 · (log P)^2` coefficient in the
`γ_1` assembly instead of only a normalized generator. It does **not** prove a Stieltjes transcendence claim,
a PNT asymptotic, or DSS. It formalizes the quotient classes and detectors that a future analytic construction
must realize functorially in the completed `(σ_p, ∂_s)` object.
