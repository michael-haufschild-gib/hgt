## Theorem

Fix an integer `p>1`, a phase `theta`, and `0<=epsilon<1/2`. If for every `m>=1`

```text
|1-(p^(-1/2+epsilon))^m exp(i m theta)|
  = |1-(p^(-1/2-epsilon))^m exp(i m theta)|,
```

then `epsilon=0`.

No prime-distribution hypothesis is used. In particular, the conclusion does not require a
short prime interval, the prime number theorem, or comparison between distinct primes.

The formal theorem is stronger than exact neutrality. Define the normalized squared-modulus
defect by dividing the reflected difference by

```text
2 * ((p^(-1/2+epsilon))^m - (p^(-1/2-epsilon))^m).
```

Pointwise decay is unnecessary. Writing the normalized defect as `D_m`, a positive displacement has
the exact Cesaro mean-square law

```text
(1/N) * sum_{m<N} D_m^2 -> 1/2,
```

except at the resonant phases `exp(2 i theta)=1`, where the limit is `1`. Consequently it is enough
to prove any eventual uniform energy bound strictly below `1/2`; vanishing is unnecessary:

```text
there exists c<1/2 such that eventually
(1/N) * sum_{m<N} D_m^2 <= c.
```

This quantitative gap is the form naturally produced by a Gram, trace, or spectral-energy
construction.

## Proof mechanism

If `epsilon>0`, squaring the modulus equality gives, for every `m>=1`,

```text
cos(m theta) = (r₊^m+r₋^m)/2,
r₊=p^(-1/2+epsilon),  r₋=p^(-1/2-epsilon).
```

Both radii lie strictly between zero and one, so the right side tends to zero. Rotation recurrence
on the circle supplies a positive constant `c` and cofinally many `m` with
`cos(m theta)>=c`. This is a contradiction.

For the mean-square theorem, when `epsilon>0` one has

```text
D_m = (r₊^m+r₋^m)/2 - cos(m theta).
```

The first term tends to zero. The Cesaro mean of `cos(m theta)^2` is `1/2` unless
`exp(2 i theta)=1`, in which case it is `1`. Hence the mean-square energy of `D_m` cannot tend to
zero.
