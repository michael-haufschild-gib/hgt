# Exact RG-clock hypertranscendence is equivalent to exact-beta hypertranscendence

- **Statement:** For any analytically linearizable one-coupling hyperbolic RG germ, the exact beta

## Setup

Let `f` be an analytic RG germ at a hyperbolic fixed point `c`, with multiplier `lambda`, and let

```text
f(Phi(z))=Phi(lambda z),              kappa=Phi^(-1),
beta(t)=(log lambda) kappa(t)/kappa'(t).                          (1)
```

Choose compatible logarithm branches on a punctured simply connected neighbourhood of `c`.  Define
the exact RG clock

```text
tau(t)=log(kappa(t))/log(lambda).                                 (2)
```

Then

```text
tau(f(t))=tau(t)+1,                                               (3)
tau'(t)=1/beta(t).                                                (4)
```

Equation `(3)` says that `tau` is not merely a coordinate: it is the exact calibration assigning one
unit of elapsed scale to one physical block transformation.

## Clock-beta equivalence

The following are equivalent.

1. `Phi` is differentially algebraic.
2. `kappa` is differentially algebraic.
3. `beta` is differentially algebraic.
4. `tau` is differentially algebraic on the punctured germ.

### Proof

Local compositional inversion gives `(1) <=> (2)`.  The relation for `beta` in `(1)` gives
`(2) => (3)`, while

```text
beta kappa'-(log lambda)kappa=0                                  (5)
```

and transitivity of differential algebraicity give `(3) => (2)`.

If `kappa` is DA, then `kappa'/kappa` is DA, so `(2)` makes `tau'` DA.  A primitive of a DA germ is
DA, proving `(2) => (4)`.  Conversely, if `tau` is DA, then `kappa` satisfies

```text
kappa'=(log lambda)tau' kappa,                                   (6)
```

so it is DA over the differential field generated by `tau`, hence over `C(t)`.  This proves
`(4) => (2)`.  Equation `(4)` also gives the direct equivalence `(3) <=> (4)`.

Therefore

```text
beta is HT  <=>  tau is HT.                                     (7)
```

## Scale observables

For any nonzero constant `a`, define the canonical multiplicative scale calibration

```text
L_a(t)=exp(a tau(t)).                                             (8)
```

Then `L_a(f(t))=exp(a)L_a(t)`.  The same first-order differential-field argument gives

```text
L_a is DA  <=>  tau is DA  <=>  beta is DA.                      (9)
```

Indeed `tau` DA makes `L_a` solve `L_a'=a tau' L_a`; conversely `L_a` DA makes
`tau'=L_a'/(a L_a)` DA, and hence `tau` DA.

If one RG step rescales physical length by `ell>1`, the canonical length clock is

```text
Xi(t)=ell^(-tau(t))=kappa(t)^(-log(ell)/log(lambda)).             (10)
```

Thus exact-beta HT is exactly HT of the canonical coupling-to-length calibration.  An actual
correlation length, mass gap, or response may contain an additional log-periodic or irrelevant-field
amplitude.  Equation `(10)` transfers to that observable only after its amplitude is proved DA and
noncancelling; no such claim is smuggled into the theorem.

## DA-scheme theorem

For a locally invertible DA scheme `u=h(t)` with DA inverse,

```text
tau_h(u)=tau(h^(-1)(u)),
beta_h(h(t))=h'(t)beta(t).                                       (11)
```

DA closure under composition and inversion proves that the DA/HT status of both clock and beta is
unchanged.  An arbitrary analytic scheme can take `u=kappa(t)` and make the clock logarithmic and
the beta linear, but in the HT case that scheme itself is HT.
