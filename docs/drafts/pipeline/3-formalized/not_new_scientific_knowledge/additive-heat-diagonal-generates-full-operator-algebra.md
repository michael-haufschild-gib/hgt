# One additive heat aperture and the particle diagonal generate all bounded operators

- **ID:** `additive-heat-diagonal-generates-full-operator-algebra`
- **Stage:** 3-formalized
- **Type:** THEOREM (paper); joint-algebra no-go
- **Scientific status:** `[BRIDGE; NOT NEW SCIENTIFIC KNOWLEDGE]`
- **Source:** the additive heat aperture and the prime-occupation diagonal on `l2(N)`
- **Statement:** Let `D=l-infinity(N)` act diagonally on `l2(N)`, and fix `x>0`.  If
  `H_x(m,n)=exp(-pi x(m-n)^2)`, then `W*(D,H_x)=B(l2(N))`.  Hence adjoining the heat aperture to the
  natural particle diagonal destroys every nontrivial normal superselection center.

## Proof

Let `P_n in D` be the rank-one projection onto the standard basis vector `e_n`.  For every
`m,n>=1`,

```text
P_m H_x P_n
  = exp(-pi x(m-n)^2) E_(mn),                           (1)
```

where `E_(mn)` is the matrix unit sending `e_n` to `e_m`.  The scalar in (1) is strictly nonzero.
Therefore every matrix unit belongs to `W*(D,H_x)`.

Finite linear combinations of matrix units are the finite-rank operators, and their strong-operator
closure is `B(l2(N))`.  Since `W*(D,H_x)` is strongly closed,

```text
W*(D,H_x)=B(l2(N)).                                    (2)
```

QED.

## Consequences for the screen architecture

The particle diagonal is generated by the joint prime-occupation projections because unique
factorization recovers the basis label `n` from all valuations `v_p(n)`.  Equation (2) therefore
shows that the most direct joint particle-plus-heat algebra is a factor with scalar center.

The product-Poisson screen is represented on a different phase/Bohr algebra, so (2) is not a formal
contradiction of that theorem.  It does prove that the heat aperture is not an ordinary
sector-preserving observable which can simply be adjoined while keeping the old sector
decomposition.  Any compatibility must be realized as a boundary relation, bimodule,
correspondence, relative index, or another explicitly controlled singular object.

This supplies an independent reason why heat-echo-to-sector descent fails: the detector is
maximally sector-mixing in the natural normal particle representation.

## Lean formalization — 2026-08-17

Two modules, both unconditional (`#print axioms` = `[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]`):

- `formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/DiagonalCommutantGeneration.lean`
- `formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/GaussianToeplitzGeneration.lean`

The Lean route replaces the strong-operator closure step by the definition of the generated von
Neumann algebra as a double commutant, `vonNeumannGen S = Set.centralizer (Set.centralizer S)`.
Under that definition the theorem is equivalent to the triviality of the commutant, which the paper
proof already establishes matrix-entry by matrix-entry, and no density argument is needed.

| Paper step | Lean name |
|---|---|
| `P_n` is the rank-one projection onto `e_n` | `DiagonalCommutant.basisProj`, `basisProj_apply` |
| `P_n in D` | `DiagonalCommutant.basisProj_mem_diagonalOps`, `GaussianToeplitz.basisProj_mem_multiplierAlgebra` |
| `T` commutes with every `P_n` implies `T` is diagonal | `DiagonalCommutant.exists_diag_of_mem_centralizer` |
| (1): `P_m H_x P_n` has the nonzero scalar `exp(-pi x(m-n)^2)`, forcing the diagonal of `T` constant | `DiagonalCommutant.eq_scalar_of_mem_centralizer'` |
| (2): `W*(D,H_x)=B(l2(N))` | `DiagonalCommutant.vonNeumannGen_eq_univ'`, `GaussianToeplitz.vonNeumannGen_multiplierAlgebra_gaussToeplitz` |
| `D=l-infinity(N)` acting diagonally | `GaussianToeplitz.diagMul`, `multiplierAlgebra` |
| `H_x(m,n)=exp(-pi x(m-n)^2)` | `GaussianToeplitz.gaussToeplitz`, `inner_stdBasis_gaussToeplitz` |
| the scalar in (1) is strictly nonzero | `GaussianToeplitz.inner_stdBasis_gaussToeplitz_ne_zero` |

`H_x` is built as the norm-convergent series
`sum_j k_j (B*)^j + sum_j k_(j+1) B^(j+1)` in the backward shift `B` on `l2(N)`, with
`k_j = exp(-pi x j^2)`; summability follows from `j <= j^2` and the geometric comparison
`exp(-pi x j^2) <= exp(-pi x)^j` (`GaussianToeplitz.summable_norm_gaussKernel`).  Its entries are
computed in `inner_stdBasis_gaussToeplitz` by evaluating each series against basis vectors
(`tsum_inner_apply`) and collapsing the resulting single-support sums.

Not formalized, and not an obligation of this card: the identification of the diagonal algebra with
the joint prime-occupation projections, and the "consequences for the screen architecture" section,
which are commentary on other cards rather than statements of this theorem.
