## Claim

Put one local determinant factor in an `n`-direction Koszul grading.  In exterior degree `k`, a
fixed direction occurs with complementary multiplicity `choose(n-1,k)`.  Its exponent in the
alternating determinant is therefore

```text
e_n = sum_(k=0)^(n-1) (-1)^k choose(n-1,k).
```

Hence

```text
e_1 = 1,
e_n = (1-1)^(n-1) = 0                    for n >= 2.
```

The one-prime complex retains its Euler factor.  Every multi-prime absolute Koszul
superdeterminant cancels each pure local determinant as well as the mixed graded terms.

## Proof

The complementary copies of a fixed direction form the full exterior algebra on the other
`n-1` directions.  The alternating multiplicity is its Euler characteristic, the displayed
binomial sum.  The binomial theorem gives one in dimension zero and zero in every positive
dimension.  Lean proves both cases as

```text
koszulComplementSuperExponent_one
koszulComplementSuperExponent_eq_zero
```

using the already-formalized finite Koszul Euler-characteristic identity.

## Consequence for the large detour

The following proposed chain is closed in its absolute form:

```text
exact prime Fredholm factors
  -> connected multi-prime Koszul complex
  -> alternating determinant cancels mixed primitive words
  -> Euler product survives
  -> completed self-adjoint determinant
  -> RH.
```

The third arrow is false.  The same Koszul grading that cancels mixed words assigns exponent zero
to every pure prime factor once a second direction is present.  Its absolute superdeterminant is
therefore blind to the Euler product.

## Positive successor

A graded prime construction can retain the Euler product only by breaking the zero Euler
characteristic of the absolute complementary Koszul grading.  The minimum admissible repair is a
source-defined **relative or augmented boundary complex** whose quotient contribution has nonzero
Euler characteristic.  It must satisfy all of the following:

1. its absolute bulk still cancels every mixed prime word;
2. its relative boundary retains each pure factor with exponent one;
3. the boundary is forced by the Euler--Gamma/theta source, not by the zeta divisor;
4. the completed relative determinant is closed and carries a positive self-duality.

The combinatorial part of this successor is now explicit and formalized.  Mark one direction in
the support of a word and alternate over the complementary support.  For support size `n` the
resulting exponent is

```text
n * sum_(k=0)^(n-1) (-1)^k choose(n-1,k) = 1_[n=1].
```

Thus the marked-support incidence weight retains every pure prime repetition and cancels every
mixed-prime word.  Lean proves this as `koszulSupportMarkedExponent_eq`.  Equivalently, on the
fermionic exterior space of the active support, insertion of the exterior-degree number operator
has supertrace `-1` in support size one and `0` in every other support size.

This solves the finite combinatorial selector but not the analytic boundary theorem.  As a scalar
word functional it annihilates the two-sided ideal generated by mixed-color words, so it factors
through the direct sum of the one-prime axes.  Any nontrivial global information must therefore
survive in the marked complex or its infinite-prime completion defect before scalar supertrace is
taken.  The refined open core is to realize that defect as a source-defined closed relative
determinant and prove a positive reflected duality on its cohomology.

The two-sided-ideal statement is formalized directly.  `IsMixedPrimeWord.append_left`,
`IsMixedPrimeWord.append_right`, and `IsMixedPrimeWord.sandwich` prove that arbitrary word context
cannot remove a mixed support.  Consequently
`primeWordSupportSelector_sandwich_eq_zero` proves that the scalar selector remains zero throughout
the full two-sided ideal.  Conversely, `primeWordSupportSelector_eq_one_iff` identifies its entire
nonzero word locus with singleton supports.

This identifies the additive/archimedean boundary as algebraically necessary, not optional.  It
also explains why the unaugmented derived-limit proposal has no determinant content and why a
block-diagonal Fredholm realization, although exact in `Re(s)>1`, supplies no RH selector.
