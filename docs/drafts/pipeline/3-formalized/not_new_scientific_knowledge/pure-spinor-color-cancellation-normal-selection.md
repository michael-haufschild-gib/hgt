## Statement

Let
\[
W=A\oplus B,\qquad \dim_{\mathbb C}A=3,\qquad \dim_{\mathbb C}B=2,
\]
and let
\[
X=\operatorname{OGr}_{+}(5,10)\subset\mathbb P(S^+)
\]
be the spinor tenfold at the pure spinor \(p=1\). In the even-form chart,
\[
T_pX\cong\Lambda^2W^*
=C\oplus M\oplus L,
\]
where
\[
C=\Lambda^2A^*,\qquad
M=A^*\otimes B^*,\qquad
L=\Lambda^2B^*.
\]
The projective normal fiber is
\[
N_pX\cong\Lambda^4W^*\cong A\oplus B.
\]

Let
\[
\mathrm{II}:\operatorname{Sym}^2(T_pX)\longrightarrow N_pX
\]
be the quadratic second fundamental form given by polarization of the Cartan pure-spinor
quadrics, and let
\(\mathrm{II}_B\) denote its projection to the colour-preserving normal doublet \(B\).
Then:

1. \(\mathrm{II}_B\) vanishes on
   \[
   C\times C,\qquad M\times M,\qquad L\times T_pX.
   \]
2. The only sector capable of producing a \(B\)-normal is the cross-sector
   \[
   C\times M\quad\text{or}\quad M\times C.
   \]
3. The induced bilinear pairing
   \[
   \beta:C\times M\longrightarrow B
   \]
   is nondegenerate in the \(C\)-slot in the following strong sense: for every
   \(0\ne c\in C\), the linear map
   \[
   \beta(c,-):M\longrightarrow B
   \]
   is surjective.

Thus every colourless normal doublet can be generated by pairing a nonzero colour bivector with a
mixed colour--weak tangent, while no same-sector quadratic tangent pair can generate it.

## Proof

The Cartan quadrics identify the second fundamental form at \(p=1\) with polarization of the
wedge product
\[
\Lambda^2W^*\times\Lambda^2W^*
\longrightarrow\Lambda^4W^*,
\qquad
(\alpha,\gamma)\longmapsto\alpha\wedge\gamma.
\tag{2}
\]
The \(3+2\) decomposition gives
\[
\Lambda^4(A^*\oplus B^*)
\cong
\left(\Lambda^3A^*\otimes B^*\right)
\oplus
\left(\Lambda^2A^*\otimes\Lambda^2B^*\right).
\tag{3}
\]
After fixing the total volume, the first summand in (3) is the \(B\)-normal and the second is the
\(A\)-normal.

Now inspect exterior degrees.

- \(C\wedge C\) would have \(A\)-degree four and therefore vanishes because
  \(\dim_{\mathbb C}A=3\).
- \(M\wedge M\) has bidegree \((2,2)\), so it lies in the second summand of (3) and contributes
  only to the \(A\)-normal.
- Every nonzero wedge involving \(L=\Lambda^2B^*\) also has \(B\)-degree two, so it contributes
  only to the \(A\)-normal.
- \(C\wedge M\) has bidegree \((3,1)\), exactly the first summand of (3), and therefore is the only
  source of the \(B\)-normal.

This proves parts 1 and 2.

Expanding \(c\wedge m\) in the chosen basis gives (1). For part 3, use the
three-dimensional volume to identify
\[
\Lambda^2A^*\cong A.
\]
A nonzero \(c\) corresponds to a nonzero vector \(v_c\in A\). Under this identification,
\(\beta(c,m)\) is, up to the fixed sign convention, evaluation of the
\(B^*\)-valued covector \(m\in A^*\otimes B^*\) on \(v_c\):
\[
\beta(c,m)=m(v_c).
\tag{4}
\]
Given any \(b\in B^*\), choose \(\ell\in A^*\) with \(\ell(v_c)=1\) and put
\[
m=\ell\otimes b.
\]
Then \(\beta(c,m)=b\). Hence \(\beta(c,-)\) is surjective for every \(c\ne0\). \(\square\)
