## Statement

Let the intrinsic arithmetic clock on coefficient modes be

```text
H |n> = log(n) |n>.
```

The all-ones zeta summation source `Omega=(1,1,...)` belongs to no finite negative Sobolev space
generated by `H`. Equivalently, for every finite integer `k>=0`,

```text
sum_(n>=2) 1/(log n)^k = infinity.                    (1)
```

Therefore no finite-order local equation in the clock generator can make the zeta pairing normal.
The first diagonal regularisations strong enough to do so are exponential in `H`, but

```text
exp(-a H)|n> = n^(-a)|n>                              (2)
```

is exactly the scale semigroup that shifts `s` to `s+a`. Thus a clock-based repair either fails at
every finite differential order or smuggles the forbidden scaling flow back into the constraint.

## Proof

For a finite Sobolev order `r`, the clock Sobolev norm has the form

```text
||x||_(H^r)^2
  = sum_n (1+log n)^(2r) |x_n|^2.                    (3)
```

The squared dual norm of the summation covector would require

```text
sum_n (1+log n)^(-2r) < infinity.                    (4)
```

But every fixed power of `log n` grows more slowly than `n`. More precisely, for every natural
`k`, eventually

```text
(log n)^k <= n.
```

Hence eventually

```text
1/(log n)^k >= 1/n,
```

and `(1)` follows from divergence of the harmonic series. Real orders reduce to a larger natural
power on the tail where `log n>=1`, so `(4)` fails for every finite real `r`.

`Real.isLittleO_pow_log_id_atTop` and comparison with the harmonic series.

## Constraint-versus-flow dichotomy

A polynomial or finite pseudodifferential expression in `H` changes coefficient weights only by
powers of `log n`; `(1)` shows that this never turns `Omega` into a finite-energy distribution.

An exponential expression does:

```text
Omega exp(-aH) Psi_s
  = sum_n n^(-(s+a)),                                 (5)
```

which is normal once `Re(s)+a>1`. But `(5)` is not a new constraint mechanism. It is imaginary-time
evolution by the scale Hamiltonian, equivalently translation along the real `s` direction. It
returns the problem to the absolutely convergent half-plane and must then analytically continue
back across the same unresolved boundary.

So finite-order Wheeler--DeWitt locality cannot contain the zeta source, while analytic smoothing
is a flow in disguise.

## Superspace meaning

Relative to the intrinsic clock spectrum `{log n}`, the zeta summation source is an
**ultradistribution**, not a finite-order distribution. A viable quantum-gravity formulation must
therefore use at least one genuinely nonlocal ingredient:

1. an analytic/Gevrey rigging rather than a Sobolev rigging;
2. a singular boundary functional not represented by a Hilbert vector;
3. a quotient or cohomological constraint imposed before completion; or
4. a nonlocal prime--archimedean coupling that is not a function of `H` alone.

## Novelty verdict

The physical consequence is a sharp route gate, but the mathematical proof is a standard
logarithm-versus-power comparison. It is therefore not new scientific knowledge. Its value is to
exclude every finite-clock-order superspace model at once and to identify exactly why exponential
smoothing violates the constraint-not-flow requirement.
