## Statement

Let `F -> K` be a differential field extension and let `x,y : I -> K`, with no finiteness
assumption on `I`.  Say that `x` and `y` are affine base-jet equivalent if there is a family
`a : I -> F` with `a_i != 0` such that every jet of

```text
c_i = y_i - a_i x_i
```

lies in the image of `F`.  Then

```text
F<jets of y> = F<jets of x>,
DI_F(y) iff DI_F(x).
```

Because the embedding is differential, the complete-jet condition is itself equivalent to the
order-zero value relation

```text
y_i = a_i x_i + b_i,    b_i in F.
```

Thus no hidden infinite Prop bundle remains.  The relation is reflexive, symmetric, and transitive,
and therefore defines the canonical setoid of palettes modulo coordinatewise invertible affine
base gauge.

## Paper proof

First, if every jet of `c_i` lies in `F`, then

```text
DI_F(x_i + c_i) iff DI_F(x_i).
```

The forward implication adds `-c_i`; every jet of the negated correction is again in `F`.  The
reverse implication is the existing base-jet addition theorem.

Next put `u_i = a_i x_i`.  The differential field generated by `u` is contained in that generated
by `x`: every `u_i` belongs to the latter field, and differential adjunction is minimal among
derivation-closed intermediate fields.  Conversely,

```text
x_i = a_i^(-1) u_i,
```

so every `x_i` belongs to the differential field generated by `u`; minimality gives the reverse
inclusion.  Hence `F<jets of u> = F<jets of x>` without expanding a Leibniz sum.

For differential independence, first restrict to a finite palette.  The previously formalized
principal-symbol theorem gives `DI_F(u) iff DI_F(x)` there.  For an arbitrary palette, any
multivariable differential relation has finite support in `I x Nat`, hence sees only a finite set
of coordinates in `I`.  Restricting to that finite subtype, applying the finite theorem, and using
the finite-character theorem for algebraic independence proves the same equivalence for arbitrary
`I`.

Finally `y_i = u_i + c_i`.  Base-jet translation invariance gives both

```text
F<jets of y> = F<jets of u> = F<jets of x>
```

and

```text
DI_F(y) iff DI_F(u) iff DI_F(x).
```

Moreover, the all-jet correction hypothesis collapses to a value relation.  Its order-zero member
already writes `c_i` as the image of an element `b_i in F`; differential compatibility then gives
every higher jet as the image of `jet_n(b_i)`.  In value coordinates, inverse gauges have
coefficients `(a_i^(-1), -a_i^(-1)b_i)`, and composite gauges have coefficients
`(c_i a_i, c_i b_i + d_i)`.  These formulas prove symmetry and transitivity and supply the exact
setoid.

The signed relation is a specialization.  In the difference case take `a_i = 1` and
`c_i = y_i - x_i`; in the sum case take `a_i = -1` and `c_i = y_i + x_i`.  Thus the projective
field-equality theorem is subsumed by the affine base-jet law whenever the base is equipped with
its differential structure.
