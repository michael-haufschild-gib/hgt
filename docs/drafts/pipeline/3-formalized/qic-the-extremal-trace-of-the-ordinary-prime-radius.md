# A bounded ordinary-prime radius forces one extremal conjugate pair

- **Stage:** 3-formalized
- **Source:** [QIC](../../../reports/rh-current-frontier-map.md#qic)

Let Θ > ½ be the supremum of real parts of nontrivial zeros. For nonzero trial frequency γ and positive trial multiplicity m, if the ordinary-prime two-interval radius at exponent Θ agrees with the trial amplitude up to O(√x log²x), the line Re s = Θ contains exactly one conjugate pair of distinct zeros. Multiplicities are unrestricted, as are all zeros below Θ. [Lean statement and axiom audit](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExceptionalLayerTheory/ABoundedQuartetRadiusHasAFiniteExtremalLayer.lean#L316)

**Circle identity.** Let F be the prime-error boundary trace, h the trial quarter-period, A = exp(Θh), and D(t) = AF(t+h)−F(t). The normalized ordinary-prime radius is the norm of (D(t),D(t+h)) up to o(1), since ψ(x)−θ(x) = O(√x). Almost periodicity turns convergence of this norm to C into the identity D(t)²+D(t+h)²=C². If Θ is unattained, F vanishes, contradicting C>0. [Boundary trace](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExceptionalLayerTheory/TheBoundaryTraceOfTheTwoIntervalPrimeRadius.lean)

**Phase separation.** At any positive ordinate difference d with exp(idh)=1, the circle identity forces the Bohr coefficient of D² at d to vanish. Each pair (u+d,u) contributes |A exp(iuh)−1|² divided by Θ²+u(u+d)+iΘd. Its imaginary part is strictly negative. Absolute convergence forbids any such pair. Thus every phase exp(iωh) identifies at most one ordinate, and each doubled-step phase has at most two ordinates and finitely many multiplicity copies. [Phase separation](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExceptionalLayerTheory/DistinctExtremalOrdinatesHaveDistinctQuarterStepPhases.lean)

**One pair.** Set p=2h. The circle identity makes |D| p-periodic. Choose an interval with |D|>δ and a multiple np that is a uniform δ-almost period of D. D(t+np)−D(t) vanishes on every p-translate of the interval. Discrete Cesàro averaging isolates each phase component there. Each is a finite trigonometric polynomial, so it vanishes everywhere. Reassembling gives an exact period np. Every extremal zero has nonzero Bohr coefficient, so its p-phase is an nth root of unity. Finite phase fibres now make D analytic. From D(t+p)²=D(t)², analytic continuation gives one global sign under translation by p. All extremal ordinates thus have the same doubled-step phase. Phase separation leaves at most two ordinates, which conjugation and the absence of real zeros identify as one pair.
