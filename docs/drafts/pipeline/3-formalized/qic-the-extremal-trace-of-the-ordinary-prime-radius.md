# A bounded ordinary-prime radius forces exactly one right conjugate pair

- **Stage:** 3-formalized
- **Source:** [QIC](../../../reports/rh-current-frontier-map.md#qic)

Let Θ>½ be the supremum of real parts of nontrivial zeros. For nonzero trial frequency γ and positive multiplicity m, suppose the ordinary-prime two-interval radius at exponent Θ agrees with the trial amplitude up to O(√x log²x). Then exactly one conjugate pair lies in Re s>½, and it lies on Re s=Θ. Reflection gives the left pair. This forces finiteness of all off-line multiplicity copies without an initial finiteness assumption. [Extremal pair](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExceptionalLayerTheory/ABoundedQuartetRadiusHasAFiniteExtremalLayer.lean#L316) · [Exclusion below Θ and finiteness](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExceptionalLayerTheory/WeightedPrimeIncrementsExcludeEveryLowerRightZero.lean#L260)

**Circle identity.** Let F be the prime-error boundary trace, h the trial quarter-period, A = exp(Θh), and D(t) = AF(t+h)−F(t). The normalized ordinary-prime radius is the norm of (D(t),D(t+h)) up to o(1), since ψ(x)−θ(x) = O(√x). Almost periodicity turns convergence of this norm to C into the identity D(t)²+D(t+h)²=C². If Θ is unattained, F vanishes, contradicting C>0. [Boundary trace](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExceptionalLayerTheory/TheBoundaryTraceOfTheTwoIntervalPrimeRadius.lean)

**Phase separation.** At any positive ordinate difference d with exp(idh)=1, the circle identity forces the Bohr coefficient of D² at d to vanish. Each pair (u+d,u) contributes |A exp(iuh)−1|² divided by Θ²+u(u+d)+iΘd. Its imaginary part is strictly negative. Absolute convergence forbids any such pair. Thus every phase exp(iωh) identifies at most one ordinate, and each doubled-step phase has at most two ordinates and finitely many multiplicity copies. [Phase separation](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExceptionalLayerTheory/DistinctExtremalOrdinatesHaveDistinctQuarterStepPhases.lean)

**One pair.** Set p=2h. The circle identity makes |D| p-periodic. Choose an interval with |D|>δ and a multiple np that is a uniform δ-almost period of D. D(t+np)−D(t) vanishes on every p-translate of the interval. Discrete Cesàro averaging isolates each phase component there. Each is a finite trigonometric polynomial, so it vanishes everywhere. Reassembling gives an exact period np. Every extremal zero has nonzero Bohr coefficient, so its p-phase is an nth root of unity. Finite phase fibres now make D analytic. From D(t+p)²=D(t)², analytic continuation gives one global sign under translation by p. All extremal ordinates thus have the same doubled-step phase. Phase separation leaves at most two ordinates, which conjugation and the absence of real zeros identify as one pair. [Almost periods and nonzero coefficients](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExceptionalLayerTheory/IncommensurableTopOrdinatesExcludeTheQuartetRadius.lean) · [Finiteness and single-pair proof](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExceptionalLayerTheory/ABoundedQuartetRadiusHasAFiniteExtremalLayer.lean)

**Squared increment.** For U(t)=exp(−Θt) Δₕ(θ(exp t)−exp t), the same bounded trial gives |U(t)²−D(t)²|≪exp((½−Θ)t)(1+t)² uniformly, including at nodes of D. Set g=U²−D². The squared radius controls g(t)+g(t+h). Subtract the next translate and sum the resulting difference along t+2nh. The error envelope contracts geometrically there, and g tends to zero. [Squared-increment theorem](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExceptionalLayerTheory/TheTwoIntervalRadiusControlsEachSquaredPrimeIncrement.lean)

**Signed increment.** For every ½<σ<Θ, both (U−D)² and |D(U−D)| are O(exp(−(Θ−σ)t)), including at zeros of D. Averaging θ over [x,x+1] costs at most log(x+1) and yields a continuous increment. At each sufficiently late crest it has the sign of D. If that sign reverses between a point and its nearest crest, the squared-amplitude bound at the crossing controls D² at the initial point. Polarization gives the product estimate. [Signed-increment theorem](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExceptionalLayerTheory/TheOrdinaryPrimeIncrementAcrossAnExtremalWave.lean)

**Weighted mass.** For every s>½ the raw signed increment error exp(Θt)(U−D) is absolutely integrable against exp(−st) on a tail. Write X=|U−D|. For ½<τ<min(Θ,s) the signed estimate gives X², |D|X ≤ A(t)=C exp((τ−Θ)t). For 0<δ<1, interpolation gives X≤A(t)ᵃ|D|⁻δ off the nodes, with a=(1+δ)/2. Choose δ close enough to 1 that Θ−a(Θ−τ)<s. The negative power of the sinusoid is integrable over a period, and the weighted integrals over successive periods form a convergent geometric series. [Weighted-increment theorem](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExceptionalLayerTheory/TheWeightedMassOfTheSignedPrimeIncrement.lean)

**Descent.** For the top-copy set S put E(t)=θ(exp t)−exp t+Re W_S(exp t). Its h-step is the raw signed increment error. Integrating |E(t+h)|≤|E(t)|+|ΔₕE(t)| against exp(−st) absorbs the translated mass with factor exp(−sh)<1. Since θ≤ψ, this bounds −x∫ₓ^∞ (ψ(y)−y+Re W_S(y))dy/y² above by O(xˢ) for ½<s<1. The subtracted-lag identity then puts each remaining right zero to the left of every s>½. [Descent proof](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExceptionalLayerTheory/WeightedPrimeIncrementsExcludeEveryLowerRightZero.lean) · [Lag converse](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/RHTower/FiniteOffLineConverse.lean#L194)

**All trial parameters.** For ½<β<1, γ>0 and m≥1, bounded discrepancy pins β=Θ. Finiteness now permits the finite-divisor amplitude theorem. The trial is bounded exactly when ζ has one off-line quartet at β+iω of multiplicity m′, where ω=(2k+1)γ for some k≥0 and m′²(β²+γ²)=m²(β²+ω²). A quartet always passes at its own frequency and multiplicity. Thus the global radial exclusion law is equivalent to absence of a lone quartet. [Classification and axiom audit](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExceptionalLayerTheory/TheOrdinaryPrimeRadiusDetectsExactlyOneOffLineQuartet.lean#L37)
