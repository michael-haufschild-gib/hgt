## Universal law

Let `I` be any nonempty finite spectral label set, let

\[
 p_i\geq0,\qquad \sum_i p_i=1,
\]

and let `E_i` be arbitrary real energies. Define survival fidelity

\[
 F(t)=\left|\sum_i p_i e^{-itE_i}\right|^2.         \tag{1}
\]

At replica order `r`, multiplicity vectors `a,b:I->N` satisfy
`sum_i a_i=sum_i b_i=r`. Put

\[
 \Delta_{a,b}=\sum_i(a_i-b_i)E_i,                   \tag{2}
\]

and let `w_(a,b)>=0` be the exact multinomial weight generated by expanding
`F(t)^r`. The normalized symmetric-window observer is

\[
 \overline F_r(T)=\frac1{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}F(t)^r\,dt.
                                                               \tag{3}
\]

Then, for every finite spectrum, every replica order, and every `T>0`,

\[
 \boxed{
 \overline F_r(T)
 =C_r(E,p)+R_r(T;E,p),
 }
                                                               \tag{4}
\]

where

\[
 C_r(E,p)=\sum_{\Delta_{a,b}=0}w_{a,b},\qquad
 R_r(T;E,p)=\sum_{\Delta_{a,b}\ne0}
 w_{a,b}\,\operatorname{sinc}(T\Delta_{a,b}).       \tag{5}
\]

Thus `T Delta_(a,b)` is the exact dimensionless observer--spectral action:
zero action is completed recurrence mass and every nonzero action is the
entire finite-window remainder.

## Intrinsic resolution and dimension-free bound

Let

\[
 \delta_r(E)=
 \min\left(
 \{1\}\cup
 \{|\Delta_{a,b}|:\Delta_{a,b}\ne0\}
 \right).                                           \tag{6}
\]

The set is finite and contains `1`; every member is positive. Therefore

\[
 \delta_r(E)>0                                      \tag{7}
\]

for every finite spectrum, without a nonresonance premise. Since all
multinomial weights are nonnegative and sum to one,

\[
 \boxed{
 |\overline F_r(T)-C_r(E,p)|
 \leq\min\{1,(T\delta_r(E))^{-1}\}.
 }                                                   \tag{8}
\]

The constant in `(8)` is exactly one, independent of Hilbert-space
dimension, replica order, degeneracy and number of nonzero characters.
Consequently

\[
 \lim_{T\to\infty}\overline F_r(T)=C_r(E,p)          \tag{9}
\]

for every finite spectrum and every fixed replica order.

## Exact plateau classification

If every `p_i>0`, the diagonal multiplicity pairs `a=b` give the unavoidable
permutation floor

\[
 B_r(p)=\sum_a w_{a,a}.                              \tag{10}
\]

Every nontrivial zero-action pair contributes a strictly positive excess:

\[
 C_r(E,p)=B_r(p)+X_r(E,p),\qquad X_r(E,p)\geq0.      \tag{11}
\]

Moreover,

\[
 \boxed{
 X_r(E,p)=0\text{ for every }r
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \sum_i c_i=0,\ \sum_i c_iE_i=0
 \Rightarrow c=0.
 }                                                   \tag{12}
\]

Hence maximal projective rational spectral rank is exactly the condition
that every observer-resolved return moment reaches its smallest possible
completed plateau. Resonance never lowers a plateau; it raises at least one
replica moment by a positive amount.

## Proof

Expand `(1)^r` by the multinomial theorem. Each ordered multiplicity pair
contributes

\[
 w_{a,b}e^{it\Delta_{a,b}}.
\]

Symmetric integration gives

\[
 \frac1{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}e^{it\Delta}\,dt
 =\operatorname{sinc}(T\Delta),                     \tag{13}
\]

with value one at `Delta=0`. Splitting the finite sum at zero proves
`(4)`--`(5)`.

Definition `(6)` immediately gives `(7)` and
`delta_r(E)<=|Delta_(a,b)|` for every nonzero action. The elementary bounds

\[
 |\operatorname{sinc}x|\leq1,
 \qquad
 |\operatorname{sinc}x|\leq|x|^{-1}\quad(x\ne0)    \tag{14}
\]

therefore bound every nonzero reporter by the right side of `(8)`. Sum
against the nonnegative coefficients. Their total is one because the full
multinomial expansion is `(sum_i p_i)^(2r)=1`. This proves `(8)` and `(9)`.

For `(11)`, split the zero-action sum into `a=b` and `a!=b`. Positivity of
the weights gives `X_r>=0`. If no nonzero balanced integer relation exists,
`Delta_(a,b)=0` forces `a=b` at every order, so every excess vanishes.
Conversely, a nonzero balanced relation `c` decomposes as `c=c^+-c^-` with
equal positive and negative masses. At that finite replica order,
`a=c^+` and `b=c^-` are distinct, have zero action, and carry strictly
positive weight. This proves `(12)`.

## Ordinary local-Hamiltonian consequence

The formalized nearest-neighbour qudit-path theorem proves that, for every
`n,q>=2`, a dense residual full-measure subset of the ordinary static
trace-zero onsite-plus-edge Hamiltonian space has maximal projective rank
`q^n-1`. Every Hamiltonian in that set therefore satisfies `(8)` with
`C_r=B_r` simultaneously for every state with positive spectral weights and
every replica order. The same Hamiltonians have exact strict-return density

\[
 \left(
 {2\arcsin(\varepsilon/2)\over\pi}
 \right)^{q^n-1}                                    \tag{15}
\]

and no nonzero exact projective return.

This local consequence does not assume random-matrix eigenvalues, stochastic
time dependence, Haar unitaries, spectral nondegeneracy as an external
premise, or a thermodynamic limit.

## Hostile audit

1. The law governs every finite-order survival-return reporter. It does not
   itself classify circuit complexity of the endpoint state.
2. `delta_r(E)` is exact but can be extremely small. Equation `(8)` is a
   sharp finite-size resolution law, not a dimension-uniform mixing-time
   estimate.
3. The nearest-neighbour statement is generic in the full inhomogeneous
   local coefficient space. Translation-invariant few-parameter families
   are not covered.
4. Equation `(15)` is asymptotic time density, not a first-hitting-time law.
5. The abstract collision expansion is elementary/known. Defensible novelty
   must come from discharging maximal projective rank inside ordinary static
   bounded-degree local Hamiltonians, not from rebranding `(13)`.
6. This theorem proves the spectral half of the proposed shortcut-action
   programme. The selector-uniform circuit-complexity upper law remains open
   and must not be inferred from it.
