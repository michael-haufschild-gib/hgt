## Exact claim

For complex shifts \(a,b\), define the opposite-orientation Gamma channel
\[
 A_{a,b}(z)=\frac{\Gamma(z+a)}{\Gamma(b-z)},
\]
its reflected intensity
\[
 I_{a,b}(z)=A_{a,b}(z)A_{a,b}(-z),
\]
its orientation-sensitive phase channel
\[
 P_{a,b}(z)=\frac{A_{a,b}(z)}{A_{a,b}(-z)},
\]
and its logarithmic Wigner--Smith current
\[
 T_{a,b}(z)=\frac{A'_{a,b}(z)}{A_{a,b}(z)}.
\]

Put
\[
 \mathcal C(a,b)\iff
 \bigl(b-a\in\mathbb Z\bigr)
 \ \lor\
 \bigl(1-2a\in\mathbb Z\ \land\ 1-2b\in\mathbb Z\bigr).
\]

Let \(\iota\) be any index type, with shifts \(a_i,b_i\in\mathbb C\), and suppose
\(\mathcal C(a_i,b_i)\) for every \(i\). Let
\[
 K_I=\mathbb C(z)\langle I_{a_i,b_i}:i\in\iota\rangle_\partial
\]
be the differential field generated by the entire intensity atlas. Then, for every \(j\in\iota\),
\[
 P_{a_j,b_j}\ \text{and}\ T_{a_j,b_j}
\]
are hypertranscendental over \(K_I\). Equivalently, neither satisfies any algebraic differential
equation whose coefficients belong to the differential field generated by all collapsed
intensities.

No finiteness, countability, independence, or distinctness hypothesis is imposed on \(\iota\).

## Dependency-closed paper proof

The exact reflected-intensity theorem gives
\[
 I_{a_i,b_i}\text{ is differentially algebraic over }\mathbb C(z)
 \iff \mathcal C(a_i,b_i).
\]
Hence every generator of \(K_I\) is differentially algebraic over \(\mathbb C(z)\).

The reflected-frequency phase theorem gives, without any condition on \(a_j,b_j\),
\[
 P_{a_j,b_j}\text{ is hypertranscendental over }\mathbb C(z).
\]
The logarithmic-source theorem and the hypertranscendence of the Gamma quotient give, again
without a parameter restriction,
\[
 T_{a_j,b_j}\text{ is hypertranscendental over }\mathbb C(z).
\]

Differential-algebraic base change is exact: adjoining an arbitrary set of elements individually
differentially algebraic over a base field preserves and reflects hypertranscendence. Applying this
to the set \(\{I_{a_i,b_i}:i\in\iota\}\) proves both conclusions over \(K_I\).

The theorem is stronger than finite-circuit non-recovery. Differential algebraicity over \(K_I\)
allows an arbitrary algebraic differential equation over the full generated observation field,
not only an expression tree made from arithmetic and differentiation.
