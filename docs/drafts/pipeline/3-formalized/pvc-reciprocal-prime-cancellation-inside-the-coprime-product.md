# Reciprocal-prime cancellation inside the coprime product

**PVC · Theorem (Lean).** Write $T=\lfloor X/v\rfloor$. In the coprime prime row keep $D<v\le X^{1/10000}$ and $T^{49999/100000}\le p\le T^{50001/100000}$, with the original restrictions $2(D+1)<p$ and $Y<pv\le X$. Uniformly in $Y$ and $1\le D,R\le X^{1/400000}$, the signed sum over this region is $O(X^{0.4999})$.

**Proof.** On every dyadic subinterval of the prime band, the [reciprocal-prime block theorem](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExceptionalLayerTheory/OrdinaryPrimeCancellationInTheSquareRootBand.lean) saves the factor $A^{-1/1000}$ at lower endpoint $A$, with coefficient mass $O(R)$. For $R\le T^{1/100000}$, summing the blocks costs $O(T^{1/10000})$ and gives $O(T^{0.4997})$, uniformly in both sharp endpoints. Bounded $T$ are absorbed by the absolute prime sum.

Since $R^{100000}v\le X$ and $v^5\le X$, integer division gives $R^{100000}\le T$ and $v^4\le T$. Thus the prime estimate applies and $v<p$ throughout the band for $v>1$. Primality gives $p\nmid v$. Intersect the prime interval with $p>\max(2(D+1),\lfloor Y/v\rfloor)$ and $p\le T$. The inner jump is the difference of two prefixes of size $O(D+1)$. Summing over $v$ costs at most $X^{1/10000}$, so $0.4997+1/400000+1/10000<0.4999$. Reversing the finite sums identifies exactly the deleted region. The same bound applies at every divided horizon $\lfloor N/d\rfloor$ and costs at most $O(N^{0.4999}\lVert b_V\rVert_1)$ after the Vaughan weighting.
