## Theorem: defect closure equals orbit closure

Let `k` be a commutative ring, let `X` be a set, and let `M` be a monoid acting on the
`k`-algebra `F` by unital `k`-algebra endomorphisms `T^*`. Let `A` be a unital `k`-subalgebra of
`F`. Define

\[
 \operatorname{Def}_M(A)
 =\operatorname{alg}_k\bigl(A,\ T^*f-f:T\in M,\ f\in A\bigr)
 \tag{1}
\]

and

\[
 \operatorname{Orb}_M(A)
 =\operatorname{alg}_k\bigl(T^*f:T\in M,\ f\in A\bigr).
 \tag{2}
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{\operatorname{Def}_M(A)=\operatorname{Orb}_M(A).}
 \tag{3}
\]

Moreover, `Orb_M(A)` is the smallest `M`-invariant unital `k`-subalgebra of `F` containing `A`.
Consequently the recursion

\[
 A_0=A,
 \qquad
 A_{\alpha+1}=\operatorname{Def}_M(A_\alpha),
 \qquad
 A_\lambda=\bigcup_{\alpha<\lambda}A_\alpha
 \tag{4}
\]

at limit ordinals stabilizes at the first successor:

\[
 A_1=A_2=\cdots=A_\alpha=\operatorname{Orb}_M(A)
 \qquad(\alpha\ge1).
 \tag{5}
\]

### Proof

Because `A` is closed under subtraction, every generator of `(1)` lies in `(2)`:

\[
 T^*f-f\in\operatorname{alg}_k(A,T^*A).
\]

Conversely, `(1)` contains `f` and `T^*f-f`, hence also

\[
 T^*f=f+(T^*f-f).
\]

Thus the two displayed generating families generate the same algebra, proving `(3)`.

For invariance, an element of `Orb_M(A)` is a polynomial in finitely many elements `T_i^*f_i`.
For `S\in M`, its pullback is the same polynomial in

\[
 S^*T_i^*f_i=(T_iS)^*f_i
\]

up to the harmless choice of left/right action convention. Since `T_iS\in M`, the result again
belongs to `Orb_M(A)`. Any `M`-invariant algebra containing `A` contains every `T^*f`, so it
contains `Orb_M(A)`. This proves minimality.

Applying `(3)` to the already invariant algebra `A_1=Orb_M(A)` gives `A_2=A_1`. Transfinite
induction proves `(5)`; every limit union after stage one is the same algebra. QED.

## Closed-subspace variant

Suppose `F` is a topological `k`-algebra, every `T^*` is continuous, and define both sides using

generate the same subalgebra before closure. The closed orbit algebra is again `M`-invariant.

Thus completing, bornologizing, or taking a source-approved topological closure cannot change the
