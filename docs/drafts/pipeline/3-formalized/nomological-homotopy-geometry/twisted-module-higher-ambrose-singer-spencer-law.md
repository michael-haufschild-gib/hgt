## 1. Flat coefficient geometry

Let `M` be a connected smooth manifold, `G` a Lie group, and `V` a finite-dimensional real
representation of `G`. Let `P->M` carry a flat `G`-connection `A`, and let

\[
 E=P\times_GV
\tag{TM.1}
\]

be the associated flat vector bundle. Write `tau_gamma:E_x->E_y` for parallel transport. Fix
`n>=2` and a covariantly closed form

\[
 H\in\Omega^n(M;E),
 \qquad d_AH=0.
\tag{TM.2}
\]

The lower transport may be nonabelian through `G`; the fixed higher coefficient group is additive
`V` with its monodromy action.

## 2. Curvature span

For a base point `x`, define

\[
 W_x(H)=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}
 \left\{
 \tau_\gamma^{-1}H_y(v_1,\ldots,v_n):
 \gamma:x\to y, v_i\in T_yM
 \right\}\subseteq E_x.
\tag{TM.3}
\]

For a smooth singular `n`-simplex `sigma`, choose a path from `x` to its first vertex and use flat
parallel transport over the simplex to integrate `H` into `E_x`. Let `P_x(H)` be the additive
subgroup generated by all such based simplex integrals. Different path choices are retained; they
encode the monodromy action.

Let `overline P_x(H)` be its closure in `E_x`. Its identity component is a vector subspace; denote
its Lie algebra by `p_x(H)`.

## 3. Higher Ambrose--Singer theorem

### Theorem 3.1 — curvature values generate restricted higher-period holonomy

\[
 \boxed{\mathfrak p_x(H)=W_x(H).}
\tag{TM.4}
\]

#### Proof

Every based simplex integral is a Riemann integral of values appearing in `(TM.3)`. The
finite-dimensional subspace `W_x(H)` is closed, so

\[
 P_x(H)\subseteq W_x(H),
 \qquad
 \mathfrak p_x(H)\subseteq W_x(H).
\tag{TM.5}
\]

For the reverse inclusion, fix `y`, a path `gamma:x->y`, and tangent vectors `v_1,...,v_n`. In a
coordinate chart at `y`, let `sigma_t` be the oriented small `n`-parallelotope of side parameter
`t` in those directions, based at `y`. Use `gamma` as its base path. Its integral satisfies

\[
 c(t):=\int_{\sigma_t}^{x}H
 =t^n\tau_\gamma^{-1}H_y(v_1,\ldots,v_n)+o(t^n).
\tag{TM.6}
\]

The curve `c(t)` lies in `P_x(H)`, begins at zero, and therefore lies in the identity component of
`overline P_x(H)`. That component is a vector subspace. Hence `t^{-n}c(t)` also lies in it for
`t!=0`; taking `t->0` shows the transported curvature value lies in `mathfrak p_x(H)`. These values
span `W_x(H)`, proving the reverse inclusion. QED.

## 4. Analytic Spencer form

Assume now `M`, `A`, and `H` are real analytic. Define

\[
 J_x(H)=\operatorname{span}
 \left\{(\nabla^kH)_x(X_1,\ldots,X_k;v_1,\ldots,v_n):k\geq0\right\}.
\tag{TM.7}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 — analytic jet generation

\[
 \boxed{W_x(H)=J_x(H).}
\tag{TM.8}
\]

#### Proof

Differentiate parallel-transported values of `H` along analytic paths through `x`; every covariant
jet belongs to `W_x(H)`, so `J_x(H) subseteq W_x(H)`. Conversely, trivialize `E` by the flat
connection on the universal cover. The quotient of `H` by the fixed subspace `J_x(H)` has every
Taylor coefficient zero at a lift of `x`, hence vanishes in a neighborhood by analyticity and then
on the connected universal cover by analytic continuation. Thus every transported value lies in
`J_x(H)`. QED.

Combining `(TM.4)` and `(TM.8)` gives

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak p_x(H)=
 \operatorname{span}\{(\nabla^kH)_x:k\geq0\}.}
\tag{TM.9}
\]

No uniform finite jet cutoff follows; finite dimensionality only gives a connection-dependent
finite generating subset.

## 5. Crossed-module realization

Take a differential crossed module with `t:V->mathfrak g` equal to zero and `V` abelian. Fake
flatness forces the ordinary connection `A` to be flat. A 2-form `B in Omega^2(M;E)` has

\[
 H=d_AB\in\Omega^3(M;E).
\tag{TM.10}
\]

Differences of based surface holonomies across 3-chains are the higher periods generated by `H`.
Theorem 3.1 therefore identifies their restricted infinitesimal span. Global flat surface characters
and lower monodromy remain independent.

## 6. Field consequences

1. The first nontrivial graded higher Ambrose--Singer law holds with nonabelian lower transport and
   an abelian higher coefficient local system.
2. Curvature generation is equivariant: lower holonomy acts on the higher curvature span.
3. Analytic Spencer jets generate exactly the same restricted higher-period algebra as transported
   curvature values.
4. Full higher holonomy still contains flat characters and component data not generated by `H`.
5. The remaining open step is genuinely nonlinear coupling among several nonzero higher brackets,
   not merely twisting an abelian coefficient system.

## 7. Hostile boundary

- Flatness of `A` is load-bearing for path-independent local coefficient integration over a simplex.
- `P_x(H)` is the restricted higher-period subgroup generated by curvature fillings; it is not a
  definition of the complete global transport 2-functor.
- If `H=0`, the theorem gives zero restricted span while flat higher monodromy may remain.
- Infinite-dimensional coefficient spaces require closure and functional-analytic hypotheses not
  used here.
