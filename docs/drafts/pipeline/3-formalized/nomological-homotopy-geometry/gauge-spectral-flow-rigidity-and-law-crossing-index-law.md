## 1. Polarized sourced Hessian path

Let `I=[a,b]` and let `H_t` be a `C^1` path of self-adjoint endomorphisms of a finite-dimensional
real or complex Hilbert space. In NHG, `H_t` is the Hessian of a source-derived cyclic master action
after choosing a positive polarization and a local trivialization.

A time `t_0` is a **crossing** when `ker H_(t_0)` is nonzero. Its crossing form is

\[
 \Gamma_{t_0}(x,y)
 =\langle x,\dot H_{t_0}y\rangle,
 \qquad x,y\in\ker H_{t_0}.
\tag{SF.1}
\]

The crossing is regular when `(SF.1)` is nondegenerate.

## 2. Gauge invariance of the crossing tensor

Let `U_t` be a `C^1` unitary path and put

\[
 H_t'=U_tH_tU_t^{-1}.
\tag{SF.2}
\]

### Theorem 2.1

Under the identification `ker H_t -> ker H_t'` by `U_t`, the crossing forms agree:

\[
 \boxed{
 \Gamma_t'(U_tx,U_ty)=\Gamma_t(x,y).}
\tag{SF.3}
\]

### Proof

Write `K_t=U_t^{-1}\dot U_t`, which is skew-adjoint. Differentiating `(SF.2)` and pulling back by
`U_t` gives

\[
 U_t^{-1}\dot H_t'U_t=\dot H_t+[K_t,H_t].
\tag{SF.4}
\]

If `x,y in ker H_t`, then

\[
 \langle x,[K_t,H_t]y\rangle
 =\langle x,K_tH_ty\rangle-\langle x,H_tK_ty\rangle=0,
\tag{SF.5}
\]

using self-adjointness of `H_t`. This proves `(SF.3)`. QED.

The restriction of the Hessian derivative to the zero-mode space is therefore a
coordinate-independent law-event tensor. The full derivative `dot H` is not: it changes by the
commutator in `(SF.4)`.

## 3. Spectral-flow formula

### Theorem 3.1 — regular law-event index

Assume the endpoints are invertible and every crossing is regular. Then crossings are isolated and

\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{sf}(H_t)
 =\sum_{t_0\in(a,b)}\operatorname{sign}\Gamma_{t_0}.}
\tag{SF.6}
\]

Here `sign Gamma` is positive index minus negative index.

### Proof

Near a regular crossing, standard perturbation of the finite-dimensional spectral projection onto
the small eigenvalue cluster gives eigenvalue branches whose first derivatives are the eigenvalues
of `(SF.1)`. Each positive derivative contributes `+1` and each negative derivative contributes
`-1` to spectral flow. Away from crossings the inertia is locally constant. Summing the local
changes proves `(SF.6)`. QED.

By Theorem 2.1, every summand and the total index are invariant under polarized unitary gauge.

## 4. Pure-gauge rigidity

### Corollary 4.1

If

\[
 H_t=U_tH_0U_t^{-1}
\tag{SF.7}
\]

for a unitary path, then the spectrum and inertia are constant and

\[
 \boxed{\operatorname{sf}(H_t)=0.}
\tag{SF.8}
\]

At every persistent zero mode, the crossing form vanishes.

Thus nonzero spectral flow cannot be generated by a structured gauge path with transported
positive polarization. It detects a transverse law event, a failure to transport polarization, an
admission/domain change, or a nonunitary observer—not gauge alone.

## 5. Cyclic law interpretation

Let `S_t` be a `C^2` family of source-derived cyclic actions and `alpha_t` a path of critical laws.
After choosing a parallel positive polarization, let

\[
 H_t=\operatorname{Hess}_{\alpha_t}S_t.
\tag{SF.9}
\]

At a crossing, `(SF.1)` is the contraction of the infinitesimal law-event tensor with two sourced
zero modes. In a cyclic `L_infinity` chart it is obtained by differentiating

\[
 \operatorname{Hess}_{\alpha_t}S_t(\eta,\xi)
 =\langle\eta,(\ell_1^{\alpha_t})_t\xi\rangle.
\tag{SF.10}
\]

Consequently `(SF.6)` is a finite-dimensional nomological index theorem:

\[
 \boxed{
 \text{spectral flow of sourced Hessian}
 =\text{sum of signatures of cyclic law-event tensors on zero modes}.}
\tag{SF.11}
\]

It is local at regular horizons and does not require choosing an absolute determinant phase.

### Theorem 5.2 — source-history transgression formula

Fix one cyclic `L_infinity` structure and let `alpha_t` be a path of Maurer--Cartan laws. Its twisted
differential is

\[
 d_{\alpha_t}x
 =\sum_{n\geq0}\frac1{n!}
 \ell_{n+1}(\alpha_t^{\otimes n},x).
\tag{SF.12}
\]

Write `v_t=dot alpha_t` and let `ell_2^(alpha_t)` be the twisted binary bracket. Then

\[
 \boxed{
 \frac d{dt}d_{\alpha_t}x
 =\ell_2^{\alpha_t}(v_t,x).}
\tag{SF.13}
\]

For zero modes `x,y in ker d_(alpha_t0)`, the sourced crossing tensor is

\[
 \boxed{
 \Gamma_{t_0}(x,y)
 =\langle x,\ell_2^{\alpha_{t_0}}(v_{t_0},y)\rangle
 =\pm\langle v_{t_0},
 \ell_2^{\alpha_{t_0}}(y,x)\rangle.}
\tag{SF.14}
\]

The last equality is cyclicity. If the velocity is pure gauge,

\[
 v_{t_0}=d_{\alpha_{t_0}}\varepsilon,
\tag{SF.15}
\]

then `(SF.14)` vanishes.

### Proof

Differentiate `(SF.12)` termwise. Choosing one of the `n` copies of `alpha_t` cancels `n!` to
`(n-1)!`, giving the twisted binary bracket and proving `(SF.13)`. Pairing with zero modes gives the
first equality in `(SF.14)`; cyclicity gives the second.

For `(SF.15)`, the twisted `L_infinity` identity expresses

\[
 \ell_2^\alpha(d_\alpha\varepsilon,y)
 =d_\alpha(\ell_2^\alpha(\varepsilon,y))
\tag{SF.16}
\]

up to the standard sign because `d_alpha y=0`. Pairing with `x` and moving `d_alpha` across the
cyclic pairing gives zero because `d_alpha x=0`. QED.

Thus the regular-crossing index is a source-history pairing before any determinant or scalar

## 6. Sharp boundaries

1. If crossings are degenerate, perturbation or a more general spectral-flow definition is needed;
   `(SF.6)` need not hold as a raw signature sum.
2. If the Hilbert domain changes, unitary conjugation of bounded matrices no longer models the
   event; graph/gap continuity is load-bearing in the Fredholm extension.
3. A varying positive metric must be transported into a fixed Hilbert chart before `(SF.2)` applies.
4. Indefinite/Krein spectral flow requires a different index theory.
5. Nonzero crossing form proves a transverse event but does not identify its source normalization.
6. Global stabilizer holonomy can be nontrivial while spectral flow is zero.
