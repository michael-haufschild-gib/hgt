## 1. Setup

Let `L=direct_sum_{j<=0} L_j` be a finite-dimensional nilpotent `L_infinity` algebra over
`mathbb R`, and let `M` be a connected smooth manifold. Write

\[
 a=\sum_{p\geq1}a_p,
 \qquad
 a_p\in\Omega^p(M;L_{1-p})
\tag{SC.1}
\]

for a total degree-one law connection, with curvature

\[
 \operatorname{Curv}(a)=\sum_{q\geq2}F_q,
 \qquad
 F_q\in\Omega^q(M;L_{2-q}).
\tag{SC.2}
\]

Let `Z_j=ker(ell_1:L_j->L_{j+1})` and `H_j=H_j(L,ell_1)`. Assume

\[
 F_2=\cdots=F_{r-1}=0
 \qquad (r\geq3).
\tag{SC.3}
\]

The induced connection on internal homology is

\[
 \nabla^{\mathcal H}=d+[\ell_2(a_1,-)].
\tag{SC.4}
\]

It is flat because `F_2=0`. The leading-curvature theorem gives a closed form

\[
 [F_r]\in\Omega^r(M;\mathcal H_{2-r}).
\tag{SC.5}
\]

This card treats the first genuinely choice-sensitive case: `(SC.5)` vanishes as an
internal-homology-valued form.

## 2. Chain variation through the next form degree

Choose

\[
 b_r\in\Omega^r(M;L_{1-r}),
 \qquad
 \ell_1(b_r)=-F_r,
\tag{SC.6}
\]

and put `a^(b)=a+b_r`. Denote its curvature components by `F_q^(b)`.

### Lemma 2.1 — two leading variation identities

\[
 \boxed{
 F_r^{(b)}=F_r+\ell_1(b_r)=0,
 \qquad
 F_{r+1}^{(b)}=F_{r+1}+\nabla b_r.}
\tag{SC.7}
\]

Here `nabla=d+ell_2(a_1,-)` is the chain-level operator inducing `(SC.4)`.

### Proof

Expand the homotopy Maurer--Cartan curvature of `a+b_r`. In form degree `r`, the only new term is
`ell_1(b_r)`. In form degree `r+1`, the only new terms are `d b_r` and
`ell_2(a_1,b_r)`. Every term containing `a_p` with `p>=2` has form degree at least `r+2`, and every
term containing two copies of `b_r` has form degree at least `2r>=r+2`. This proves `(SC.7)`. QED.

## 3. Canonical secondary curvature

Since `a^(b)` has zero curvature through form degree `r`, the leading-curvature theorem applied at
degree `r+1` gives a closed form

\[
 s_{r+1}(a;b_r)
 :=[F_{r+1}+\nabla b_r]
 \in\Omega^{r+1}(M;\mathcal H_{1-r}).
\tag{SC.8}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 — secondary class is independent of the nullhomotopy

The twisted de Rham class

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak o_{r+1}(a)
 :=[s_{r+1}(a;b_r)]_{d_{\nabla^{\mathcal H}}}
 \in H^{r+1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(M;\mathcal H_{1-r})}
\tag{SC.9}
\]

is independent of the choice of `b_r` satisfying `(SC.6)`.

### Proof

If `b_r'` is another choice, then `z_r=b_r'-b_r` satisfies `ell_1(z_r)=0`. Formula `(SC.7)` gives

\[
 s_{r+1}(a;b_r')-s_{r+1}(a;b_r)
 =\nabla^{\mathcal H}[z_r].
\tag{SC.10}
\]

Thus the representatives differ by a twisted de Rham coboundary. QED.

The raw secondary form is choice-dependent. Its cohomology class is the coordinate-free curvature
datum. Calling a selected representative "the secondary curvature" without retaining `(SC.9)` is
gauge fixing.

## 4. Exact extension criterion

### Theorem 4.1 — vanishing is equivalent to one more flattening step

The following are equivalent:

1. `mathfrak o_(r+1)(a)=0`;
2. there exist

   \[
   z_r\in\Omega^r(M;Z_{1-r}),
   \qquad
   b_{r+1}\in\Omega^{r+1}(M;L_{-r})
   \tag{SC.11}
   \]

   such that the connection

   \[
   a'=a+b_r+z_r+b_{r+1}
   \tag{SC.12}
   \]

   has

   \[
   F_2'=\cdots=F_{r+1}'=0.
   \tag{SC.13}
   \]

### Proof

If `(SC.12)`--`(SC.13)` hold, the form-degree `r+1` variation identity gives

\[
 F_{r+1}+\nabla(b_r+z_r)+\ell_1(b_{r+1})=0.
\tag{SC.14}
\]

Passing first to internal homology and then to twisted de Rham cohomology proves
`mathfrak o_(r+1)(a)=0`.

Conversely, suppose `(SC.9)` vanishes. Choose an internal-cycle representative `z_r` of a twisted
de Rham primitive with sign chosen so that

\[
 [F_{r+1}+\nabla(b_r+z_r)]=0
 \quad\text{in }\Omega^{r+1}(M;\mathcal H_{1-r}).
\tag{SC.15}
\]

Finite-dimensional linear splittings of `Z_{1-r}->H_{1-r}` and
`ell_1:L_{-r}->im ell_1` give smooth global lifts. Hence choose `b_(r+1)` so that `(SC.14)` holds.
Terms involving `b_(r+1)` cannot alter lower form degrees, proving `(SC.13)`. QED.

Thus secondary curvature is not decorative: it is exactly the obstruction to extending a partial
flat law connection by one form degree.

### Counterexample 4.2 — zero primary layer with nonzero secondary obstruction

Let `L` be the two-step nilpotent differential graded Lie algebra generated by

\[
 x\in L_{-1},\qquad v\in L_{-2},
 \qquad \ell_1=0,\qquad [x,x]=v,
\tag{SC.15a}
\]

with every bracket involving `v` equal to zero. Let `M=S^2 times S^2`, choose closed area forms
`omega_1,omega_2`, put `omega=omega_1+omega_2`, and take the total law connection

\[
 a_2=\omega x,\qquad a_p=0\quad(p\neq2).
\tag{SC.15b}
\]

Then

\[
 F_2=F_3=0,
 \qquad
 F_4=\frac12\omega\wedge\omega\,v
 =\omega_1\wedge\omega_2\,v.
\tag{SC.15c}
\]

Since `[omega_1 wedge omega_2]` is nonzero in `H_dR^4(S^2 times S^2)`, the secondary obstruction
`mathfrak o_4(a)` is nonzero. Thus exact primary flattening does not imply the next flattening step,
even for a finite-dimensional two-step nilpotent strict differential graded Lie algebra.

## 5. Full obstruction spectrum

For `n>=r`, let `P_n(a)` be the set of corrections

\[
 \beta=b_r+b_{r+1}+\cdots+b_n,
 \qquad
 b_p\in\Omega^p(M;L_{1-p}),
\tag{SC.16}
\]

for which

\[
 \operatorname{Curv}(a+\beta)_q=0
 \qquad(2\leq q\leq n).
\tag{SC.17}
\]

For `beta in P_n(a)`, the next component defines

\[
 o_{n+1}(\beta)
 \in H^{n+1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(M;\mathcal H_{1-n}).
\tag{SC.18}
\]

Define the **curvature obstruction spectrum**

\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{Ob}_{n+1}(a)
 =\{o_{n+1}(\beta):\beta\in P_n(a)\}.}
\tag{SC.19}
\]

### Theorem 5.1 — recursive extension law

For every `n>=r`,

\[
 \boxed{
 P_{n+1}(a)\neq\varnothing
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 0\in\operatorname{Ob}_{n+1}(a).}
\tag{SC.20}
\]

Moreover:

1. `Ob_(r+1)(a)` is the singleton `{mathfrak o_(r+1)(a)}`;
2. later `Ob_(n+1)(a)` need not be singletons, because different lower flattenings change higher
   representatives through nonlinear brackets;
3. pullback along a smooth map and strict `L_infinity` isomorphism transport the entire family
   `P_n`, `Ob_(n+1)` naturally.

### Proof

Fix `beta in P_n(a)`. The leading-curvature theorem applied to `a+beta` makes its degree-`n+1`
curvature a closed form in `mathcal H_(1-n)`. Repeating the proof of Theorem 4.1 with `n` in place
of `r` shows that `beta` extends through degree `n+1` exactly when `o_(n+1)(beta)=0`. Taking the
union over `P_n(a)` proves `(SC.20)`. The singleton statement is Theorem 3.1. Nonlinear dependence
at later stages is the usual Massey-type indeterminacy: changing an earlier nullhomotopy can enter a
higher curvature component through mixed brackets. Naturality follows from the curvature variation
formula. QED.

The invariant is therefore neither one scalar nor one infinite list of arbitrarily chosen forms. It
is the tower of partial-flattening spaces together with their obstruction spectra.

### Counterexample 5.2 — later obstruction spectrum is genuinely multivalued

Let `L` be the three-step graded Lie algebra with zero unary differential and generators

\[
 x\in L_{-1},\qquad y\in L_{-2},\qquad w\in L_{-3},
 \qquad [x,y]=w,
\tag{SC.24}
\]

all other generator brackets being zero. On `M=S^2 times S^3`, choose closed forms `omega` and
`eta` representing the two fundamental cohomology classes. Put `a_2=omega x` and all other
components of `a` equal to zero. Since `[x,x]=0`, this connection is flat.

Both `beta=0` and `beta'=eta y` belong to `P_4(a)`: the correction `eta y` first contributes to
curvature in form degree five. Their next obstructions are

\[
 o_5(0)=0,
 \qquad
 o_5(\eta y)=[\omega\wedge\eta\,w]\neq0.
\tag{SC.25}
\]

Hence `Ob_5(a)` contains at least two elements. The later invariant is necessarily a spectrum over
partial flattenings, not a single cohomology class. This example is finite-dimensional, nilpotent,
and independent of completion or global monodromy.

## 6. Finite-base termination and the pro-Spencer boundary

### Corollary 6.1

If `dim M=d<infinity`, then no local form-degree obstruction occurs above degree `d`. If
`P_d(a)` is nonempty, the corrected total law connection is locally curvature-flat.

Consequently an infinite curvature-obstruction migration cannot live in one fixed
finite-dimensional smooth chart. It requires at least one of:

1. a pro-manifold or infinite jet/Spencer base;
2. unbounded vertical amplitude or an infinite filtered coefficient object;
3. a completion boundary not represented by ordinary differential forms.

This gives hypertranscendental prolongation a precise possible role in NHG: it can make the
obstruction base noncompact in jet depth. It does not imply noncompact ordinary holonomy.

## 7. Hostile countermodels and scope

1. **Raw secondary form is intrinsic — false.** Equation `(SC.10)` changes it by an exact term.
2. **Primary vanishing forces flattening — false.** The class `(SC.9)` can obstruct the next step.
3. **One higher class determines the future tower — false.** Later spectra have Massey-type
   indeterminacy.
4. **Infinite obstruction migration occurs on a fixed finite base — false.** Corollary 6.1 bounds
   form degree.
5. **Local flattening removes global history — false.** Flat monodromy and differential-character
   data remain independent.
6. **Every obstruction spectrum is a vector subspace — false.** It is generally a subset or torsor
   image determined by the partial-flattening space.
7. **Vanishing of every finite obstruction automatically gives a limit — false without
   completeness.** Infinite filtered corrections require convergence in the declared topology.
