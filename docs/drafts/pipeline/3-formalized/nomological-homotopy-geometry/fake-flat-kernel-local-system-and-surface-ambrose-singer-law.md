## 1. Geometric input

Let

\[
 t:H\longrightarrow G
\tag{FK.1}
\]

be a finite-dimensional Lie crossed module, with differential crossed module
`(t:mathfrak h->mathfrak g,triangleright)`. Let `P->M` be a principal `G`-bundle over a connected
smooth manifold. A strict 2-connection consists of a connection `A` on `P` and

\[
 B\in\Omega^2(M;P\times_G\mathfrak h).
\tag{FK.2}
\]

Write

\[
 \mathcal F=F_A-t(B),
 \qquad
 H_3=d_AB.
\tag{FK.3}
\]

Assume fake flatness, `mathcal F=0`. Put

\[
 \mathfrak k=\ker(t:\mathfrak h\to\mathfrak g),
 \qquad
 E_K=P\times_G\mathfrak k.
\tag{FK.4}
\]

The `G`-action preserves `mathfrak k`, so `A` induces a connection `nabla^K` on `E_K`.

## 2. Kernel reduction theorem

### Theorem 2.1 — fake-flat higher curvature is a closed flat-local-system form

The induced connection on `E_K` is flat, and

\[
 \boxed{
 H_3\in\Omega^3(M;E_K),
 \qquad
 d_{\nabla^K}H_3=0.}
\tag{FK.5}
\]

Thus every fake-flat strict 2-connection canonically reduces its higher-curvature sector to a flat
local coefficient system, even when `t` is nonzero and the lower connection `A` is not flat.

#### Proof

The differential Peiffer identities are

\[
 t(X\triangleright Y)=[X,t(Y)],
 \qquad
 t(Y)\triangleright Y'=[Y,Y'].
\tag{FK.6}
\]

If `k in mathfrak k`, then `[k,Y]=t(k)\triangleright Y=0`; hence `mathfrak k` is central in
`mathfrak h`. Equivariance of `t` shows that `d_A` preserves `E_K`.

For a section `k` of `E_K`, fake flatness and the second Peiffer identity give

\[
 (\nabla^K)^2k
 =F_A\triangleright k
 =t(B)\triangleright k
 =[B,k]
 =0.
\tag{FK.7}
\]

Therefore `nabla^K` is flat.

Ordinary Bianchi and equivariance give

\[
 t(H_3)=t(d_AB)=d_At(B)=d_AF_A=0,
\tag{FK.8}
\]

so `H_3` is `E_K`-valued. Finally,

\[
 d_{\nabla^K}H_3
 =d_A^2B
 =F_A\triangleright B
 =t(B)\triangleright B
 =[B,B].
\tag{FK.9}
\]

The bracket on Lie-algebra-valued differential forms is graded antisymmetric. Since `B` has even
form degree two,

\[
 [B,B]=-(-1)^{2\cdot2}[B,B]=-[B,B].
\]

Over `mathbb R`, this forces `[B,B]=0`, proving `(FK.5)`. QED.

## 3. Lifted restricted surface holonomy

Fix `x in M`. Parallel transport for the flat bundle `E_K` is written `tau_gamma`. Define the
curvature span

\[
 W_x(H_3)=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}
 \left\{
 \tau_\gamma^{-1}H_{3,y}(u,v,w):
 \gamma:x\to y
 \right\}\subseteq (E_K)_x.
\tag{FK.10}
\]

Let `P_x(H_3)` be the additive subgroup of `(E_K)_x` generated by all based integrals of `H_3` over
smooth 3-simplices. Let `mathfrak p_x(H_3)` be the Lie algebra of the identity component of its
closure in the vector space `(E_K)_x`.

Assume the 2-connection integrates to a strict transport 2-functor with the standard
three-dimensional Stokes variation. Then a based 3-simplex changes boundary surface holonomy by the
exponential of its transported `H_3`-integral. The group `P_x(H_3)` is therefore the universal-cover
or logarithmic lift of the curvature-generated restricted surface holonomy.

### Theorem 3.1 — surface Ambrose--Singer generation

\[
 \boxed{\mathfrak p_x(H_3)=W_x(H_3).}
\tag{FK.11}
\]

Equivalently, the immersed restricted surface-holonomy group has tangent algebra generated by all
parallel transports of pointwise 3-curvature values.

#### Proof

Every based simplex integral is a Riemann integral of transported values in `(FK.10)`. The
finite-dimensional subspace `W_x(H_3)` is closed, hence

\[
 P_x(H_3)\subseteq W_x(H_3),
 \qquad
 \mathfrak p_x(H_3)\subseteq W_x(H_3).
\tag{FK.12}
\]

Conversely, fix a transported value

\[
 z=\tau_\gamma^{-1}H_{3,y}(u,v,w).
\]

Let `sigma_s` be a small oriented 3-parallelotope at `y` with edge parameter `s` and tangent
directions `u,v,w`. Its based integral satisfies

\[
 c(s)=\int_{\sigma_s}^{x}H_3=s^3z+o(s^3).
\tag{FK.13}
\]

The curve `c(s)` lies in `P_x(H_3)`, begins at zero, and hence lies in the identity component of its
closure. That component is a vector subspace. Thus `s^{-3}c(s)` lies in it for `s!=0`; taking
`s->0` gives `z in mathfrak p_x(H_3)`. These values span `W_x(H_3)`, proving `(FK.11)`. QED.

## 4. Analytic Spencer generation

Assume `M`, `A`, and `B` are real analytic. Then

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak p_x(H_3)
 =W_x(H_3)
 =\operatorname{span}
 \{(\nabla^K)^jH_3|_x:j\geq0\}.}
\tag{FK.14}
\]

#### Proof

The first equality is Theorem 3.1. Differentiating transported values along analytic paths shows
that every covariant jet lies in `W_x(H_3)`. For the reverse inclusion, trivialize `E_K` on the
universal cover using its flat connection. Modulo the span of all jets at a lift of `x`, every
Taylor coefficient of `H_3` vanishes. Real analyticity gives local vanishing, and analytic
continuation on the connected universal cover gives global vanishing. Every transported value is
therefore in the jet span. QED.

## 5. Immersed holonomy versus observer closure

Put `mathcal K=ker(t:H->G)`. Let `(mathcal K_x)^0` be the identity component of the corresponding
associated group-bundle fibre and

\[
 \exp:(E_K)_x\longrightarrow (\mathcal K_x)^0
\]

its exponential map. Equation `(FK.11)` concerns the lifted or immersed restricted holonomy. Its
topological closure in `(mathcal K_x)^0` can be larger. If `(mathcal K_x)^0` has a period lattice,
an irrational subspace `W_x(H_3)` can exponentiate densely in a larger torus.

Thus NHG must distinguish three objects:

1. local curvature span `W_x(H_3)`;
2. immersed restricted higher holonomy generated by it;
3. topological observer closure after quotient by the exponential lattice.

The third is not determined by local rank alone.

## 6. Field consequences

1. The strict 2-group case with nonzero boundary map `t` reduces canonically to the twisted-module
   higher Ambrose--Singer theorem on `ker t`.
2. Lower nonabelian transport survives as the monodromy action on the flat kernel local system.
3. Fixed-degree closed surface curvature is abelian; nonabelianity lies in cross-degree action and
   extension data.
4. Analytic Spencer jets generate the same lifted restricted surface-holonomy algebra as transported
   curvature values.
5. Completion can enlarge observer closure without changing the immersed curvature-generated
   algebra. This is a structural holonomy/completion separation law.

## 7. Parent boundary

Fake flatness as the condition for transport 2-functors is standard in
[Baez--Schreiber](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0412325). Nonabelian gerbe surface transport is
developed by [Schreiber--Waldorf](https://arxiv.org/abs/0808.1923). Derivative and three-dimensional
Stokes formulas are treated by [Glass](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09775) and
[Williams](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.05155). The field theorem here is the exact kernel-local-system
reduction plus the lifted Ambrose--Singer--Spencer generation statement. No priority claim is made.

## 8. Hostile boundary

- Fake flatness is load-bearing. Without it, the induced connection on `E_K` need not be flat and
  `H_3` need not be `E_K`-valued.
- Characteristic different from two requires a separate sign audit; this theorem is real.
- The theorem generates curvature-period or immersed restricted holonomy, not independent flat
  surface characters or discrete components.
- Topological closure after exponentiation can strictly enlarge the Lie algebra visible to a compact
  observer.
- Semistrict 2-groups and genuinely multigraded nonlinear `L_infinity` transport remain open.
