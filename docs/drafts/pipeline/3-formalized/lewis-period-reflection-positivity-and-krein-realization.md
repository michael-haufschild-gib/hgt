### 1. Theorem 2A — Modular Inversion Blindness at Fixed $s$
For $s \in \mathbb{C}$ and $f : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{C}$, define the Lewis modular inversion on $(0, \infty)$:
$$(I_s f)(a) := a^{-2s} f(a^{-1}) \qquad (a > 0).$$
1. For every $s \in \mathbb{C}$ and $a > 0$, $I_s(I_s f)(a) = a^{-2s}(a^{-1})^{-2s} f((a^{-1})^{-1}) = f(a)$.
2. At every Mayer scattering resonance $s$ ($0 < \operatorname{Re} s < \tfrac{1}{2}$, $\zeta(2s) = 0$):
   - By `MayerGlobalPeriods.reciprocity` applied to `state (2s)` (since `regularized (2s) hw (state (2s)) = 1 • state (2s) + constant 0`), the global Lewis period function $\psi_s(a) := \mathrm{period}(2s, 1, 0, \mathrm{state}(2s), a)$ satisfies $(I_s \psi_s)(a) = \psi_s(a)$ for all $a > 0$, with $\psi_s(1) = 2\zeta(2s-1) \neq 0$.
   - For $\operatorname{Im} s > 0$, `MayerScatteringCore.fieldCore_reciprocity` gives $(I_s \phi_{2s})(a) = \phi_{2s}(a)$ for all $a > 0$.
3. Let $V$ be any $\mathbb{C}$-vector space equipped with a positive-semidefinite sesquilinear form $h : V \times V \to \mathbb{C}$ ($\forall v,\; 0 \le \operatorname{Re} h(v,v)$ and $\operatorname{Im} h(v,v) = 0$) and any linear map $T : V \to V$. For EVERY $v \in V$ satisfying $T v = v$, both $h(v,v) \ge 0$ and the $T$-induced Krein quadratic form $k_T(v) := h(v, T v) = h(v, v) \ge 0$ (and $> 0$ if $h$ is anisotropic and $v \neq 0$). Applied to $v = \psi_s$, neither $h(\psi_s, \psi_s)$ nor $h(\psi_s, I_s \psi_s)$ can ever take a negative value at an off-line resonance $\operatorname{Re} s \neq \tfrac{1}{4}$.
4. Moreover, on $L^2((0,\infty), a^{2\operatorname{Re} s - 1}\,da)$, changing variables $a = b^{-1}$ ($da = b^{-2}\,db$) gives
$$\int_0^\infty a^{2\operatorname{Re} s - 1} |(I_s f)(a)|^2\,da = \int_0^\infty b^{1 - 2\operatorname{Re} s} (b^{4\operatorname{Re} s} |f(b)|^2) b^{-2}\,db = \int_0^\infty b^{2\operatorname{Re} s - 1} |f(b)|^2\,db$$
for ALL $s \in \mathbb{C}$, regardless of whether $\operatorname{Re} s = \tfrac{1}{4}$. $\square$

### 2. Theorem 2B — Canonical Critical-Line Period Hilbert Space $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{LP}}$ and Strict Co-Isometry Negativity Off $\operatorname{Re} s = \tfrac{1}{4}$
Define the positive-definite $s$-independent Möbius–critical weight and Hermitian pairing on measurable functions on $(0, \infty)$:
$$w_{\mathrm{LP}}(a) := (a + a^{-1})^{-6} a^{-1/2}, \qquad \langle f, g\rangle_{\mathrm{LP}} := \int_0^\infty f(a)\,\overline{g(a)}\,w_{\mathrm{LP}}(a)\,da, \qquad \|f\|_{\mathrm{LP}}^2 := \int_0^\infty \|f(a)\|^2\,w_{\mathrm{LP}}(a)\,da.$$

**Lemma 2B.1 (Global Lewis period functions belong to $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{LP}}$ across the entire strip).**
Let $0 < \operatorname{Re} s < \tfrac{1}{2}$ ($w = 2s$, $0 < \operatorname{Re} w < 1$), $\varepsilon, c \in \mathbb{C}$ with $\varepsilon^2 = 1$, and $f \in \mathrm{DiscSpace}$ satisfy $\mathrm{regularized}(2s, f) = \varepsilon \cdot f + \mathrm{constant}(c)$, and set $\psi(a) := \mathrm{period}(2s, \varepsilon, c, f, a)$ for $a > 0$. Then both $\|\psi\|_{\mathrm{LP}}^2$ and $\|I_{1/2 - \bar{s}} \psi\|_{\mathrm{LP}}^2$ are integrable on $(0, \infty)$.

*Proof.* By `MayerGlobalPeriods.extension_differentiableOn`, $\psi$ is continuous on $(0, \infty)$, so $M := \max(\sup_{x \in [1, 2]} \|\psi(x)\|, 1) < \infty$. For any $u \ge 1$, `period_shift` gives $\psi(u+1) = \psi(u) - \varepsilon u^{-w}\psi(1 + u^{-1})$ with $|\varepsilon| = 1$, $|u^{-w}| = u^{-\operatorname{Re} w} \le 1$, and $1 + u^{-1} \in [1, 2]$, so $\|\psi(u+1)\| \le \|\psi(u)\| + M$. Induction on $n = \lfloor b - 1\rfloor$ gives $\|\psi(b)\| \le M b$ for all $b \ge 1$. On $(0, 1]$, `reciprocity` gives $\psi(a) = \varepsilon a^{-w}\psi(a^{-1})$ with $a^{-1} \ge 1$, so $\|\psi(a)\| \le M a^{-\operatorname{Re} w - 1}$. Against $w_{\mathrm{LP}}(a) \le a^{11/2}$ on $(0, 1]$, $\|\psi(a)\|^2 w_{\mathrm{LP}}(a) \le M^2 a^{7/2 - 2\operatorname{Re} w} \le M^2$ (since $\operatorname{Re} w < 1$); against $w_{\mathrm{LP}}(b) \le b^{-13/2}$ on $(1, \infty)$, $\|\psi(b)\|^2 w_{\mathrm{LP}}(b) \le M^2 b^{-9/2}$, integrable on $(1, \infty)$. Similarly, $\|(I_{1/2 - \bar{s}}\psi)(a)\| = a^{\operatorname{Re} w - 1}\|\psi(a^{-1})\|$ satisfies $\le M a^{\operatorname{Re} w - 2}$ on $(0, 1]$ (giving $\le M^2 a^{2\operatorname{Re} w + 3/2} \le M^2$) and $\le M a^{2\operatorname{Re} w}$ on $(1, \infty)$ (giving $\le M^2 b^{4\operatorname{Re} w - 13/2} \le M^2 b^{-5/2}$ since $\operatorname{Re} w < 1$), integrable on $(0, \infty)$. $\square$

**Lemma 2B.2 (Adjoint reflection $I_s^\dagger = I_{1/2 - \bar{s}}$ and unitarity criterion).**
1. For any measurable $f, g : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{C}$,
$$\langle I_s f, g\rangle_{\mathrm{LP}} = \langle f, I_{1/2 - \bar{s}} g\rangle_{\mathrm{LP}}, \qquad \|I_s f\|_{\mathrm{LP}}^2 = \int_0^\infty \|f(b)\|^2\,(b + b^{-1})^{-6} b^{4\operatorname{Re} s - 3/2}\,db.$$
2. $I_s$ preserves $\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{LP}}$ on all compactly supported indicator/continuous functions on $(0, \infty)$ if and only if $\operatorname{Re} s = \tfrac{1}{4}$ (equivalently $I_{1/2 - \bar{s}} = I_s$).

*Proof.* Under the diffeomorphism $\Phi(b) = b^{-1}$ of $(0, \infty)$ with $|\Phi'(b)| = b^{-2}$, $(b^{-1} + (b^{-1})^{-1})^{-6} = (b + b^{-1})^{-6}$. For $b > 0$, $(b^{-1})^{-2s} = b^{2s} = \overline{b^{2\bar{s}}}$, and $(b^{-1})^{-1/2} b^{-2} = b^{-3/2} = b^{-1} b^{-1/2}$, so
$$((b^{-1})^{-2s} f(b))\,\overline{g(b^{-1})}\,(b^{-1} + b)^{-6}(b^{-1})^{-1/2} b^{-2} = f(b)\,\overline{b^{2\bar{s} - 1} g(b^{-1})}\,(b + b^{-1})^{-6} b^{-1/2} = f(b)\,\overline{(I_{1/2 - \bar{s}} g)(b)}\,w_{\mathrm{LP}}(b),$$
since $-2(\tfrac{1}{2} - \bar{s}) = 2\bar{s} - 1$. Integrating over $(0, \infty)$ proves the adjoint identity; setting $g = I_s f$ (or substituting directly in $\|I_s f\|_{\mathrm{LP}}^2$) gives the norm identity. When $\operatorname{Re} s = \tfrac{1}{4}$, $4\operatorname{Re} s - 3/2 = -1/2$, so $\|I_s f\|_{\mathrm{LP}}^2 = \|f\|_{\mathrm{LP}}^2$ identically. Conversely, testing $f = \mathbf{1}_{(1, 2)}$ gives $\int_1^2 (b + b^{-1})^{-6}(b^{4\operatorname{Re} s - 3/2} - b^{-1/2})\,db = 0$, which forces $4\operatorname{Re} s - 3/2 = -1/2$ because $b^{4\operatorname{Re} s - 3/2} - b^{-1/2}$ is strictly one-signed on $(1, 2]$ whenever $\operatorname{Re} s \neq \tfrac{1}{4}$. $\square$

**Theorem 2B.3 (Exact Co-Isometry Defect Formula and Strict Negativity Off $\operatorname{Re} s = \tfrac{1}{4}$).**
Define the **co-isometry / reflected Krein defect** on $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{LP}}$:
$$\mathcal{Q}_s(\psi) := \|\psi\|_{\mathrm{LP}}^2 - \|I_{1/2 - \bar{s}}\psi\|_{\mathrm{LP}}^2.$$
Let $\psi : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{C}$ satisfy $(I_s\psi)(a) = \psi(a)$ for all $a > 0$, with $\|\psi\|_{\mathrm{LP}}^2$ and $\|I_{1/2 - \bar{s}}\psi\|_{\mathrm{LP}}^2$ integrable on $(0, \infty)$. Put $\delta := 2\operatorname{Re} s - \tfrac{1}{2}$.
1. **Exact integral identity:**
$$\mathcal{Q}_s(\psi) = -\int_1^\infty (b + b^{-1})^{-6}\,b^{2\operatorname{Re} s - 1}\,\bigl(b^\delta + b^{-\delta}\bigr)\,\bigl(b^\delta - b^{-\delta}\bigr)^2\,\|\psi(b)\|^2\,db.$$
2. **Sharp resonance dichotomy:** For every Mayer scattering resonance $s$ ($0 < \operatorname{Re} s < \tfrac{1}{2}$) and its canonical global Lewis period eigenfunction $\psi_s(a) = \mathrm{period}(2s, 1, 0, \mathrm{state}(2s), a)$:
   - $\mathcal{Q}_s(\psi_s) \le 0$ unconditionally;
   - $\mathcal{Q}_s(\psi_s) = 0 \iff \operatorname{Re} s = \tfrac{1}{4}$;
   - $\mathcal{Q}_s(\psi_s) < 0 \iff \operatorname{Re} s \neq \tfrac{1}{4}$.

*Proof.* Since $\psi(a^{-1}) = a^{2s}\psi(a)$ for $a > 0$, $(I_{1/2 - \bar{s}}\psi)(a) = a^{2\bar{s} - 1} a^{2s}\psi(a) = a^{4\operatorname{Re} s - 1}\psi(a)$, so $\|(I_{1/2 - \bar{s}}\psi)(a)\|^2 = a^{8\operatorname{Re} s - 2}\|\psi(a)\|^2 = a^{4\delta}\|\psi(a)\|^2$. Splitting $(0, \infty) = (0, 1] \sqcup (1, \infty)$ and substituting $a = b^{-1}$ on $(0, 1)$ (using $\|\psi(b^{-1})\|^2 = b^{4\operatorname{Re} s}\|\psi(b)\|^2$ and $b^{4\operatorname{Re} s} b^{1/2} b^{-2} = b^{4\operatorname{Re} s - 3/2}$):
$$\int_0^\infty a^\alpha (a + a^{-1})^{-6}\|\psi(a)\|^2\,da = \int_1^\infty (b + b^{-1})^{-6}\bigl(b^\alpha + b^{4\operatorname{Re} s - 2 - \alpha}\bigr)\|\psi(b)\|^2\,db$$
for both $\alpha = -\tfrac{1}{2}$ (giving $4\operatorname{Re} s - 2 - (-\tfrac{1}{2}) = 2\delta - \tfrac{1}{2}$) and $\alpha = 4\delta - \tfrac{1}{2}$ (giving $4\operatorname{Re} s - 2 - (4\delta - \tfrac{1}{2}) = -2\delta - \tfrac{1}{2}$). Factoring out $b^{\delta - 1/2} = b^{2\operatorname{Re} s - 1}$ and setting $x := b^\delta > 0$:
$$\bigl(b^{-1/2} + b^{2\delta - 1/2}\bigr) - \bigl(b^{4\delta - 1/2} + b^{-2\delta - 1/2}\bigr) = b^{2\operatorname{Re} s - 1}\bigl((x^{-1} + x) - (x^3 + x^{-3})\bigr) = -b^{2\operatorname{Re} s - 1}(x + x^{-1})(x - x^{-1})^2,$$
by the exact algebraic identity $(x + x^{-1}) - (x^3 + x^{-3}) = -(x + x^{-1})(x - x^{-1})^2$ for $x \neq 0$. This proves (1).

For (2), if $\operatorname{Re} s = \tfrac{1}{4}$, then $\delta = 0$, $b^0 - b^0 = 0$, so $\mathcal{Q}_s(\psi_s) = 0$. Conversely, suppose $\operatorname{Re} s \neq \tfrac{1}{4}$, so $\delta \neq 0$. At $b = 1$, $\psi_s(1) = \mathrm{extension}(2s, 1, 0, \mathrm{state}(2s), 0) = (\mathrm{state}(2s))(0) = 2\zeta(2s - 1) \neq 0$. Since $\psi_s$ is continuous at $1$, there exists $r \in (0, 1)$ such that $\|\psi_s(b)\| \ge \tfrac{1}{2}\|\psi_s(1)\| > 0$ for all $b \in [1 + r/4, 1 + r/2]$. On $K = [1 + r/4, 1 + r/2] \subset (1, \infty)$, every factor $(b + b^{-1})^{-6}$, $b^{2\operatorname{Re} s - 1}$, $(b^\delta + b^{-\delta})$, $(b^\delta - b^{-\delta})^2$ (since $b > 1$ and $\delta \neq 0 \implies b^\delta \neq b^{-\delta}$), and $\|\psi_s(b)\|^2$ is continuous and strictly positive, hence bounded below on $K$ by some $m_0 > 0$. Since $\mathrm{volume}(1 + r/4, 1 + r/2] = r/4 > 0$ and the integrand is nonnegative everywhere on $(1, \infty)$, the integral over $(1, \infty)$ is $\ge m_0 (r/4) > 0$, whence $\mathcal{Q}_s(\psi_s) \le -m_0(r/4) < 0$. $\square$

### 3. Theorem 2C — Unconditional Linear Independence of Resonant Period Cores and Canonical Krein–Lax–Phillips Realization
Define the upper Mayer resonance set
$$\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{PM}}^+ := \{s \in \mathbb{C} \mid 0 < \operatorname{Re} s < \tfrac{1}{2},\; 0 < \operatorname{Im} s,\; \mathrm{IsMayerScatteringResonance}(s)\}.$$
For each $s \in \mathbb{C}$, consider the actual Mayer scattering core $\phi_s^{\mathrm{core}} := \mathrm{fieldCore}(2s) : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{C}$ and its actual Cauchy-transformed Lewis period core $\psi_s^{\mathrm{core}} := \mathrm{positiveMellinConvolution}(\mathrm{cauchyKernel}(2s), \mathrm{fieldCore}(2s)) : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{C}$.

**Theorem 2C.1 (Unconditional Linear Independence on $(0, \infty)$).**
Both families $\{\phi_s^{\mathrm{core}}\}_{s \in \mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{PM}}^+}$ and $\{\psi_s^{\mathrm{core}}\}_{s \in \mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{PM}}^+}$ are linearly independent over $\mathbb{C}$ as functions $\mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{C}$ (in fact, even after restriction to $(0, \infty)$).

*Proof.* Let $s_1, \dots, s_n \in \mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{PM}}^+$ be distinct ($n \ge 1$) and suppose $\sum_{j=1}^n c_j \psi_{s_j}^{\mathrm{core}}(a) = 0$ for all $a > 0$ (the proof for $\phi_{s_j}^{\mathrm{core}}$ is identical with the factor $m_{\mathrm{Cauchy}}(2s_j, u)$ omitted). Set $\mu := \min_{1 \le j \le n} (2\operatorname{Re} s_j) \in (0, 1)$. For every $u$ in the non-empty open domain $U_0 := \{u \in \mathbb{C} \mid 0 < \operatorname{Re} u < \mu,\; 0 < \operatorname{Im} u\}$, we have $0 < \operatorname{Re} u < 1$ and $0 < \operatorname{Re}(2s_j - u) < 2\operatorname{Re} s_j < 1$, and $-1 < \operatorname{Re} u < 2\operatorname{Re} s_j + 1$. By `MayerCoreMellin.core_hasMellin_gammaR`, `MayerCauchyMellinSymbol.cauchyKernel_hasMellin`, and `RHTower.hasMellin_positiveMellinConvolution`, each $\psi_{s_j}^{\mathrm{core}}$ has absolutely convergent Mellin transform on $U_0$ given by
$$\mathcal{M}[\psi_{s_j}^{\mathrm{core}}](u) = m_{\mathrm{Cauchy}}(2s_j, u)\,\frac{\Lambda(u)\,\Lambda(2s_j - u)}{\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(2s_j)} = m_{\mathrm{Cauchy}}(2s_j, u)\,\frac{4\,\xi(u)\,\xi(2s_j - u)}{\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(2s_j)\,u(u-1)(2s_j - u)(2s_j - 1 - u)},$$
using $\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(z)\zeta(z) = \Lambda(z) = 2\xi(z)/(z(z-1))$ for $z \notin \{0, 1\}$ (`RHTower.xi_eq_half_mul`). Thus $\sum_{j=1}^n c_j \mathcal{M}[\psi_{s_j}^{\mathrm{core}}](u) = 0$ on $U_0$. Clearing denominators by $u(u-1)\prod_{m=1}^n (2s_m - u)(2s_m - 1 - u)$, define on the convex (hence preconnected) open quarter-plane $U := \{u \in \mathbb{C} \mid \operatorname{Re} u < \mu,\; 0 < \operatorname{Im} u\} \supset U_0$:
$$F(u) := \sum_{j=1}^n \frac{4 c_j}{\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(2s_j)}\,m_{\mathrm{Cauchy}}(2s_j, u)\,\xi(u)\,\xi(2s_j - u) \prod_{m \neq j} (2s_m - u)(2s_m - 1 - u).$$
On $U$: $\xi$ is entire (`xi_analyticOnNhd_univ`), while $m_{\mathrm{Cauchy}}(2s_j, u) = \Gamma(\tfrac{u+1}{2})\Gamma(\tfrac{2s_j + 1 - u}{2})/(\pi^{1/2}\Gamma(\tfrac{2s_j + 1}{2}))$ is analytic and nowhere zero on $U$ because $\operatorname{Im}(\tfrac{u+1}{2}) = \tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{Im} u > 0$ (avoiding $-\mathbb{N}$) and $\operatorname{Re}(\tfrac{2s_j + 1 - u}{2}) > \tfrac{\mu + 1 - \mu}{2} = \tfrac{1}{2} > 0$. Since $F$ is analytic on $U$ and vanishes on the non-empty open subset $U_0 \subset U$ (e.g. near $\tfrac{\mu}{2} + i$), the identity theorem (`AnalyticOnNhd.eqOn_zero_of_preconnected_of_eventuallyEq_zero`) gives $F(u) = 0$ for all $u \in U$. Fix $k \in \{1, \dots, n\}$ and evaluate at $u_k := 2s_k - 1$. Since $\operatorname{Re} u_k = 2\operatorname{Re} s_k - 1 < 0 < \mu$ and $\operatorname{Im} u_k = 2\operatorname{Im} s_k > 0$, $u_k \in U$! Every $j \neq k$ term vanishes at $u_k$ via the factor $(2s_k - 1 - u_k) = 0$, leaving
$$0 = F(u_k) = \frac{4 c_k}{\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(2s_k)}\,m_{\mathrm{Cauchy}}(2s_k, u_k)\,\xi(2s_k - 1)\,\xi(1)\prod_{m \neq k} (2s_m - 2s_k + 1)(2s_m - 2s_k).$$
Here $\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(2s_k) \neq 0$, $m_{\mathrm{Cauchy}}(2s_k, u_k) \neq 0$ ($u_k \in U$), $\xi(1) = \tfrac{1}{2} \neq 0$, $\xi(2s_k - 1) = \xi(2 - 2s_k) \neq 0$ ($\operatorname{Re}(2 - 2s_k) > 1$), $2s_m - 2s_k \neq 0$ ($s_m \neq s_k$), and $\operatorname{Re}(2s_m - 2s_k + 1) = 1 + 2\operatorname{Re} s_m - 2\operatorname{Re} s_k > 0$, so $2s_m - 2s_k + 1 \neq 0$. Hence $c_k = 0$ for every $k$. $\square$

**Theorem 2C.2 (Canonical $s$-Independent Krein–Lax–Phillips Realization on $\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{PM}}$).**
Let $\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{PM}} := \operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{\psi_s^{\mathrm{core}} \mid s \in \mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{PM}}^+\} \subset (\mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{C})$ be the actual function subspace spanned by the resonant Lewis period cores. By Theorem 2C.1, $b(s) := \psi_s^{\mathrm{core}}$ forms a `Module.Basis` of $\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{PM}}$ indexed by $\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{PM}}^+$. Notice $s \in \mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{PM}}^+ \iff s^\sharp := \tfrac{1}{2} - \bar{s} \in \mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{PM}}^+$ (since $0 < \tfrac{1}{2} - \operatorname{Re} s < \tfrac{1}{2}$, $\operatorname{Im} s^\sharp = \operatorname{Im} s > 0$, and $2s^\sharp = 1 - 2\bar{s}$). Define on $\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{PM}}$:
