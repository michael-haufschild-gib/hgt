# Can one-to-two exchanges cover density one?

- **Stage** 3-formalized
- **Theorem** For each \(\varepsilon>0\), every sufficiently large \((X,2X]\) has a matching by actual one-to-two prime exchanges with at most \(\varepsilon X\) unmatched squarefree vertices and displacement at most \(\varepsilon X\).

**A band of reversible exchanges.** Fix an integer \(J\ge1\). In each octave \((T,2T]\), divide the interval into \(J\) equal sharp cells. The deficient semiprimes \(w=qr\) with \(2T^{1/3}<q\le T^{5/12}\) lie in the cube-root band. In every cell their number is at most its prime count once \(T\) is sufficiently large, uniformly in its endpoints. Pair them to distinct primes \(p\) in that cell. Both ratios \(p/w,w/p\) are at most \(1+1/J\). Over the entire octave there are at least \(T/(260\log T)\) pairs by the deficient-product lower bound.

Take \(Y=2^k\) and the \(k\) separated octaves with \(t=2^j\), \(T=t^{12}\), \(k\le j<2k\). Each prime appearing in these exchanges belongs to \(B=(Y,Y^{24}]\). Different octaves use different products and different single primes. Each supplies at least \(t^{12}/(260\log t)\) switches. Since each paired prime is at most \(2t^{12}\) and \(\log t\le2k\log2\), the reciprocal sum of the paired primes in \(B\) is at least \(1/(1040\log 2)\).

**Local density.** Write \(P=\prod_{p\in B}p\). For a paired prime \(p\), exactly \(\varphi(P/p)\) residues modulo \(P\) have gcd \(p\) with \(P\). These classes are disjoint. Since \(P\) is squarefree, their relative mass is \((\varphi(P)/P)/(p-1)\). Mertens' first theorem gives \(\sum_{p\in B}1/p\le25\) for large \(k\), using \(\log p\ge\log Y\) and \(\log(Y^{24})=24\log Y\). Hence \(\varphi(P)/P=\prod_B(1-1/p)\ge e^{-50}\) by \(\log(1-z)\ge-2z\) on \([0,1/2]\). The probability of eligibility in one band is bounded below by one absolute \(\kappa>0\), independently of both \(k\) and \(J\).

**Composition without retries.** Choose finitely many disjoint bands, each above the threshold for \(J\). In each band eligibility means that the complete local divisor is either member of a designated pair. On a squarefree integer, switch the local divisor in the *first* eligible band and retain its complementary cofactor. This changes the Möbius sign, preserves squarefreeness, leaves all other bands' local divisors intact, and is its own inverse. The Chinese remainder theorem bounds the fraction failing every test in a complete period by \((1-\kappa)^m\) for \(m\) bands. The period is fixed before \(X\to\infty\), so its incomplete part costs \(O(1)\) with respect to \(X\).

Restrict this involution to pairs entirely in \((X,2X]\). If \((1+1/J)X<n\le2X/(1+1/J)\), either switch remains in the block. The two discarded boundary strips contain at most \(3X/J+2\) integers and the displacement of a retained switch is at most \(2X/J\). Choose \(J\), then \(m\), then \(X\) to obtain the target. The same involution acts on all squarefree integers for a given tolerance. At every sharp prefix its fixed points and the vertices with partner above the cutoff recover the Möbius sum exactly. The former are confined to the missed residue classes, the latter to the last interval of width \(X/J\). Thus one fixed map gives at most \(\varepsilon X\) unpaid vertices for every sufficiently large prefix. A power rate requires estimates beyond the fixed-period argument.

A fixed finite set of permitted primes has a positive-density coprime obstruction. The set of permitted primes here depends on the requested tolerance.
