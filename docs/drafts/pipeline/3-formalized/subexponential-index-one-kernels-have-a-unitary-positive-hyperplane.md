# The index-one dichotomy and its subexponential boundary

- **ID:** index-one-unitary-hyperplane
- **Stage:** 3-formalized.
- **Type:** THEOREM (Lean)
- **Source:** B9 in `docs/reports/review-3110/route-obstacles.md`.
- **Statement:** Every Hermitian kernel of negative index at most one on an
  abelian group either has a positive unitary-character mass hyperplane,
  or is `p + pairKernel χ ω`, with `p` positive definite, `χ` nonunitary,
  and `ω ≠ 0`. Cyclic subexponential growth excludes the second alternative.
- **Count:** Contribution 4; supporting lemmas are not separate contributions.

## Consequence

If for every `g` and every `ε>0` there is `C` with
`|k(g^n)| ≤ C exp(ε n)` for every natural `n`, some unitary character has
positive mass hyperplane. Equivalently, a unitary twist of `k` is
conditionally positive. No growth assumption on translated coefficients
`k(h g^n)` is made. No freeness or countability is needed.

## Paper proof [PROVED HERE]

Positive-definite kernels admit the trivial unitary character. Otherwise
complete the positive column complement as in the companion theorem. This
realizes the kernel as `k(g)=B(e₀,Tg e₀)` in `H×ℂ`, where
`B((x,a),(y,b))=⟪x,y⟫-conjugate(a)b`. Translations are commuting bounded
invertible form-isometries, and the orbit of `e₀` spans a dense subspace.
There is a common nonpositive line generated by `w=(z,1)`; write
`Tg w=α(g)w`. If `α` is unitary, the reflected eigencharacter is unitary
and its mass hyperplane is positive, as already formalized.

Suppose `λ=α(g₀)` has modulus different from one. Form preservation gives
`B(w,w)=|λ|²B(w,w)`, hence `B(w,w)=0` and `‖z‖=1`. Put
`d=(z/2,-1/2)` and `K=z⊥⊆H`. Then `B(w,d)=1` and both `w,d` are null.
Every vector has unique coordinates

`v=R(u)+a w+b d`,
`u=v.first-⟪z,v.first⟫z`,
`a=(⟪z,v.first⟫+v.second)/2`,
`b=⟪z,v.first⟫-v.second`,

where `R(u)=(u,0)`. In these coordinates the form is
`⟪u,u′⟫+conjugate(a)b′+conjugate(b)a′`.

Let `T=Tg₀` and `μ=1/conjugate(λ)`. Invariance gives `b(Tv)=μb(v)`.
Thus on `b=0`,

`T R(u)=R(Uu)+β(u)w`.

The coordinate map `U:K→K` preserves the Hilbert inner product. Its inverse
is the positive coordinate of `T⁻¹R(u)`: applying `T⁻¹T` or `TT⁻¹` cancels
the residual multiple of `w`, since both operators preserve `ℂw`. Hence
`U` is unitary on the complete Hilbert space `K`.
Write `Td=R(q)+γw+μd`. The off-circle resolvent gives the unique solution
`x` of `(U-μ)x=-q`. Also `λ≠μ`, since equality would force `|λ|=1`.
Therefore

`v=d+R(x)-(γ+β(x))/(λ-μ) w`

satisfies `Tv=μv` and `B(w,v)=1`. Its square is zero by form preservation
and `|μ|≠1`. The `μ`-eigenspace is one-dimensional by the formalized
index-one nonunit-eigenspace theorem. Every commuting translation preserves
it, so `Tg v=β₀(g)v` for a character `β₀`. Pairing the two eigenvectors
gives `conjugate(α(g))β₀(g)=1`; hence `β₀=reflectChar α`.

Set `a=B(v,e₀)`, `b=B(w,e₀)` and `r=e₀-a w-b v`. Then `r` is orthogonal
to both null lines. Their common orthogonal complement is invariant and
positive: it lies in `w⊥`, whose form is nonnegative. Thus
`p(g)=B(r,Tg r)` is positive definite. Expanding the coefficient gives

`k(g)=p(g)+conjugate(b)a α(g)+conjugate(a)b reflectChar α(g)`.

Both `a` and `b` are nonzero. If either pairing vanished on `e₀`, its
eigencharacter transformation law would make it vanish on every translate
of `e₀`, hence on their dense span. Continuity would make it the zero
functional, contradicting `B(w,v)=B(v,w)=1`. Therefore
`ω=conjugate(b)a≠0`, proving the dichotomy.

For the growth consequence choose `g₀` with `|χ(g₀)|≠1`. The two pair nodes
`χ(g₀)` and `1/conjugate(χ(g₀))` are distinct; one has modulus `R>1`.
Choose `1<r<R` and `ε=log r`. Cyclic subexponential growth gives a bound
`C r^n`. HKT `not_norm_le_of_lt_depth`, applied to this one nonzero pair
and its positive remainder, forbids that bound. Only the unitary-hyperplane
alternative remains.

## Exact growth boundary

The companion `quadratic-growth-is-the-unitary-index-one-boundary.md`
formalizes the converse: a positive unitary-character hyperplane gives
cyclic quadratic growth. Thus the unitary-hyperplane, cyclic subexponential,
and cyclic quadratic conditions are equivalent. This strengthens the same
contribution.
