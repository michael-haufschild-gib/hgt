## Statement

Let a connected observable graph carry reversible multiplicative edge calibrations `c(u,v)` in
an arbitrary group.  The ordered reading along a finite walk is the product of its edge
calibrations.  A local change of clock standards `g` acts by

```text
c^g(u,v) = g(u)^(-1) c(u,v) g(v).
```

Then:

1. open-walk readings transform only at their endpoints;
2. closed-walk readings transform by conjugation, and are literally gauge invariant for an
   abelian calibration group;
3. every scalar-field calibration `c(u,v)=g(u)^(-1)g(v)` telescopes to the endpoint ratio and has
   unit holonomy on every loop;
4. reversible calibration is induced by one global scalar field if and only if every finite
   closed-walk reading is the unit;
5. a single nonunit loop is a positive obstruction to every scalar-clock presentation.

This is a constraint theorem about which clock comparisons can arise from a scalar potential.  A
walk is only an observable incidence word; it is not temporal evolution.

## Physics source and transfer

Weyl geometry transports a clock or vector scale by a multiplicative line factor.  In the usual
abelian presentation, the ratio after a closed path is

```text
l/l_0 = exp((1/2) integral_loop sigma).
```

The second-clock effect is the nonunit closed-loop reading.  In Weyl-integrable geometry
`sigma=d phi`, the loop integral vanishes and the scale is path independent.  The discrete theorem
formalized here retains exactly that endpoint/loop algebra and drops the spacetime, differentiable,
and dynamical assumptions.

Primary literature used for the transfer:

- R. Avalos, F. Dahia, and C. Romero, *A note on the problem of proper time in Weyl space-time*,
  arXiv:1611.10198.
- T. A. T. Sanomiya, I. P. Lobo, J. B. Formiga, F. Dahia, and C. Romero, *An Invariant Approach
  to Weyl's unified field theory*, arXiv:2002.00285.
- I. Quiros, *Gauge invariant approach to nonmetricity theories and the second clock effect*,
  arXiv:2201.03076.
- E. E. Flanagan, A. M. Grant, A. I. Harte, and D. A. Nichols, *Persistent gravitational wave
  observables: general framework*, arXiv:1901.00021.  Their proper-time, rotation, and holonomy
  memories motivate retaining conjugacy-class readings rather than a chosen gauge representative.
- V. S. H. Lee and K. M. Zurek, *Proper Time Observables of General Gravitational Perturbations in
  Laser Interferometry-based Gravitational Wave Detectors*, arXiv:2408.03363.  Their proper-time
  observable is explicitly gauge invariant and agrees with detector strain for a plane wave.

The physical status of the second-clock effect in particular Weyl gauge theories is disputed; the
formal theorem does not depend on that dispute.  It uses only the common gauge/holonomy algebra.

## RH boundary

The theorem does not prove RH.  It does sharpen route triage: any proposed scalar clock or scalar
field behind an `RHTower` holonomy must make every closed incidence word trivial.  Therefore the
already nontrivial Xi puncture/root-trace holonomies cannot be generated by a globally defined
scalar clock on the same connected observable graph.  A live RH route must retain a puncture,
branch, source divisor, or genuinely non-flat cocycle; scalar-clock deparametrization erases the
very charge it needs to detect.
