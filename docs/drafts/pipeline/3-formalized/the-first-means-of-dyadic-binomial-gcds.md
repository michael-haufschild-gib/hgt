# First means of the dyadic binomial gcds

Put $q=2^j$ and $G_j(n)=\gcd_{1\le k\le j}\binom{2^kn}{2^{k-1}n}$. RH is equivalent to the following statement. For every $1/2<b<1$, arbitrarily large fixed $j$ admit $A,C$ such that
$$\left|\sum_{n<N}(\log G_j(n)-Aqn)\right|\le C N^{1+b}\qquad(N\ge1).$$
Under RH every depth works with the harmonic density of the quotient class as slope. [Lean equivalence](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExceptionalLayerTheory/FirstMeansOfDyadicBinomialGcdsAndTheZetaZeros.lean)

For $a_q(d)=\mathbf1_{q\mid d+1}-\mathbf1_{q\mid d}$, put $S_q(X)=\sum_{m\le X}\Lambda(m)\mathbf1_{q\mid\lfloor X/m\rfloor+1}$ and let $I_q$ count the same integers with unit weights. The divisor hyperbola at $\lfloor\sqrt X\rfloor$ gives $|I_q(X)-c_qX|\le18\sqrt X$, uniformly in $q\ge2$, with $c_q=\sum_{d\ge1}a_q(d)/d$. Rounding the short row and transposing the tail each cost at most $\sqrt X$, since every jump prefix is zero or one. The omitted harmonic tail is $O(1/\sqrt X)$. [Integer density](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExceptionalLayerTheory/TheIntegerDensityOfTheLastQuotientClass.lean)

Discrete partial summation bounds $X^{-1}|\sum_{k<X}(S_q(k)-I_q(k))|$ by $\sum_{n\le X}|\psi(n)-n|/(n+1)$. Above the zero abscissa this is $O_b(X^b)$. Sampling at multiples of $q$ changes the cumulative prime error by $O_q(X^{3/2})$ after replacing the counts by log gcds. This uses the $\log(k+1)$ bound per increment and the square-root comparison at the sampled points. [First-mean estimate](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExceptionalLayerTheory/FirstMeansOfBoundedQuotientClasses.lean)

Conversely, for the centered increment sequence $c(n)=(\Lambda*a_q)(n)-A$, the triangular profile $P(y)=\sum_{n\ge1}c(n)(1-ny)_+$ satisfies $XP(1/X)=\sum_{k<X}(S_q(k)-Ak)$. It is affine between adjacent reciprocal integers, so a bound at the samples gives $P(y)=O(y^{-b})$. Its Mellin transform times $s(s+1)$ continues the coefficient series to $\Re s>b$. On the right this series is $(-\zeta'/\zeta)D_q-A\zeta$, where $D_q$ is the continued Dirichlet series of $a_q$. A zero $\rho$ to the right of $b$ forces $D_q(\rho)=0$. For each such zero, $D_q(\rho)\ne0$ at every sufficiently large dyadic width.
