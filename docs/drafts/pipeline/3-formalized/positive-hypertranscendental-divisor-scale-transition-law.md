## Statement

For \(K\ge12\), let \(c_{K,j}(n)\in\{0,1\}\) be the complete largest-prime-exponent
resolution: \(j=0\) is the \(K\)-free head and \(j\ge1\) is the shell in which the largest prime
exponent is \(K+j-1\). Let
\[
 S_{K,j}(s)=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{c_{K,j}(n)}{n^s}
            =\zeta(s)M_{K,j}(s)
\]
on \(\Re s>1\), and set \(P_{K,N}=\sum_{j=0}^{N}S_{K,j}\).

Then:

1. the \(c_{K,j}\) are pairwise disjoint and sum coefficientwise to \(1\);
2. \((M_{K,j})_{j\ge0}\) is differentially algebraically independent over the differential field
   generated by \(\zeta\);
3. every \(S_{K,j}\) has exactly the zeta divisor on \(\Re s>\tfrac12\), away from \(s=1\);
4. every finite prefix obeys the exact transition identity
   \[
   P_{K,N}(s)=\frac{\zeta(s)}{\zeta((K+N)s)};
   \]
5. for \(A_m(s)=\zeta(s)/\zeta(ms)\), every vanishing observation scale
   \(\varepsilon_n\to0\) is selected by its renormalized depth
   \(\delta_n=m_n\varepsilon_n\):
   \[
   \delta_n\to\lambda,\quad \zeta(\lambda u)\ne0,\quad\lambda u\ne1
   \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
   A_{m_n}(\varepsilon_nu)\to\frac{\zeta(0)}{\zeta(\lambda u)};
   \]
   while
   \[
   \Re(\delta_nu)\to+\infty
   \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
   A_{m_n}(\varepsilon_nu)\to\zeta(0).
   \]
   Consequently \(\lambda=0\) gives the identity limit \(1\), every finite nonzero
   \(\lambda\) retains a scaled reciprocal zeta divisor, and right-half-plane escape erases the
   divisor to the endpoint constant.
6. in particular,
   \[
   A_m(u/m)\to-\frac1{2\zeta(u)},\qquad
   A_{(n+1)^2}(u/(n+1))\to-\frac12,\qquad
   A_{n+1}(u/(n+1)^2)\to1,
   \]
   with the first statement read away from the poles of the limiting meromorphic function.

This conjunction is called the **positive hypertranscendental divisor scale-transition law**.
The finite nonzero renormalized-depth window is exactly the boundary regime that retains divisor
information.

## Complete proof

### Positive arithmetic resolution

Every integer \(n\) has a finite multiset of prime exponents. Exactly one of the following holds:
all exponents are \(<K\), or their maximum equals \(K+j-1\) for a unique \(j\ge1\). Hence the
indicators are \(0/1\), pairwise disjoint, and sum coefficientwise to \(1\). Absolute convergence
on \(\Re s>1\) permits termwise summation and gives the stated channel Dirichlet series.

For \(j=0\), the Euler product is
\[
M_{K,0}(s)=\zeta(Ks)^{-1}.
\]
For \(j\ge1\), subtracting consecutive bounded-exponent Euler products gives
\[
M_{K,j}(s)=\zeta((K+j)s)^{-1}-\zeta((K+j-1)s)^{-1}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\sum_{j=0}^{N}M_{K,j}(s)=\zeta((K+N)s)^{-1},
\]
and multiplication by \(\zeta(s)\) proves the exact prefix identity.

### Relative hypertranscendence

Order a nonzero differential polynomial relation in finitely many \(M_{K,j}\) by its largest
dilation exponent. After replacing shells by consecutive differences, the inverse transformation
is unitriangular, so a relation would give one among
\(\zeta(Ks)^{-1},\ldots,\zeta((K+r)s)^{-1}\). The established dilation-separation theorem for
\(K\ge12\) isolates the unique largest exponential scale in the right-end asymptotics of every
differential monomial. Its coefficient cannot cancel against lower dilation scales, including
coefficients from the full zeta differential field. Descending on the leading monomial forces every
coefficient to vanish. Thus the family is differentially algebraically independent over that
field.

### Common divisor

On \(\Re s>\tfrac12\), each multiplier \(M_{K,j}\) is holomorphic and nowhere zero for \(K\ge12\).
The product rule for analytic order therefore gives
\[
\operatorname{ord}_s S_{K,j}=\operatorname{ord}_s\zeta
\operatorname{ord}_sM_{K,j}=\operatorname{ord}_s\zeta
\]
for \(s\ne1\), with multiplicity.

### Scale selector

Write
\[
A_{m_n}(\varepsilon_nu)
=\frac{\zeta(\varepsilon_nu)}{\zeta(\delta_nu)},
\qquad \delta_n=m_n\varepsilon_n.
\]
Since zeta is holomorphic at \(0\), the numerator tends to
\(\zeta(0)=-\tfrac12\). If \(\delta_n\to\lambda\), continuity at
\(\lambda u\ne1\), together with \(\zeta(\lambda u)\ne0\), gives the finite-window limit.
For \(\lambda=0\), numerator and denominator have the same nonzero limit \(\zeta(0)\), hence the
limit is \(1\).

If \(\Re(\delta_nu)\to+\infty\), the absolutely convergent Dirichlet series and
\[
\left|\zeta(w)-1\right|\le\frac1{\Re w-1}\qquad(\Re w>1)
\]
give \(\zeta(\delta_nu)\to1\). The quotient therefore tends to \(\zeta(0)\).
Substitution of \(\varepsilon_n=(n+1)^{-1}\) with \(m_n=(n+1)^2\), and of
\(\varepsilon_n=(n+1)^{-2}\) with \(m_n=n+1\), proves the two displayed witness limits. Taking
\(\varepsilon_m=m^{-1}\) proves the reciprocal boundary.

Every argument is unconditional. No zero-distribution hypothesis enters.

## Hostile proof audit

- The telescope alone does not imply the theorem: disjoint positive coefficients, maximal relative
  differential rank, exact channelwise divisor replication, and the scale selector are independent
  components.
- “Unique boundary scale” means exactly finite nonzero renormalized depth among the proved
  convergent regimes. It does not exclude oscillatory scales and is not stated as a regular
  variation classification.
- Limits involving \(1/\zeta\) are meromorphic limits away from their poles; no totalized point
  value at a zero is interpreted analytically.
- The law concerns zeta. Archimedean completion and Xi reflection are separate consequences, not
  produced by the bounded-exponent source.
- The theorem neither proves RH nor excludes off-line zeros.
