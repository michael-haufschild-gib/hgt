# Haar period defects have a finite affine orbit

- **ID:** HAAR-PERIOD-DEFECT-AFFINE-ORBIT
- **Stage:** 3-formalized
- **Type:** THEOREM (Lean verified)
- **Parent:** [Relative Haar splitting](finite-index-haar-carriers-split-modulo-polynomial-kernels.md)
- **Statement:** For a Hermitian affine kernel with period-one translation reading and negative index at most `r`, the affine orbit of the period defect `v_(T_1)−v_1` spans a finite-dimensional invariant space `S`, with `dim S≤r²`. In particular, this applies to every integer Haar successor.

## Dependency-closed paper proof

Let `R` span `v_(T_(q+1))−v_(T_q)`. Pairing each defect with `v_(T_b)` gives `k(T_(q+1−b))−k(T_(q−b))=0`, so `R` is totally isotropic. Translations preserve `R`; affine conjugation shows that every dilation image is also isotropic and translation-invariant. The affine group is generated by translations and dilations, hence `S=span_a D_aR`. The [isotropic unipotence theorem](../3-formalized/isotropic-translation-defects-are-unipotent.md) gives `(T_b−1)^r=0` on each image and therefore on `S`.

Let `N=(T_1−1)|S`. A vector `y∈ker N` is fixed by every integer translation. For `a=p/q`, therefore `T_a^q y=T_p y=y`. A periodic vector in a unipotent orbit over characteristic zero is fixed: induction on its nilpotence degree first makes `(T_a−1)y` fixed, then `T_a^q y=y+q(T_a−1)y` forces `(T_a−1)y=0`.

Thus every vector in `ker N` is fixed by all rational translations. Each affine translate of a period defect has the form `(T_a−1)v_g`, with `a=slope(g)`. Invariance of the column form makes it orthogonal to every common translation-fixed vector. Hence `S` is orthogonal to `ker N`; in particular `ker N` is totally isotropic and has dimension at most `r`.

Choose a linear projection `P` onto `ker N`. The map `x↦(P N^j x)_(0≤j<r)` is injective: starting from `N^r x=0`, descend in `j`; if `N^(j+1)x=0`, then `N^j x∈ker N` and `P N^j x=N^j x=0`. Thus `S` embeds into `(ker N)^r` and `dim S≤r·dim ker N≤r²`, without any prior finite-dimensionality assumption on `S`.

The defect space is explicitly affine-invariant and contains every translation-period defect.

This produces a covariant finite-dimensional defect space from periodicity itself. It does not yet construct a Hermitian zero-axis polynomial correction or split the remaining periodic carrier into positive and slope parts.
