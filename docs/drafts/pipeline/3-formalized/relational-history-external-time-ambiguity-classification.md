## Theorem A: arbitrary-cardinality relational clock quotient

Let `G` be any group, let `I` be any inhabited index type, and let a clock configuration be
`x : I -> G`. Fix an internal anchor `a : I` and define

```text
R_a(x)_i = x_a^(-1) x_i.
```

The diagonal change of clock origin is

```text
(g · x)_i = g x_i.
```

Then:

1. `R_a(g · x)=R_a(x)` for every `g`;
2. `R_a(x)=R_a(y)` iff there is a unique `g` with `y=g · x`;
3. the full configuration is reconstructed from `(x_a,R_a(x))`;
4. every origin-invariant reporter factors through `R_a`;
5. no reporter into `G` can be both origin-invariant and origin-covariant unless `G` is trivial.

No finiteness, commutativity, topology, differentiability, or quantum hypothesis is used. In
particular, adding infinitely many internal clocks never supplies the missing common origin.

### Proof

For (1),

```text
(g x_a)^(-1)(g x_i)=x_a^(-1)g^(-1)g x_i=x_a^(-1)x_i.
```

For (2), equality of ratios gives

```text
y_i=y_a x_a^(-1)x_i.
```

Thus `g=y_a x_a^(-1)` works. Evaluation at the anchor forces this value of `g`, proving
uniqueness. Formula (3) is `x_i=x_a R_a(x)_i`. For (4), applying origin invariance with
`g=x_a^(-1)` gives `f(x)=f(R_a(x))`. For (5), invariance and covariance would give
`g f(x)=f(x)` for every `g`; right cancellation forces every `g` to be the identity. ∎

## Theorem B: evolution without an external evolution parameter

Let `G` act on any state space `X`. A relational history is a function `h : G -> X` satisfying

```text
h(g t)=g · h(t).
```

Every relational history is uniquely of the form

```text
h_x(t)=t · x
```

for the conditional state `x=h(1)`. Under the diagonal action

```text
(g ⋆ h)(t)=g · h(g^(-1)t),
```

every such history is globally stationary:

```text
g ⋆ h_x = h_x.
```

Conversely, global stationarity under every `g` implies the relational-history law. Therefore
conditioning a stationary global history at an internal clock reading and ordinary covariant
state evolution contain exactly the same data. This holds for finite, compact, discrete,
continuous, and noncommutative clock groups whenever the relevant action exists.

### Proof

Put `t=1` in covariance to get `h(g)=g·h(1)`. Conversely the action law gives
`h_x(gt)=(gt)·x=g·(t·x)`. Finally,

```text
(g ⋆ h_x)(t)=g·((g^(-1)t)·x)=t·x=h_x(t).
```

For the converse, stationarity at `g^(-1)` and argument `gt` gives
`h(gt)=g·h(t)`. ∎

## Theorem C: exact residual ambiguity of a physically labelled law

Let `A` be any noncommutative ring of generators and observables, and let `S subset A` be the
physically labelled observable family. Define the infinitesimal observable law generated by `H`
as

```text
d_H(X)=H X-X H.
```

For any two candidate generators `H,K`,

```text
d_H(X)=d_K(X) for every X in S
  iff
H-K commutes with every X in S.                              (C1)
```

Hence the ambiguity after physical observable meanings are fixed is exactly the commutant
`S'`, not the set of arbitrary Hamiltonians. If `S'` consists only of scalar/central operators,
the physical law is unique modulo the ordinary invisible energy-origin/global-phase freedom.

For every finite-dimensional quantum system over a commutative scalar field, take `S` to be the
complete matrix observable algebra. Its commutant consists exactly of scalar matrices, so

```text
d_H(X)=d_K(X) for every matrix observable X
  iff
H-K=c I for one scalar c.
```

This is dimension-independent and includes the zero- and one-dimensional degeneracies.

### Proof

For each `X`,

```text
d_H(X)-d_K(X)=(H-K)X-X(H-K).
```

Therefore equality of the two derivations on `S` is equivalent pointwise to membership of
`H-K` in `S'`. ∎
