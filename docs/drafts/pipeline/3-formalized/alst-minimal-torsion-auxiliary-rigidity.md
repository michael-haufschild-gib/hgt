# Rigidity of minimal torsion auxiliaries

- **ID:** alst-minimal-torsion-auxiliary-rigidity
- **Stage:** 3-formalized
- **Type:** THEOREM (Lean)
- **Statement:** For a finite prime set with least prime `p`, an integer polynomial of length at most `p^(qr)` with multiplicity `r` at every order-`q` torsion point is an integer scalar multiple of `(u_p^q − 1)^r`.

## Mathematical purpose
Classify equality in the full torsion support law for integer auxiliaries; answer that case of ArithmeticHarmonicAnalysis Q2 and identify the exact auxiliaries available to ALST at the smallest admissible length. Realizes S1/S3 on the complete torsion locus, with exact multiplicity and price `p^(qr)`. Source-independent: does not separate zeta from integer-norm one-offs.

## Proof route
Fourier extraction of shifted Euler words detects any polynomial whose quotient exponents have total order below `r`. Choose a maximal quotient monomial. Among exponents with its residue, every other term has a zero binomial factor unless its quotient dominates coordinatewise; maximal total order then forces equality. Hence its extracted coefficient is its original nonzero coefficient, contradicting multiplicity.

For least prime `p`, a monomial of quotient order `d` has arithmetic weight at least `p^(qd)`. At length `p^(qr)`, subtract the coefficient of `u_p^(qr)` times `(u_p^q−1)^r`. Unique factorization makes `u_p^(qr)` the only exponent of boundary weight. The difference has strictly smaller length and still has multiplicity `r`, so detection kills it. The argument includes `r=0`; only `q>0` is required.

This strengthens a sharp inequality to a classification; the existing boundary example alone does not prove it. Integrality determines the scalar but is unnecessary for the Fourier detection lemma.
