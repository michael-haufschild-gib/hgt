# Gamma-Zeta Category Selection Trilemma

- **ID:** gamma-zeta-category-selection-trilemma
- **Stage:** 0-backlog
- **Type:** CONJECTURE / META-OBSTRUCTION
- **Source:** Gamma c-point control, vertical-strip tameness, DA strip-dense counterexample, G1, 2026-06-23
- **Statement:** The native `Gamma ⟂ zeta` proof route is blocked by a category-selection trilemma: natural categories are either too small and already exclude zeta by known bridge results, or too broad and admit DA strip-dense counterexamples.

## Goal

Explain why the current Gamma-zeta moonshot keeps slipping between true-but-known and false-too-broad statements.

This is not a proof of `Gamma ⟂ zeta`. It is a map of the admissible-category problem that any native proof must solve.

## Trilemma

### 1. Rational finite Gamma field: true but source-adjacent

Category:

`C(s)(Gamma, Gamma', ..., Gamma^(n))`

for finite `n`, and its finite rational unions.

Status:

- Strip-divisor tameness is plausible/partly established.
- Turing reported single-generator support.
- Lu-style proofs use the same vertical-strip + minimal Gamma-degree + Stirling mechanism.

Problem:

This category is too small. It proves field-level divisor non-encoding, not differential independence. It is also high-risk on novelty.

### 2. Full differential-algebraic closure: broad enough but false for strip invariants

Category:

all functions differentially algebraic over `C(s)<Gamma>`.

Status:

False for strip-divisor obstruction.

Counterexample:

`y = sin(s exp(s))`

is single-valued entire and differentially algebraic over `C(s)`, hence over `C(s)<Gamma>`, with zeros

`s = W_k(n*pi)`.

In any fixed vertical strip, it has `asymp T^2` zeros up to height `T`.

Problem:

Any invariant saying "DA-over-Gamma cannot create dense strip-confined divisors" is false.

### 3. Sigma-finite / Gamma-shift PV category: structurally sharp but excludes zeta by G1

Category:

parameterized PV extensions generated by finite linear difference systems over the Gamma shift field

`sigma_infty: s -> s+1`.

Status:

This category naturally excludes examples like `sin(s exp(s))` because they are not finite Gamma-shift modules. But it also excludes zeta-like single-shift behavior by G1: zeta satisfies no finite-order linear shift equation.

Problem:

The exclusion becomes a known BRIDGE result, not a new native proof mechanism.

## Recurrent category selected

`[PROVED; NOVELTY UNVERIFIED]` The card
`gamma-projective-boundary-finite-phase-law.md` supplies a precise intermediate category:

```text
holomorphic vertically uniformly almost-periodic functions
that are DA over C(s)<Gamma>.
```

It is large enough to contain every convergent Dirichlet-series restriction, including `zeta` in
`Re(s)>1`; it excludes `sin(s exp(s))` because that function is not vertically uniformly almost
periodic; and it is not a Gamma-shift PV category. Its invariant is rational Bohr-frequency rank.
The one-point projective boundary of every finite Gamma differential coefficient tuple forces every
member of this category to be autonomously DA and therefore finite-rank.

This positively resolves the category-selection problem on the recurrent side: an infinite prime
module must be carried explicitly rather than generated by a finite Gamma-differential state.

The zeta--Gamma differential-independence specialization is known independently: Wang--Yao (2024)
settled Markus's conjecture and Lue (2025) generalized it. The project-native value, if the novelty
audit survives, is the general projective-boundary and almost-periodic finite-phase law.

## Remaining live gap outside the recurrent category

A native proof route must define an intermediate category that satisfies all four constraints:

1. It is large enough that `zeta` being DA over `C(s)<Gamma>` would force `zeta` or `zeta'/zeta` into the category.
2. It is small enough to exclude DA strip-dense counterexamples like `sin(s exp(s))`.
3. It is not merely sigma-finite, or G1 kills it as a known bridge.
4. It carries a new invariant detecting FE-prime residual structure, not just growth, height, leading density, or strip location.

## Conjectural Reading

[CONJECTURE] Outside the recurrent holomorphic category, the real obstruction remains the absence
of an invariant stable under moving poles and general meromorphic DA operations. The general
divisor-residual problem still requires a presentation-independent Gamma subtraction; the
function-frequency problem no longer does.

## Falsification Tests

1. Produce a category satisfying the four live-gap constraints.
2. Show `zeta` DA over `C(s)<Gamma>` would imply membership in a sigma-finite or rational Gamma category, without assuming what must be proved.
3. Produce a DA-over-`C(s)<Gamma>` function with zeta-like FE-prime residual law, not just dense strip zeros.
4. Source-kill the trilemma by finding this exact category-selection obstruction in the Li-Ye/Lu/Nevanlinna literature.

## Candidate Escape From The Trilemma: Finite-Order DA Category

[CONJECTURE / live candidate] Replace the failed broad DA category by:

single-valued meromorphic functions that are differentially algebraic over `C(s)<Gamma>` and have finite Nevanlinna order, especially order `<=1`.

Why this may evade the counterexample:

- `sin(s exp(s))` is differentially algebraic over `C(s)`, but it has infinite order because `s exp(s)` creates double-exponential-size growth through `sin` along suitable directions.
- `zeta` and `zeta'/zeta` have order `1` / finite order behavior.
- Gamma-field elements such as `1/(Gamma-1)` have order `1`, but their `T log T` pole branches escape fixed vertical strips.

Possible theorem shape:

> A single-valued meromorphic function of finite order `<=1` that is differentially algebraic over `C(s)<Gamma>` cannot have a strip-confined divisor with zeta's residual FE-prime law.

This revives the earlier T* finite-order/single-valuedness route in a sharper way. It is not proved. It is also source-risky: it may reduce to classical Bank-Laine / Nevanlinna theory for finite-order solutions of algebraic differential equations. But it is a plausible intermediate category satisfying the trilemma constraints better than full DA closure or sigma-finite PV.

Immediate falsification target: find a single-valued meromorphic finite-order DA function over `C(s)` with `T log T` zeros inside a fixed vertical strip. The previous counterexamples fail this gate: `sin(s exp(s))` is infinite order; `tan(s log s)` is multivalued.

### Finite Order Alone Is Too Broad

The finite-order escape must be restricted near order `<=1`. Finite order without an order bound is false as a strip-divisor obstruction.

Counterexample: `y=sin(s^2)` is entire, D-finite, and has order `2`. Its zeros satisfy `s^2=n*pi`; for negative `n`, this gives zeros `s=± i sqrt(|n|pi)` on the imaginary axis. Hence the fixed vertical strip containing `Re(s)=0` has `asymp T^2` zeros up to height `T`.

Thus the live category cannot be merely "single-valued meromorphic DA of finite order." It must be order `<=1` or must include a sharper normal/maximal-type restriction.

### Source Check On The Order-One DA Boundary

First-pass source check points to classical Nevanlinna/Malmquist-Yosida territory. A.E. Eremenko, *Meromorphic solutions of algebraic differential equations*, Russian Math. Surveys 37:4 (1982), surveys meromorphic solutions of algebraic differential equations, including Malmquist-Yosida type theorems, admissible solutions, asymptotic behavior, and finite-order entire solution constraints. In particular, the survey records that for first-order equations with rational coefficients, entire solutions have asymptotic growth `T(r,y)=(const+o(1)) r^(n/2)` in a Malmquist theorem setting, and every entire transcendental first-order solution has order at least `1/2`.

Status impact: the order-one DA boundary is source-adjacent and should not be promoted without a precise novelty delta. It remains a useful attack target because the exact needed statement is narrower: single-valued meromorphic DA over `C(s)<Gamma>`, order `<=1`, with strip-confined `T log T` zeros or FE-prime residual law.

Source: A.E. Eremenko, `https://www.math.purdue.edu/~eremenko/dvi/mer_ade.pdf`.

## Candidate Escape: Finite Spectral-Rank Barrier

Turing correctly objected that `sin(s exp(s))` need not be excluded merely because it has dense strip zeros. It is archimedean/finitely generated in frequency content, not prime-arithmetic. The correct separator, if any, must be spectral rather than count-based.

However, the naive spectral statement also needs correction: DA functions over `C(s)` can synthesize any finite prime-log frequency because `e^{(log p)s}=p^s` satisfies a first-order linear ODE. Thus the possible separator is not the presence of a prime-log line, but the infinite non-finitely-generated prime-log module with arithmetic weights.

Canonical candidate card: `docs/drafts/pipeline/0-backlog/finite-spectral-rank-da-barrier.md`.

Live conjectural repair:

> order-`<=1`, single-valued meromorphic DA functions over `C(s)<Gamma>` have only finitely generated divisor-frequency modules after archimedean/Gamma residuals are removed; zeta's explicit-formula residual uses the infinite prime-log module `{log p}` with von Mangoldt weights.

Immediate falsifier: a single-valued order-`<=1` DA function over `C(s)` or `C(s)<Gamma>` whose divisor residual has infinitely many rationally independent discrete frequencies.

## Algebraic Gamma-Zeta Separation Is Source-Killed

Haiku proposed promoting the algebraic theorem `zeta` not algebraic over the finite Gamma differential field. This is rejected as novel.

Feng Lu, *On algebraic differential equations of gamma function and Riemann zeta function*, Ann. Acad. Sci. Fenn. Math. 44 (2019), 1031-1040, proves nonexistence of nontrivial distinguished polynomial relations involving `Gamma`, its derivatives, and `f(zeta)` for `f` with at least one zero, under small meromorphic coefficients. Taking `f(w)=w` covers the algebraic Gamma-zeta separation in the relevant polynomial form. The paper also cites prior Li-Ye and Lu 2018 results in the same lane.

Status impact:

- algebraic separation: valid source/import, not novel;
- project value: useful gate/control;
- promotion as new knowledge: KILL.

Therefore the trilemma remains: the algebraic/rational field theorem is true but too small and source-adjacent; native novelty can only appear in a sharper category/invariant beyond this source-known algebraic layer.

## Weld-Constraint Separator Audit

[HOLD/KILL pressure test, 2026-06-23]

Haiku proposed promoting a weld-constraint theorem: a function in the Gamma differential closure whose arithmetic quotient carries multiplicative structure should not satisfy the zeta Gamma-factor functional equation unless it has zeta's exact arithmetic data.

Professor audit: as stated this is not promotable. It splits into a trilemma.

1. If the weld constraint means only Gamma-completion plus functional-equation symmetry, it is false as a separator. Davenport-Heilbronn / Epstein-style objects give functional-equation or crystallinity witnesses with off-line zeros or non-zeta behavior. FE symmetry alone is RH-blind and zeta-nonunique.
2. If multiplicative coefficients or an Euler product are added, the separating content becomes classical: multiplicative coefficients are equivalent to Euler product / Bohr factorization, and non-multiplicative FE fakes fail that filter. Degree-one Selberg/converse-theorem rigidity then pushes toward Dirichlet `L`-functions, with `zeta` isolated only after pole/character/conductor hypotheses. This is an import, not a new native separator.
3. If the condition is strengthened to membership in the Gamma differential closure, the hard theorem remains exactly the old one: prove that the Gamma differential closure cannot produce an object with the required Selberg/Euler data. Available routes appear to reduce to Lu/ADH hypertranscendence or the infinite-prime-rank obstruction.

Additional correction: von Mangoldt weights are not Dirichlet coefficients of `zeta`; they occur in `-zeta'/zeta`. Any theorem using von Mangoldt weights is explicitly using prime-log/Euler-product content and therefore fails the support-purged invariant test.

Status: weld constraint remains a useful lens/category label for `M+`, but not a promoted separator. To rescue it, one must state an invariant that remains after removing FE-only symmetry, Euler-product/Bohr factorization, degree-one Selberg rigidity, and prime-log/von-Mangoldt content.

## Meta-Theorem Reduction Kill

[HOLD/KILL, 2026-06-23]

Wheeler proposed a category-level meta-theorem: every analytic witness of `zeta`'s non-`K_Gamma`-ness reduces to an Euler-product witness. Turing supplied the decisive kill for promotion.

The proposed proof used strip-confinement for the difference-differential closure, but the broad closure already has counterexamples:

1. `sin(s exp(s))` is DA and has dense strip zeros.
2. `(1-2^{-s})` has strip zeros and carries the finite prime frequency `log 2`.

Thus a proof based on plain strip count or strip confinement is false. If repaired by replacing strip count with the finite-spectral-rank separator, the statement becomes: every valid witness reduces to the infinite prime-frequency module. That is the already-identified prime/Euler-product obstruction, not a new `M+` theorem.

Status: keep the convergence as a useful map-cleanup statement, but do not promote it as novel. The only possible rescue would be a theorem about the `K_Gamma` closure that avoids both false strip-confinement and explicit infinite-prime-frequency content.
