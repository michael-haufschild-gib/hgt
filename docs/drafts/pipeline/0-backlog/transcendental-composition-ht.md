# Transcendental Composition and Hypertranscendence

- **ID:** transcendental-composition-ht
- **Stage:** 0-backlog
- **Type:** OPEN CONJECTURE / GATEKEEPER THEOREM
- **Source:** Turing id=1736, Wheeler id=1740; shared bottleneck for Feigenbaum-HT and Gamma∘Gamma prize
- **Priority:** HIGH — gatekeeper for two open lanes

## The question

**Does hypertranscendence preserve under transcendental composition?**

Precise form: if f and g are entire HT functions over ℂ(z), and g is transcendental (as a function), is f∘g(z) = f(g(z)) HT over ℂ(z)?

Known: **Algebraic composition preserves HT.** If p(z) is an algebraic function over ℂ (i.e., satisfies a polynomial over ℂ(z)) and f is HT, then f(p(z)) is HT. Proof: if f(p(z)) were DA over ℂ(z), set w = p(z) (algebraic over ℂ(z) means d/dz is expressible as a rational multiple of d/dw times an algebraic factor). The resulting ADE in w would make f DA over ℂ(w), contradicting f HT. [Proved: Turing id=1736]

Open: **The case where the inner function is transcendental.**

## Why it is hard — the evaluation-at-transcendental-point obstruction

Suppose Γ(Γ(z)) satisfies ADE P(z, f, f', ..., f^{(n)}) = 0. By the chain rule:

  f(z) = Γ(Γ(z))
  f'(z) = Γ'(Γ(z)) · Γ'(z)
  f''(z) = Γ''(Γ(z)) · (Γ'(z))² + Γ'(Γ(z)) · Γ''(z)
  ...

Each f^{(j)} is a polynomial in X := {Γ^{(i)}(z) : 0 ≤ i ≤ j} and Y := {Γ^{(i)}(Γ(z)) : 0 ≤ i ≤ j}.

So P(z, f, ..., f^{(n)}) = R(z, X, Y) = 0 for some polynomial R derived from P and the chain rule.

**Key question:** Are X and Y algebraically independent over ℂ(z)?

- X alone: algebraically independent over ℂ(z) (Γ is HT) ✓
- Y alone: Γ^{(j)}(w) are algebraically independent over ℂ(w) for any w by HT of Γ, but we need independence as FUNCTIONS OF z — they are evaluations of Γ^{(j)} at the transcendental point w = Γ(z).

The crux: is Γ^{(j)}(Γ(z)) algebraically independent of {Γ^{(i)}(z)} over ℂ(z)?

This is a **function-level analogue of "values of Γ at algebraically independent arguments are algebraically independent"** — itself a wide-open problem in transcendence theory (Baker-Wüstholz type, but for function values at irrational arguments).

## Why the algebraic-composition proof breaks

In the algebraic case: w = p(z) algebraic ⟹ d/dz = p'(z)·d/dw with p'(z) algebraic ⟹ any ADE over ℂ(z) can be rewritten as an ADE over ℂ(w) (with algebraic-in-w coefficients). The crucial property: the change-of-variable z ↦ w = p(z) makes z an algebraic function of w.

In the transcendental case: w = Γ(z) ⟹ z = Γ^{-1}(w) is NOT algebraic over w (not even meromorphic in a global domain). The transport ℂ(z) → ℂ(w) crosses a transcendental barrier — the field ℂ(z) viewed inside ℂ(w, z) has z transcendental over w, so there is no algebraic coefficient expression for d/dz in terms of d/dw.

## Instances / sub-cases

### Case 1: Γ(Γ(z)) — immediate prize

Target: prove Γ∘Γ HT. Needed: algebraic independence of {Γ^{(j)}(Γ(z))} from {Γ^{(i)}(z)} over ℂ(z).

Connection to asymptotic-scale-recession-ht: if Γ∘Γ is HT, then 1/Γ(Γ(z)) is also HT (by field closure) and by iterated Stirling: 1/Γ(Γ(z)) ~ e^{-Γ(z) log Γ(z)} ~ e^{-Γ(z)^{log Γ(z)}} — decays FASTER than any 1/Γ(z^k) = e^{-z^k log z}. This gives an HT/DA pair invisible at ALL finite z^{-z^k} transseries levels → the universal-defeat prize.

### Case 2: Feigenbaum g(g(λx))

Target: prove the Feigenbaum fixed-point g is HT via the renormalization functional equation g = R[g]. The structural difficulty: g(g(λx)) involves self-composition with the transcendental g. Any ADE for g would give a relation involving g^{(j)}(λx) (evaluated at the algebraic point λx) and g^{(j)}(g(λx)) (evaluated at the transcendental g(λx)). Same structure as Γ∘Γ.

The Feigenbaum equation itself says g^{(0)}(g^{(0)}(λx)) = −α·g^{(0)}(x), which is an identity in g and g∘g∘(linear) — a composition identity, not an ADE. Whether an ADE for g forces a contradiction through this identity requires the same transcendental-composition technology.

(b) close the Gamma∘Gamma prize (asymptotic-scale-recession universal defeat)

## Candidate approaches

### Attack 1: Nesterenko-type independence for functions

Nesterenko proved algebraic independence of values (π, e^π, Γ(1/4)) via modular functions. The function-level analogue: prove algebraic independence of Γ^{(j)}(Γ(z)) from Γ^{(i)}(z) as functions (not just at a single z-value). This would require a function-field version of the Nesterenko theorem — unknown.

### Attack 2: Differential algebra of composed functions

Study the differential ring generated by {Γ^{(j)}(Γ(z))} as a module over the ring ℂ(z)[{Γ^{(i)}(z)}]. If this module is free (no polynomial relations), that's algebraic independence. The module structure is governed by the chain rule tower — whether any polynomial relation can close on itself requires analyzing the degree-growth of the tower.

### Attack 3: Mahler-type substitution into the functional equation

Γ satisfies no functional equation of Mahler type (no σ_λ: z ↦ λz). But Γ(z+1) = z·Γ(z) is a SHIFT functional equation. Composing: Γ(Γ(z)+1) = Γ(z)·Γ(Γ(z)). This is an identity relating Γ(Γ(z)) to Γ(z)·Γ(Γ(z)) — i.e., a DIFFERENCE equation for H(z) = Γ(Γ(z)) of the form H(z) shifted by Γ(z)'s shift-argument satisfies H = f·H. This might give a Mahler/difference-Galois approach to H's transcendence. [OPEN — rough idea only]

### Attack 4: Ecalle alien calculus

In the Écalle transseries framework, composition with a transcendental function is handled by alien derivatives that act on the transmonomial basis. Whether the alien derivative structure of f∘g is trivially zero (f∘g DA) or nontrivial (f∘g HT) might follow from the alien derivatives of f and g separately. This requires expertise in resurgence theory not currently in the hgt corpus.

## 2026-06-24 Haiku route kill -- infinite Nevanlinna order does not imply HT

Wheeler id=1750 proposed a possible proof of `Gamma(Gamma(z))` being HT: show `Gamma∘Gamma` has infinite
Nevanlinna order, then cite a theorem that every meromorphic solution of an algebraic differential equation over
`C(z)` has finite order. The required theorem is false for nonlinear ADEs.

### Counterexample

Let

`F(z)=exp(exp(z))`.

Then `F` is entire and has infinite Nevanlinna/order growth. Along the positive real axis,

`log log M(r,F) >= log(exp(r)) = r`,

so the order is infinite.

But `F` is differentially algebraic over `C(z)`. Indeed

`F'=exp(z) F`,

`F''=(exp(z)+exp(2z)) F`,

hence

`F F'' - (F')^2 = exp(z) F^2 = F F'`.

Therefore `F` satisfies the constant-coefficient algebraic differential equation

`F F'' - (F')^2 - F F' = 0`.

So infinite Nevanlinna order cannot certify hypertranscendence for arbitrary nonlinear algebraic differential
equations over `C(z)`.

### Consequence for `Gamma∘Gamma`

The growth/pole explosion of `Gamma∘Gamma` may still be useful, but it cannot be paired with a nonexistent
"ADE solutions have finite order" theorem. A viable Nevanlinna route must use a narrower result whose hypotheses
exclude elementary DA infinite-order examples such as `exp(exp z)`: for example, a Bank--Kaufman style theorem
about Gamma-type pole distributions, a deficiency/pole-value condition, or a restricted ADE class. Until such a
precise theorem is found, `Gamma∘Gamma` remains open.

This kill reinforces the earlier local-inverse gate: neither abstract HT composition closure nor bare growth
separates `Gamma∘Gamma` from DA functions. The missing ingredient has to be source-specific.

## 2026-06-24 Haiku correction -- `Gamma∘Gamma` is not plane-meromorphic

There is a second defect in the Nevanlinna-order route: `Gamma(Gamma(z))` is not meromorphic on the whole complex
plane.

At every pole `a` of the inner Gamma function, `Gamma(z) -> infinity`. Locally,

`Gamma(z) = r_a/(z-a) + holomorphic`, with `r_a != 0`.

For all large integers `n`, the equation `Gamma(z)=-n` therefore has a solution near

`z = a - r_a/n + O(n^-2)`.

At such points the outer Gamma has a pole, because its argument is a negative integer. Thus the poles of
`Gamma(Gamma(z))` accumulate at each pole `a` of the inner Gamma. In particular, poles accumulate at `0`.

Consequently `Gamma∘Gamma` is not a meromorphic function on `C`, and its full-plane Nevanlinna characteristic
`T(r,Gamma∘Gamma)` is not defined in the usual sense. The right object is a local analytic germ, or a branch on a
restricted domain avoiding the inner poles and the accumulating preimage sets.

Any future proof of `Gamma∘Gamma` HT must therefore be formulated as a germ/continuation theorem. A possible
route is: if a germ satisfying an ADE over `C(z)` has analytic continuation constrained by the ODE, can it force
or forbid the finite accumulation pattern above? That is an open analytic-continuation obstruction, not the
full-plane Nevanlinna-order argument.

## 2026-06-24 Wheeler id=1851 — Theorem D: HT ⟺ logarithmic derivative is HT (equivalence) `[PROVED HERE]`

**Theorem D:** For nonzero meromorphic G over ℂ(z), G is HT over ℂ(z) if and only if (log G)' = G'/G is HT over ℂ(z).

**Proof (⟹): G HT → (log G)' HT.**
Suppose (log G)' = φ is DA. Then log G = ∫φ is DA (antiderivative of DA is DA). Then G = exp(log G) is DA [exp of DA is DA: exp(f)' = f'·exp(f), a 1st-order linear ODE with DA coefficient f']. But G is HT. Contradiction. ∎

**Proof (⟸): (log G)' HT → G HT.**
Suppose G is DA. Then G'/G = (log G)' is DA (derivative of DA divided by nonzero DA is DA). But (log G)' is HT. Contradiction. ∎

**Auxiliary: F' HT ⟺ F HT.** F' DA → F = ∫F' DA (antiderivative of DA is DA) ✓; F DA → F' DA ✓. Both directions close.

**Corollary 1:** G = exp(F) is HT ⟺ F is HT. Proof: G HT ⟺ (log G)' = F' HT ⟺ F HT.

**Corollary 2:** Γ HT ⟺ log Γ HT ⟺ ψ = (log Γ)' HT. All three hold.

**Corollary 3:** ζ is HT ⟺ ζ'/ζ is HT. New equivalence; characterizing ζ'/ζ may be easier.

**Corollary 4:** Λ = Γ_R·ζ HT ⟺ (log Λ)' = ψ_R + ζ'/ζ HT. ψ_R is HT (Theorem A). If ζ is DA: ψ_R + DA = HT (HT + nonzero DA = HT), so Λ HT — unconditional. If ζ HT: ζ'/ζ HT, and whether ψ_R + ζ'/ζ is HT depends on possible cancellation.

**Significance:** The log derivative is additive under products — (log fg)' = (log f)' + (log g)' — making it an HT-preserving operation with a clean equivalence structure. Theorem D converts "is F HT?" to "is F'/F HT?" and unlocks chain arguments via multiplication.
