# Finite Spectral-Rank DA Barrier

- **ID:** finite-spectral-rank-da-barrier
- **Stage:** 0-backlog
- **Type:** CONJECTURE / MOONSHOT
- **Source:** Turing spectral-separator attack and Professor correction, 2026-06-23
- **Statement:** The live Gamma-zeta separator is not zero count or strip density, but spectral rank: finite-order DA constructions over the Gamma field should carry only finitely generated divisor-frequency modules, whereas zeta's residual explicit-formula spectrum uses the infinite prime-log module.

## Goal

Repair the category-selection trilemma after the `sin(s exp(s))` counterexample.

The mistake was trying to exclude every DA function with dense strip-confined zeros. That is false and unnecessary. Dense DA divisors are allowed if their residual frequency content is finitely generated or archimedean. The target is zeta's infinite prime spectrum.

## Critical Correction

It is false to say that a DA function over `C(s)` cannot synthesize prime-log frequencies.

For any fixed prime `p`,

`e^{(log p)s} = p^s`

satisfies

`y' = (log p)y`,

so it is DA over `C(s)`. Therefore finite prime-log spectral lines are not external to the DA closure when the constant field is `C`.

The possible separator must be infinite-rank:

finite DA generation should allow only a finitely generated frequency module, while zeta's explicit formula uses the infinite rationally independent module generated by `{log p}`.

## Candidate Barrier

[CONJECTURE] Let `f` be a single-valued meromorphic function of order `<=1` that is differentially algebraic over `C(s)<Gamma>`. If the divisor-counting residual of `f` admits a discrete almost-periodic spectral expansion, then its frequency module is finitely generated over `Q`, modulo archimedean Gamma/Stirling terms.

Zeta violates this pattern: after subtracting the Gamma phase, its zero-counting residual `S(T)` is tied by the explicit formula to the prime-power frequencies `{log n}`, equivalently the infinite prime-log module `{log p}` with von Mangoldt weights.

## Examples And Non-Examples

Allowed:

1. `sin(s exp(s))`: DA over `C(s)`, strip-dense zeros, but archimedean/finitely generated frequency content; also infinite order.
2. `sin(s e^{i(log 2)s})`: DA over `C(s)` and carries a prime-looking frequency `log 2`, but only finite rank.
3. finite products over finitely many primes: still finite spectral rank.
4. Gamma c-point controls: `T log T` leading count, but inverse-Stirling/archimedean residuals and horizontally escaping branches.

Target excluded object:

`zeta` or `zeta'/zeta`, whose explicit-formula residual involves infinitely many prime frequencies with arithmetic weights.

## Falsification Tests

1. Construct a single-valued meromorphic order-`<=1` DA function over `C(s)` or `C(s)<Gamma>` whose divisor residual has infinitely many rationally independent discrete frequencies.
2. Construct such a function with the specific prime-log module `{log p}` and nontrivial von Mangoldt-like weights.
3. Show that zeta's `S(T)` does not define a legitimate invariant in this category, e.g. the spectral lines are an artifact of test functions rather than divisor residual.
4. Source-kill the conjecture by finding it as a known theorem or known false statement in exponential-polynomial, almost-periodic, Nevanlinna, or differential-algebraic literature.

## Why This Is Still A Leap

This conjecture is not proved. It is plausible only after imposing order/single-valuedness and finite DA generation. Full DA closure without order control is too large, and finite frequency examples are easy.

The main technical issue is to define "divisor-counting residual" and "discrete almost-periodic spectral expansion" precisely enough that:

1. zeta qualifies through the explicit formula;
2. DA functions like `sin(s exp(s))` do not falsely qualify as infinite-rank;
3. the property is invariant under the operations allowed in `C(s)<Gamma>`.

## First-Order Exponential Subcase

[CLASSICAL SUBCASE / FOOTHOLD] Let `K_Gamma=C(s)<Gamma>` and let `y` solve a rank-one linear differential equation

`y'/y = a(s)`, with `a in K_Gamma`.

A single-valued meromorphic solution can exist only when the residues of the one-form `a(s) ds` at finite poles are integers and its periods vanish. This is the standard monodromy condition for `exp(int a ds)` to descend to a meromorphic function on `C`.

In particular, if `a=c psi(s)+r(s)` with `psi=Gamma'/Gamma` and `r` rational, then the poles of `psi` at the non-positive integers have residue `-1`. The local monodromy of `exp(c int psi ds)=Gamma^c` is trivial only when the total residues are integers. Thus arbitrary nonintegral Gamma phases such as `Gamma^i` or `sin(log Gamma)` are multivalued, not single-valued meromorphic functions.

Consequence: the tempting counterexample route that uses noninteger Gamma-phase oscillation is blocked in the rank-one Liouvillian/PV subclass. Single-valuedness quantizes Gamma powers to integer exponents, which do not create zeta's infinite prime residual spectrum.

This is classical residue/monodromy, not global novelty. Its role is a foothold: the spectral-rank barrier survives the simplest first-order exponential extension. The next falsifier must be a higher-order or genuinely nonlinear order-`<=1` DA function with infinite rationally independent divisor-frequency module.
