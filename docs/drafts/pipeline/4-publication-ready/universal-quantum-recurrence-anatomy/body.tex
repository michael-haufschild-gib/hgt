\section{Question, scope, and relation to earlier work}

A recurrence theorem guarantees returns but does not identify their
accuracy dependence, the information retained by an observer, or the part
of an infinite-system spectrum visible in long-time statistics. These
questions involve different limits. We address two of them: exact
small-tolerance first returns in finite local networks, and complete
stationary survival statistics for arbitrary probability energy spectra.

Our main result concerns deterministic, unmonitored unitary evolution.
An observer here is a matrix whose entire displacement is measured, or a
finite family of such matrices. This includes density-matrix return.
It does not mean repeated quantum detection, nor merely return of a scalar
expectation value. Interference between terms in a scalar expectation
requires a different analysis.

Quantitative quantum recurrence has substantial antecedents. Bocchieri
and Loinger established the quantum recurrence theorem
\cite{BocchieriLoinger1957}. Gimeno and Sotoca obtained mixed-state upper
bounds \cite{GimenoSotoca2017}. Kotowski and Oszmaniec prove quantitative
bounds involving exit times and random-Hamiltonian saturation
\cite{KotowskiOszmaniec2026}. Gupta and Short bound simultaneous recurrence
of all states \cite{GuptaShort2026}. Our return time starts after a
\emph{fixed positive cutoff}; it is not their exit-based definition.
Our conclusion is an exact logarithmic small-tolerance exponent in physical
local coefficient space, with the observer quantifier inside one conull
Hamiltonian event.

Lee, Pilatowsky-Cameo, and Choi prove generic all-order spectral
nonresonance for local qubit lattice Hamiltonians and obtain bounds on
average recurrence intervals for typical high-temperature product states
\cite{LeePilatowskyChoi2026}. Thus generic local nonresonance is not a
priority claim of this paper. The extra input needed here is real affine
spectral nondegeneracy, strong enough for metric Diophantine extremality.
We establish this input on every finite connected graph with heterogeneous
local dimensions, and use it to classify all matrix observers
simultaneously.

The abstract mechanisms have classical parents. Diophantine approximation
controls torus returns; the nondegenerate-manifold theorem of Kleinbock
and Margulis supplies the metric lower bound \cite{KleinbockMargulis1998}.
Observation recurrence in general dynamical systems has been studied by
Rousseau and Saussol \cite{RousseauSaussol2010,Rousseau2012}; their
mixing-based hypotheses do not apply to our quasiperiodic flows.
Exact Floquet recurrences have a different arithmetic classification
\cite{AnandEtAl2026}. The stationary law below belongs to the
Wiener--almost-periodic tradition \cite{Wiener1930}; its role here is an
exact spectral interpretation, not a claim to have discovered pure-point
selection.

\section{A simultaneous law for every matrix observer}

Let $\Gamma=(V,\mathcal E)$ be a finite connected simple graph with
$V\ne\varnothing$. At site $v$ choose a complex Hilbert space of dimension
$q_v\geq2$, and put
\[
 \cH=\bigotimes_{v\in V}\C^{q_v},\qquad D=\prod_{v\in V}q_v.
\]
An operator supported on $S\subseteq V$ acts as the identity outside $S$.
Write $\cL_\Gamma$ for the real span of all Hermitian operators supported
on one site or on an edge of $\Gamma$. All Hermitian directions on these
supports are allowed. Fix real basis coordinates on this finite-dimensional
space; almost every refers to Lebesgue measure in these coordinates.
No random-matrix ensemble is assumed.

For $H\in\cL_\Gamma$, set $U_H(t)=\exp(-\ii tH)$, with $\hbar=1$.
Given a finite matrix family $\mathcal B=(B_a)_a$ and weights
$\lambda_a\geq0$, define
\begin{align}
 \Delta_{\mathcal B,H}(t)
   &=\sum_a\lambda_a\|U_H(t)B_aU_H(t)^*-B_a\|_{\HS}^2,
       \label{eq:defect}\\
 T_{\mathcal B,H}(\delta;t_0)
   &=\inf\{t\geq t_0:\Delta_{\mathcal B,H}(t)<\delta\},
       \qquad t_0>0,\quad \delta>0.                 \label{eq:return}
\end{align}
The matrices need not be Hermitian. Finite-dimensional recurrence makes
the set in \eqref{eq:return} nonempty.

For a simple $H$, use any orthonormal energy basis, labelled by
$1,\ldots,D$. Its \emph{observer coherence graph} $G_{\mathcal B,H}$ has
an edge $\{i,j\}$, $i\ne j$, exactly when
\begin{equation}
 a_{ij}:=\sum_a\lambda_a
       \bigl(|(B_a)_{ij}|^2+|(B_a)_{ji}|^2\bigr)>0.
 \label{eq:weights}
\end{equation}
Let $c(G)$ count all connected components, including isolated vertices,
and write $r(G)=D-c(G)$. Rephasing or relabelling the energy basis does not
change this integer. We use $[x]_+=\max(x,0)$.

\begin{theorem}[Connected-network observer law]\label{thm:network}
For every $\Gamma$ and every $(q_v)_{v\in V}$ as above there is a
Lebesgue-conull subset $\mathcal U_\Gamma\subseteq\cL_\Gamma$ such that
every $H\in\mathcal U_\Gamma$ has simple spectrum and, simultaneously for
every finite $\mathcal B$, every nonnegative weight family, and every
$t_0>0$,
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{\delta\downarrow0}
 \frac{\log T_{\mathcal B,H}(\delta;t_0)}{\log(1/\delta)}
       =\frac{[r(G_{\mathcal B,H})-1]_+}{2}.
 \label{eq:main}
\end{equation}
The same event can be chosen so that the energies satisfy no nonzero
balanced rational relation.
\end{theorem}

The order of quantifiers matters. The Hamiltonian is selected before the
observers. This is stronger than an almost-everywhere statement for each
fixed observer separately. It does not make the comparison constants or
the onset of the limit uniform over observers. Zero weights are allowed;
an empty family gives exponent zero.

The interaction graph $\Gamma$ lives on physical sites.
The coherence graph $G_{\mathcal B,H}$ lives on energy eigenstates.
They are not interchangeable. Connectedness of $\Gamma$ makes the
spectral arithmetic sufficiently rich; connectivity of
$G_{\mathcal B,H}$ determines how many relative phases this observer
requires to return.

\begin{corollary}[Support-contained hypergraph interactions]
\label{cor:hyper}
Let $\mathfrak H$ be a finite family of site subsets. Suppose its
two-section graph, joining distinct sites contained in a common
hyperedge, is connected. Replace $\cL_\Gamma$ by the real span of all
Hermitian onsite operators and all Hermitian operators supported
\emph{inside} a hyperedge. Then Theorem~\ref{thm:network} holds in this
full coefficient space.
\end{corollary}

The support-contained convention includes lower-body terms on a
hyperedge. The corollary does not assert the same result for a restricted
space containing only traceless genuinely $k$-body interactions.

\begin{corollary}[Pure states and trace-radius returns]\label{cor:states}
Fix $H\in\mathcal U_\Gamma$.
For every normalized pure state $\psi$, let $s$ be the number of its
nonzero energy-basis coefficients and define
\[
 T_{\psi,H}^{\rm fid}(\delta;t_0)=
 \inf\{t\geq t_0:1-|\langle\psi,U_H(t)\psi\rangle|^2<\delta\}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{\delta\downarrow0}
 \frac{\log T_{\psi,H}^{\rm fid}(\delta;t_0)}{\log(1/\delta)}
     =\frac{[s-2]_+}{2}.                       \label{eq:pure}
\end{equation}
For every density matrix $\rho$, let $G_{\rho,H}$ be its coherence graph
and define
\[
 T_{\rho,H}^{\rm tr}(\varepsilon;t_0)=
 \inf\{t\geq t_0:\|U_H(t)\rho U_H(t)^*-\rho\|_1<\varepsilon\}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}
 \frac{\log T_{\rho,H}^{\rm tr}(\varepsilon;t_0)}
      {\log(1/\varepsilon)}
       =[D-c(G_{\rho,H})-1]_+.                 \label{eq:trace}
\end{equation}
Using the conventional trace distance $\frac12\|\cdot\|_1$
gives the same exponent.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For $P_\psi=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|$, the coherence graph is complete on
the exact support and isolated elsewhere, so $r=s-1$. Direct expansion
gives
\[
 \|U_H(t)P_\psi U_H(t)^*-P_\psi\|_{\HS}^2
       =2\bigl(1-|\langle\psi,U_H(t)\psi\rangle|^2\bigr).
\]
A positive constant multiplying a tolerance does not change a logarithmic
exponent. For the second assertion, finite-dimensional singular-value
norms satisfy $\|M\|_{\HS}\leq\|M\|_1\leq\sqrt D\,\|M\|_{\HS}$.
The associated return sets sandwich the trace-radius time between
squared-Hilbert--Schmidt times at thresholds differing by constant
multiples of $\varepsilon^2$. Apply \eqref{eq:main}.
\end{proof}

A stationary energy-diagonal matrix has $r=0$. A graph with $r=1$ sees
one nonzero frequency and has exact periodic returns. Both boundary
exponents are zero. A full-support pure state has exponent $(D-2)/2$.
The theorem also classifies states chosen to have any smaller exact
support, after $H$ is fixed. It makes no assertion that an arbitrary
fixed physical local probe has a connected energy-basis graph.

\section{The observer metric and the continuous clock}

In an energy basis, \eqref{eq:defect} is exactly
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_{\mathcal B,H}(t)
   =\sum_{i<j}a_{ij}
      |\ee^{-\ii tE_i}-\ee^{-\ii tE_j}|^2.
 \label{eq:graph-energy}
\end{equation}
Consequently only the positive-edge graph matters for its exponent;
positive edge weights affect constants. Here
$\T=\{z\in\C:|z|=1\}$ is the unit circle.

\begin{lemma}[Forest comparison]\label{lem:forest}
Let $G$ be a finite graph with positive edge weights, and let
\[
 \Phi(z)=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E(G)}a_{ij}|z_i-z_j|^2,
 \qquad z\in\T^D.
\]
Choose one root $o(C)$ in each component. There are constants $A,B>0$
such that
\begin{equation}
 A\sum_C\sum_{i\in C\setminus\{o(C)\}}|z_i-z_{o(C)}|^2
 \ \leq\ \Phi(z)\ \leq\
 B\sum_C\sum_{i\in C\setminus\{o(C)\}}|z_i-z_{o(C)}|^2.
 \label{eq:forest}
\end{equation}
There are exactly $r=D-c(G)$ root-relative coordinates.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $G$ has no edges, both energies vanish and take $A=B=1$. Otherwise
choose a spanning tree in each component. Every root chord is a sum of
edge chords along its tree path; Cauchy--Schwarz and the least positive
tree-edge weight give the first inequality. Every edge chord is the
difference of two root chords; the triangle inequality squared and
summation against the weights give the second. Isolated vertices
contribute neither an edge nor a coordinate.
\end{proof}

This comparison is global on the phase torus. Near the origin, each
circle chord is comparable to angular distance modulo $2\pi$.
For $r\geq2$, let $g_1,\ldots,g_r$ be the corresponding root-relative
energy gaps. Reverse their common sign if necessary to choose $g_1>0$,
and put
$\alpha=(g_2/g_1,\ldots,g_r/g_1)\in\R^{r-1}$.
The relevant metric Diophantine property is that $\alpha$ is not
very well approximable: for every $\eta>0$, only finitely many positive
integers $q$ satisfy
\[
 \max_j\|q\alpha_j\|_{\R/\Z}<q^{-1/(r-1)-\eta}.
\]
This property is obtained from the Hamiltonian model in the next section;
it is not an assumption in Theorem~\ref{thm:network}.

\begin{lemma}[Clock reduction]\label{lem:clock}
If the visible gap-ratio vector above is not very well approximable,
the first time after $t_0>0$ at which $\Phi(\ee^{-\ii tE})<\delta$
has logarithmic exponent $(r-1)/2$.
For $r=0$ or $r=1$ the exponent is zero.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Use phase radius $\varepsilon$ first. At times
$t=2\pi q/g_1$, the first relative phase is exactly zero.
Dirichlet approximation in $r-1$ coordinates gives returns with
$\log t/\log(1/\varepsilon)$ at most $r-1$ in the limit.
Non-very-well-approximability excludes exact simultaneous returns and
forces the integer return denominators to tend to infinity, so any
fixed cutoff is eventually exceeded.

Conversely, at a phase return of radius $\varepsilon$, round
$tg_1/(2\pi)$ to its nearest integer $q$. Then
$\|q\alpha_j\|_{\R/\Z}\leq C\varepsilon$ for all $j$, with $C$ depending
only on the gaps. The non-very-well-approximable estimate gives the
matching lower logarithmic exponent. A fixed compact interval
$[t_0,M]$ contains no exact return, hence its continuous defect has a
positive minimum; this excludes bounded early returns as
$\varepsilon\downarrow0$. The forest comparison changes only constants.
Finally $\varepsilon$ is proportional to $\sqrt\delta$, giving the
factor $1/2$.

If $r=0$ the defect vanishes identically. If $r=1$ there is one relative
frequency, hence a positive period (or an identically zero defect);
an exact return exists after every cutoff. In either case the return
times stay between $t_0$ and a fixed finite time.
\end{proof}

The subtraction of one is a continuous-clock effect, not a loss of one
observer. Nor does a torus target's volume determine a first-return
time without arithmetic information. In particular, an occupation
estimate or an average inter-return interval cannot replace the lower
bound in Lemma~\ref{lem:clock}.

\section{Real spectral nondegeneracy from physical locality}

Call a finite energy packet $E=(E_i)$ \emph{maximally projective} when
\[
 \sum_i k_i=0,\quad \sum_i k_iE_i=0,\quad k_i\in\Z
       \quad\Longrightarrow\quad k_i=0\text{ for all }i.
\]
This is equivalent to rational independence of any anchored gap packet.
For the metric theorem we require a stronger property of a
\emph{family}, involving real rather than rational coefficients.

\begin{definition}[Real affine spectral escape]
A Hermitian family $\mathcal S$ escapes real affine spectral relations
if, for every $c\in\R^D\setminus\{0\}$ with $\sum_i c_i=0$ and every
$a\in\R$, it contains a matrix with a simple real energy packet $E$ such
that
\begin{equation}
 \sum_i c_i E_{\sigma(i)}\ne a
          \quad\hbox{for every permutation }\sigma.
 \label{eq:escape}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Connected-network affine escape]\label{thm:escape}
The full local Hermitian space $\cL_\Gamma$ of every connected finite
interaction graph with $q_v\geq2$ escapes real affine spectral
relations. The same is true for every hypergraph space in
Corollary~\ref{cor:hyper}.
\end{theorem}

We give the local analytic argument and the physical induction
separately. Their distinction prevents a spectral witness from being
mistaken for a physically local Hamiltonian.

\subsection{The rigidity of a leaf extension}

Let $E_i$ be a maximally projective old energy packet, and let $A$ be a
Hermitian matrix in this energy basis. Its transition graph joins
$i\ne j$ when $A_{ij}\ne0$. Suppose this graph is connected and
nonbipartite. Attach a factor with $q\geq2$ levels. The allowed spectral
slice has the form
\begin{equation}
 M(u,B)=\operatorname{diag}(E)\otimes I+
          I\otimes\operatorname{diag}(u)+A\otimes B,
 \qquad u\in\R^q,\quad B=B^{\mathsf T}\in M_q(\R).
 \label{eq:leaf}
\end{equation}
Select $u^0$ so that all $E_i+u^0_r$ are distinct. This is possible by
avoiding finitely many affine hyperplanes.

\begin{lemma}[Constant-action rigidity]\label{lem:rigidity}
On a simple analytic eigenvalue chart of \eqref{eq:leaf} around
$(u^0,0)$, a linear spectral action
$\sum_{i,r}c_{ir}\lambda_{ir}(u,B)$ can be constant only if all
$c_{ir}$ are equal.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix distinct layers $r,s$ and vary the real symmetric coupling supported
on their two off-diagonal entries. The second eigenvalue derivative at
zero coupling is the simple-root perturbation expression.
Collecting the terms of the constant action gives the rational identity
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{i,j}
 \frac{(c_{ir}-c_{js})|A_{ij}|^2}{E_i-E_j+u_r-u_s}=0
 \label{eq:residue}
\end{equation}
for layer shifts in an open collision-free interval. Multiplication
by the product of denominators makes this a polynomial identity.
Maximal projectivity makes every nonzero oriented old gap
$E_i-E_j$ distinct from every other such gap unless the ordered
pairs coincide. The residue at the corresponding pole of
\eqref{eq:residue} is therefore
$(c_{ir}-c_{js})|A_{ij}|^2$. Hence
$c_{ir}=c_{js}$ on every old transition edge and every pair of
different layers.

The graph on pairs $(i,r)$ with these edges is connected.
Indeed it is the direct product of the old transition graph and the
complete graph on the layer labels. A connected graph with an odd
cycle allows walks of both parities between any two vertices after
inserting closed walks; combining these with layer walks proves
connectedness also when there are only two layers. Thus equality of
coefficients propagates to every product label.
\end{proof}

For a nonzero balanced coefficient vector the constant case is
impossible. Each permuted action minus its prescribed target is
therefore a nonzero real analytic function on one connected chart.
A finite union of their zero sets cannot cover that chart.
One parameter choice avoids all of them. This proves real affine
escape for the slice \eqref{eq:leaf}.

If $A$ is the energy-basis image of an operator on the physical parent
site, conjugating \eqref{eq:leaf} back by the old energy basis gives
exactly
\begin{equation}
 H_{\rm old}\otimes I+
 I\otimes\operatorname{diag}(u)+A_{\rm parent}\otimes B.
 \label{eq:physical-leaf}
\end{equation}
Every term is allowed on the old architecture, the new site, or the
new parent--leaf edge. No spectral-basis locality assumption is made.

\subsection{Physical witnesses on branching networks}

We need old witnesses with a good parent probe at whichever site the
next leaf is attached. A finite Hermitian probe family is called good
if the union of its nonzero off-diagonal transition entries is a
connected nonbipartite graph.

\begin{lemma}[Physical witness induction]\label{lem:witness}
On a connected site graph with at least two sites and dimensions at
least two, there is a maximally projective local Hermitian Hamiltonian
with a good family of physically one-site probes at every site.
The same assertion holds on one site of dimension at least three.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On a single factor of dimension at least three, choose rationally
independent real energies and the full family of Hermitian matrix
units. The transition graph is complete and contains a triangle.

When all factors are qubits, start with an edge. Use the two-qubit
graph basis
\[
 U=\mathrm{CZ}(H_2\otimes H_2),\qquad
 H_2=2^{-1/2}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix},
\]
and let the physical Hamiltonian be $U\operatorname{diag}(E)U^*$
with a maximally projective four-level packet. This is a legitimate
two-site operator. Conjugating the three Pauli probes at either
endpoint translates the two-bit spectral labels by the three
nonzero bit vectors, with nonzero phases. Their union graph is
$K_4$. This seed avoids requiring a nonbipartite transition graph
on a single qubit.

For the induction, assume a good witness on the old network and attach
a new factor at any old site. A generic real combination of that
site's finite probes has every edge of their union graph nonzero:
each forbidden cancellation is a proper real linear subspace.
Choose such a Hermitian parent attachment $A$.

Use layer shifts $u_r=n\ell_r$, with distinct integer codes $\ell_r$,
one anchor code zero, and a star coupling between the anchor and the
other layers. All old-site graph edges persist within each layer for
small coupling. To connect these layer copies for an old probe $O$,
its cross-layer first-order response contains the rational packet
\begin{equation}
 R_{ab}^{A,O}(x)=
 \sum_k\frac{A_{ak}O_{kb}}{x+E_a-E_k}
 +\sum_k\frac{O_{ak}A_{kb}}{-x+E_b-E_k}.
 \label{eq:old-response}
\end{equation}
For each old site choose $a\ne g$ and $g\ne b$ with
$A_{ag}O_{gb}\ne0$. Such a two-step channel exists because the attachment graph and the
old site's union graph are connected; the probe $O$ is chosen from that
site's family for the selected edge.
Maximal projectivity isolates the pole contributed by this product;
a pole in the opposite sum could coincide only through the trivial
balanced relation, which would force an excluded adjacent equality.
Thus the cleared numerator of \eqref{eq:old-response} is not zero.

There are only finitely many required channels and layer differences.
The product of their scaled numerator polynomials, together with all
product-energy collision factors, is nonzero. Choose one integer
$n$ outside its finite root set. This supplies a single simultaneous
layer-shift choice. Every selected cross-layer response is then
nonzero, so its analytic matrix entry is nonzero on a sufficiently
small punctured real coupling interval. Each old-site probe graph
contains its connected nonbipartite graph in every layer, joined to
the anchor layer.

For the new site, use a directed matrix unit $|s\rangle\langle r|$
with an incident star edge $r\!\sim\!s$. Its same-layer transition
between old labels $a\ne b$ has first derivative proportional to
$A_{ab}/(E_a+u_r-E_b-u_s)$, which is nonzero on every parent
transition edge. The directed unit is a complex linear combination
of its Hermitian cosine and sine parts. At least one of these two
physical probes therefore has nonzero derivative. This gives
within-layer copies of the parent transition graph.
Off-diagonal anchor--layer matrix units already have nonzero entries
at zero coupling, and supply bridges between the copies.
Consequently the new site's graph is also connected and nonbipartite.
All these are finitely many open nonvanishing conditions.

The leaf slice escapes real affine relations by
Lemma~\ref{lem:rigidity}. For each nonzero balanced integer relation,
the product over spectral permutations is a nonzero polynomial in
the characteristic coefficients. Its pullback to the local
coefficient space is a nonzero polynomial, whose zero set has
Lebesgue measure zero. The countable union over integer relations
is null. Therefore the open set of simultaneous good probes contains
a maximally projective Hamiltonian. This closes the induction.

Grow a spanning tree from the higher-dimensional seed if one exists;
otherwise grow from a qubit edge. At each stage
\eqref{eq:physical-leaf} preserves the named site tensor factors and
uses one genuine graph edge. Extra edges enlarge the admissible
space and do not obstruct the construction.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:escape}]
For one site all Hermitian matrices are allowed. For two sites,
connectedness supplies their full two-site matrix space. Diagonal
energies avoid the finitely many hyperplanes in \eqref{eq:escape},
as well as the collision hyperplanes.

For at least three sites choose a vertex whose deletion leaves a
connected graph, for example a leaf of a spanning tree.
The remaining network has at least two sites.
Lemma~\ref{lem:witness} gives a maximally projective witness and good
one-site probes there. Select a surviving neighbour of the removed
vertex and apply the leaf slice at that site. Its transported
operators have the form \eqref{eq:physical-leaf}, hence belong to
$\cL_\Gamma$. The slice's affine escape proves escape of the larger
space. This argument uses a spanning tree, not a spanning path.

For a support-contained hypergraph, every onsite or two-section
edge operator is supported inside some permitted hyperedge.
Its full local space therefore contains the graph-local escaping
space. Escape persists under this inclusion.
\end{proof}

\subsection{From escape to a common conull event}

\begin{lemma}[Simultaneous observer extremality]\label{lem:extremality}
In a finite real linear Hermitian family with affine spectral escape
and a maximally projective member, almost every coefficient vector
has simple spectrum and satisfies the clock property of
Lemma~\ref{lem:clock} for every labelled coherence graph of capacity
at least two.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A single maximally projective member makes every nontrivial balanced
integer spectral obstruction a nonzero polynomial in the
coefficients. Removing their countable union gives maximal
projectivity and, in particular, simple spectrum almost everywhere.

Cover the simple-spectrum locus by countably many connected analytic
eigenvalue charts. Fix a labelled graph $G$ and one root per component.
The incidence vectors $e_i-e_{o(C)}$ are linearly independent over
$\R$. Indeed their nonroot coordinates recover their coefficients
individually. Let $g_1,\ldots,g_r$ be the corresponding energy gaps.
They are nonzero on a simple chart. The ratio map
\[
 \theta\longmapsto
 (g_2(\theta)/g_1(\theta),\ldots,g_r(\theta)/g_1(\theta))
\]
has no affine relation on any nonempty open part of that chart.
Otherwise clearing $g_1$ produces a nonzero balanced real linear
spectral action that vanishes there.

To justify propagation beyond this particular chart, form the product
of that action over all spectral permutations. It is a symmetric
polynomial in the eigenvalues, hence a polynomial in characteristic
coefficients and in the original real parameters. Vanishing on an
open set makes it identically zero. Affine spectral escape supplies
a matrix where every factor is nonzero, a contradiction.

An analytic map with no local affine relation is nondegenerate
almost everywhere. Kleinbock--Margulis extremality then excludes
very well approximable ratio vectors on a null set in this chart.
There are finitely many labelled graphs on $D$ vertices and
countably many charts. Intersecting these conull events gives one
event for all graphs. There is no uncountable intersection over
observer matrices or real spectral coefficients.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:network} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:hyper}]
Theorem~\ref{thm:escape} supplies affine escape of the full physical
coefficient space. Lemma~\ref{lem:witness} supplies a maximally
projective member; the one-site case follows directly from diagonal
matrices. Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:extremality}. For each subsequently
chosen observer family use \eqref{eq:graph-energy},
Lemma~\ref{lem:forest}, and Lemma~\ref{lem:clock}, including the two
zero-exponent boundaries. The same construction applies inside the
hypergraph coefficient space.
\end{proof}

\section{A universal stationary law for arbitrary energy spectra}

The finite-system first-return law does not extend unchanged to
continuous spectra. There is, however, an exact statistical statement
with no spectral regularity assumption.

Let $\mu$ be any Borel probability measure on $\R$. Define its
survival amplitude and fidelity by
\[
 A_\mu(t)=\int_\R\ee^{\ii tE}\,d\mu(E),\qquad
 F_\mu(t)=|A_\mu(t)|^2.
\]
For a normalized vector and a self-adjoint Hamiltonian, $\mu$ is its
spectral energy measure. No energy moment or bounded-support assumption
is needed for the theorem. Let
\[
 \Law_{\mu,T}=(F_\mu)_*
       \left(\frac{\mathbf1_{[-T,T]}(t)}{2T}\,dt\right),\qquad T>0,
\]
a probability measure on $[0,1]$. Write
\[
 \mu_\pp=\sum_{E:\,\mu\{E\}>0}\mu\{E\}\,\delta_E,\qquad
 p=\mu_\pp(\R).
\]
When $p>0$, set $\nu=\mu_\pp/p$.

\begin{theorem}[Stationary persistence and interval support]
\label{thm:stationary}
For every probability energy measure $\mu$, the measures
$\Law_{\mu,T}$ converge weakly as $T\to\infty$ to one probability
measure $\Law_\mu$ on $[0,1]$. If $p>0$ then
\begin{equation}
 \Law_\mu=(x\mapsto p^2x)_*\Law_\nu;
 \label{eq:scaling}
\end{equation}
if $p=0$ then $\Law_\mu=\delta_0$.
There is $\ell_\mu\in[0,p^2]$ such that
\begin{equation}
 \supp\Law_\mu=[\ell_\mu,p^2],
 \qquad
 p=\sqrt{\max\supp\Law_\mu}.
 \label{eq:edge}
\end{equation}
The interval may degenerate to one point. All exact energy relations
and all atomic weights are allowed.
\end{theorem}

Thus the \emph{whole} stationary survival law ignores the continuous
part except through its removal from the total mass. Its upper
support edge recovers total point-spectrum weight. The mean alone,
given by the sum of squared atomic weights, measures a different
quantity.

\begin{proof}[Existence and pure-point scaling]
Let $\widetilde\mu$ be the reflected measure, and let
$\eta=\mu*\widetilde\mu$. The identity
$F_\mu=\widehat\eta$ gives, for every integer $n\geq1$,
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{T\to\infty}\frac1{2T}\int_{-T}^T F_\mu(t)^n\,dt
       =\eta^{*n}\{0\}.
 \label{eq:moments}
\end{equation}
Indeed the centered average of $\ee^{\ii tx}$ is
$\sin(Tx)/(Tx)$, interpreted as one at $x=0$. It is bounded by one
and tends to zero away from zero, so dominated convergence proves
\eqref{eq:moments}. The zeroth moment is one.
Probability measures on $[0,1]$ form a weakly compact space, and
polynomials are dense in continuous functions there. Hence every
subsequential limit has these same moments, and these moments
determine a unique measure. The full family converges.

A convolution singleton mass depends only on the singleton masses
of its factors: any term containing an atomless factor has zero
mass at a singleton. Apply this observation repeatedly to
\eqref{eq:moments}. For $p>0$, the positive moments are precisely
$p^{2n}$ times those of $\Law_\nu$. Equality of all moments on
$[0,1]$ proves \eqref{eq:scaling}. For $p=0$ all positive moments
vanish, giving $\delta_0$. In particular,
\[
 \int_0^1 x\,d\Law_\mu(x)=\sum_E\mu\{E\}^2,
\]
the Wiener mean-square identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Connected support and its upper edge]
It remains to treat a countable pure-point probability packet
$\nu=\sum_i w_i\delta_{E_i}$, with $w_i>0$ and $\sum_iw_i=1$.
In the product of circles on ordered pairs, let $K$ be the closure
of the one-parameter group
\[
 t\longmapsto(\ee^{\ii t(E_i-E_j)})_{i,j}.
\]
It is a compact connected abelian group: the real line is connected
and the closure of its continuous image is connected.
The series
\[
 f(z)=\sum_{i,j}w_iw_j\operatorname{Re}z_{ij}
\]
converges uniformly, because $\sum_{i,j}w_iw_j=1$.
On the dense time orbit it equals $F_\nu(t)$; hence
$f(K)\subseteq[0,1]$ and $f(1)=1$.

Time averages of continuous functions on $K$ converge to Haar
averages. This follows first for characters, whose nonconstant
time averages vanish, and then for all continuous functions by
character approximation. Consequently
$\Law_\nu=f_*m_K$, where $m_K$ is normalized Haar measure.
Haar measure has full support on $K$. A continuous map from a
compact space sends this support exactly onto the support of its
pushforward. Therefore
$\supp\Law_\nu=f(K)$. Connectedness, compactness, and $f(1)=1$
give $f(K)=[\ell_\nu,1]$. Scaling proves
\eqref{eq:edge}; the case $p=0$ was already settled.
\end{proof}

The support is that of a completed \emph{distribution}.
Equation~\eqref{eq:edge} is not a pointwise upper bound on
$F_\mu(t)$ for every sufficiently late time. In particular, the
proof uses averaged cancellation of continuous spectral
contributions; it does not assume their Fourier transform
converges pointwise to zero. It therefore includes singular
continuous measures without asserting pointwise decay.

Nor does knowing this support determine a first-return exponent.
Support says which fidelity values retain mass in every open
neighbourhood under completed time sampling. Theorem~\ref{thm:network}
additionally controls arithmetic of finite phase clocks, which is
the information used in the first-hit lower bound.

\section{Finite-horizon stability and the limits of the laws}

\begin{proposition}[Uniform observable stability]\label{prop:stability}
For bounded self-adjoint $H,K$ in a unital complex $C^*$-algebra,
every bounded $O$, and every real $t$,
\begin{equation}
 \|\ee^{-\ii tH}O\ee^{\ii tH}
          -\ee^{-\ii tK}O\ee^{\ii tK}\|
       \leq2|t|\,\|H-K\|\,\|O\|.
 \label{eq:stability}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The unitary Duhamel estimate gives
$\|\ee^{-\ii tH}-\ee^{-\ii tK}\|\leq|t|\|H-K\|$.
Writing the difference of the two conjugations as
$(U-V)OU^*+VO(U^*-V^*)$ and using unitary norms gives
\eqref{eq:stability}.
\end{proof}

This dimension-independent estimate concerns the entire finite-time
matrix history. Our asymptotic results supply no lower separation of
nearby histories and no uniform time at which their completed
arithmetic behaviour becomes visible.

Several distinctions are essential for applying the results.
First, the graph theorem uses the full local Hermitian coefficient
space. It does not automatically descend to a symmetry-constrained,
translation-invariant, fixed-coupling, or commuting subfamily; a null
set in the full space can contain such a subfamily. Second, exact
spectral support and exact nonzero coherence entries determine the
exponents. Small positive weights can postpone the asymptotic regime
without changing its exponent. Third, all limits in
Theorem~\ref{thm:network} hold at fixed finite system size. Replacing
$D$ by a many-body dimension in \eqref{eq:main} does not yield a
fixed-error thermodynamic lower bound. Fourth, neither graph
connectivity nor spectral nonresonance is used as a definition or
proof of chaos, thermalization, or nonintegrability.

The results identify two distinct forms of universality.
Across finite connected physical networks, one generic Hamiltonian
event accommodates every finite matrix observer, with an exponent
determined by visible coherence connectivity. Across arbitrary
probability energy spectra, the complete survival distribution is
selected by the pure-point carrier and has an exact support edge.
A quantitative onset estimate for specified Hamiltonian families,
and a comparable classification for scalar expectation histories,
remain separate questions.
