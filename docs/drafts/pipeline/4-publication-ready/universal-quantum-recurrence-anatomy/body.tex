\section{The locality question for quantum recurrence}

Quantum recurrence begins with a qualitative fact.  A finite isolated system
with time-independent Hamiltonian returns arbitrarily close to its initial
state \cite{BocchieriLoinger1957}.  Quantitative work asks how close, how
soon, and for which states or observables.  General upper bounds follow from
simultaneous Diophantine approximation, while concentration and random-model
arguments show that many-body recurrences can be extremely rare
\cite{RiddellPagliaroliAlhambra2023,KotowskiOszmaniec2026,GuptaShort2026}.

A basic structural question remains between these viewpoints.  A generic
$D\times D$ Hermitian matrix can have $D-1$ rationally independent relative
energies.  A one-dimensional nearest-neighbour Hamiltonian, by contrast, is
specified by only linearly many local couplings.  Must this severe spatial
restriction create exact spectral relations and reduce recurrence
dimension?  Nondegenerate energy gaps are known to hold almost everywhere
in broad local families \cite{Huang2021}, but nondegeneracy excludes only
two-gap coincidences.  Maximal recurrence dimension requires exclusion of
\emph{every} balanced integer relation, at every order.

This paper proves that locality does not impose such a reduction.  The result
is organized by one invariant, the balanced spectral relation lattice, and
four consequences of that invariant:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{Law \arabic*.}]
\item \emph{Constraint conservation.}  Exact relation rank plus independent
  projective phase rank is always $D-1$.
\item \emph{Transverse first-return scaling.}  A nondegenerate $d$-phase
  family has first-hit exponent $d$ almost everywhere.
\item \emph{Collision completeness.}  Maximal projective rank is equivalent
  to attaining the permutation floor at every replica order and in every
  full-support survival-fidelity moment.
\item \emph{Local saturation.}  Static open nearest-neighbour paths attain
  all preceding maxima generically; for qubits, the conclusions hold for
  one and the same Hamiltonian on every simple-spectrum chart.
\end{enumerate}

These laws are not four unrelated effects.  They are kernel, image,
Diophantine, and moment manifestations of one linear map from balanced
spectral characters to energy detunings.

\subsection{Relation to closest results}

The novelty is deliberately narrower than the component techniques.
Kronecker--Weyl equidistribution, Dirichlet approximation, metric extremality
on nondegenerate manifolds, and multinomial moment expansions are established
tools \cite{Weyl1916,KleinbockMargulis1998}.  Recent recurrence bounds apply
to arbitrary finite systems or show generic saturation for random
Hamiltonians \cite{KotowskiOszmaniec2026,GuptaShort2026}.  Local free-fermion
models with rationally independent single-particle spectra are also known,
but their many-body energies retain additive free-particle structure
\cite{RiddellBertini2024}.  Exact spectral-form-factor moment identities under
rational-independence hypotheses have been derived independently
\cite{CamposVenutiOdavicHamma2026}.  Arithmetic classification of exact,
state-independent Floquet recurrences is complementary: it concerns
cyclotomic spectra of periodically driven unitaries rather than generic
approximate returns of static local Hamiltonians \cite{AnandEtAl2026}.

The new point is the intersection of these issues: an explicit full
\emph{interacting}, static, bounded-degree, one-dimensional local coefficient
family is proved to escape every real affine spectral relation; this produces
sharp metric return exponents and all-order collision extremality for the
same generic Hamiltonian.  No randomness, chaos assumption, time averaging
of the Hamiltonian, or all-to-all coupling enters the local theorem.

\section{One invariant: balanced spectral relations}

Let $H$ be a finite-dimensional Hamiltonian with real energy packet
$E=(E_1,\ldots,E_D)$.  Global phase is physically irrelevant to recurrence,
so the natural characters are balanced.

\begin{definition}[Balanced relation lattice]
The projective spectral relation lattice is
\begin{equation}
 \Rel_E:=\left\{k\in\Z^D:
  \sum_{j=1}^D k_j=0,\quad \sum_{j=1}^D k_jE_j=0\right\}.
 \label{eq:lattice}
\end{equation}
Its rational rank is
$r(E):=\dim_\Q\operatorname{span}_\Q\Rel_E$.
For a reference level $E_1$, define the temporal rank
\begin{equation}
 \tau(E):=\dim_\Q\operatorname{span}_\Q
 \{E_j-E_1:2\leq j\leq D\}.
 \label{eq:temporal-rank}
\end{equation}

The spectrum is \emph{maximally projective} when $\Rel_E=\{0\}$,
equivalently when $\tau(E)=D-1$.
\end{definition}

The definition is independent of the chosen reference.  It includes all
finite-order no-resonance conditions at once: an equality between two sums
of the same number of energies is exactly an element of \eqref{eq:lattice}.

\begin{theorem}[Law 1: projective rank conservation]
For every nonempty finite spectrum,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\tau(E)+r(E)=D-1.}
 \label{eq:rank-conservation}
\end{equation}
More precisely, let
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_E:\{c\in\Q^D:\textstyle\sum_jc_j=0\}\longrightarrow\R,
 \qquad \Phi_E(c)=\sum_jc_jE_j.
 \label{eq:detuning-map}
\end{equation}

Then $\ker\Phi_E$ is the rational span of $\Rel_E$, and
$\operatorname{im}\Phi_E$ is the rational span of the anchored gaps.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The domain of $\Phi_E$ has rational dimension $D-1$.  Clearing
denominators shows that a rational balanced vector lies in the kernel exactly
when it lies in $\operatorname{span}_\Q\Rel_E$.  Every image element can be
written as
\[
 \sum_jc_jE_j=\sum_{j=2}^D c_j(E_j-E_1)
\]
because $\sum_jc_j=0$.  Conversely each anchored gap is the image of the
balanced vector $e_j-e_1$.  Rank--nullity gives
\eqref{eq:rank-conservation}.
\end{proof}

Equation \eqref{eq:rank-conservation} is the universal principle of the
paper.  An exact relation does not merely correlate two energies: it removes
one rational direction from the completed projective phase motion.  Maximal
projective rank means that the relative phase orbit is dense in the full
$(D-1)$-torus.  When $D\geq3$, it also rules out every nonzero \emph{exact}
simultaneous projective return; recurrence remains approximate rather than
periodic.

\section{Sharp first-hit laws}

For $x\in\R$, write
$\lVert x\rVert_\Z:=\min_{p\in\Z}|x-p|$.  Given
$\beta=(\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_d)\in\R^d$ and $r>0$, define
\begin{equation}
 T_\beta(r):=\min\left\{q\in\N:q\geq1,
  \max_{1\leq j\leq d}\lVert q\beta_j\rVert_\Z<r\right\}.
 \label{eq:first-hit}
\end{equation}
Dirichlet's theorem gives $T_\beta(r)\leq\lceil1/r\rceil^d$.
The matching exponent fails precisely on an exceptional set of vectors that
are too well approximable.

\begin{definition}
A vector $\beta\in\R^d$ is very well approximable if some $\delta>0$
admits infinitely many $q\geq1$ for which
\[
 \max_j\lVert q\beta_j\rVert_\Z
 \leq q^{-1/d-\delta}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Law 2: metric first-hit exponent]
If $d\geq1$ and $\beta$ is not very well approximable, then
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{r\downarrow0}
 \frac{\log T_\beta(r)}{\log(1/r)}=d.
 \label{eq:first-hit-exponent}
\end{equation}
Consequently $T_\beta(r)=r^{-d+o(1)}$.
\end{theorem}

The metric theorem of Kleinbock and Margulis implies that almost every point
on a real-analytic nondegenerate manifold is not very well approximable
\cite{KleinbockMargulis1998}.  In the present setting, nondegeneracy is not
assumed.  Section~\ref{sec:local-proof} proves it from exact spectral escape
of the local Hamiltonian family.

For a simple spectrum, order energies as
$\lambda_1<\cdots<\lambda_D$ and introduce two canonical vectors:
\begin{align}
 \beta^{\rm disc}_j&=\frac{\lambda_{j+1}-\lambda_1}{2\pi},
   &&1\leq j\leq D-1,                                      \label{eq:disc-beta}\\
 \beta^{\rm wind}_j&=\frac{\lambda_{j+2}-\lambda_1}
                           {\lambda_2-\lambda_1},
   &&1\leq j\leq D-2.                                      \label{eq:wind-beta}
\end{align}
The first counts simultaneous anchored-gap returns at integer observation
times.  The second spends the first positive gap as a clock and counts the
winding needed to align every remaining gap.  It is a clock-normalized
winding statement, not a claim about the unrestricted continuous-time
first-return distribution.

\section{Replica collisions and return moments}

Let $w_j>0$ with $\sum_jw_j=1$.  These are spectral weights of a state with
nonzero overlap on every energy eigenvector.  Define survival amplitude and
fidelity
\begin{equation}
 A(t)=\sum_{j=1}^D w_j\ee^{-\ii E_jt},\qquad F(t)=|A(t)|^2.
 \label{eq:survival}
\end{equation}
For a multiplicity vector $p\in\N^D$, put
$|p|=\sum_jp_j$ and
$\binom{m}{p}=m!/\prod_jp_j!$.  The unavoidable order-$m$ permutation floor
is
\begin{equation}
 \Pi_m(w):=
 \sum_{\substack{p\in\N^D\\|p|=m}}
 \binom{m}{p}^{\!2}\prod_{j=1}^D w_j^{2p_j}.
 \label{eq:floor}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Law 3: all-order collision equivalence]
For a finite spectrum and strictly positive weights, the symmetric long-time
moments satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{T\to\infty}\frac1{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}F(t)^m\,dt
 =\sum_{\substack{|p|=|q|=m\\p\cdot E=q\cdot E}}
 \binom{m}{p}\binom{m}{q}
 \prod_jw_j^{p_j+q_j}.                                    \label{eq:moment-collision}
\end{equation}
The right side is at least $\Pi_m(w)$.  Moreover, the following are
equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\Rel_E=\{0\}$;
\item equality with $\Pi_m(w)$ holds for every $m\geq0$;
\item every long-time moment in \eqref{eq:moment-collision} equals
  $\Pi_m(w)$.
\end{enumerate}
The equivalence holds for every choice of strictly positive weights.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The multinomial expansion of $F(t)^m$ is a finite sum indexed by pairs
$(p,q)$ with $|p|=|q|=m$.  Its phase is
$\exp[-\ii t(p-q)\cdot E]$ and its coefficient is strictly positive.
Symmetric time averaging sends a nonzero detuning to zero and a zero
detuning to one, giving \eqref{eq:moment-collision}.  Terms with $p=q$ give
\eqref{eq:floor}.  If $\Rel_E=\{0\}$, every balanced equality
$(p-q)\cdot E=0$ forces $p=q$.  Conversely, any nonzero
$k\in\Rel_E$ has positive and negative parts $k^+,k^-$ with equal mass;
the unequal pair $(k^+,k^-)$ contributes a strictly positive excess at that
replica order.  This proves all equivalences.
\end{proof}

The theorem gives an operational reading of the full relation lattice.
Second-order gap nondegeneracy detects only a shallow part of it.  All
replica orders together are complete: every nontrivial balanced relation
appears at the order equal to its positive mass, and positivity prevents
cancellation from hiding it.

\section{Static nearest-neighbour paths attain the maximum}
\label{sec:local-main}

For $n$ qubits, let $\mathcal P_n$ be the set of nonidentity Pauli strings
whose support is one site or one nearest-neighbour edge of the open path.
The full traceless path-local family is
\begin{equation}
 H_n(\theta)=\sum_{P\in\mathcal P_n}\theta_P P,
 \qquad \theta\in\R^{\mathcal P_n}.
 \label{eq:pauli-path}
\end{equation}
It is time independent, deterministic after $\theta$ is chosen, and has
interaction degree at most two.  One-qubit fields and all two-qubit Pauli
couplings on each path edge are allowed; no longer-range term is present.

\begin{theorem}[Law 4: same-Hamiltonian local saturation]
\label{thm:local-capstone}
Let $n\geq3$, $D=2^n$, and let $U\subset\R^{\mathcal P_n}$ be any nonempty,
connected, open set on which $H_n(\theta)$ has simple spectrum.  For
Lebesgue-almost every $\theta\in U$, one and the same Hamiltonian satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item $\Rel_{E(\theta)}=\{0\}$ and $\tau(E(\theta))=D-1$;
\item for the ordered spectrum, the stroboscopic vector
  \eqref{eq:disc-beta} obeys
  \[
   \lim_{r\downarrow0}
   \frac{\log T_{\beta^{\rm disc}}(r)}{\log(1/r)}=D-1;
  \]
\item the clock-normalized vector \eqref{eq:wind-beta} obeys
  \[
   \lim_{r\downarrow0}
   \frac{\log T_{\beta^{\rm wind}}(r)}{\log(1/r)}=D-2;
  \]
\item for every strictly positive spectral weight and every $m\geq0$, the
  replica collision mass and the long-time survival-fidelity moment equal
  the permutation floor \eqref{eq:floor}.
\end{enumerate}

In addition, there is no nonzero exact simultaneous projective return.
\end{theorem}

The key point is quantifier order.  A single full-measure set of coefficients
works simultaneously for maximal rank, both exponent laws, every positive
weight, and every replica order.  The Hamiltonian is not changed between
clauses.

\begin{corollary}[No locality discount]
For almost every Hamiltonian in each chart of Theorem~\ref{thm:local-capstone},
the stroboscopic return exponent is $2^n-1$.  Thus spatial dimension one,
bounded interaction degree, and only nearest-neighbour terms do not reduce
the exponent below the full projective Hilbert-space dimension.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Uniform local dimension}

The rank statement is not peculiar to Pauli matrices.  Let each path site
have $q\geq2$ levels, and take the full real trace-zero space of Hermitian
onsite and nearest-neighbour terms.

\begin{theorem}[Qudit path maximality]
For every $n,q\geq2$, the set of path-local Hamiltonians with
\begin{equation}
 \tau(E)=q^n-1
 \label{eq:qudit-maximality}
\end{equation}
is dense, residual, and of full Lebesgue measure in the exact local
coefficient space.  For each such Hamiltonian, all finite-order balanced
energy-sum equalities are trivial, all full-support replica collision masses
attain their permutation floors, and every long-time survival-fidelity
moment attains the same floor.
\end{theorem}

The qubit theorem is stronger in a different direction: it packages the two
sharp metric exponents and all collision conclusions on every chart for the
same coefficient draw.

\section{Exact completed density and the thermodynamic law}

Fix a reference energy $E_1$ and define the projective chord defect
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_E(t)=\max_{2\leq j\leq D}
 \left|\ee^{-\ii t(E_j-E_1)}-1\right|.
 \label{eq:chord-defect}
\end{equation}
\par\smallskip\noindent
For a measurable time set $S$, use centered completed density
\begin{equation}
 \dens(S)=\lim_{T\to\infty}
 \frac{\bigl|S\cap[-T,T]\bigr|}{2T},
 \label{eq:density}
\end{equation}
\par\smallskip\noindent
whenever the limit exists.

\begin{theorem}[Exact projective-return density]
If $E$ is maximally projective and $0<\varepsilon<2$, then
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \dens\{t:\Delta_E(t)<\varepsilon\}
 =\left(\frac{2\arcsin(\varepsilon/2)}{\pi}\right)^{D-1}.}
 \label{eq:exact-density}
\end{equation}
The same value is obtained with a non-strict inequality.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Maximal projective rank makes the anchored phase flow equidistributed on
$\T^{D-1}$.  In one circle, chord distance below $\varepsilon$ from the
identity is an arc of normalized Haar measure
$2\arcsin(\varepsilon/2)/\pi$.  The target is the Cartesian product of
$D-1$ such arcs.  Its boundary has Haar measure zero, so equidistribution
gives \eqref{eq:exact-density} for strict and closed targets.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Thermodynamic limit}

For an $n$-site $q$-level path, set
\begin{align}
 b_\varepsilon=\frac{2\arcsin(\varepsilon/2)}{\pi},\qquad
 I_\varepsilon&=-\log b_\varepsilon>0,                       \label{eq:rate}\\
 \rho_{n,q}(\varepsilon)&=b_\varepsilon^{q^n-1}.             \label{eq:rho-definition}
\end{align}

\begin{theorem}[Thermodynamic projective-recurrence extinction]
For every $q\geq2$ and $0<\varepsilon<2$, the exact finite-size identity and
its three limits are
\begin{align}
 -\log\rho_{n,q}(\varepsilon)&=(q^n-1)I_\varepsilon,          \label{eq:finite-rate}\\
 \rho_{n,q}(\varepsilon)&\longrightarrow0,                  \label{eq:rho-zero}\\
 \frac{-\log\rho_{n,q}(\varepsilon)}{q^n}
   &\longrightarrow I_\varepsilon,                          \label{eq:hilbert-rate}\\
 \frac{\log[-\log\rho_{n,q}(\varepsilon)]}{n}
   &\longrightarrow\log q.                                  \label{eq:double-log}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Order of limits.}
Equation \eqref{eq:double-log} is the precise double-exponential statement.
The order of limits matters: completed-time density is taken at each fixed
finite $n$, and only then is $n\to\infty$ taken.  This theorem does not
construct or assert recurrence of an infinite-volume global orbit.

\section{Comparison with the literature}

Closest results divide into four groups.  Bocchieri and Loinger prove
qualitative recurrence, without a sharp generic exponent or local saturation
\cite{BocchieriLoinger1957}.  Concentration estimates and general recurrence
bounds establish rare-event scaling or universal upper bounds, rather than
the exact local-family codimension
\cite{RiddellPagliaroliAlhambra2023,KotowskiOszmaniec2026,GuptaShort2026}.
Huang proves nondegenerate gaps for almost every geometrically local
Hamiltonian, but pairwise gap nondegeneracy is weaker than excluding every
balanced relation \cite{Huang2021}.  Riddell and Bertini construct local
free-fermion models with rationally independent single-particle spectra, while
their many-body spectra retain additive free-particle structure
\cite{RiddellBertini2024}.  Finally, exact spectral-form-factor moments under
rational-independence hypotheses are prior art; our advance is proving the
needed all-order independence generically inside a static interacting local
family and coupling it to sharp first-hit laws
\cite{CamposVenutiOdavicHamma2026}.  Anand \emph{et al.} classify exact
state-independent recurrences in Floquet systems arithmetically; their
cyclotomic exact-return problem is distinct from static local
almost-recurrence studied here \cite{AnandEtAl2026}.

To our knowledge, none of the cited results proves the joint statement of
Theorem~\ref{thm:local-capstone}: almost-everywhere maximal projective rank,
the $D-1$ and $D-2$ sharp first-hit exponents, and all-order moment floors for
one Hamiltonian drawn from the full static nearest-neighbour qubit path
family.

\section{Interpretation and scope}

The result changes how locality enters recurrence theory.  Locality strongly
restricts the number and support of Hamiltonian terms, but recurrence
dimension is not the dimension of coefficient space.  It is the rational
rank of the resulting energy gaps.  A low-dimensional analytic family can
trace a nondegenerate manifold in a much higher-dimensional spectral space;
almost every point of that manifold can still be Diophantine-extremal.

The collision theorem adds a second perspective.  Maximal projective rank is
not only a statement about rare near-returns.  It is equivalent to absence of
every positive excess term in every survival-fidelity replica moment.  One
arithmetic invariant therefore governs both the geometry of first returns
and the algebra of long-time moments.

Several boundaries are essential.
\begin{itemize}
\item Results concern finite, closed, time-independent systems.
\item Generic means Lebesgue-almost every coefficient in the stated local
  family or chart, not every local Hamiltonian.  Commuting and integrable
  subfamilies can have many exact relations.
\item Exponent $D-1$ is for integer-time anchored-gap hits.  Exponent $D-2$
  is for first-gap-clock-normalized winding.  Neither is presented as an
  unrestricted continuous-time first-return distribution.
\item Exact density is a completed-time statement.  It gives no finite-time
  convergence rate without additional quantitative Diophantine information.
\item Thermodynamic limit is ordered: time completion precedes growth of
  system size.  No infinite-volume global recurrence is claimed.
\end{itemize}

Within those boundaries, locality conclusion is exact: a static
one-dimensional bounded-degree interaction family generically realizes the
largest projective recurrence codimension permitted by its Hilbert space.

\section{Proof of the first-hit exponent}
\label{app:first-hit}

We record the short argument behind Law~2.  Dirichlet approximation gives
\begin{equation}
 T_\beta(r)\leq\lceil1/r\rceil^d,
 \label{eq:dirichlet-upper}
\end{equation}
and therefore
\[
 \limsup_{r\downarrow0}
 \frac{\log T_\beta(r)}{\log(1/r)}\leq d.
\]
Suppose $\beta$ is not very well approximable.  For each $c>1/d$, only
finitely many $q$ satisfy
$\max_j\lVert q\beta_j\rVert_\Z\leq q^{-c}$.  Hence, below a sufficiently
small radius, a first hit $q=T_\beta(r)$ must satisfy $q^{-c}<r$, or
$q>r^{-1/c}$.  Thus
\[
 \liminf_{r\downarrow0}
 \frac{\log T_\beta(r)}{\log(1/r)}\geq\frac1c.
\]
Letting $c\downarrow1/d$ gives the lower bound $d$, matching
\eqref{eq:dirichlet-upper}.

\section{Linear spectral families escape affine levels}
\label{app:affine}

The metric step needs absence of constant affine relations on open spectral
charts.  The local construction first supplies homogeneous escape.  The
following elementary bridge is useful beyond paths.

\begin{lemma}[Homogeneous-to-affine escape]
Let $\mathcal H$ be a real linear family of $D$-level Hermitian matrices.
Assume that for every nonzero balanced $c\in\R^D$ there is a simple-spectrum
$H\in\mathcal H$, with a labeling $E$, such that
\begin{equation}
 \sum_i c_iE_{\sigma(i)}\neq0
 \quad\text{for every }\sigma\in S_D.                       \label{eq:hom-escape}
\end{equation}
Then for every target $a\in\R$ there is $H_a\in\mathcal H$ for which
\begin{equation}
 \sum_i c_iE^{(a)}_{\sigma(i)}\neq a
 \quad\text{for every }\sigma\in S_D.                       \label{eq:aff-escape}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For the witness in \eqref{eq:hom-escape}, put
$A_\sigma=\sum_i c_iE_{\sigma(i)}\neq0$.  The scaled Hamiltonian $sH$ has
energies $sE_i$.  Equality $sA_\sigma=a$ excludes one value
$s=a/A_\sigma$ for each of the finitely many permutations; $s=0$ is also
excluded to preserve simplicity.  Choose any other real $s$.  Then $sH$ is
the required member of the linear family.
\end{proof}

\section{Local spectral escape and genericity}
\label{sec:local-proof}

We isolate the algebraic input to Theorem~\ref{thm:local-capstone}.

\begin{lemma}[Nearest-neighbour Pauli path escape]
For every $n\geq3$ and every nonzero balanced $c\in\R^{2^n}$, the family
\eqref{eq:pauli-path} contains a simple-spectrum Hamiltonian whose energies
satisfy \eqref{eq:hom-escape} for every labeling permutation.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The proof is an induction by endpoint extension.  For a simple Hamiltonian
$K$ on the first $m$ sites and an endpoint observable $A$, form the graph on
energy labels in which $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are adjacent when
$\langle\alpha|A|\beta\rangle\neq0$.  Two properties are open: simplicity of
$K$, and connected nonbipartiteness of this endpoint graph.

For two sites, onsite and edge Pauli strings span every traceless Hermitian
matrix.  Choose a simple spectrum and an eigenbasis in which an endpoint
observable has connected nonbipartite transition graph.  This supplies the
base.

For the induction step, attach one qubit.  A diagonal onsite field first
separates the two copies of the old spectrum.  Edge operators of the form
$A\otimes X$, $A\otimes Y$, and $A\otimes Z$, with $A$ ranging over the full
endpoint Hermitian algebra, independently vary the cross-block and
within-block transition derivatives.  If a proposed balanced real
combination of the new eigenbranches vanished for every such variation,
first-order perturbation along diagonal directions would make its total
coefficient vanish in each block.  Off-diagonal second-order variations
then equate coefficients across every edge of the endpoint graph.  Connectedness
makes each block coefficient constant; an odd cycle removes the possible
sign alternation between blocks.  Balance forces that constant to be zero,
contradicting $c\neq0$.  Hence some allowed endpoint perturbation escapes the
relation.  The same perturbations preserve simplicity and produce a
connected nonbipartite endpoint graph on the enlarged spectrum.  This closes
the induction.  Applying the argument to each permuted coefficient vector
and avoiding the resulting finite union of proper zero sets gives
\eqref{eq:hom-escape} simultaneously for all permutations.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Null exceptional sets]
For the path family \eqref{eq:pauli-path}:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item Hamiltonians with a nontrivial balanced integer spectral relation form
  a Lebesgue-null set;
\item on every connected open simple-spectrum chart, both maps
  $\theta\mapsto\beta^{\rm disc}(\theta)$ and
  $\theta\mapsto\beta^{\rm wind}(\theta)$ are real analytic and
  nondegenerate in the metric-Diophantine sense.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On a simple-spectrum chart, ordered eigenvalues are real analytic.  For a
fixed nonzero balanced integer vector $k$, multiply
$\sum_i k_iE_{\sigma(i)}$ over all permutations $\sigma$.  This is a
symmetric polynomial in the eigenvalues, hence a polynomial in the
characteristic coefficients and therefore a real polynomial in $\theta$.
The escape lemma makes it nonzero somewhere.  Its zero set is null.  Taking
the countable union over $k\in\Z^D\setminus\{0\}$ proves (i).

For (ii), any real affine relation constant on a nonempty open subset of a
chart extends by analyticity throughout that connected chart.  Symmetrizing
over labelings gives an affine spectral obstruction on the full family.
The preceding escape lemma and Appendix~\ref{app:affine} rule this out.
Thus $1$ together with the coordinate functions of each gap map is linearly
independent on every open subset, which is analytic nondegeneracy.  Division
by $\lambda_2-\lambda_1$ is analytic and nonzero on a simple ordered chart,
so the same conclusion holds for the ratio map.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:local-capstone}]
By the null-set lemma, maximal projective rank holds almost everywhere.
Metric extremality of nondegenerate analytic maps
\cite{KleinbockMargulis1998}, followed by Law~2, gives exponents $D-1$ and
$D-2$ for the two gap maps.  The intersection of these three full-measure
events remains full measure.  On that same event, Law~3 gives every
collision and moment conclusion for all positive weights and all replica
orders.  Finally, maximal projective rank with $D\geq3$ makes two anchored
frequencies rationally independent, so a common exact phase return would
create a nontrivial rational relation unless $t=0$.
\end{proof}

\section{Thermodynamic identities}

For completeness, $0<b_\varepsilon<1$ follows from
$0<\arcsin(\varepsilon/2)<\pi/2$.  Equation
\eqref{eq:finite-rate} is the logarithm of
$\rho_{n,q}=b_\varepsilon^{q^n-1}$.  Dividing by $q^n$ gives
$(1-q^{-n})I_\varepsilon\to I_\varepsilon$, proving
\eqref{eq:hilbert-rate}.  Since $q^n-1\to\infty$ and
$b_\varepsilon<1$, \eqref{eq:rho-zero} follows.  Finally,
\[
 \frac{\log[-\log\rho_{n,q}]}n
 =\frac{\log(q^n-1)+\log I_\varepsilon}{n}
 \longrightarrow\log q,
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:double-log}.
