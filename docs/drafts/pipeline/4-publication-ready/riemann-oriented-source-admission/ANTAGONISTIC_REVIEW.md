# Antagonistic general-journal review

Manuscript: **Orientation–transgression completeness at the Riemann divisor**

Review date: 2026-08-02

## Recommendation

**Reject as a general-scientific-journal research article in its current form. Retain as a
strong internal frontier synthesis and formal preprint draft.**

This verdict is not a correctness objection. The load-bearing mathematical statements compile,
the axiom surface is ordinary, the claim trace is unusually explicit, and the manuscript now
distinguishes genuinely different routes instead of presenting one completed-incidence route as
the whole frontier. Rejection follows from scientific significance and completeness criteria.

## What the paper genuinely contributes

1. It supplies a real object rather than another decorative RH equivalence: the all-prime
   oriented Calderón–Witt source and its canonical observer decomposition
   
   \[
   F=F_{\mathrm{abs}}+F_{\partial}.
   \]

   The decomposition is unique under exact boundary/orientation vanishing laws.

2. It identifies the complete relative invariant after admission-sector restriction:

   \[
   \mathfrak o_S(x)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad hx\in S.
   \]

   This is a sharper statement than “orientation is lost.” It identifies where additional
   information lives and what must be preserved.

3. It corrects a material research-management error. Corpus does not have one remaining source,
   transport, or positivity lemma. It has multiple open cores at different arrows. Route L has a
   completed carrier but lacks graph-domain landing; Routes E, C1, N, O, P, and Q lack source
   estimates or carriers; Routes G, R, analytic–Hurwitz, and HT/recurrence lack faithful
   continuation; Route J lacks source-relative polarization after constructing countertransport.

4. It gives a minimal completed-incidence classification. No bounded boundary extension or full
   algebraic-core comparison is logically required; closed source landing and one exact detector
   trace suffice. This is useful frontier localization.

5. It explicitly prevents circular credit. A closed set chosen after reading divisor displacement
   is not a producer theorem. The source must construct the graph domain and prove landing before
   divisor scalarization.

These are meaningful internal advances. They explain why source formation, transport, admission,
and consumer equivalences must be audited separately.

## Fatal objections under the requested criteria

### 1. No proof of RH and no new unconditional zero exclusion

The paper proves neither RH nor any new zero-free region, density theorem, zero-spacing theorem,
or one-sided divisor estimate. Its completed-incidence corollary classifies admission as
equivalent to critical-line placement. The source-derived graph-domain landing theorem remains
unproved.

### 2. The universal law is structurally strong but its abstract core is elementary

The observer split follows from a Green decomposition, and the dense-admission threshold follows
from the elementary fact that a closed set containing a dense range is the whole target. The
scientific content therefore rests entirely on the arithmetic source realizing these hypotheses
canonically and on cross-route consequences. A hostile reviewer will ask:

- Why is this not split exact linear algebra applied to a purpose-built complex?
- Which theorem about zeta zeros becomes easier after the split?
- Which previously open statement is proved, rather than renamed as relative primitive escape?
- What route-to-route deduction follows that was unavailable from the individual constructions?

Current manuscript answers the first question partially, but not the remaining three.

### 3. “Subsumes the routes” is explanatory, not yet a formal transfer theorem

The paper gives a persuasive common vocabulary for dense radicals, maximal verticality,
finite-Rees failure, Ward mass, Calkin regularization, differential independence, and recurrence
protection. It does not construct a single functorial shadow map from every named route into the
oriented source complex. Consequently “these are shadows of one law” remains a scientific
interpretation assembled from separately proved facts, not one theorem whose hypotheses have been
instantiated for every row.

### 4. Novelty against literature is unverified

User instruction deferred literature review, so no external search was performed. That is
compatible with current research workflow but incompatible with general-journal submission.
Paper has no bibliography or comparison to exact sequences, Hodge contraction, relative
cohomology, boundary triples, rigged Hilbert spaces, Connes–Meyer/Burnol source formalisms, or
existing RH equivalence literature. A reviewer cannot assess priority or novelty.

### 5. Manuscript deliberately leaves eleven decisive open producer theorems

Section 8 is honest and useful, but it directly violates the requested criterion “not leave open
questions, but answers them.” Naming each exact first unproved theorem is not solving it. At least
one producer theorem must be proved independently of RH to turn framework into an RH advance.

### 6. The paper does not prove a classic route impossible

It proves strong no-gos for bounded, finite, product-topology, finite-Rees, and related
subcategories. It correctly avoids converting those into a route-wide impossibility theorem for
CCM, Hilbert–Pólya, Hedenmalm, Suzuki, Deninger, or Connes. Therefore requested alternative (c) is
not met.

## What a renowned general-journal reviewer would demand

1. **One non-equivalent arithmetic consequence.** Prove a source-derived estimate, domain theorem,
   compactness statement, or sign which was not already known equivalent to RH. Ideally it should
   close one arrow in at least one live route.

2. **Functorial universality.** State a category of oriented source contractions and prove the
   primary/secondary split is natural under source-faithful chain maps. Instantiate it for at least
   three mathematically independent corpus routes with explicit shadow maps.

3. **A cross-route theorem.** Derive a new no-go or positive theorem in Route E, G, J, N, O, P, Q,
   or R solely from the universal law plus that route's source map. Without this, “unification” is
   taxonomy.

4. **Canonicality audit.** Prove the orientation line, Green retraction class, and relative
   obstruction are invariant under allowed choices of section, contraction, prime pair, and source
   presentation. A purpose-built split is not a universal physical law unless choice dependence is
   controlled.

5. **External novelty review.** Compare precise theorem statements with existing relative
   cohomology, homological perturbation, boundary-triple, rigged-Hilbert, co-Poisson, and RH source
   frameworks. Add real citations and narrow priority claims.

6. **A discriminating prediction.** Give a theorem or falsifiable consequence which distinguishes
   orientation–transgression completeness from generic exact-sequence bookkeeping.
