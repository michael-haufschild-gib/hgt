# Antagonist referee report

Manuscript: *Cofinite Universality of Reflected Lambert Spectra and a Local-Source Barrier for the Riemann Hypothesis*

Review standard: renowned general mathematics journal. No novelty, significance, or publication
credit is assigned to mechanized verification.

Date: 2026-07-27

## Verdict

**Reject at the requested significance threshold; potentially publishable after reframing in a
specialist analysis journal.**

The central reflected-Lambert completeness theorem is mathematically coherent and apparently new.
It is universal over positive spectra satisfying the stated translated Blaschke-divergence
hypothesis, stable under finite deletion, and upgraded from linear density to a pole-free
nonlinear local-factor orbit. The prime-logarithm specialization is clean and elementary.

That does not yet make the RH consequence a major advance. The no-classifier theorem excludes only
the simultaneous conjunction of compact-open local-germ dependence, continuity, and finite-factor
invariance. None of Hilbert--Pólya, CCM, Weil positivity, trace formulae, or global Euler-product
methods is excluded. The surviving “global weighted-tail” direction is identified but not
constructed. Thus the manuscript does not meet the project's stronger alternatives:

- it does not prove RH;
- it does not deliver a new source mechanism that advances an RH proof;
- it does not close a famous classical route;
- it does not classify all spectra for which the reflected system is complete.

## Hostile objections

### 1. “Universal” is conditional and method-relative

The law covers arbitrary injective positive spectra satisfying strong translated
sector-Blaschke divergence. The paper correctly states this. It does not prove that the condition
is necessary, nor that every complete reflected-Lambert spectrum satisfies an equivalent density
law. “Universal” is defensible only as “arithmetic-independent under one explicit spectral
hypothesis,” not as a maximal classification.

### 2. The RH barrier targets a chosen information model

Compact-open germ topology intentionally forgets the global Euler presentation. A continuous
finite-factor-invariant reader is therefore a narrow target. A skeptical referee can reasonably
ask which active RH program actually asserts all three hypotheses. Without a named natural
construction forced into that category, the theorem is a structural warning rather than a
decisive route closure.

### 3. The survivor is descriptive, not constructive

The trilemma says that a successful method must retain weighted-tail information, react to finite
factors, or be discontinuous. This is logically complete, but it does not supply or control a
surviving RH detector. The paper answers its local question while leaving the main source-side
positivity problem unchanged.

### 4. The Hankel criterion does not create significance by itself

The manuscript appropriately disclaims novelty for Hankel reformulations. Coupling an
RH-equivalent criterion to a dense local orbit proves incompatibility with one classifier class;
it does not move the criterion's missing sign estimate closer to proof.

### 5. General-journal case needs a positive global theorem

The strongest revision would pair the local collapse with an explicit global escape theorem:
one source-defined weighted-tail observable that lies outside the collapsed topology, reconstructs
the complete off-critical divisor, and quantitatively classifies every false-RH configuration.
That would turn the trilemma from a no-go into a local-to-global structure theorem.

## Correctness notes requiring final copy revision

- Definition of the generator group should state explicitly that \(b\in\mathbb R\).
- The classifier theorem should say that the map is evaluated on the trace image
  \(\mathcal T g\), not directly on an even germ \(g\).
- “Same closed orbit” should be formulated by stating that the difference of the two trace images
  lies in the closed increment group.
- The trace-class detector uses conjugation only in its application; the standalone statement and
  proof should make clear that an absent conjugate causes no problem.

These are repairable presentation points, not evidence of a false central theorem.

## Publication-threshold conclusion

The manuscript is genuinely interesting, but not at the strength requested by the project. It is
as broad as the current Blaschke--Lambert proof mechanism, not as universal as mathematically
possible. An honest referee cannot confirm the user's significance criterion until the negative
local law is joined to a substantive global survivor or strengthened to a natural famous-route
no-go.
