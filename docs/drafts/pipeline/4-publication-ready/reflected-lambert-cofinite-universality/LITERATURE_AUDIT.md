# Literature and novelty audit

Audit date: 2026-07-27  
Scope: reflected derivative-Lambert completeness, cofinite spectral deletion, nonlinear
pole-free local-factor orbit closure, continuous invariant quotients, finite Euler products,
zeta universality, and Hankel criteria for RH.

## Verdict

No checked source contains the paper's exact theorem package:

1. translated sector-Blaschke divergence for an arbitrary positive spectrum;
2. carrier-adapted derivative-Lambert completeness via Lambert–Möbius recovery;
3. carrier-independent FE-reflected Schwarz-real completeness;
4. cofinite stability under arbitrary finite spectral deletion;
5. dense orbit generated only by pole-free local multiplicative increments;
6. one-point maximal continuous Hausdorff quotient on the analytic fibre;
7. specialization to prime logarithms using only Euler's reciprocal-prime divergence;
8. obstruction to a continuous, finite-factor-invariant local classifier for the
   RH-equivalent source-Hankel sign.

Novelty claim should remain “to best of our knowledge” until specialist external review.
No novelty is claimed for Blaschke theory, Möbius inversion, ordinary exponential
completeness, zeta universality, or Hankel reformulations of RH.

## Priority boundaries

Paper may claim:

- new universal sufficient condition for reflected derivative-Lambert completeness;
- new pole-free nonlinear orbit law and quotient classification;
- new exact local-source trilemma when joined to prime logs and the source-Hankel RH
  criterion.

Paper must not claim:

- first Hankel criterion for RH;
- a proof or disproof of RH;
- a no-go for Hilbert–Pólya, CCM, trace formulas, or Euler products in general;
- necessity of strong sector-Blaschke completeness for all possible completeness
  proofs;
- identity of globally distinct Euler products;
- novelty from machine verification.

## Significance assessment

Prime-only tangent density is too narrow for a broad journal. Abstracting to arbitrary
strongly sector-Blaschke-complete spectra changes the result into a reusable theorem in
complex approximation, while pole-free nonlinear density supplies a genuine dynamical
classification rather than an infinitesimal observation. The RH application is then a
sharp methodological consequence, not the sole source of interest.

Expected audience: complex analysts, approximation theorists, analytic number theorists,
and researchers on spectral/trace-formula approaches to RH.
