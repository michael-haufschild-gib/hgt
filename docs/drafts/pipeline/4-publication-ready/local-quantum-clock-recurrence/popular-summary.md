# Popular summary

## Headline

One quantum past can conceal every possible return

## Standfirst

However accurately one return signal is watched for any finite time, the
same starting state, same return test and same list of energy levels can
still be compatible with every possible return probability at a chosen
later instant—from none to perfect revival.

## Summary

Imagine continuously asking a quantum system one question: “How much have
you returned to where you started?” Watch as long as desired short of a
deadline and make the error bar as small as desired. Does that history fix
the answer at the deadline?

Not without more information. For any positive error bar, keep the starting
state and return measurement fixed. A whole family of quantum energy
operators can track one common return record throughout the observed window.
They even have exactly the same energy levels. At the deadline, however,
family members realize every probability between zero and one: exact
orthogonality, perfect revival and everything between.

In a dual version, one uniform field acts identically on every qubit. That
Hamiltonian is fixed and invariant under every rearrangement of sites;
different symmetric quantum preparations hide different futures. The
fixed-preparation version instead changes Hamiltonian eigenvectors without
changing any energy level.

Perfect return has a sharp physical price in the nearest-neighbour version:
at least one link in the path must be exactly severed. This is unavoidable,
not a convenient feature of one construction. Every less-than-perfect
future remains realizable with a connected path.

This freedom survives a strong physical restriction. Every probability,
including perfect certainty, can be realized by a one-dimensional chain in
which sites couple only to nearest neighbours, while starting site, return
test and complete list of energy levels stay fixed. At perfect return, the
active chain is accompanied by an uncoupled set of spectator sites; the
result claims locality, not connectedness.

The chain can sit inside a full noninteracting fermion system. Every
particle-number sector then has exactly the same complete many-body energy
list across the whole family; the possible energies are all sums of the
shared one-particle energies. The hidden future difference is visible in
the one-excitation return experiment, not in the spectrum.

Even any preselected finite list of energy moments can be identical. The
hidden disagreement is suppressed exponentially before the target and the
construction uses the minimum number of distinct energy levels permitted by
those moment constraints.

No algorithm escapes the ambiguity from that record alone. Its single
forecast must miss by at least half the probability scale for one compatible
endpoint.

This is not quantum randomness. Given the exact Hamiltonian, unitary dynamics
fixes the future. Result says that even knowing its complete energy spectrum,
the starting state and the return measurement does not make one
finite-precision return history universally predictive; orientation of the
Hamiltonian eigenvectors still matters.

The claim is also not about black holes, cosmology or the origin of time,
and it does not cover interacting matter. Its surprising content is
narrower: one apparently complete quantum return history can conceal every
possible answer to the same return question at a later chosen instant,
even inside an isospectral free-fermion many-body setting.
