# Novelty and collision audit

Search date: 28 July 2026.

## Exact surviving contribution

For every finite moment order, positive target time, strictly shorter
continuous observation window and positive error radius, one fixed pure
state and its fixed return projector evolve under a continuum of finite
nonnegative self-adjoint Hamiltonians. The Hamiltonians are all unitarily
equivalent and have exactly the same spectrum. One baseline
survival-probability record is uniformly compatible with every candidate on
the whole window; fixed-state energy moments agree; yet terminal survival
probability ranges exactly over \([0,1]\). An equivalent realization uses
one fixed permutation-invariant uniform onsite qubit field and different
symmetric pure preparations. For every terminal probability
\(p\in[0,1]\), the fixed-protocol Hamiltonian can additionally be chosen
tridiagonal, giving an exact nearest-neighbour one-particle chain. At
\(p=1\), it is a Krylov block plus an uncoupled diagonal spectator block.
Every tridiagonal fixed-protocol realization at that endpoint must contain
at least one zero path coupling; connectedness fails by necessity. For
every \(p<1\), the positive-weight Krylov representative has every adjacent
coupling nonzero. Thus connectedness changes sharply only at exact perfect
return.
It embeds as the exact one-excitation block of a Hermitian
particle-number-preserving nearest-neighbour occupation matrix. Canonical
second quantization gives, at every fermion number, a complete Slater
eigenbasis and the same subset-sum spectrum for every candidate. The
construction gives an exact sine-power discrepancy, an
algorithm-independent one-half endpoint lower bound and a sharp
\(n+1\)-level support threshold.

## Direct collision: Manos–Weilenmann–Navascués

[Manos, Weilenmann and Navascués, *Timed demolition
measurements*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.06912), version 2 (2026), is
conceptually close and must be treated as such.

It studies extrapolation of quantum measurement timelines with unknown
Hamiltonian, state, observable and dimension under energy constraints. It
proves superexponential precision requirements and exhibits “fog banks”:
datasets with complete unpredictability at one future time and recovered
predictability later. Appendix J uses binomial coefficients and sine/complex
power suppression to construct hard extrapolation instances under an energy
bound. A separate fog-bank example obtains a continuum of future outcome
distributions by convex combination.

Consequences for priority:

- Generic quantum-extrapolation impossibility is not new here.
- “Some structural prior is necessary” is not a defensible novelty claim.
- Complete future uncertainty and a continuum of possible measurement
  outcomes are not new at the conceptual level.
- Sine-power/binomial hiding is not a new mathematical mechanism.

The surviving distinction is now stronger than the original conjunction:
one fixed preparation and projector; candidate Hamiltonians with one exactly
common spectrum; continuous full-window survival agreement; arbitrary
finite fixed-state energy-moment matching; every exact terminal return
probability; nearest-neighbour realizations for every probability in the
closed interval; an exact occupation-space embedding; common complete spectra
in every intrinsic free-fermion sector; and minimum common spectral
support. The dual same-Hamiltonian realization is also supplied. No
searched source states that conjunction. Search cannot establish priority.

## Targeted isospectral and nearest-neighbour search

Searches for fixed-state survival ambiguity under isospectral Hamiltonians,
isospectral return amplitudes, Jacobi-matrix endpoint return laws and
isospectral quantum-walk paths found no direct collision.

- Brüning, Chelkak and Korotyaev,
  [*Inverse spectral analysis for finite matrix-valued Jacobi
  operators*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0607809), proves a general inverse
  spectral correspondence for finite Jacobi operators. It establishes the
  background link between spectral weights and tridiagonal operators, not
  the finite-window ambiguity law.
- Jafarov and Van der Jeugt,
  [*Quantum state transfer in spin chains with q-deformed interaction
  terms*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1005.2912), uses Jacobi matrices of discrete
  orthogonal polynomials for nearest-neighbour state transfer. It does not
  give one fixed endpoint record, one common spectrum and every later
  return probability.
- Searches also returned general survival-probability, Krylov and
  isospectral-transformation work, but no result with the manuscript's full
  quantifier conjunction.

The nearest-neighbour step itself is an application of the classical
Krylov/Jacobi mechanism. Novelty is claimed for combining it with the exact
continuum ambiguity, fixed protocol, common spectrum, moment matching and
uniform finite-window law—not for tridiagonalization in isolation.
Likewise, endpoint cyclicity for an irreducible Jacobi matrix is classical.
Novelty is claimed only for the resulting sharp perfect-return boundary
inside the full fixed-protocol law.

Chakrabarti and Van der Jeugt (2010) identify Jacobi matrices with
single-excitation blocks of inhomogeneous nearest-neighbour XY chains.
Bernard et al. (2025) solve inhomogeneous XY chains through their
free-fermion one-particle matrices. These are background for the occupation
and exterior-power lifts; neither states the return-record ambiguity law.

## Other prior art

- [Valls et al., *Forecasting Quantum Observables: A Compressed Sensing
  Approach with Performance Guarantees*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14897)
  gives certificates under sparse, well-separated Bohr frequencies. It is a
  positive, structured counterpart, not a collision with the exact
  survival-record theorem.
- Stoler, Saleh and Teich (1985) introduced binomial states. Zhang et al.
  (2009) studied even and odd binomial states. The state families are not
  claimed as new.
- Hsu (1954), *On characteristic functions which coincide in the
  neighborhood of zero*, collides with the earlier infinite exact-local
  characteristic-function route. That route remains retired.
- Vandermonde moment determinacy on a fixed finite support is classical.
  Novelty is not claimed for the linear-algebra lemma itself.
- Classical ill-conditioning of analytic continuation and trigonometric
  extrapolation is background, not a claimed contribution.

## Claims excluded

- No claim about full state histories or process tomography.
- No fixed dimension or bandwidth as precision tends to zero.
- No claim that a finite Loschmidt zero is a thermodynamic phase transition.
- No experimental demonstration.
- No translationally invariant or fixed-coupling realization in the
  fixed-preparation protocol; no interacting or thermodynamic-limit
  realization in either protocol. The dual varying-preparation protocol
  does have one fixed permutation-invariant uniform onsite qubit field.
- No inference about black holes, inflation, cosmology or fundamental time.

## Nature significance status

Nature asks for original research of outstanding importance, interdisciplinary
interest, and results that are novel, arresting, and have immediate and
far-reaching implications. Fixed preparation, fixed projector and identical
spectrum materially strengthen novelty and remove the most obvious
operational objection. Exact nearest-neighbour chains now remove the
dense-matrix objection on the whole closed interval, although the
perfect-return representative is necessarily disconnected. The
uniform-field qubit
theorem removes spatial inhomogeneity in the dual varying-preparation
protocol, and the all-sector subset-sum theorem removes the claim that only
the one-particle spectrum is controlled. Direct 2026 collision still absorbs
broad extrapolation-impossibility framing. Without an interacting or
homogeneous fixed-preparation extension, an experimental realization, or a
new quantitative physical prediction, likely editorial outcome remains
rejection without review.
