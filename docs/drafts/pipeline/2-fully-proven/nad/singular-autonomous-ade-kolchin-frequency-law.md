# Singular autonomous ADEs obey a finite Kolchin-frequency law

- **ID:** `singular-autonomous-ade-kolchin-frequency-law`
- **Stage:** 2-fully-proven / needs-audit
- **Type:** THEOREM (paper) / GENERAL HYPERTRANSCENDENCE LANDSCAPE
- **Scientific status:** `[PROVED] [NOVELTY UNVERIFIED]`
- **Statement:** Every holomorphic Bohr-almost-periodic solution of a nonzero autonomous algebraic
  differential equation has finite rational Bohr-frequency rank, even when the equation is
  implicit, singular, reducible, and nonunique as an initial-value problem. The forced finite
  carrier is the uniform algebraic dimension of the equation's prolonged jet varieties.

## 1. Why the regular-flow theorem is not enough

The regular theorem converts an order-`n` equation

```text
f^(n)=R(f,...,f^(n-1))                                    (1)
```

into a uniquely solvable autonomous flow on `C^n`. Cartwright--O'Brien then bounds the Bohr rank by
`2n`. An implicit equation

```text
P(f,f',...,f^(n))=0,
0!=P in C[Y_0,...,Y_n],                                   (2)
```

may fail the implicit-function theorem on its separant locus. Distinct analytic branches can share
the same low jet, so the jet map into `C^n` need not be injective. This is the exact singular escape
left by the regular proof.

The replacement is not another flow. It is the algebraic size of the whole compatible jet tower.

## 2. Kolchin jet carrier

Let

```text
C{Y}=C[Y_0,Y_1,Y_2,...],
delta(Y_j)=Y_(j+1)                                       (3)
```

be the ordinary differential-polynomial ring. Let

```text
I_P=sqrt[delta]((P))                                      (4)
```

be the radical differential ideal generated by `P`. The existence of the analytic solution makes
`I_P` proper. For `m>=0`, put

```text
I_(P,m)=I_P intersection C[Y_0,...,Y_m],
V_(P,m)=Z(I_(P,m)) subset C^(m+1).                        (5)
```

Define the **Kolchin jet capacity**

```text
kappa(P)=sup_(m>=0) dim_C V_(P,m).                        (6)
```

`[PROVED]` This number is finite.

### Proof

Ritt--Raudenbush theory, via the finite essential-prime decomposition for radical ordinary
differential ideals, writes the proper radical differential ideal as a finite
intersection of prime differential ideals:

```text
I_P=intersection_(a=1)^q p_a.                             (7)
```

Every `p_a` contains the nonzero differential polynomial `P`. Hence the image of the single
differential indeterminate `Y` in `Frac(C{Y}/p_a)` is differentially algebraic over `C`; the prime
component has differential transcendence degree zero.

For an ordinary prime differential ideal of differential type zero, Kolchin's dimension-polynomial
theorem says that

```text
m |-> trdeg_C Frac(C[Y_0,...,Y_m]/(p_a intersection C[Y_0,...,Y_m]))
                                                               (8)
```

is eventually constant. Therefore the dimensions of all truncated varieties belonging to `p_a`
are uniformly bounded. There are only finitely many components in `(7)`, and only finitely many
initial truncation levels before eventual constancy. Taking their maximum proves `kappa(P)<infinity`.

This step includes every singular component. No separant is inverted and no initial-value
uniqueness is assumed.

## 3. The compact jet tower

Let `f` be holomorphic and uniformly Bohr almost periodic on a horizontal strip. Let `X_f` be its
compact translation hull in the topology of uniform convergence on closed substrips. Cauchy
estimates make every derivative continuous on the hull.

For `m>=0`, define

```text
J_m:X_f->C^(m+1),
J_m(g)=(g(0),g'(0),...,g^(m)(0)),
X_m=J_m(X_f).                                             (9)
```

Every translate of `f` satisfies `(2)` because the equation is autonomous. Local uniform limits
and derivative convergence show that every `g in X_f` also satisfies `(2)`. Evaluating every
differential polynomial on `g` defines a differential-ring homomorphism whose kernel is a radical
differential ideal containing `P`; hence it contains `I_P`. Evaluating at zero gives

```text
X_m subset V_(P,m).                                       (10)
```

Since `X_m` is compact and `V_(P,m)` is a complex algebraic variety,

```text
dim_cover X_m<=2 dim_C V_(P,m)<=2 kappa(P).               (11)
```

The truncation maps `X_(m+1)->X_m` are surjective. Moreover

```text
X_f homeomorphic inverse_limit_m X_m.                     (12)
```

Indeed, the infinite jet map is injective by the holomorphic identity theorem. Conversely, a
compatible sequence of finite jets defines nested nonempty closed fibers in the compact space
`X_f`; their intersection contains a hull function realizing the full jet, and injectivity makes it
unique.

Covering dimension does not increase under inverse limits of compact metric spaces with surjective
bonding maps. Equations `(11)`--`(12)` therefore give

```text
dim_cover X_f<=2 kappa(P)<infinity.                        (13)
```

## 4. Singular Kolchin-frequency theorem

`[PROVED]` Let `Gamma(f)` be the additive group generated by the Bohr spectrum of `f`. Then

```text
rank_Q Gamma(f)=dim_cover X_f<=2 kappa(P).                 (14)
```

### Proof

The hull of a Bohr almost-periodic function is a compact connected metrizable abelian group: real
translation has dense image. Its character group is the frequency group generated by the Bohr
spectrum. For compact connected abelian groups, Pontryagin--Takahashi dimension theory identifies
covering dimension with the rational rank of the character group. Combine this equality with
`(13)`.

The theorem is weaker numerically than the regular `rank<2n` estimate, because `kappa(P)` records
all singular differential components. It is stronger categorically: it requires neither an
explicit vector field nor uniqueness.

## 5. Divisor and observable carriers

For a nonzero holomorphic hull function, the zero divisor is the Poincare--Lelong distribution

```text
Div(g)=(1/(2 pi)) Delta log|g|.                            (15)
```

The divisor map is continuous under local uniform convergence and is translation-equivariant. Its
image hull is therefore a compact abelian factor of `X_f`. The same is true for any continuous
translation-equivariant observable `O:X_f->Y` into a compact abelian carrier. Pontryagin duality and
`(14)` give

```text
rank_Q Gamma(Div(f))<=2 kappa(P),
rank_Q Gamma(O(f))<=2 kappa(P).                            (16)
```

Thus singular branching cannot hide additional prime-frequency rank in a divisor or other
continuous equivariant shadow.

## 6. Positive complexity law

For a finite prime cutoff, let

```text
V_N=span_Q{log p:p<=N},
dim_Q V_N=pi(N).                                          (17)
```

Suppose a scalar almost-periodic carrier `f_N`, or a continuous equivariant observable of its hull,
retains `V_N` and satisfies a nonzero autonomous implicit ADE `P_N=0`. Then `(14)`--`(17)` force

```text
kappa(P_N)>=ceil(pi(N)/2).                                (18)
```

> **Singular ADE carrier law.** Every successful cofinal autonomous algebraic-differential
> realization is forced to grow its Kolchin jet capacity at least linearly with the number of
> retained primes. Singular or nonunique equations do not provide a zero-cost finite-state escape;
> their differential varieties must acquire unbounded jet dimension.

For a fixed equation `P`, `kappa(P)` is fixed, so it cannot carry all prime cutoffs. The positive
ways forward are:

1. a sequence `P_N` with unbounded Kolchin jet capacity;
2. a nonautonomous coefficient field that explicitly carries the prime module;
3. an infinite-dimensional operator, test space, or determinant carrier;
4. a global constraint not asserting finite algebraic-differential closure;
5. failure of compact almost-periodic hull hypotheses, accompanied by a separate cofinal carrier
   theorem.

## 7. Consequences for classical RH implementations

The theorem does not reject an RH-equivalent criterion. It closes a concrete implementation escape
and specifies the replacement resource.

1. **Hilbert--Polya and Connes.** A finite singular scalar ODE or DAE realization must expose a jet
   variety with capacity at least `(18)`; otherwise the arithmetic state must live in the operator
   domain, spectral measure, determinant, or quotient algebra.
2. **de Branges.** A singular finite-order equation for the Hermite--Biehler phase or canonical
   Hamiltonian must have growing Kolchin capacity, or the nested spaces themselves must carry the
   prime-frequency module.
3. **Nyman--Beurling.** Any finite implicit differential closure of the dilation orbit must pay
   `(18)`; a viable closure instead retains an infinite-dimensional approximation carrier.
4. **Weil positivity.** A finite singular differential parametrization of the test-function response
   must have growing jet capacity; otherwise the complete test space and prime distribution remain
   the carrier.
5. **Li criteria.** A fixed singular recurrence/ADE for the coefficient generator cannot retain the
   cofinal prime module. The coefficient hierarchy, its nonautonomous data, or an infinite divisor
   object must supply the growing capacity.

## 8. Attacks and sharp boundaries

1. **Low-jet nonuniqueness is real.** For `(y')^2=4y`, the germs `y=0` and `y=z^2` share the same
   order-one initial jet at the singular point. The proof therefore uses the entire compatible jet
   tower, not an injective order-`n` jet map.
2. **Spurious prolongation components are removed.** Using only `P,delta P,...` can leave singular
   algebraic components of artificially growing dimension. The radical differential ideal in `(4)`
   and its finite prime decomposition are load-bearing.
3. **Analyticity is load-bearing.** Smooth nonunique ODEs can glue branches with arbitrarily long
   flat contact. Holomorphic full jets determine the germ.
4. **Autonomy is load-bearing.** An almost-periodic coefficient field may import an arbitrary
   frequency module. Such coefficients must be counted as a carrier rather than hidden in `P`.
5. **Compact Bohr hull is load-bearing.** Without compactness, the inverse-limit fiber argument and
   covering-dimension theorem do not apply.
6. **One differential unknown is load-bearing only for notation.** A finite tuple of unknowns obeying
   a differential-type-zero autonomous ideal has the same proof with its Kolchin dimension
   polynomial; the capacity replaces `(6)`.
7. **The theorem supplies finite rank, not regularity.** It does not turn a singular equation into a
   flow or claim a unique continuation law from a low jet.
