## 1. Setup

Let `f,g:X->X` be legal unary laws and let `r:X->B` be a declared conserved reporter. Let `W(f,g)`
be the free word monoid on the two laws, acting on `X`. Define

\[
 K_r^\infty(x,y)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 r(w x)=r(w y)\text{ for every }w\in W(f,g).
 \tag{FR.1}
\]

This is not the present observer kernel `r(x)=r(y)`. It is the kernel of the entire future reporter
orbit.

## 2. Greatest conserved future congruence

### Theorem 2.1

`K_r^infinity` is an equivalence relation preserved by both `f` and `g`, and

\[
 K_r^\infty(x,y)\Longrightarrow r(x)=r(y).
 \tag{FR.2}
\]

It is greatest with these properties. Precisely, if `E` is any equivalence relation preserved by
`f,g` and

\[
 E(x,y)\Longrightarrow r(x)=r(y),
 \tag{FR.3}
\]

then `E` is contained in `K_r^infinity`.

### Proof

Reflexivity, symmetry, and transitivity hold pointwise for every word. Prefixing a word by `f` or
`g` proves preservation. The empty word gives `(FR.2)`.

For maximality, suppose `E(x,y)`. Induct on a word. The empty word uses `(FR.3)`. At an `f`-prefix,
preservation gives `E(fx,fy)` and induction applies; similarly for `g`. Thus every future reporter
value agrees. QED.

### Interpretation

`K_r^infinity` is the largest amount of identification that can be made without sacrificing the
reporter now or after any later legal execution. It is generated by a mechanism -- future testing
under the law monoid -- not supplied as an independent geometric object.

## 3. Exact future-context criterion

Let `R_(f,g)` be the minimal exchange congruence from ULF-OPM-2.

### Theorem 3.1

The following are equivalent:

1. `R_(f,g)` is contained in `K_r^infinity`;
2. every exposed exchange pair remains reporter-indistinguishable in every future context:

   \[
   r(w f(gx))=r(w g(fx))
   \quad
   \text{for every }x\in X,\ w\in W(f,g).
   \tag{FR.4}
   \]

### Proof

If `R_(f,g) subset K_r^infinity`, each generating exchange pair belongs to the future kernel, which
is exactly `(FR.4)`. Conversely, Theorem 2.1 makes `K_r^infinity` an equivalence preserved by both
laws, and `(FR.4)` says it contains every exchange generator. Minimality of `R_(f,g)` gives the
inclusion. QED.

## 4. Strict-promotion corridor

Call an equivalence `E` an `r`-conserving strict exchange promotion when:

1. `E` is preserved by `f` and `g`;
2. `E(f(gx),g(fx))` for every `x`;
3. `E(x,y)` implies `r(x)=r(y)`.

### Theorem 4.1 -- corridor and existence

Every `r`-conserving strict exchange promotion lies in the exact interval

\[
 R_{f,g}\ \subseteq\ E\ \subseteq\ K_r^\infty.
 \tag{FR.5}
\]

Such a promotion exists if and only if

\[
 R_{f,g}\subseteq K_r^\infty,
 \tag{FR.6}
\]

equivalently if and only if the future-context identities `(FR.4)` hold.

### Proof

The left inclusion follows from minimality of `R_(f,g)`. The right inclusion follows from the
maximality theorem for `K_r^infinity`. This proves necessity. For sufficiency choose
`E=R_(f,g)`. Its equivalence and law-preservation are already proved; `(FR.6)` followed by the empty
word gives reporter conservation. QED.

Thus selection is neither a scalar score nor a choice of favourite completion. It is an exact
interval test between a least required law change and a greatest reporter-permitted law change.

## 5. Delayed-detection countermodel

There is a five-state system with a state `x` such that

\[
 r(f(gx))=r(g(fx)),
 \tag{FR.7}
\]

but

\[
 r(f(f(gx)))\neq r(f(g(fx))).
 \tag{FR.8}
\]

Take states

```text
seed, leftInput, rightInput, leftResult, rightResult.
```

Define

```text
g(seed)=leftInput,       f(leftInput)=leftResult,
f(seed)=rightInput,      g(rightInput)=rightResult,
f(leftResult)=seed,      f(rightResult)=rightResult,
```

and make unspecified transitions fixed. Let `r(seed)=true` and `r=false` elsewhere. At `seed`, the
raw exchange results are `leftResult` and `rightResult`, so `(FR.7)` holds. One later `f` sends them
to `seed` and `rightResult`, so `(FR.8)` holds.

Consequently present reporter equality is insufficient for promotion selection. A law can amplify a
currently hidden residual into a future conserved charge.

## 6. Strongest mechanism consequence

Promotion selection has two independently generated boundaries:

- **lower boundary:** equations required to execute the desired compatibility;
- **upper boundary:** identifications permitted by all conserved future reporters.

A strict promotion is legal exactly when the boundaries do not cross. If they cross, strict

relative history, add higher coherence, change admission, or declare the reporter nonconserved.
The theorem does not choose among those remaining modes; it removes one mode by a future-stable
law.
