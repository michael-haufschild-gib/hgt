## 1. Horizon and observer notation

Let `X` be a topological space, `Y` a set, `q:X->Y`, and let

\[
 W_0\supseteq W_1\supseteq W_2\supseteq\cdots
\tag{EVM1.1}
\]

be closed subsets of `X`. Define

\[
 O_{\mathrm{fin}}=\bigcap_{n\ge0}q(W_n),\qquad
 O_\infty=q\!\left(\bigcap_{n\ge0}W_n\right).
\tag{EVM1.2}
\]

`O_fin` consists of observed states having a possibly different source lift at every finite
horizon. `O_infinity` consists of observed states having one source lift surviving all horizons.
For `y in Y`, write `F_y=q^{-1}({y})`.

## 2. Compact-fibre observer descent

### Theorem 2.1 -- observer/cofinal exchange

Always

\[
 O_\infty\subseteq O_{\mathrm{fin}}.
\tag{EVM1.3}
\]

For every `y` whose fibre `F_y` is compact,

\[
 y\in O_{\mathrm{fin}}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad y\in O_\infty.
\tag{EVM1.4}
\]

Consequently, if every fibre of `q` is compact, then

\[
 \boxed{q(\bigcap_nW_n)=\bigcap_nq(W_n).}
\tag{EVM1.5}
\]

In particular the conclusion holds when `q` is proper in any setting where properness implies
compact fibres.

#### Proof

If `y=q(x)` for some `x in intersection_n W_n`, then `y in q(W_n)` for every `n`; this proves
(EVM1.3).

Now assume `F_y` compact and `y in O_fin`. Put `C_n=F_y intersect W_n`. Every `C_n` is nonempty
because `y in q(W_n)`. It is closed in `F_y` because `W_n` is closed in `X`. The family is nested.
Every finite subfamily has nonempty intersection: its intersection is the member with largest
index. Compactness of `F_y` and the finite-intersection property give `intersection_n C_n`
nonempty. Choose `x` in this intersection. Then `q(x)=y` and `x in W_n` for all `n`, so
`y in O_infinity`. The reverse implication is (EVM1.3). QED.

### Corollary 2.2 -- lifetime attainment

For compact `F_y`, these are equivalent:

1. for every `n` there exists `x_n in F_y` surviving horizon `n`;
2. one `x in F_y` survives every horizon.

An observed state cannot have unbounded finite lifetime without attained infinite lifetime when
its source fibre is compact and survival sets are closed and coherent.

## 3. False immortality forces escape

### Theorem 3.1 -- compact-escape theorem

Assume

\[
 y\in O_{\mathrm{fin}}\setminus O_\infty.
\tag{EVM1.6}
\]

Then `F_y` is noncompact. More sharply, for every compact `K subseteq F_y`, there exists `N` such
that

\[
 K\cap W_n=\varnothing\qquad(n\ge N).
\tag{EVM1.7}
\]

Hence every choice `x_n in F_y intersect W_n` eventually leaves every compact subset of `F_y`.

#### Proof

Noncompactness follows from Theorem 2.1. Fix compact `K subseteq F_y`. If `K intersect W_n` were
nonempty for every `n`, these sets would form a nested family of nonempty closed subsets of compact
`K`. Their total intersection would contain `x` with `q(x)=y` and `x in W_n` for all `n`,
contradicting (EVM1.6). Therefore `K intersect W_N` is empty for some `N`. Nestedness gives
(EVM1.7) for every `n>=N`. QED.

### Theorem 3.2 -- proper resource divergence

Let `P:F_y->[0,infinity]` be a predeclared cost whose sublevel sets

\[
 K_R=\{x\in F_y:P(x)\le R\}
\tag{EVM1.8}
\]

are compact for every finite `R`. Under (EVM1.6), for every finite `R` there exists `N` such that

\[
 x\in F_y\cap W_n,\ n\ge N\quad\Longrightarrow\quad P(x)>R.
\tag{EVM1.9}
\]

#### Proof

Apply Theorem 3.1 to compact sublevel `K_R`. QED.

This is stronger than saying one chosen witness sequence has unbounded cost: every sufficiently
long-horizon witness exceeds every fixed source-cost budget.

## 4. Endogenous predecessor mechanism

Let `S subseteq X` be a closed safe-law set and `U` a compact topological space of currently legal
repair labels. For `x in X` and `u in U`, let

- `Legal(x,u)` be current admission;
- `C(x,u)` be challenges generated by executing `u` at `x`;
- `T(x,u,c) in X` be next law state after challenge `c`.

Lawbook, reporter, and admission data may be coordinates of `x`, so `T` can change next legal
challenge grammar. For `A subseteq X`, define

\[
 G_A(x)=\{u\in U:\operatorname{Legal}(x,u)\ \text{and}\
                T(x,u,c)\in A\ \text{for every }c\in C(x,u)\},
\tag{EVM1.10}
\]

\[
 \Phi(A)=\{x\in S:G_A(x)\ne\varnothing\}.
\tag{EVM1.11}
\]

Assume:

1. for every closed `A subseteq X` and every `x`, `G_A(x)` is closed in `U`;
2. `Phi(A)` is closed in `X` whenever `A` is closed.

These are exact closed-admission hypotheses. No target truth or observer is used.

### Lemma 4.1 -- monotonicity and exact control intersection

If `A subseteq B`, then `Phi(A) subseteq Phi(B)`. For every decreasing family `(A_n)` and every
`x`,

\[
 G_{\cap_nA_n}(x)=\bigcap_nG_{A_n}(x).
\tag{EVM1.12}
\]

#### Proof

Both follow from universal quantification over the same generated challenge set: a successor in
`A` lies in `B`, and it lies in every `A_n` exactly when it lies in their intersection. Legality is
unchanged. QED.

### Theorem 4.2 -- predecessor/cofinal exchange

For every decreasing sequence of closed sets `(A_n)`,

\[
 \boxed{\Phi(\bigcap_nA_n)=\bigcap_n\Phi(A_n).}
\tag{EVM1.13}
\]

#### Proof

Forward inclusion follows from monotonicity. Conversely, let `x in intersection_n Phi(A_n)`.
Then `x in S`, and `G_(A_n)(x)` are nonempty, closed, nested subsets of compact `U`. Their total
intersection is nonempty. By (EVM1.12), a member belongs to `G_(intersection_n A_n)(x)`, so
`x in Phi(intersection_n A_n)`. QED.

### Theorem 4.3 -- greatest viable law

Define

\[
 W_0=S,\qquad W_{n+1}=\Phi(W_n),\qquad W_\infty=\bigcap_nW_n.
\tag{EVM1.14}
\]

Then every `W_n` is closed, `W_(n+1) subseteq W_n`, `Phi(W_infinity)=W_infinity`, and every
`A subseteq S` satisfying `A subseteq Phi(A)` is contained in `W_infinity`. Thus `W_infinity` is
greatest post-fixed viable law inside `S`, and hence greatest fixed point there.

#### Proof

Closedness follows by induction. Since `Phi(A) subseteq S`, `W_1 subseteq W_0`. Monotonicity gives

\[
 W_{n+2}=\Phi(W_{n+1})\subseteq\Phi(W_n)=W_{n+1}.
\]

Theorem 4.2 gives

\[
 \Phi(W_\infty)=\Phi(\bigcap_nW_n)=\bigcap_n\Phi(W_n)
 =\bigcap_nW_{n+1}=\bigcap_nW_n=W_\infty.
\]

For maximality, prove `A subseteq W_n` by induction. It holds at zero. If true at `n`, then
`A subseteq Phi(A) subseteq Phi(W_n)=W_(n+1)`. Intersect over `n`. QED.

### Corollary 4.4 -- finite plateau rigidity

If `W_(n+1)=W_n` at one finite stage, then `W_m=W_n` for every `m>=n`, and `W_n=W_infinity`.

#### Proof

Apply `Phi` repeatedly and intersect the stationary tail. QED.

## 5. Exact promotion trilemma

### Theorem 5.1

Let `(H_n)` be nonempty horizon sets in source fibre `F_y`. If every `H_n` is closed in `F_y`, the
family is decreasing, and `F_y` is compact, then `intersection_n H_n` is nonempty. Consequently,
finite observed survival without a global source lift implies at least one of:

1. some horizon admission set is not closed;
2. source fibre is not compact;
3. horizon family is not decreasing/coherent.

#### Proof

Positive statement is the finite-intersection property in compact `F_y`. Final statement is its
contrapositive. QED.

## 6. Sharp countermodels

### 6.1 Noncompact escape

Let `X=N` with discrete topology, `Y={*}`, `q` constant, and `W_n={m in N:m>=n}`. Every `W_n` is
closed, nested, and nonempty, so `* in O_fin`; their intersection is empty. Cost `P(m)=m` has finite,
hence compact, sublevels.

### 6.2 Nonclosed admission

Let `X=[0,1]`, `Y={*}`, `q` constant, and `W_n=(0,1/(n+1)]`. Source fibre is compact and sets are
nested and nonempty, but not closed; their intersection is empty.

### 6.3 Incoherent horizons

Let `X={0,1}` be discrete compact, `Y={*}`, and let `H_n={0}` for even `n`, `H_n={1}` for odd `n`.
Every horizon is nonempty and closed, but family is not decreasing and total intersection is empty.

These examples show compactness, closedness, and coherence are independently load-bearing.
