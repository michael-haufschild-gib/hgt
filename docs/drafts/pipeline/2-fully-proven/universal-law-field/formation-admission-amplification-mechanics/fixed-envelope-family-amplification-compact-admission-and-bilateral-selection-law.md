  `[BILATERAL SELECTION] [NOT RH]`
- **Statement:** A fixed decaying source envelope can hide a radial character at finitely many
  formation degrees, but not at every degree.  Source-normalized packets form multiplicatively;

## 1. Why this changes the selection problem

The current proof frontier repeatedly asks for a new local relation that kills an already visible
off-neutral class.  That request has produced the same obstruction in many languages: the relation
must be source-native, survive completion, remember orientation, commute with reflection, and not
assume the desired zero locus.

This theorem changes the mechanism.  It asks no cancelling relation.  It asks whether one fixed
source envelope remains admissible under the entire family generated by independent formation.
The selection pressure then comes from an incompatibility between two laws:

1. independent formation multiplies a radial character, hence multiplies its exponent by degree;
2. the source envelope is fixed before formation and therefore has one finite decay exponent.

Any positive radial drift eventually outruns that fixed envelope.  Reflection exposes negative
drift on the opposite leg.  Neutrality is the only bilateral all-degree compact phase.

This is the same strategic move that makes family and tensor methods stronger than one-object
positivity: amplify the offending weight while conserving the source normalization.

## 2. Setup

Let

\[
 h:\mathbb N\longrightarrow\mathbb R,
 \qquad h_n\longrightarrow+\infty,
 \tag{FA.1}
\]

be a proper source height.  Fix a source decay exponent `kappa>0`, a radial exponent `a in R`, and
a formation degree `d in N`.  Define the fixed source envelope, normalized formed packet, and
enveloped defect by

\[
 w_n=e^{-\kappa h_n},\qquad
 P_{d,n}(a)=e^{dah_n},\qquad
 D_{d,n}(a)=w_n\bigl(P_{d,n}(a)-1\bigr).
 \tag{FA.2}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
 \boxed{D_{d,n}(a)=e^{(da-\kappa)h_n}-e^{-\kappa h_n}.}
 \tag{FA.3}
\]

We call degree `d` **compactly admitted** when

\[
 D_{d,n}(a)\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{FA.4}
\]

For a diagonal receiver on `ell^2(N)`, `(FA.4)` is exactly the eigenvalue-vanishing condition that
makes the corresponding bounded diagonal multiplier compact.  The theorem itself is stated at the
coefficient level, so it does not depend on a chosen Hilbert realization.

## 3. Formation and reconstruction laws

### Theorem 3.1 — packet fusion

For all `d,e,n`,

\[
 \boxed{P_{d+e,n}(a)=P_{d,n}(a)P_{e,n}(a).}
 \tag{FA.5}
\]

Moreover,

\[
 \boxed{P_{d,n}(-a)=P_{d,n}(a)^{-1}.}
 \tag{FA.6}
\]

Thus independent formation is the additive monoid of degrees represented in the multiplicative
packet group, while functional-equation reflection is group inversion.

### Theorem 3.2 — joint reconstruction and information-neutral reflection

Whenever `d>0` and `h_n` is nonzero,

\[
 \boxed{
 a=\frac{\log P_{d,n}(a)}{d h_n}.}
 \tag{FA.7}
\]

Reflection acts by

\[
 \log P_{d,n}(-a)=-\log P_{d,n}(a).
 \tag{FA.8}
\]

Hence the reflection lift through the common logarithmic recovery map is information-neutral: it
changes orientation, not recoverable information.  No phase quotient or positive square is used.

#### Proof

Equations `(FA.5)` and `(FA.6)` are the exponential identities

\[
 e^{(d+e)ah}=e^{dah}e^{eah},\qquad e^{-dah}=(e^{dah})^{-1}.
\]

Since every packet is positive, the real logarithm is single-valued and gives `(FA.7)`--`(FA.8)`.
QED.

## 4. Exact compact-admission phase diagram

### Theorem 4.1 — one-degree classification

For every formation degree `d`, exactly one of the following occurs:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c|c}
 da<\kappa & D_{d,n}(a)\to0,\\
 da=\kappa & D_{d,n}(a)\to1,\\
 da>\kappa & D_{d,n}(a)\to+\infty.
\end{array}}
\tag{FA.9}
\]

In particular,

\[
 \boxed{
 \text{degree }d\text{ is compactly admitted}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad da<\kappa.}
 \tag{FA.10}
\]

#### Proof

Formula `(FA.3)` is the difference of two exponentials.  The second tends to zero because
`kappa>0` and `h_n` tends to positive infinity.  The first tends respectively to zero, one, or
positive infinity according to the sign of `da-kappa`.  This proves `(FA.9)` and `(FA.10)`. QED.

This strict trichotomy matters.  The boundary is not compact: it retains a unit essential packet.
The selector cannot be weakened from `<` to `<=`.

## 5. Formation-depth properness and selection

### Theorem 5.1 — finite detecting degree

If `a>0`, there is a positive integer `d` satisfying

\[
 \kappa<da
 \tag{FA.11}
\]

and degree `d` is not compactly admitted.  Any integer

\[
 d>\frac{\kappa}{a}
 \tag{FA.12}
\]

is detecting.  Thus the compact-admission degrees of a positive drift form a finite initial
window.  Formation depth is a proper detector of positive drift.

#### Proof

Archimedean order supplies an integer `d>kappa/a`; multiplication by positive `a` gives `(FA.11)`,
and Theorem 4.1 gives nonadmission. QED.

### Theorem 5.2 — one-sided all-degree selection

\[
 \boxed{
 \bigl(\forall d\ge1,\ D_{d,n}(a)\to0\bigr)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a\le0.}
 \tag{FA.13}
\]

#### Proof

If `a<=0`, then `da<kappa` for every positive `d`, so Theorem 4.1 gives compact admission.  If
`a>0`, Theorem 5.1 produces a rejecting degree. QED.

### Theorem 5.3 — bilateral global selection

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 &\bigl(\forall d\ge1,\ D_{d,n}(a)\to0\bigr)\\
 &\qquad\land
 \bigl(\forall d\ge1,\ D_{d,n}(-a)\to0\bigr)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a=0.
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{FA.14}
\]

#### Proof

The first all-degree condition says `a<=0`; the reflected condition says `-a<=0`, hence `a>=0`.
Together they give `a=0`.  At `a=0` every defect is identically zero. QED.

This is the selection theorem.  It is global in formation degree and does not assume positivity of
the original RH carrier, an annihilating boundary, or a source-normal null-homology.

## 6. Fusion of four independent organs

The theorem is not a relabeling of one route.  Four structures are load-bearing and none is
recoverable from the others alone.

| Organ | Exact law |
|---|---|
| source normalization | one envelope `w_n=e^{-kappa h_n}` fixed before every formation |
| independent formation | `P_(d+e)=P_d P_e` and exponent `a -> da` |
| topology/admission | the formed defect must vanish at the completion boundary |
| reflection/orientation | `a -> -a`, equivalently `P -> P^(-1)` |

Their conservation identity is the factorization

\[
 \boxed{D_d=w(P_d-1),\qquad P_{d+e}=P_dP_e.}
 \tag{FA.15}
\]

The joint packet `(w,D_d)` reconstructs `P_d=1+D_d/w`, and `(FA.7)` reconstructs `a`.  Compact
admission then measures whether the formed character outruns the fixed normalization.

## 7. Fourth-organ predictions

The phase diagram predicts results in organs not used to derive packet fusion.

### 7.1 Essential/Calkin prediction

In any diagonal Hilbert realization, the first integer `d` with `da>=kappa` is the first formation
degree whose packet has a nonzero essential remainder.  Equality produces a unit boundary class;
strict inequality produces an unbounded escape unless the receiver changes domain.

### 7.2 Resource prediction

For `a>0`, compact-admission sublevels in formation depth are finite.  The least detecting degree is
Archimedean of size `kappa/a`.  Near neutrality the required family depth diverges.  This gives a
quantitative reason why every fixed-degree proof can look arbitrarily close to complete while the
all-degree theorem remains decisive.

### 7.3 Recurrence prediction

Compact phase recurrence can coexist with off-neutral drift at every fixed low degree.  Formation
does not need to destroy recurrence; it exposes the radial lift by eventually breaking compact
admission.  Closed observer orbit and open lifted history are therefore compatible shadows of the
same exponent.

### 7.4 Hypertranscendence/prolongation prediction

Finite differential or Hankel order cannot be a universal selector when its order plays the role
of bounded formation degree.  A valid comparison must preserve the unbounded degree variable.  The
theorem predicts a finite first rejecting order for every positive drift, but no uniform order as
`a` approaches zero.

These are consequences of `(FA.9)`--`(FA.14)`, not additional route identifications.

## 8. Sharp countermodel: adaptive decay erases selection

Fixed source normalization is load-bearing.  If the envelope is allowed to change with formation
degree, define

\[
 \widetilde D_{d,n}(a)
 =e^{-h_n}-e^{-(1+da)h_n},\qquad a\ge0.
 \tag{FA.16}
\]

For every fixed `d`, both terms tend to zero, so

\[
 \widetilde D_{d,n}(a)\longrightarrow0
 \tag{FA.17}
\]

even when `a=1`.  Thus “compact at every degree” has selecting power only when the same source
envelope is transported through the entire formation family.  A degree-dependent renormalization
can pay for the drift and makes the criterion tautological.

This separates the theorem from formal tensoring after specialization.  The source must prove that
formation changes the character but does not silently strengthen the decay budget.

## 9. Relation to the current RH frontier

For a zero `rho`, write

\[
 a_\rho=\operatorname{Re}\rho-\tfrac12.
 \tag{FA.18}
\]

The corpus already supplies many local shadows of the character `p^(c a_rho)` and its reflected
inverse.  The theorem says that the final selection architecture need not construct a new local
annihilator.  It may instead prove two source statements:

1. **literal independent formation:** degree `d` sends the same pre-zero radial character to its
   `d`-fold product while preserving the marked recovery data;
2. **fixed-envelope bilateral admission:** one source-derived decay exponent `kappa` compactly
   admits every formed packet on both functional-equation legs.

If those statements hold for the actual coefficient-faithful pre-divisor source, `(FA.14)` forces
`a_rho=0` for every zero and therefore gives RH through the existing zero/carrier dictionary.

Neither statement is proved here for the literal RH source.  The new open core is nevertheless
strictly different from the old one:

> construct a source-native formation semigroup with a conserved normalization envelope, rather
> than construct a source-native boundary that individually kills every off-line class.

That is a global stability problem.  It is allowed to use the thousands of existing local route
theorems as gauges checking formation, recovery, admission, and reflection separately.

## 10. Failure boundaries

1. **Finite maximum degree is insufficient.** Admission only through degree `D` gives
   `a<kappa/D`, not `a=0`.
2. **One-sided admission selects only a chamber.** Reflection or another independent opposite
   orientation is necessary for equality.
3. **Formation must be literal.** Formal powers introduced after Xi specialization do not constrain
   the source.
4. **Envelope must be conserved.** Degree-dependent smoothing gives the countermodel `(FA.16)`.
5. **Proper height is required.** A bounded height supplies no asymptotic admission boundary.
6. **Scalar recovery must remain marked.** Squaring or taking absolute value can erase reflection
   orientation even though compactness remains visible.
7. **This theorem is not RH.** The two literal source statements in Section 9 remain the arithmetic
   construction gate.
