## 1. Source

Let `A` range over isomorphism classes of finite abelian groups.  Put

\[
 E(A)=\log|A|,
 \qquad
 Q(A)=\log|\operatorname{soc}A|.
\tag{AZC.1}
\]

For

\[
 \Theta=\{(\sigma,\tau)\in\mathbb R^2:
 \sigma>0,\ \sigma+\tau>1\},
\tag{AZC.2}
\]

the abelian-zeta-gas theorem gives the probability law

\[
 P_{\sigma,\tau}(A)
 =Z(\sigma,\tau)^{-1}e^{-\sigma E(A)-\tau Q(A)},
 \qquad
 Z(\sigma,\tau)=\prod_{j\ge1}\zeta(j\sigma+\tau).
\tag{AZC.3}
\]

Every group has positive mass.  In particular `Q` is not almost surely constant: the trivial group
has `Q=0`, while `Z/p` has `Q=log p`.

No zeta zero is used to define the source, observables, transformations, or probability law.

## 2. Three law formations

Define partial transformations of the parameter chamber:

\[
 H_u(\sigma,\tau)=(\sigma+u,\tau),
\tag{AZC.4}
\]

whenever source and target lie in `Theta`, and define the canonical socle tilt

\[
 S(\sigma,\tau)=(\sigma,\tau+\sigma).
\tag{AZC.5}

\[

The primitive marked source operations are `H_u` and `S`.  The third formation `V_u` will be
generated by their exchange.

## 3. Exact exchange and central birth

### Theorem 3.1 — semigroup exchange

For every real `u`, wherever both schedules are admitted,

\[
 \boxed{S\circ H_u=V_u\circ H_u\circ S.}
\tag{AZC.7}
\]

Moreover `V_u` commutes with every `H_v`, every `S`, and every `V_v`, and

\[
 V_uV_v=V_{u+v}.
\tag{AZC.8}
\]

If the inverse schedules stay in an admitted local chart, then

\[
 \boxed{[S,H_u]=S H_u S^{-1}H_{-u}=V_u.}
\tag{AZC.9}
\]

Thus the source formations realize a Heisenberg central promotion: a temperature displacement is
transduced by schedule exchange into a pure socle-fugacity intervention of the same magnitude.

### Proof

Direct substitution gives

\[
 S H_u(\sigma,\tau)
 =(\sigma+u,\tau+\sigma+u),
\]

while

\[
 H_u S(\sigma,\tau)
 =(\sigma+u,\tau+\sigma).
\]

Applying `V_u` proves (AZC.7).  Since `V_u` changes only `tau` by a constant, it commutes with
`H_v` and with the `tau`-translations in `S,V_v`; (AZC.8) is immediate.  Expanding the four inverse
maps gives (AZC.9).  QED.

\[

## 5. Withheld positive exchange cost

Let `P_0=P_(sigma,tau)` and `P_u=P_(sigma,tau+u)` for real admitted `u`.  Write

\[
 J(P_0,P_u)=D(P_0\Vert P_u)+D(P_u\Vert P_0).
\tag{AZC.14}
\]

### Theorem 5.1 — commutator work law

\[
 \boxed{
 J(P_0,P_u)
 =u\bigl(\mathbb E_0Q-\mathbb E_uQ\bigr)
 =u\int_0^u\operatorname{Var}_{\sigma,\tau+t}(Q)\,dt
 \ge0.}
\tag{AZC.15}
\]

If `u` is nonzero, the inequality is strict.  Explicitly,

\[
 \boxed{
 J(P_0,P_u)
 =u\sum_{j\ge1}
 \left[
 \left(-\frac{\zeta'}\zeta\right)(j\sigma+\tau)
 -
 \left(-\frac{\zeta'}\zeta\right)(j\sigma+\tau+u)
 \right].}
\tag{AZC.16}
\]

This positive quantity was not used to define `H,S`, or `V`.  It is the withheld response forced
by the exchange-generated intervention.

### Proof

From (AZC.13),

\[
 \log\frac{dP_u}{dP_0}
 =-uQ-\log\mathcal C(u).
\]

Taking expectations under `P_u` and `P_0` and adding cancels the normalizer, giving the first
equality in (AZC.15).  Differentiation under the absolutely convergent exponential family gives

\[
 \frac d{dt}\mathbb E_{\sigma,\tau+t}Q
 =-\operatorname{Var}_{\sigma,\tau+t}(Q).
\]

The fundamental theorem of calculus gives the integral identity, with the same sign for positive
or negative `u`.  Since `Q` is not almost surely constant and all atoms retain positive mass, its
variance is strictly positive throughout the chamber; strictness follows.  Finally the geometric
prime-power modes give

\[
 \mathbb E_{\sigma,\tau}Q
 =\sum_{j\ge1}\left(-\frac{\zeta'}\zeta\right)(j\sigma+\tau),
\]

which yields (AZC.16).  Every series converges absolutely in the stated chamber.  QED.
