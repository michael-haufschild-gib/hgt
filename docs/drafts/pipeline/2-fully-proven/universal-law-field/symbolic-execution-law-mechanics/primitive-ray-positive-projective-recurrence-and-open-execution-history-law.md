## 1. Setup

Fix a failure family `H` on an index set `I` and a primitive locally admitted law

\[
 p=(d,a),\qquad d>0,quad a:I\to\mathbb N.
 \tag{PR.1}
\]

Its `n`-fold execution ray is

\[
 p_n=(nd,na),
 \qquad
 (na)(i)=n a(i),
 \qquad n\ge1.
 \tag{PR.2}
\]

For a positive integer `q`, define two observers:

1. the positive projective profile
   \[
   \Pi(p_n)(i)=\frac{n a(i)}{nd}\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0};
   \tag{PR.3}
   \]
2. the cyclic grade phase
   \[
   \varphi_q(p_n)=[nd]_q\in\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z.
   \tag{PR.4}
   \]

The paired observer is

\[
 \mathcal O_q(p_n)=(\Pi(p_n),\varphi_q(p_n)).
 \tag{PR.5}
\]

This observer is declared data.  The theorem does not claim every positive or physical observer has
this form.

## 2. Primitive-ray execution theorem

### Theorem 2.1

For every `n>=1`:

1. `p_n` is locally admitted;
2. `p_n` is generated by `n` copies of the primitive atom `p`;
3. if `d<=D`, then the same derivation lies in the `D`-bounded primitive grammar.

### Proof

For every failure kernel `K`, local admission of `p` gives

\[
 nd\le n\sum_{i\in K}a(i)=\sum_{i\in K}n a(i),
\]

proving local admission of `p_n`.  The execution statements follow by induction on `n`: the first
law is the primitive atom, and composing one more copy adds `d` to the grade and `a` to the weight.
The same induction works in the bounded grammar when the atom degree satisfies `d<=D`. ∎

## 3. Positive projective quotient

### Theorem 3.1

For every `n>=1`,

\[
 \boxed{\Pi(p_n)(i)=\frac{a(i)}d\quad(i\in I).}
 \tag{PR.6}
\]

In particular the positive projective observer is constant on the entire execution ray.

### Proof

Both `n` and `d` are nonzero, so cancellation in `Q` gives

\[
 \frac{na(i)}{nd}=\frac{a(i)}d.
\]

All coordinates are nonnegative because `a(i)>=0` and `d>0`. ∎

Positivity itself did not erase scale.  The erasure is caused by projectivization.

## 4. Cyclic recurrence and open lifted history

### Theorem 4.1

For every `n>=1`,

\[
 \boxed{\varphi_q(p_{n+q})=\varphi_q(p_n)}
 \tag{PR.7}
\]

and therefore

\[
 \boxed{\mathcal O_q(p_{n+q})=\mathcal O_q(p_n).}
 \tag{PR.8}

The exact grades do not close:

\[
 (n+q)d>nd.
 \tag{PR.9}

Moreover, for every `N` there is `n>=1` with

\[
 N<nd.
 \tag{PR.10}

### Proof

Modulo `q`,

\[
 (n+q)d=nd+qd\equiv nd.
\]

Equation `(PR.6)` supplies equality of the projective coordinates, proving `(PR.8)`.  Since
`q,d>0`, equation `(PR.9)` is strict.  For `(PR.10)`, take `n=N+1`; then `d>=1` gives
`(N+1)d>N`. ∎

Thus the normalized/cyclic observer history is exactly recurrent while the source execution lift
is open and unbounded.

## 6. Hostile scope

1. The theorem is discrete and algebraic; it proves no continuous-time recurrence.
2. The positive projective observer is chosen, not forced by local admission alone.
3. The cyclic quotient is chosen.  The full grade observer is nonrecurrent.
4. `q>0`, `d>0`, and `n>0` are load-bearing.  At `n=0` the projective ratio is undefined; at
   `q=0` there is no finite cyclic quotient.
5. A general generated history can branch across multiple primitive rays; the theorem covers one
   ray and does not classify interference between rays.
6. Unbounded grade is not unbounded primitive degree.  A single bounded-degree primitive already
   yields the open ray.
7. The theorem supplies no hypertranscendence, compactness, physical cost, time metric, source
   realization, or RH implication.
