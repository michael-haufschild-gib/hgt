## 1. Purpose

The source-complete defect-corona theorem assumes two independent inputs:

1. compact reporters whose common commutant is scalar;
2. a proper positive cost excluding escape to the corona.

That separation is mathematically sufficient but constitutively weak. A chosen reporter packet and
a chosen cost can encode the desired conclusion. This card proves that one source pair can generate
both structures.

The mechanism is:

\[
 (L,v)
 \longmapsto
 S_t=e^{-tL}
 \longmapsto
 G=\int_0^\infty |S_tv\rangle\langle S_tv|\,dt
 \longmapsto
 C=G^{-1}.
 \tag{OG.1}
\]

The orbit Gramian `G` forms all decayed source responses. Its inverse `C` is not an auxiliary
penalty: it is the minimum energy needed to synthesize a history from that same orbit. The weak
Lyapunov equation

\[
 LG+GL=|v\rangle\langle v|
 \tag{OG.2}
\]

is the exact conservation law joining source, formation, and cost.

## 3. Main theorem

### Theorem 3.1 — source-generated observation and properness

Under `(OG.3)`--`(OG.7)` the following hold.

#### I. Trace-class formation and exact kernel

The integral `(OG.7)` converges in trace norm, `G` is positive trace class, and

\[
 \operatorname{tr}G
 =\int_0^\infty\|S_tv\|^2dt
 =\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{|v_j|^2}{2\lambda_j}
 \le {1\over2\lambda_1}.
 \tag{OG.8}
\]

In the source eigenbasis,

\[
 \boxed{\langle e_i,Ge_j\rangle
 =\frac{\langle e_i,Pe_j\rangle}{\lambda_i+\lambda_j}.}
 \tag{OG.9}
\]

Thus `G` is the unique bounded operator satisfying the weak Lyapunov law

\[
 \langle Le_i,Ge_j\rangle+\langle e_i,GL e_j\rangle
 =\langle e_i,Pe_j\rangle
 \qquad(i,j\ge1).
 \tag{OG.10}
\]

#### II. Complete heat observation

`G` is injective. Equivalently,

\[
 \boxed{
 \int_0^\infty|\langle x,S_tv\rangle|^2dt=0
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=0.}
 \tag{OG.11}
\]

Hence the heat orbit of the one marked source vector is total even though its Gramian is compact.

#### III. Endogenous proper cost

Let `(gamma_k,f_k)` be a spectral resolution of `G`, ordered so that

\[
 \gamma_1\ge\gamma_2\ge\cdots>0,
 \qquad \gamma_k\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{OG.12}
\]

Then `C=G^(-1)` is a positive self-adjoint operator with compact resolvent. Its closed quadratic
form is

\[
 q_G(x)=\|G^{-1/2}x\|^2
       =\sum_{k\ge1}{|\langle x,f_k\rangle|^2\over\gamma_k},
 \tag{OG.13}
\]

with value `+infinity` outside `Dom(G^(-1/2))`. Every sublevel

\[
 \{x\in H:q_G(x)\le M\}
 \tag{OG.14}
\]

is compact in `H`.

Define the reachability operator

\[
 W:L^2(0,\infty)\longrightarrow H,
 \qquad Wu=\int_0^\infty u(t)S_tv\,dt.
 \tag{OG.15}
\]

Then `WW*=G`, `Ran W=Ran G^(1/2)`, and for every reachable `x`,

\[
 \boxed{
 q_G(x)=\min\{\|u\|_{L^2}^2:Wu=x\}.}
 \tag{OG.16}
\]

Thus the proper cost is generated by the same orbit that generates observation.

#### IV. Source completeness

#### V. Endogenous-cost selection

For a positive trace-class operator `T`, define its extended orbit cost

\[
 \mathcal E_G(T)
 =\sum_{k\ge1}\gamma_k^{-1}\langle f_k,Tf_k\rangle

Let `(T_n)` be any sequence of positive trace-class operators such that

\[
 \|[T_n,D]\|_1+\|[T_n,P]\|_1\longrightarrow0

\[
 \sup_n\mathcal E_G(T_n)<\infty.
 \tag{OG.20}

Then

\[
 \boxed{\|T_n\|_1=\operatorname{tr}T_n\longrightarrow0.}
 \tag{OG.21}
\]

This is a global formation/properness theorem: approximate source invariance plus bounded cost
generated by that source leaves no nonzero normal defect.

#### VI. Naturality and scale covariance

For a unitary `U`, replacing `(L,v)` by `(ULU*,Uv)` replaces `(D,P,G,C)` by simultaneous unitary

\[
 G_{aL}=a^{-1}G_L,

 \qquad q_{G_{aL}}=a q_{G_L}.
 \tag{OG.22}

Consequently bounded-cost selection is independent of source coordinates and of the unit chosen for

#### VII. Formation reconstruction and least capital

Let `U` be a separable Hilbert space and let `X:U->H` be Hilbert--Schmidt. Then

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal E_G(XX^*)

 B:U\to L^2(0,\infty)\text{ Hilbert--Schmidt},\ WB=X\right\},}
 \tag{OG.22a}

where the minimum is `+infinity` when there is no such factorization. In particular, the following
are equivalent:

1. `E_G(XX*)<infinity`;
2. `G^(-1/2)X` is Hilbert--Schmidt;
3. `X=WB` for some Hilbert--Schmidt `B`.

If `Y=G^(-1/2)X`, the unique least-capital factor is

 B_{\min}=V^*Y,

where `W=G^(1/2)V` is the coisometric polar factorization from the proof. Every factor has the

\[
 B=B_{\min}+C,
 \qquad VC=0,

 \|B\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
 =\|B_{\min}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2+\|C\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2.
 \tag{OG.22c}
\]

Consequently, for a supplied Hilbert--Schmidt `B:U->L^2(0,infinity)`, form

\[
 X=WB:U\longrightarrow H,
 \qquad T=XX^*.
 \tag{OG.22d}

Then `T` is positive trace class and

\[
 \boxed{

Consequently, if `T_n=X_nX_n^*` with `X_n=WB_n`, `sup_n||B_n||_HS<infinity`, and `(OG.19)`

Thus finite endogenous cost is exactly finite source-formation capital, and the common value is the

Thus every positive-width average is source-formable, but the exact injected atom is not. Atomic

## 4. Proof

### 4.1 Trace-class formation

The trace norm of a rank-one positive operator is its trace. Since `lambda_j>=lambda_1`,

\[
 \big\||S_tv\rangle\langle S_tv|\big\|_1
 =\|S_tv\|^2
 =\sum_j|v_j|^2e^{-2\lambda_jt}
 \le e^{-2\lambda_1t}.
 \tag{OG.23}
\]

The right side is integrable. Strong continuity of `S_t` and the standard rank-one estimate

\[
 \||a\rangle\langle a|-|b\rangle\langle b|\|_1
 \le(\|a\|+\|b\|)\|a-b\|
 \tag{OG.24}
\]

give trace-norm continuity of the integrand. Hence `(OG.7)` is a trace-class Bochner integral.
Tonelli and continuity of the trace yield `(OG.8)`. Taking the `(i,j)` matrix coefficient and
integrating `exp(-(lambda_i+lambda_j)t)` gives `(OG.9)`, hence `(OG.10)`. Conversely `(OG.10)`
forces every matrix coefficient to equal `(OG.9)`, proving uniqueness.

### 4.2 Injectivity

For `x in H`, positivity and Tonelli give

\[
 \langle x,Gx\rangle
 =\int_0^\infty|\langle x,S_tv\rangle|^2dt.
 \tag{OG.25}
\]

If the left side is zero, continuity makes

\[
 F(t)=\langle x,S_tv\rangle
     =\sum_{j\ge1}a_je^{-\lambda_jt}
 \tag{OG.26}
\]

zero for every `t>=0`, where `sum_j |a_j|<infinity` by Cauchy--Schwarz. Multiply `(OG.26)` by
`e^(lambda_1 t)` and let `t` tend to infinity. Dominated convergence gives `a_1=0`. Inductively,
after `a_1,...,a_(r-1)` vanish, multiplication by `e^(lambda_r t)` gives `a_r=0`. Thus all
`a_j=0`. Since every `v_j` is nonzero, every coordinate of `x` is zero. This proves `(OG.11)`.

### 4.3 Properness and minimum energy

The spectral theorem for the positive compact injective operator `G` gives `(OG.12)`. The inverse
defined by `Cf_k=gamma_k^(-1)f_k` is positive self-adjoint, and

\[
 (C+1)^{-1}f_k={\gamma_k\over1+\gamma_k}f_k.
 \tag{OG.27}
\]

These eigenvalues tend to zero, so the resolvent is compact.

If `q_G(x)<=M`, the spectral tail satisfies

\[
 \sum_{k>N}|\langle x,f_k\rangle|^2
 \le \gamma_{N+1}M.
 \tag{OG.28}
\]

The head lies in a bounded subset of a finite-dimensional space and the tail is uniformly small;
therefore `(OG.14)` is compact.

The operator `W` is Hilbert--Schmidt because the squared norm of its kernel is the integral in
`(OG.8)`. Its adjoint is

\[
 (W^*x)(t)=\langle x,S_tv\rangle,
 \tag{OG.29}
\]

so `(OG.25)` gives `WW*=G`. Polar decomposition gives `Ran W=Ran G^(1/2)`. Since `G` is injective,
the partial isometry in that decomposition is a coisometry onto `H`. For
`x=G^(1/2)y`, the least-norm preimage is the adjoint-partial-isometry image of `y`; every other
preimage differs from it by an element of `ker W`. Orthogonality gives minimum norm `||y||`, which
is exactly `(OG.13)`. This proves `(OG.16)`.

### 4.4 Scalar common commutant

Let `A` commute with `D`. The eigenvalues of `D` are simple, so `Ae_j=a_je_j`. Commutation with
`P` gives, in every `(i,j)` matrix coefficient,

\[
 (a_i-a_j)v_i\overline{v_j}=0.
 \tag{OG.30}
\]

Full support forces `a_i=a_j` for all `i,j`, hence `A` is scalar. This proves `(OG.17)`.

### 4.5 Selection

Write `M=sup_n E_G(T_n)`. Since `gamma_k<=||G||`, positivity gives

\[
 \operatorname{tr}T_n
 \le\|G\|\mathcal E_G(T_n)
 \le\|G\|M.
 \tag{OG.31}
\]

Thus `(T_n)` is bounded in trace class. Since `H` is separable,
`S_1(H)=K(H)^*` has weak-star sequentially compact bounded sets. Let `T` be a weak-star cluster
point. For every compact `K`,

\[
 \operatorname{tr}([T,D]K)
 =\operatorname{tr}(T(DK-KD))=0,
 \tag{OG.32}
\]

because `DK-KD` is compact and `(OG.19)` passes to the limit. Hence `[T,D]=0`; similarly
`[T,P]=0`. By `(OG.17)`, `T=c1`. But a scalar operator on an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space is
trace class only when `c=0`. Therefore every weak-star cluster point is zero, and

\[
 \operatorname{tr}(KT_n)\longrightarrow0
 \qquad(K\in\mathcal K(H)).
 \tag{OG.33}
\]

Let `Q_N` project onto `span{f_1,...,f_N}`. It is finite rank, so the head trace tends to zero by
`(OG.33)`. Positivity and `(OG.18)` give

\[
 \operatorname{tr}((1-Q_N)T_n)
 \le \gamma_{N+1}\mathcal E_G(T_n)
 \le M\gamma_{N+1}.
 \tag{OG.34}
\]

First let `n` tend to infinity and then `N` tend to infinity. Since `gamma_(N+1)->0`, `(OG.21)`
follows.

### 4.6 Naturality

Functional calculus gives `e^(-tULU*)=Ue^(-tL)U*`; substitution in `(OG.7)` proves unitary
covariance. For `a>0`, substitute `s=at` in the integral:

\[
 G_{aL}=\int_0^\infty S_{at}PS_{at}dt
       =a^{-1}\int_0^\infty S_sPS_sds.
 \tag{OG.35}
\]

Taking inverses and forms proves `(OG.22)`.

### 4.7 Formation reconstruction

For every Hilbert--Schmidt `X:U->H`, positivity and the spectral definition `(OG.18)` give

\[
 \mathcal E_G(XX^*)
 =\sum_k\gamma_k^{-1}\|X^*f_k\|^2
 =\|G^{-1/2}X\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2,
 \tag{OG.35a}
\]

with extended value `+infinity` when the composition is not Hilbert--Schmidt. The polar
decomposition used in Section 4.3 has the form

\[
 W=G^{1/2}V,
 \tag{OG.35b}
\]

where `V:L^2(0,infinity)->H` is a contraction (indeed a coisometry onto `H`). Therefore

\[
 G^{-1/2}WB=VB,
 \qquad
 \|VB\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\le\|B\|_{\mathrm{HS}}.
 \tag{OG.35c}
\]

This first proves `(OG.22e)`. Conversely, suppose `(OG.35a)` is finite and put
`Y=G^(-1/2)X`. Since `V` is a coisometry, `VV*=1_H`, so

\[
 B_{\min}=V^*Y,
 \qquad
 WB_{\min}=G^{1/2}VV^*Y=G^{1/2}Y=X,
 \qquad
 \|B_{\min}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}=\|Y\|_{\mathrm{HS}}.
 \tag{OG.35d}
\]

If `WB=X`, applying `G^(-1/2)` gives `VB=Y`. Hence

\[
 B=V^*Y+(1-V^*V)B.
 \tag{OG.35e}
\]

The two terms are Hilbert--Schmidt orthogonal, because `V*V` is the orthogonal projection onto
`(ker V)^perp`, while `(1-V*V)B` has range in `ker V=ker W`. Therefore

\[
 \|B\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
 =\|Y\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2+\|(1-V^*V)B\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2.
 \tag{OG.35f}
\]

This proves `(OG.22a)`--`(OG.22c)` and uniqueness of the least-capital factor. If the energy in
`(OG.35a)` is infinite, no Hilbert--Schmidt factorization can exist by `(OG.35c)`. The selection
corollary is Theorem 3.1.V applied with `M=sup_n||B_n||_HS^2`.

### 4.8 Atomic-refinement wall

Strong continuity gives `x_T->v`. Since

\[
 x_T=W\left(T^{-1}\mathbf1_{[0,T]}\right),
 \qquad
 \left\|T^{-1}\mathbf1_{[0,T]}\right\|_{L^2}^2={1\over T},
 \tag{OG.35g}
\]

the minimum-energy identity `(OG.16)` gives `q_G(x_T)<=1/T`.

Suppose `v=Wh` for some `h in L^2(0,infinity)`. Since every `v_j` is nonzero, the `j`th coordinate
would give

\[
 1=\int_0^\infty h(t)e^{-\lambda_jt}dt.
 \tag{OG.35h}
\]

But Cauchy--Schwarz gives

\[
 \left|\int_0^\infty h(t)e^{-\lambda_jt}dt\right|
 \le {\|h\|_{L^2}\over\sqrt{2\lambda_j}}
 \longrightarrow0,
 \tag{OG.35i}
\]

a contradiction. Hence `v` is not in `Ran W=Dom(G^(-1/2))`, so `q_G(v)=+infinity`.

If `q_G(x_T)` did not tend to infinity, some sequence `T_n downarrow 0` would remain in one compact
sublevel `(OG.14)`. A subsequence would converge inside that sublevel, but `x_(T_n)->v`; closedness
of the sublevel would force `q_G(v)<infinity`. This proves `(OG.22g)`.

### 4.9 Positive aggregation

For the finite partial sums `T_N`, the spectral definition gives

\[
 \mathcal E_G(T_N)
 =\sum_k\gamma_k^{-1}\sum_{r\le N}w_r\|X_r^*f_k\|^2
 =\sum_{r\le N}w_r\mathcal E_G(X_rX_r^*).
 \tag{OG.35j}
\]

All summands are nonnegative. Monotone convergence in `N` and then Tonelli in `(r,k)` prove
`(OG.22i)`. Trace-norm convergence ensures that `(OG.22h)` defines the advertised positive
trace-class operator; the energy identity itself is extended-valued.

## 5. Sharp countermodels and load-bearing hypotheses

### 5.1 Properness cannot be omitted

Let `R_n=|e_n><e_n|`. Then

\[
 \operatorname{tr}R_n=1,
 \qquad [R_n,D]=0,
 \qquad \|[R_n,P]\|_1\le2|v_n|\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{OG.36}
\]

Thus complete compact observation alone only pushes normalized mass to infinity. The theorem forces

\[
 \boxed{\mathcal E_G(R_n)\longrightarrow\infty.}
 \tag{OG.37}
\]

Indeed, a bounded subsequence would contradict `(OG.21)`. This is the exact positive survivor of
the compact-observer escape obstruction.

### 5.2 Full support is load-bearing

If `v_j=0`, then `e_j` lies in `ker G` and the coordinate projection onto `e_j` commutes with both
`D` and `P`. Neither complete observation nor the inverse cost exists on that direction.

### 5.3 Simple spectrum is load-bearing

If one eigenspace of `L` has dimension at least two, a direction in that eigenspace orthogonal to
the component of `v` is invisible to the heat orbit. A single rank-one seed no longer has scalar
commutant.

### 5.4 Positive spectral gap is load-bearing

If `lambda_1=0` and `v_1` is nonzero, the integrand has a nondecaying component and the infinite-time
Gramian is not trace class. One must remove the stationary mode, add a discount, or work with a
relative Gramian.

### 5.5 Infinite dimension is load-bearing for extinction

In finite dimension the same hypotheses make `G` invertible with bounded inverse, but a scalar
positive density survives. The normal-to-corona extinction mechanism is specifically infinite
dimensional.

## 6. Literal Euler-shell specialization

Fix an exponent shell `A_m={n>=2:a_m(n)=1}` and `sigma>1`. Let

\[
 H_{m,\sigma}=\ell^2(A_m),
 \quad Le_n=(\log n)e_n,

 \tag{OG.38}
\]

The spectrum is positive, simple, and compact-resolvent; the vector has full support. Therefore the
theorem applies. The complex orbit satisfies

\[

 \tag{OG.39}

throughout its absolute-convergence half-plane. The orbit Gramian has the explicit source kernel

\[
 \boxed{
 G_{m,\sigma}(n,k)
 ={(nk)^{-\sigma/2}\over

 \tag{OG.40}
\]

The imaginary orbit is norm recurrent. Indeed, for every `epsilon>0`, choose a finite set
`F subset A_m` whose squared-amplitude tail is below `epsilon^2/16`. Dirichlet simultaneous
approximation supplies arbitrarily large `t` for which

\[
 |e^{-it\log n}-1|<\frac{\epsilon}{2}
 \qquad(n\in F).
 \tag{OG.41}
\]

The finite head then contributes at most `epsilon^2/4` to
`||e^(-itL)v-v||^2`, while the tail contributes at most four times its mass. Hence there is a
sequence `t_r->infinity` with

\[
 \boxed{e^{-it_rL}v_{m,\sigma}\longrightarrow v_{m,\sigma}.}
 \tag{OG.42}
\]

The Euler-shell reachability map is injective. To prove this, suppose `Wh=0` and form the Laplace
transform

\[
 \mathcal Lh(z)=\int_0^\infty h(t)e^{-zt}dt,
 \qquad \Re z>0.
 \tag{OG.43}
\]

Paley--Wiener theory puts `Lh` in the Hardy space `H^2(C_+)`. Since every shell coordinate of `v`
is nonzero, `Wh=0` implies

\[
 \mathcal Lh(\log n)=0
 \qquad(n\in A_m).
 \tag{OG.44}
\]

For every odd prime `p`, the integer `2^m p` belongs to `A_m`. A nonzero Hardy function on the
right half-plane has a Blaschke zero set, hence its positive real zeros `(z_r)` must satisfy

\[
 \sum_r{z_r\over1+z_r^2}<\infty.
 \tag{OG.45}
\]

But `z_p=log(2^m p)` violates `(OG.45)`: for all large `p`,

\[
 {z_p\over1+z_p^2}\ge {1\over2z_p}\ge {c_m\over p},
 \tag{OG.46}
\]

and Euler's theorem gives `sum_p 1/p=infinity`. Thus `Lh=0`; injectivity of the Laplace transform
gives `h=0`. Consequently the control in `(OG.35g)` is unique, and the shell atomic-refinement law
is exact:

\[
 \boxed{
 q_{G_{m,\sigma}}(x_T)={1\over T}
 \qquad(T>0).}
 \tag{OG.47}
\]

One literal source now has five exact faces.

| organ | face of the same source law |

| real generation | `e^(-tL)v` is the unnormalized Gibbs shift `sigma -> sigma+2t` |
