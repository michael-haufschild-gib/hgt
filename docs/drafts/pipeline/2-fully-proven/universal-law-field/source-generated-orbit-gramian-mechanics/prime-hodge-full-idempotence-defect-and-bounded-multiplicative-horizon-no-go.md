## 3. Abstract fullness theorem

Let `H` be an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space and let

\[
 0\le T\in\mathcal K(H),\qquad \ker T=0.
 \tag{PHFI.3}
\]

Define `Delta_T=T-T^2`.

### Theorem 3.1 -- faithful compact formation has a full idempotence defect

If `T` is nonzero and faithful, then

\[
 \Delta_T\ne0,
 \qquad
 \overline{\mathcal K(H)\Delta_T\mathcal K(H)}=\mathcal K(H).
 \tag{PHFI.4}
\]

### Proof

Suppose `Delta_T=0`.  Then `T^2=T`.  For every `x`,

\[
 T(x-Tx)=Tx-T^2x=0.
\]

Faithfulness gives `x=Tx`; hence `T=I`.  But the identity on an infinite-dimensional Hilbert
space is not compact, contradicting `T in K(H)`.  Thus `Delta_T` is nonzero.

The compact-operator C*-algebra `K(H)` is simple: its only closed two-sided ideals are zero and
`K(H)`.  The closed ideal generated by the nonzero element `Delta_T` is therefore all of `K(H)`.
QED.

Positivity is not used in the proof once faithfulness and compactness are known; it records the
literal source provenance of the application.

### Theorem 3.2 -- bounded multiplicative horizon exclusion

Let `B` be a Banach algebra and let

\[
 \pi:\mathcal K(H)\longrightarrow B
 \tag{PHFI.5}
\]

be any bounded algebra homomorphism, not assumed unital, positive, star-preserving, or
trace-preserving.  If `pi(T)` is idempotent, then

\[
 \boxed{\pi(T)=0.}
 \tag{PHFI.6}
\]

In particular no bounded multiplicative completion sends `T` to a nonzero projection.

### Proof

If `pi(T)^2=pi(T)`, multiplicativity gives

\[
 \pi(\Delta_T)=\pi(T)-\pi(T)^2=0.
 \tag{PHFI.7}
\]

The kernel of a bounded algebra homomorphism is a closed two-sided ideal.  By Theorem 3.1 it
contains the closed ideal generated by `Delta_T`, namely all of `K(H)`.  Hence `pi` vanishes on
`K(H)`, and in particular `pi(T)=0`.  QED.
