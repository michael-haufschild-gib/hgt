## 3. Main theorem

### Theorem 3.1 -- endogenous conservative completion

For every `N>=1`:

1. `->_N` is well-founded.
2. `R_N` is not confluent.
3. `ell_(N+1)` and `r_(N+1)` are distinct `R_N`-normal forms reached from the same word `w_N`.
4. The rule `rho_(N+1)` is absent at stage `N` and present at stage `N+1`.
5. Adding `rho_(N+1)` changes no generated semantics:

   \[
     \equiv_{N+1}=\equiv_N.
     \tag{EC.5}
   \]

6. The enlarged system `R_(N+1)` is again nonconfluent, with the next marked failure generated by
   overlap of the persistent seed `rho_1` and the newly admitted rule `rho_(N+1)`.

Consequently every execution terminates, no finite completion stage is confluent, the oriented

## 4. Proof

### 4.1 Exact marked peak

The newest rule acts on the suffix of `w_N`:

\[
  w_N=ab\ell_N\longrightarrow_N ab r_N=ab^{N+1}a=\ell_{N+1}.
  \tag{EC.6}
\]

The seed acts first on the prefix, after which the newest rule acts:

\[
  w_N
  =\ell_1b^Na
  \longrightarrow_N ba b^Na
  =b\ell_N
  \longrightarrow_N br_N
  =b^{N+1}a=r_{N+1}.
  \tag{EC.7}
\]

Thus both endpoints lie in the `R_N`-reduction cone of `w_N`.

### 4.2 Normality and inequality

Every left side has exactly two occurrences of `a`.  The word `r_(N+1)` has one, so it contains no
left side.

The word `ell_(N+1)` has exactly two occurrences of `a`, both at its endpoints.  Any occurrence of
some `ell_n` inside it must therefore use both endpoints.  Hence `n=N+1`, which is not admitted at
stage `N`.  Therefore `ell_(N+1)` is also normal.  The endpoints are distinct because their numbers
of `a` letters are respectively two and one.  This proves nonconfluence.

### 4.3 Execution terminates

Each contextual rewrite replaces `ell_n`, containing two `a` letters, by `r_n`, containing one.
The total number of `a` letters decreases by exactly one.  Pulling back the well-founded strict
order on natural numbers along this count proves well-foundedness of `->_N`.

### 4.4 Repair is semantically conservative

Equations `(EC.6)--(EC.7)` give

\[
  \ell_{N+1}\equiv_N w_N\equiv_N r_{N+1}.
  \tag{EC.8}
\]

Because `R_N` is closed under contexts, every contextual instance

\[
  p\ell_{N+1}s\longrightarrow p r_{N+1}s
\]

of the new rule already belongs to `equiv_N`.  Every old rule belongs to `R_(N+1)`, so
`equiv_N` is contained in `equiv_(N+1)`; the preceding argument gives the reverse containment.
This proves `(EC.5)`.

### 4.5 Failure produces its successor

The direct rule `rho_(N+1)` is absent from `R_N` by definition and is admitted in `R_(N+1)`.  Replace
`N` by `N+1` in `(EC.6)--(EC.7)`.  The new rule now occupies the role formerly played by `rho_N`,
and its overlap with the unchanged seed `rho_1` produces the distinct normal pair
`ell_(N+2),r_(N+2)`.  The production law is recursive and uses the changed executable rulebook; it
is not a precomputed sequence of observations of a fixed operator.  This completes the proof.
