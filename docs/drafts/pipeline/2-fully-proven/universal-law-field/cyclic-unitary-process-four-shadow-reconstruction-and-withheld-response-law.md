## 1. Source process

Let (U\) be a unitary operator on \(\mathbb C^r\) with distinct eigenvalues

\[
\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r\in S^1.
\]

Let (v\) be a unit vector having nonzero component in every eigenspace. In an eigenbasis, write

\[
v=\sum_{j=1}^r\sqrt{w_j}\,e_j,
\qquad
w_j>0,
\qquad
\sum_jw_j=1.
\tag{1}
\]

The intervention/response moments are

\[
m_n=\langle v,U^n v\rangle
=\sum_{j=1}^r w_j\lambda_j^n
\qquad(n\ge0).
\tag{2}
\]

No observer output is used to choose (U) or (v); the marked cyclic process is the source.

## 2. Analytic and positive shadows

### Theorem 2.1

The transfer function

\[
F(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}m_nz^n
\]

is rational on the unit disc:

\[
\boxed{
F(z)=\sum_{j=1}^r\frac{w_j}{1-\lambda_jz}.}
\tag{3}
\]

Put (m_{-n}=\overline{m_n}). The Toeplitz Gram matrices

\[
G_N=(m_{j-i})_{0\le i,j\le N}
\tag{4}
\]

are positive semidefinite and

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}G_N=\min(N+1,r).}
\tag{5}
\]

### Proof

Equation (3) follows by summing the geometric series in (2). Also

\[
(G_N)_{ij}=\langle U^iv,U^jv\rangle,
\]

so (G_N\) is the Gram matrix of (v,Uv,\ldots,U^Nv). In the eigenbasis, their coordinate matrix is
the diagonal matrix \(\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{w_j})\) times a Vandermonde matrix in the distinct
\(\lambda_j\). Its rank is \(\min(N+1,r)\), proving (5). QED.

Thus analytic pole data and positive capacity/rank are forced shadows of one process.

## 3. Exact finite reconstruction

Let

\[
p(x)=\prod_{j=1}^r(x-\lambda_j)
=x^r+a_{r-1}x^{r-1}+\cdots+a_0.
\tag{6}
\]

### Theorem 3.1

The moments obey the order-(r) recurrence

\[
\boxed{
m_{n+r}+a_{r-1}m_{n+r-1}+\cdots+a_0m_n=0
\qquad(n\ge0).}
\tag{7}
\]

The finite record

\[
m_0,m_1,\ldots,m_{2r-1}
\tag{8}
\]

determines:

1. the recurrence coefficients (a_0,\ldots,a_{r-1});
2. the unordered eigenvalue set \(\{\lambda_j\}\);
3. the weights \(w_j\);
4. the process ((U,v)) up to unitary equivalence;
5. every later moment (m_n\), (n\ge2r\).

### Proof

For every (n\ge0),

\[
\sum_{k=0}^r a_km_{n+k}
=\sum_jw_j\lambda_j^np(\lambda_j)=0,
\]

where (a_r=1), proving (7). Define the Hankel matrix

\[
H=(m_{i+j})_{0\le i,j<r}.
\]

If (V_{ij}=\lambda_j^i), then

\[
H=V\operatorname{diag}(w_j)V^T,
\]

so

\[
\det H=(\det V)^2\prod_jw_j\ne0.
\tag{9}
\]

The first (r) recurrence equations form the invertible system

\[
H
\begin{pmatrix}a_0\\ \vdots\\ a_{r-1}\end{pmatrix}
=-
\begin{pmatrix}m_r\\ \vdots\\ m_{2r-1}\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{10}
\]

Hence (8) determines (p), whose roots give the \(\lambda_j\). The first (r) moment equations are
an invertible Vandermonde system for the weights. Spectral decomposition then determines the cyclic
unitary pair up to unitary equivalence. Finally (7) recursively determines all later moments. QED.
