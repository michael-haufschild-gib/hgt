## 1. The grade-four gate

ULF-SRSM-5 reduced higher formation to one precise sequence of symmetric polynomials:

\[
 R_r(z;\lambda)
 =\frac{\Delta_r(\lambda)P_r(z;\lambda)}
 {D_r(z;\lambda)},
 \qquad
 D_r=\prod_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq[r]}(z+\lambda_S).
 \tag{GFH.1}
\]

At grades two and three, the exact alternant becomes a positive subset-grade formation after
inverting `P_r` causally.  Grade four is the first place where `P_r` can have genuinely higher
spectral geometry.  A right-half-plane zero would kill every stable causal exactifier of this
form.

The result is the opposite: the grade-four numerator is Hurwitz stable throughout the entire
positive source chamber.  The proof comes from a deletion law generated by the schedule chambers,
not from fitting a stable polynomial after seeing the target.

## 2. Universal deletion--interpolation recursion

Retain the notation of `(GFH.1)`.  Put

\[
 A=\sum_{j=1}^r\lambda_j,
 \qquad
 V_i=\prod_{j\ne i}(\lambda_i-\lambda_j),
 \tag{GFH.2}
\]

and define the proper-subset incidence factor

\[
 E_{r,i}(z;\lambda)
 =\prod_{\substack{S\subsetneq[r]\\i\in S}}(z+\lambda_S).
 \tag{GFH.3}
\]

The empty subset does not occur because it cannot contain `i`.

### Theorem 2.1 — formation-numerator deletion law

For pairwise distinct grades and every `r≥2`,

\[
 \boxed{
 P_r(z;\lambda)
 =\sum_{i=1}^r
 \frac{P_{r-1}(z;\widehat\lambda_i)E_{r,i}(z;\lambda)}{V_i}.}
 \tag{GFH.4}
\]

The right side extends polynomially across all collision hyperplanes.  Thus `(GFH.4)` is an exact
recursion for the symmetric polynomial `P_r`, not merely a rational identity on one chamber.

### Proof

Partition the permutations in the defining alternant `R_r` by their first letter.  If the first
letter is `i`, the first suffix sum is `A`, and the remaining suffix chain is the grade-`r-1`
chain on the deleted alphabet.  The permutation sign changes by `(-1)^(i-1)`.  Hence

\[
 R_r(z;\lambda)
 =\frac1{z+A}\sum_{i=1}^r(-1)^{i-1}
 R_{r-1}(z;\widehat\lambda_i).
 \tag{GFH.5}
\]

Moreover,

\[
 \Delta_r=(-1)^{i-1}V_i\Delta_{r-1}(\widehat\lambda_i)
 \tag{GFH.6}
\]

and direct cancellation of subset factors gives

\[
 \frac{D_r}{(z+A)D_{r-1}(\widehat\lambda_i)}=E_{r,i}.
 \tag{GFH.7}
\]

Insert the factorization `(GFH.1)` for every deleted alternant into `(GFH.5)`, then use
`(GFH.6)`--`(GFH.7)`.  This proves `(GFH.4)` away from the collision hyperplanes.  Both sides equal
the polynomial quotient \(D_rR_r/\Delta_r\) there, so polynomial continuation proves the identity
everywhere.  QED.

This recursion is the first formation law coupling three distinct geometries:

1. deletion of one schedule letter;
2. type-A barycentric interpolation through `V_i`;
3. multiplication by every proper subset phase containing that letter.

It replaces the factorial schedule sum by an `r`-term constitutive recursion.

### Theorem 2.2 — complement-reflection law

For every grade `r≥1`, put `A=sum_i lambda_i`.  The formation numerator satisfies

\[
 \boxed{
 P_r(-A-z;\lambda)
 =(-1)^{\frac{r(r+1)}2+1}P_r(z;\lambda).}
 \tag{GFH.R1}
\]

Equivalently, in the centered variable `x=z+A/2`, the polynomial is even for
`r≡1,2 (mod 4)` and odd for `r≡0,3 (mod 4)`.  In the odd cases,

\[
 \boxed{2z+A\ \text{ divides }\ P_r(z;\lambda).}
 \tag{GFH.R2}
\]

### Proof

For a schedule `pi`, write its suffix sums as

\[
 S_k=\sum_{j=k}^r\lambda_{\pi(j)}.
\]

At the reflected spectral parameter,

\[
 \prod_{k=1}^r\frac1{-A-z+S_k}
 =(-1)^r\frac{z+A}{z}
 \prod_{k=1}^r\frac1{z+S_k(\pi^{\mathrm{rev}})},
 \tag{GFH.R3}
\]

because the complements `A-S_k` are zero followed by the proper prefix sums, while the suffixes of
the reversed schedule are `A` followed by those same prefix sums.  Reversal has sign
`(-1)^(r(r-1)/2)`.  Therefore

\[
 R_r(-A-z)
 =(-1)^{\frac{r(r+1)}2}\frac{z+A}{z}R_r(z).
 \tag{GFH.R4}
\]

Complementing every nonempty subset gives

\[
 D_r(-A-z)=-\frac{z}{z+A}D_r(z).
 \tag{GFH.R5}
\]

Insert `R_r=Delta_r P_r/D_r` into `(GFH.R4)` and use `(GFH.R5)`.  The common rational factors
cancel and give `(GFH.R1)`.  Centered parity and divisibility follow immediately.  QED.

This is a genuine functional equation of the generated formation law.  Unlike an imposed endpoint
reflection, it is forced simultaneously by schedule reversal and complement duality in the full
subset denominator.

## 3. Exact grade-four numerator

Let the four positive grades be `a,b,c,d`, and write their elementary symmetric functions as

\[
 e_1=a+b+c+d,
 \quad e_2=\sum_{i<j}\lambda_i\lambda_j,
 \quad e_3=\sum_{i<j<k}\lambda_i\lambda_j\lambda_k,
 \quad e_4=abcd.
 \tag{GFH.8}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 — grade-four factorization

The grade-four formation numerator is

\[
 \boxed{P_4(z)=(2z+e_1)Q_4(z),}
 \tag{GFH.9}
\]

where

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 Q_4(z)={}&6z^4+12e_1z^3+(8e_1^2+2e_2)z^2\\
          &+(2e_1^3+2e_1e_2)z
            +(e_1^2e_2-e_1e_3+e_4).
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{GFH.10}
\]

The factor `2z+e_1` is forced before calculation by Theorem 2.2, since `4≡0 (mod 4)`.

### Proof

For `r=4`, `(GFH.3)` becomes

\[
 E_{4,i}
 =(z+\lambda_i)
 \prod_{j\ne i}(z+\lambda_i+\lambda_j)
 \prod_{j\ne i}(z+e_1-\lambda_j).
 \tag{GFH.11}
\]

Since `P_3(z;\widehat\lambda_i)=2z+e_1-\lambda_i`, the recursion gives the exact interpolation
formula

\[
 P_4(z)=\sum_{i=1}^4
 \frac{(2z+e_1-\lambda_i)E_{4,i}}{
       \prod_{j\ne i}(\lambda_i-\lambda_j)}.
 \tag{GFH.12}
\]

To collect it without a 24-schedule expansion, use the standard Lagrange coefficient identities

\[
 \sum_i\frac{\lambda_i^k}{V_i}=0\ (0\le k\le2),
 \qquad
 \sum_i\frac{\lambda_i^{3+m}}{V_i}=h_m(\lambda)\ (m\ge0),
 \tag{GFH.13}
\]

where `h_m` is the complete homogeneous symmetric polynomial.  Expanding the numerator of
`(GFH.12)` in powers of `lambda_i`, applying `(GFH.13)`, and rewriting the resulting `h_m` in the
elementary basis yields

\[
 (2z+e_1)
 \left[6z^4+12e_1z^3+(8e_1^2+2e_2)z^2
 +(2e_1^3+2e_1e_2)z+e_1^2e_2-e_1e_3+e_4\right].
\]

Every operation is a polynomial identity, so the result also holds at repeated grades.  QED.

## 4. Hurwitz stability on the full positive chamber

The apparent negative term in the constant coefficient does not threaten positivity.  Indeed,
`e_1e_2>e_3` for positive grades, so

\[
 e_1^2e_2-e_1e_3+e_4>0.
 \tag{GFH.14}
\]

We use the following real quartic form of the Routh--Hurwitz criterion: if

\[
 q(z)=a_4z^4+a_3z^3+a_2z^2+a_1z+a_0
\]

has positive coefficients and

\[
 a_3a_2a_1-a_4a_1^2-a_3^2a_0>0,
 \tag{GFH.15}
\]

then every zero of `q` has strictly negative real part.  The criterion follows directly by
factoring the real quadratic associated with a hypothetical conjugate pair in the closed right
half-plane; it is also proved self-contained in the audited corpus module
`RHTower/GibbsQuarticStability.lean`.

### Theorem 4.1 — unconditional grade-four causal stability

For every `a,b,c,d>0`, every zero of `P_4` lies in `Re z<0`.

### Proof

For `Q_4`, put

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 a_4&=6, &a_3&=12e_1, &a_2&=8e_1^2+2e_2,\\
 a_1&=2e_1(e_1^2+e_2),
 &a_0&=e_1^2e_2-e_1e_3+e_4.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{GFH.16}
\]

All are positive.  The first Hurwitz minor is

\[
 a_3a_2-a_4a_1=12e_1(7e_1^2+e_2)>0.
 \tag{GFH.17}
\]

The decisive minor is

\[
 \boxed{
 a_3a_2a_1-a_4a_1^2-a_3^2a_0
 =24e_1^2
 \bigl(7e_1^4+2e_1^2e_2+e_2^2+6e_1e_3-6e_4\bigr).}
 \tag{GFH.18}
\]

Now `e_2^2≥6e_4`: the three products of disjoint pair monomials contribute exactly `6e_4` to
`e_2^2`, and every remaining term is nonnegative.  Therefore the bracket in `(GFH.18)` is strictly
positive.  The quartic criterion places every zero of `Q_4` in the open left half-plane.  The
remaining factor `2z+e_1` has zero `-e_1/2<0`.  QED.

### Corollary 4.2 — bounded causal Green exactifier

For fixed positive grades, `1/P_4(z)` is the Laplace transform of a causal kernel `g_4` that is a
finite sum of polynomial--exponential terms and belongs to
\(L^1(0,\infty)\cap L^2(0,\infty)\).  Consequently convolution by `g_4` is bounded on every
\(L^p\), \(1\le p\le\infty\).

### Proof

The rational function `1/P_4` is strictly proper and all poles have strictly negative real part by
Theorem 4.1.  Partial fractions therefore give the claimed causal kernel and exponential decay.
Young's convolution inequality gives boundedness.  QED.

This corollary proves stability, not positivity of `g_4`.  Positivity below belongs to the exact
formed range, not necessarily to the Green kernel pointwise.

## 5. Fifteen-phase exactification

For \(\pi\in S_4\), define

\[
 B_\pi^{(4)}(t)=
 \int_{\substack{u_1,\ldots,u_4\ge0\\u_1+\cdots+u_4\le t}}
 \exp\!\left[-\sum_{k=1}^4
 \left(\sum_{j=k}^4\lambda_{\pi(j)}\right)u_k\right]du,
 \tag{GFH.19}
\]

and the source-normalized alternant

\[
 F_4(t)=abcd\sum_{\pi\in S_4}\operatorname{sgn}(\pi)B_\pi^{(4)}(t).
 \tag{GFH.20}
\]

Put

\[
 W_4=\prod_{\substack{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq[4]\\|S|\ge2}}\lambda_S,
 \qquad
 M_4=abcd\,W_4=\prod_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq[4]}\lambda_S.
 \tag{GFH.21}
\]

Let `H_4` be the distribution function of the sum of fifteen independent exponential variables,
one with rate `lambda_S` for every nonempty subset `S⊆[4]`.

### Theorem 5.1 — grade-four subset-phase conservation law

For `Re z>0`,

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal LF_4(z)=\frac{abcd\,\Delta_4P_4(z)}{zD_4(z)},
 \qquad
 \mathcal LH_4(z)=\frac{M_4}{zD_4(z)}.}
 \tag{GFH.22}
\]

Hence, for distinct grades,

\[
 \boxed{W_4F_4=\Delta_4P_4(\partial_t)H_4,}
 \tag{GFH.23}
\]

and with the stable causal inverse \(\mathcal K_4g=g_4*g\),

\[
 \boxed{H_4=W_4\mathcal K_4\Delta_4^{-1}F_4.}
 \tag{GFH.24}
\]

At repeated grades, the CDF definition supplies the continuous formed law while the alternant
vanishes.

### Proof

Laplace transformation of `(GFH.19)` gives `z^(-1)` times the suffix-chain product defining
`R_4`.  The first identity in `(GFH.22)` follows from `(GFH.1)`.  Independence of the fifteen
exponentials gives density transform `M_4/D_4`; division by `z` gives the second identity.

The CDF of a sum of fifteen positive continuous variables vanishes to order fifteen at the origin.
Thus applying the degree-five differential polynomial \(P_4(\partial_t)\) produces no Laplace
boundary terms.  Comparing the transforms proves `(GFH.23)`.  Corollary 4.2 gives the unique causal
inverse on the exact range and proves `(GFH.24)`.  QED.

## 6. Uniform all-prime quadruple formation

Let \(\lambda_0=\log 2\) and define the Erlang-fifteen CDF

\[
 G_{15}(t)=1-e^{-\lambda_0t}\sum_{j=0}^{14}
 \frac{(\lambda_0t)^j}{j!}.
 \tag{GFH.25}
\]

Every nonempty subset rate of four prime logarithms is at least `lambda_0`.  Coupling each

\[
 \boxed{H_4(t)\ge G_{15}(t)\qquad(t\ge0).}
 \tag{GFH.26}

For `T>0`, put

\[
 c_{T,15}^2=\int_0^TG_{15}(t)^2dt>0.
 \tag{GFH.27}

Let `P^(4)` be the unordered quadruples of distinct primes, with orthonormal endpoint vectors

\[
 (\mathcal G_T^{(4)}d)(t)
 =\sum_{p<q<r<s}d_{p,q,r,s}
 H_{\log p,\log q,\log r,\log s}(t)e_{pqrs}.
 \tag{GFH.28}

### Theorem 6.1 — global grade-four frame

For every `T>0`,

\[
 \boxed{
 c_{T,15}^2\|d\|^2
 \le\|\mathcal G_T^{(4)}d\|^2

 \tag{GFH.29}

Thus the grade-four formed response is injective with closed range and inverse norm at most

### Proof

Distinct arithmetic quadruple endpoints are orthogonal.  Expand the squared norm as the sum of
\(|d_{pqrs}|^2\int_0^T H_{pqrs}(t)^2dt\), then use \(G_{15}\le H_4\le1\).  QED.

## 7. What the theorem changes

The grade-four gate could have killed the proposed field by producing an unstable causal
numerator.  Instead it proves three new laws.

1. **Deletion is constitutive.**  Every higher numerator is generated from lower grades by
   `(GFH.4)`; factorial route enumeration is not fundamental.
2. **Stability survives nontrivially.**  Grade four is not stable because coefficients happen to
   be positive.  It is stable because the source symmetric functions satisfy the strict Hurwitz
   conservation inequality `(GFH.18)`.
3. **Exact-range causality restores properness.**  A possibly signed Green kernel nevertheless
   maps the certified alternant range to a positive fifteen-phase CDF with a uniform arithmetic
   floor.

The next falsifiable gate is grade five.  The degree bound there is sixteen, and Theorem 2.2 makes
`P_5(z)` an even polynomial in `z+A/2`.  The deletion recursion `(GFH.4)` reduces
the computation to five deleted grade-four numerators and their proper-subset incidence factors.
The live question is whether `P_5` remains Hurwitz stable, factors into lower stable pieces, or
forces the first signed phase-type formation.

## 8. Hostile corrections and scope

1. **Positive coefficients prove stability. — False.**  The load-bearing statement is the strict
   Hurwitz minor `(GFH.18)`.
2. **Hurwitz stability makes the Green kernel positive. — False.**  Stability gives an integrable
   causal kernel.  Positivity is proved only after restricting to the exact generated alternant
   range through `(GFH.24)`.
3. **The grade-four frame couples different quadruple channels. — False.**  Global assembly uses
   orthogonal arithmetic endpoints.  New content is the uniform coefficient produced before that
   assembly.
4. **The recursion proves every `P_r` stable. — False.**  Barycentric terms in `(GFH.4)` have
   alternating signs.  Grade five remains open.
5. **The ambient Vandermonde inverse is bounded. — False.**  It exists only on the exact alternant
   range; prime-log crowding still makes the unsoldered inverse singular.
6. **The theorem chooses an RH displacement. — False.**  It proves formation and properness, not a
   source-null law or selector.
7. **The theorem is presentation-free without a marked intervention alphabet. — False.**  The
   permutohedral schedules are intrinsic only relative to source-marked legal letters.
