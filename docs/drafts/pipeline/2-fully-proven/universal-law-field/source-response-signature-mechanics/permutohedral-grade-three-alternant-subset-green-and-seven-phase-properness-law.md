## 7. General permutohedral denominator law

Grade three exposes a general algebraic mechanism even before higher positivity is known.

For `r≥1`, positive variables `λ₁,…,λ_r`, and a permutation `π∈S_r`, define

\[
 R_r(z;\lambda)
 =\sum_{\pi\in S_r}\operatorname{sgn}(\pi)

 \frac{1}{z+\sum_{j=k}^r\lambda_{\pi(j)}}.
 \tag{PGE.23}
\]

Put

\[
 D_r(z;\lambda)
 =\prod_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq[r]}(z+\lambda_S),
 \qquad
 \Delta_r(\lambda)=\prod_{i<j}(\lambda_i-\lambda_j).
 \tag{PGE.24}
\]

### Theorem 7.1 — permutohedral alternant factorization

There is a symmetric polynomial `P_r(z;λ)` such that

\[
 \boxed{

 =\frac{\Delta_r(\lambda)P_r(z;\lambda)}{D_r(z;\lambda)}.}
 \tag{PGE.25}
\]

It has total degree at most

\[
 \boxed{
 2^r-1-\frac{r(r+1)}2.}
 \tag{PGE.26}
\]

At the first three grades,

\[
 P_1=1,\qquad P_2=1,\qquad P_3=2z+\lambda_1+\lambda_2+\lambda_3.
 \tag{PGE.27}
\]

### Proof

Every denominator chain in `(PGE.23)` is a chain of nonempty subsets, hence divides `D_r`.
Therefore `N_r=D_rR_r` is a polynomial.  The product `D_r` is symmetric, while `R_r` changes sign
under every transposition of two `λ` variables.  Thus `N_r` is alternating.  It vanishes on every
hyperplane `λ_i=λ_j`, so each `λ_i-λ_j` divides it.  These relatively prime linear factors give

\[

\]

with `P_r` symmetric.

The common denominator has degree `2^r-1`.  Each permutation term contains `r` denominator
factors, so `N_r` has degree at most `2^r-1-r`.  Subtracting the Vandermonde degree
`r(r-1)/2` gives `(PGE.26)`.  The values in `(PGE.27)` are the grade-one identity, the grade-two
