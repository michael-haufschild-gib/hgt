## 1. Fusion data

Let `E,F_1,F_2,F_3` be real normed vector spaces. Let

\[
 R_i:E\longrightarrow F_i\qquad(i=1,2,3)
 \tag{TRF.1}
\]

be continuous linear reporters and define

\[
 R=(R_1,R_2,R_3):E\longrightarrow F_1\times F_2\times F_3.
 \tag{TRF.2}
\]

Assume a continuous linear common decoder

\[
 Q:F_1\times F_2\times F_3\longrightarrow E,
 \qquad QR=\operatorname{id}_E.
 \tag{TRF.3}
\]

Each organ carries a continuous energy `W_i:F_i->R` satisfying:

1. `W_i>=0`;
2. `W_i` is strictly convex;
3. every sublevel `{u:W_i(u)<=c}` is compact.

Define fused energy

\[
 W(z)=W_1(R_1z)+W_2(R_2z)+W_3(R_3z).
 \tag{TRF.4}
\]

## 2. Joint reconstruction

### Theorem 2.1 -- source injectivity

The product reporter `R` is injective.

### Proof

If `R(a)=R(b)`, apply `Q` and use `QR=id` to get `a=b`. QED.

### Theorem 2.2 -- formed-core reconstruction

If a history `x_n` lies in a compact convex source carrier, then

\[
 \boxed{

 \qquad
 Q(\mathfrak F(Rx))=\mathfrak F(x).
 }
 \tag{TRF.5}
\]

### Proof

Apply continuous-affine law-core exchange first to `R`, then to `Q`. The decoder identity changes
the twice-reported history `QRx` back to `x`. QED.

This is joint reconstruction. No individual reporter is required to be faithful.

## 3. Exact additive dissipation conservation

For any energy `V` define midpoint dissipation

\[
 \Delta_V(a,b)=\frac12V(a)+\frac12V(b)-
 V\!\left(\frac{a+b}{2}\right).
 \tag{TRF.6}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 -- fusion identity

For every `a,b in E`,

\[
 \boxed{
 \Delta_W(a,b)=
 \Delta_{W_1}(R_1a,R_1b)+
 \Delta_{W_2}(R_2a,R_2b)+
 \Delta_{W_3}(R_3a,R_3b).
 }
 \tag{TRF.7}
\]

### Proof

Each linear reporter preserves the midpoint. Substitute `(TRF.4)` into `(TRF.6)`, distribute the
three sums, and regroup by reporter. QED.

This is an exact conservation identity, not a scalar analogy among routes. It says total nonlinear
formation loss has three typed nonnegative contributions.

## 4. Joint strictness

### Theorem 4.1 -- fused strict convexity

`W` is strictly convex on `E`.

### Proof

For distinct `a,b`, injectivity of `R` implies `R_i(a)!=R_i(b)` for at least one `i`. Strict
convexity gives a strict Jensen inequality in that organ; convexity gives weak inequalities in the
other two. Adding them gives the strict inequality for `W`. QED.

Thus strictness is a joint property. No reporter needs to distinguish every source pair alone.

## 5. Component properness generates source properness

### Theorem 5.1 -- fused compact sublevels

For every real `c`,

\[
 \boxed{\{z\in E:W(z)\le c\}\text{ is compact}.}
 \tag{TRF.8}
\]

### Proof

Nonnegativity implies

\[
 W(z)\le c\quad\Longrightarrow\quad W_i(R_i z)\le c
 \qquad(i=1,2,3).
\]

Hence the product reporter maps the fused sublevel into the compact product

\[
 K_c=\prod_{i=1}^3\{u_i:W_i(u_i)\le c\}.

Because `QR=id`, every source point in the fused sublevel lies in `Q(K_c)`. The latter is compact.
The fused sublevel is closed by continuity of `W`, hence is a closed subset of `Q(K_c)` and is
compact. QED.

This is the global properness construction missing from a mere product-of-analogy proposal.
Properness is generated by reporter laws plus recovery.

## 9. Sharp boundaries

1. Common recovery is load-bearing. Without it, joint reporters may erase a direction and fused
   energy need not be strict or proper on source space.
2. Nonnegativity is load-bearing for component-to-source properness; otherwise organ energies may
   cancel while individual coordinates escape.
3. Strict convexity in all three organs is stronger than necessary but clean. The exact weaker
   condition is: every distinct source pair is strictly distinguished by at least one organ while
   every other organ is convex on that pair.
4. Compact component sublevels are load-bearing for this properness theorem.
5. Nonlinear no-loss is the selection equation. It is not implied by affine common recovery.
6. The theorem proves no RH claim until source-native reporter maps, energies, convergence, and
