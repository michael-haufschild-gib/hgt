## 2. Setup

Let `I` be a nonempty finite set. Let `w_i>=0`, with `w_i>0` for at least one `i`, and let
`r_i in R`. For `t in R`, define

\[
 a_i(t)=w_i e^{-r_i t},\qquad
 Z(t)=\sum_i a_i(t),\qquad
 M_k(t)=\sum_i a_i(t)r_i^k\quad(k=1,2).
\tag{SH.1}
\]

Then `Z(t)>0`. Define posterior weights, posterior mean rate, posterior variance, and local apparent
exponent by

\[
 p_i(t)=\frac{a_i(t)}{Z(t)},\qquad
 m(t)=\frac{M_1(t)}{Z(t)},\qquad
 v(t)=\frac{M_2(t)}{Z(t)}-m(t)^2,
\tag{SH.2}
\]

\[
 \alpha(t)=t\,m(t).
\tag{SH.3}
\]

## 3. Main theorem

### Theorem 3.1 (posterior curvature and observer balance)

For every real `t`:

1. `sum_i p_i(t)=1` and every `p_i(t)>=0`.
2. The partition and first moment satisfy
   \[
   Z'(t)=-M_1(t),\qquad M_1'(t)=-M_2(t).
   \tag{SH.4}
   \]
3. Posterior mean dissipates exactly by posterior variance:
   \[
   \boxed{m'(t)=-v(t).}
   \tag{SH.5}
   \]
4. Apparent exponent obeys the exact balance law
   \[
   \boxed{\alpha'(t)=m(t)-t v(t),\qquad
   t\alpha'(t)=\alpha(t)-t^2v(t).}
   \tag{SH.6}
   \]
5. Curvature has equivalent determinant, square, and pairwise forms:
   \[
   \boxed{Z(t)M_2(t)-M_1(t)^2=Z(t)^2v(t)\ge0,}
   \tag{SH.7}
   \]
   \[
   v(t)=\sum_i p_i(t)(r_i-m(t))^2,
   \tag{SH.8}
   \]
   \[
   2v(t)=\sum_{i,j}p_i(t)p_j(t)(r_i-r_j)^2.
   \tag{SH.9}
   \]
6. Rigidity: `v(t)=0` for one, hence every, `t` iff all active rates are equal:
   \[
   w_i>0,\ w_j>0\Longrightarrow r_i=r_j.
   \tag{SH.10}
   \]

#### Proof

Every exponential is positive. Nonnegative summands and one positive weight give `Z(t)>0`.
Division of `sum_i a_i=Z` by `Z` proves normalization and positivity.

Finite differentiation is termwise. Since `a_i'=-r_i a_i`, summing gives (SH.4). The quotient rule
then gives

\[
m'=\frac{(-M_2)Z-M_1(-M_1)}{Z^2}
=-\left(\frac{M_2}{Z}-\frac{M_1^2}{Z^2}\right)=-v.
\]

The product rule applied to `alpha=t m` proves the first identity in (SH.6); multiplying by `t` and
using `alpha=tm` proves the second.

Expanding the right side of (SH.8), using `sum p_i=1` and `sum p_i r_i=m`, gives `M_2/Z-m^2`.
Thus it is nonnegative. Multiplying by `Z^2` gives (SH.7). Expanding the normalized double sum in
(SH.9) gives `2(M_2/Z-(M_1/Z)^2)`, proving the pairwise formula.

If active rates are equal, (SH.8) vanishes. Conversely, if `v(t)=0`, every summand in the
nonnegative double sum (SH.9) vanishes. For active `i,j`, both `p_i(t)` and `p_j(t)` are positive,
so `(r_i-r_j)^2=0`. Hence `r_i=r_j`. This condition does not depend on `t`. QED.

### Theorem 3.2 (finite-window properness)

Assume additionally that there is `r_0>0` such that `r_i>=r_0` for every active rate. For every
`t>=0`,

\[
\boxed{m(t)\ge r_0,\qquad \alpha(t)\ge r_0t.}
\tag{SH.11}
\]

Consequently, for every `A in R`,

\[
\boxed{t\ge0\ \text{and}\ \alpha(t)\le A\quad\Longrightarrow\quad t\le A/r_0.}
\tag{SH.12}
\]

Thus every bounded apparent-exponent sublevel has bounded clock projection. In particular no
positive-rate packet can support a globally bounded apparent exponent on the whole positive clock
ray.

#### Proof

For every index, `w_i(r_i-r_0)e^{-r_it}>=0`; inactive terms vanish. Therefore

\[
M_1-r_0Z=\sum_iw_i(r_i-r_0)e^{-r_it}\ge0.
\]

Divide by the positive `Z` to get `m>=r_0`. Multiplication by `t>=0` gives
`alpha=tm>=r_0t`. If also `alpha<=A`, then `r_0t<=A`; division by `r_0>0` gives (SH.12). QED.

## 4. Interpretation as a field law

The primitive is a law of generation, not an object:

1. static heterogeneity supplies an unresolved rate packet;
2. elapsed time generates a posterior tilt `p_i(t)`;
3. curvature `v(t)` is exactly the information lost when the packet is replaced by its fitted local
   exponent;
4. curvature drives mean-rate dissipation through `m'=-v`;
5. a positive rate floor makes the observer map proper on exponent sublevels, so a plateau can only
   be local in clock time.

This separates two claims that curve fitting conflates:

- **observer regularity:** `alpha` can be nearly constant on a selected finite interval;
- **source scale invariance:** `alpha` remains bounded/constant under global formation.

The second cannot hold for this positive-rate mechanism. The invariant diagnostic is posterior
variance plus the clock-sublevel bound, not fitted exponent value.

## 5. Corpus integrations

The theorem is useful wherever corpus data admit a positive finite spectral/rate decomposition:

- finite Gibbs and GNS charts: (SH.5) is the missing local flow law behind static Fisher variance;
- closed recurrent observers: recurrence of phases does not remove radial posterior dissipation;
- formation/properness: (SH.12) gives an explicit coercive clock projection;
- finite Hankel/spectral packets: (SH.7) is the first `2x2` moment determinant and (SH.10) classifies
  its collapse.

These are specializations of one mechanism, not claims that all corpus organs reduce to scalar
mixtures.

## 6. Sharp boundaries

1. If `r_0=0`, finite-window properness can fail; zero rates can retain a nondecaying component.
2. Signed or complex amplitudes destroy posterior positivity and the variance argument.
3. Infinite packets require summability and differentiation hypotheses not supplied here.
4. KWW-tempered first-passage laws with spatially quenched dynamics need not be completely monotone
   scalar mixtures. The theorem extracts only their positive rate-mixture shadow.
5. A plateau near `alpha=1` is not predicted universally. The theorem explains how a plateau can be
   observer-generated and how to falsify global scale invariance.
6. No RH zero, source selector, or arithmetic landing is asserted.
