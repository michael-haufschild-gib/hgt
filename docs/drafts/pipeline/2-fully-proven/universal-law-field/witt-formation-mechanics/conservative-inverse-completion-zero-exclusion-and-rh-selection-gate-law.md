## 2. Abstract conservative-completion theorem

Let `A` be a Hausdorff topological unital algebra whose multiplication is jointly continuous.  Let

\[
 H_N,E_N\in A,\qquad H_NE_N=1
 \tag{CIC.2}
\]

for every finite stage `N`.  Suppose, in one and the same topology,

\[
 H_N\longrightarrow H,\qquad E_N\longrightarrow E.
 \tag{CIC.3}
\]

### Theorem 2.1 — inverse conservation survives joint completion

Then

\[
 \boxed{HE=1.}
 \tag{CIC.4}
\]

Consequently `H` is right-invertible.  If `A` is commutative it is a unit with inverse `E`.
For every continuous unital multiplicative receiver

\[
 \chi:A\longrightarrow\mathbb C,
 \tag{CIC.5}
\]

one has

\[
 \boxed{\chi(H)\chi(E)=1,\qquad \chi(H)\ne0.}
 \tag{CIC.6}

### Proof

Joint continuity of multiplication sends `(CIC.3)` to

\[
 H_NE_N\longrightarrow HE.
\]

By `(CIC.2)` the left side is the constant net `1`, hence converges to `1`.  Hausdorff uniqueness
of limits gives `(CIC.4)`.  Applying `chi` gives `(CIC.6)`.  QED.

The theorem is elementary; its role is load-bearing.  It identifies the exact completion exchange
which finite formation conservation needs before it can exclude a divisor.

## 3. Holomorphic receiver theorem

Let `Omega` be a connected complex domain.  Let `H_N,E_N` be holomorphic scalar functions on
`Omega` with

\[
 H_N(z)E_N(z)=1\qquad(z\in\Omega).
 \tag{CIC.7}
\]

If both sequences converge locally uniformly to holomorphic functions `H,E`, then pointwise
application of Theorem 2.1 gives

\[
 \boxed{H(z)E(z)=1\text{ for every }z\in\Omega.}
 \tag{CIC.8}

Thus `H` and `E` are zero-free.  This is the compact-open analytic form of conservative inverse
completion.

Hurwitz's theorem shows that locally uniform convergence of the zero-free `H_N` alone already
forces a nonzero holomorphic limit to be zero-free.  The joint theorem is stronger as a field law:
it transports the actual exterior companion and preserves the finite conservation identity, not
only the zero set.

## 4. Sharp completion obstruction

The inverse coordinate cannot be omitted in a general topological algebra.  In `R`, the units

\[
 H_N=N^{-1}
\]

converge to the nonunit `0`, while their inverses `E_N=N` escape every bounded set.  Thus the unit
group need not be closed; loss of the exterior companion is exactly how completion can create a
divisor.

Likewise, convergence after a nonfaithful observer is insufficient.  If a quotient erases the
unbounded inverse direction, its observed formation may converge while `(CIC.4)` has no lift.
This is the common mechanism behind dense-radical, nonclosability, singular-source, and
finite-to-cofinal no-gos in the frontier map.
