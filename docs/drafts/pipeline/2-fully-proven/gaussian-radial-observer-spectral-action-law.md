## Statement

Let `V` be an `m`-dimensional real Euclidean space, `m >= 1`, let

\[
 H:V\longrightarrow \operatorname{Herm}(\mathcal H)
\]

be real-linear on a finite-dimensional complex Hilbert space, and fix a
unit vector `Phi`.  If `J` is standard Gaussian in `V`, define the
projective endpoint law

\[
 \mu_t=(J\mapsto[e^{-itH(J)}\Phi])_*\gamma_m .       \tag{1}
\]

Write `J=R Omega` in Gaussian polar coordinates.  Thus `Omega` is uniform
on the unit sphere, `R` is independent of `Omega`, and

\[
 \rho_m(r)=
 {2^{1-m/2}\over\Gamma(m/2)}r^{m-1}e^{-r^2/2}
 \mathbf 1_{(0,\infty)}(r)                           \tag{2}
\]

is the density of `R`.

For each direction `omega`, let `G_omega` be the compact projective closure
of the one-parameter group generated by `H(omega)` on the cyclic support of
`Phi`, and let `nu_omega` be normalized Haar measure on `G_omega`.  Then
there is a unique probability measure `mu_infinity` on projective state
space such that, for every continuous reporter `F`,

\[
 \boxed{
 \lim_{t\to\infty}\int F\,d\mu_t
 =\int_{S(V)}\int_{G_\omega}
 F(g\Phi)\,d\nu_\omega(g)\,d\sigma(\omega).
 }                                                       \tag{3}
\]

Equivalently, `mu_t` converges weakly to the angular mixture of the exact
completed spectral-orbit laws.  No generic spectrum, nondegeneracy,
Diophantine, chaos, random-matrix, design, or Haar-eigenvector premise is
used.

If a Borel target `E` is a continuity set for `mu_infinity`, then

\[
 \lim_{t\to\infty}\Pr\{[e^{-itH(J)}\Phi]\in E\}
 =\int_{S(V)}\nu_\omega\{g:g\Phi\in E\}\,d\sigma(\omega).
                                                               \tag{4}
\]

For the full fixed-`q` Gaussian Pauli ensemble, the right side of `(4)` is
exactly the completed shortcut mass used in equation `(2)` of the parent
card, provided its circuit-easy target is a continuity set.  The theorem
does not compare that mass with Fubini--Study measure and supplies no
dimension-uniform convergence rate.

## Exact character form

Fix `omega`.  Let `E_a` and `Pi_a` be the distinct eigenvalues and spectral
projections of `H(omega)`.  For an integer `k >= 1` and an arbitrary
operator `A` on `H^(tensor k)`, put

\[
 F_A(\psi)=\operatorname{Tr}
 \bigl(A\,|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|^{\otimes k}\bigr).
\]

For multi-indices `a=(a_1,...,a_k)` and `b=(b_1,...,b_k)`, define

\[
 \Delta_{a,b}(\omega)
 =\sum_{j=1}^k(E_{a_j}-E_{b_j})                       \tag{5}
\]

and

\[
 c_{a,b}(\omega)
 =\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[
 A\bigotimes_{j=1}^k
   \bigl(\Pi_{a_j}|\Phi\rangle\langle\Phi|\Pi_{b_j}\bigr)
 \right].                                             \tag{6}
\]

With

\[
 \widehat\rho_m(u)=\int_0^\infty e^{-iur}\rho_m(r)\,dr,
\]

Gaussian radial averaging obeys the exact finite formula

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathbb E_R F_A(e^{-itR H(\omega)}\Phi)
 =\sum_{a,b}c_{a,b}(\omega)
   \widehat\rho_m\bigl(t\Delta_{a,b}(\omega)\bigr).
 }                                                       \tag{7}
\]

The completed value is exactly its zero-action sector:

\[
 \int_{G_\omega}F_A(g\Phi)\,d\nu_\omega(g)
 =\sum_{\Delta_{a,b}(\omega)=0}c_{a,b}(\omega).       \tag{8}
\]

Thus the dimensionless quantity

\[
 \mathfrak a_{a,b}(t,\omega)
 =t\Delta_{a,b}(\omega)                               \tag{9}
\]

is the exact observer--spectral action.  Zero action is persistent plateau
mass; every nonzero action is a transient multiplied by one universal
radial Fourier kernel.  Equation `(7)`, rather than a Haar approximation,
is the exact finite-time transfer law.

## Proof

Gaussian polar integration and linearity give, for every continuous `F`,

\[
 \int F\,d\mu_t
 =\int_{S(V)}\int_0^\infty
 F([e^{-itrH(\omega)}\Phi])\rho_m(r)\,dr\,d\sigma(\omega).
                                                               \tag{10}
\]

Fix `omega` and write

\[
 f_\omega(s)=F([e^{-isH(\omega)}\Phi]).              \tag{11}
\]

The function `f_omega` is the pullback of a continuous function on the
compact monothetic abelian group `G_omega`.  Its characters are uniformly
dense in `C(G_omega)`.  If `chi` is such a character, then on the dense
one-parameter subgroup

\[
 \chi(e^{-isH(\omega)})=e^{is\delta_\chi}             \tag{12}
\]

for one real number `delta_chi`.  If `chi` is nontrivial, then
`delta_chi != 0`; otherwise `(12)` would make `chi` equal to one on a dense
subgroup and hence everywhere.

For the trivial character, radial averaging is one.  For every nontrivial
character, extend `rho_m` by zero to the negative half-line.  It lies in
`L^1(R)`, so the Riemann--Lebesgue lemma gives

\[
 \int_0^\infty e^{itr\delta_\chi}\rho_m(r)\,dr
 \longrightarrow0.                                  \tag{13}
\]

Uniform character approximation of `f_omega` now proves

\[
 \int_0^\infty f_\omega(tr)\rho_m(r)\,dr
 \longrightarrow
 \int_{G_\omega}F(g\Phi)\,d\nu_\omega(g).           \tag{14}
\]

The limit in `(14)` is measurable in `omega`: it is also the limit of the
ordinary finite-time Cesaro means of the jointly continuous map
`(omega,s) -> F([e^(-isH(omega)) Phi])`.  Both sides of `(14)` are bounded
by `||F||_infinity`.  Dominated convergence in `(10)` proves `(3)`.
Positivity, normalization and boundedness of the limiting functional on the
compact projective state space give `mu_infinity` by the Riesz
representation theorem.  The portmanteau theorem gives `(4)`.

For `(7)`, insert the spectral resolution

\[
 e^{-isH(\omega)}=\sum_a e^{-isE_a}\Pi_a
\]

in every tensor factor.  The finite expansion is exactly `(5)`--`(7)`.
Haar integration over `G_omega` kills every nontrivial restricted
character and retains exactly the terms with `Delta_(a,b)=0`, proving
`(8)`.

## What this proves and what it does not

This closes the qualitative local-Hamiltonian ensemble-transfer gap:
isotropic Gaussian coefficients do converge to the exact mixture of their
own completed spectral phase laws.  It also proves that near-resonant
spectral actions are not one possible obstruction among many; after polar
disintegration they are the complete transient remainder.

It does **not** prove the parent shortcut-action conjecture.  Applying `(7)`
to a circuit-easy indicator requires uniform approximation at replica order
large enough to survive the complete circuit union.  The remaining theorem
is a dimension-uniform angular estimate for

\[
 \sigma\{\omega:
 |\Delta_{a,b}(\omega)|\le\delta\}
\]

simultaneously over the growing replica characters selected by all short
circuits.  Pointwise nonresonance or fixed-order level repulsion does not
supply this estimate.

## Hostile audit

1. Equation `(3)` is weak convergence, not a mixing-time bound.
2. Equation `(4)` requires a continuity-set boundary condition; it is not
   asserted for every Borel set.
3. The limiting law is completed-orbit Haar, not Fubini--Study or full
   unitary Haar.
4. The direction `omega` is retained in the mixture.  Averaging completed
   masses before taking a logarithm does not by itself prove the proposed
   two-cost minimum.
5. The theorem applies to every finite-dimensional real-linear Hermitian
   family, including integrable and degenerate families.  That universality
   is why no quantitative uniform rate follows without new angular input.
6. The proof is harmonic-analysis infrastructure.  No standalone novelty or
   Nature-level significance claim is made.
