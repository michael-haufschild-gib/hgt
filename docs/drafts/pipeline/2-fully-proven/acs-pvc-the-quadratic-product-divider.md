# The quadratic transition in the returned prime endpoint

Let \(L\) be the spline horizon, \(f\) the dyadic spline, \(w_n(k)=f(k/n^2)/n\), and \(D=\min(L+1,\lfloor(q+1)/2\rfloor^2)\). In the compensated one-factor sum, subtract the zero-based quotient mesh from the mesh starting at \(q\), keeping the same far cut \(D\). For \(2<q\le L\), \(q\le D\). The difference is exactly \(+w_n(q)\) because the removed segment telescopes with quotient weight one.

**Subquadratic and superquadratic cuts.** For \(2\le U<e^{-8}n^2\), all samples below the cut vanish. Thus the retained ordinary-prime endpoint equals the entire dyadic prime contact. Every fixed cut \(\lfloor n^\alpha\rfloor\) with \(0<\alpha<2\) has this property eventually, including \(17/9\). The finitely many initial differences have bounded total price on every fragment. For \(\alpha>2\), the retained endpoint eventually vanishes since \(L\ll n^2\). At \(17/9\) the discrepancy from the rational price is \(O(n^2)\) per conductor. Its Dirichlet series is holomorphic on \(\Re s>3\), preserving the shifted right-zero residues.

**Quadratic cut.** Let \(A(v)=\int_0^v f(x)\,dx\). At \(K\le L\), the lower prime price is \(n^3A(K/n^2)+O(n^{25/9})\), uniformly in \(K\). Abel summation of the centered prime measure gives the terminal term \(n^2(\theta(K)-K)w_n(K+1)\) and the signed first differences inside the interval. Use \(|\theta(k)-k|\ll k^{8/9}\), \(|w_n|\ll n^{-1}\), \(|\Delta w_n|\ll n^{-3}\), and \(L\ll n^2\). Replacing the integer density by its integral costs \(O(n)\). The upper price has main term \(-n^3A(K/n^2)\) by exact recombination and the zero integral of \(f\).

The logarithmic formula expresses \(A(v)\) as four times the integral of \(e^{t/2}\) times the spline autocorrelation on \((\log v-\log4,\log v)\). The autocorrelation is positive on \((-8,8)\) and zero outside its closure. Hence \(A(v)>0\) precisely for \(e^{-8}<v<4e^8\). At \(K=n^2\), the lower and upper prices have opposite signs on every late fragment, each with magnitude at least \(c\sum n^3\) for a fixed \(c>0\). Their Dirichlet series have respective terms \(+A(1)\zeta(s-3)\) and \(-A(1)\zeta(s-3)\), with holomorphic remainders for \(\Re s>34/9\). The poles at four cancel. The one-sided estimate on the recombined price remains open.

**Fixing the prime cutoff after summing conductors.** Write \(w=f/2\) on the positive half-line. The integrated profile is \(F(v)=v\int_v^\infty w(t)t^{-2}\,dt\). For \(0<v\le e^{-8}\), this equals \(cv\) exactly, where the spline Mellin formula gives
\[
c=\mathcal M w(-1)=\frac{15}{8}\left(\frac{\sinh(3/2)}{3/2}\right)^8>0.
\]
Consequently the primes \(p\le K\le e^{-8}N^2\) in the integrated conductor prefix contribute exactly \(c\sum_{p\le K}p\log p\). The complementary integrated profile plus this first moment approximates \(\log P(N)\) to \(O(N^3)\) uniformly in \(K\). Partial summation with \(\theta(x)=x+O(x^{8/9})\) gives \(cK^2/2+O(K^{17/9})\). At the fixed terminal cutoff \(K=\lfloor N^{17/9}\rfloor\), deleting these primes loses a positive term of order \(N^{34/9}\), already above the intermediate target \(N^{11/3}\). In the original rowwise cutoff the omitted samples eventually vanish. The difference comes from conductors of order \(\sqrt p\) earlier in the prefix.
