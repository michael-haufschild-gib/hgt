### 1. Cofinal Zero-Width Reading and Equivalence of the Obstruction Criterion

**Lemma 1.1 (Uniform Supercritical Floor from a Single Zero).** *Let $\rho \in \mathbb{C}$ satisfy $\zeta(\rho) = 0$, and let $a \in (0, \frac{1}{2}]$ satisfy $\Re \rho > 1 - a$. *Proof.* Since $\zeta(s) \ne 0$ for $\Re s \ge 1$ (`riemannZeta_ne_zero_of_one_le_re`), $\beta := \Re \rho \le 1$, while $a \le \frac{1}{2}$ and $\beta > 1 - a$ give $\beta > \frac{1}{2}$. Put $\delta := \beta + a - 1 > 0$. Since $(1 + \log K) K^{-\delta / 2} \to 0$ as $K \to \infty$, choose $K_0 \ge 1$ so that $4800 \|\rho\| ((1 + \log K) K^{-\delta / 2})^2 < 1$ for all $K \ge K_0$. Suppose $(c, m, B)$ is a producer on $[1, K]$ ($K \ge K_0$) with $\varepsilon := \mathrm{rate}(c, m, B) \le K^{-a} \le 1$. By `IsProducer.peakRatio_le`, the peak ratio $x := \mathrm{peak}(c)/m \ge 1$ satisfies $x \le 100(1 + \log K)^2(K \varepsilon + 1) \le 200(1 + \log K)^2 K^{1 - a}$. By `IsProducer.zero_law`, $1 \le 24 \|\rho\| x^{1 - \beta} \varepsilon^\beta \le 4800 \|\rho\| ((1 + \log K) K^{-\delta/2})^2 < 1$, a contradiction. $\square$

**Corollary 1.2 (Supercritical Floor Above $1 - \Theta_\zeta$).** *For every $a > 1 - \Theta_\zeta$, for all sufficiently large $K$, $\mathrm{bestRate}(K) \ge K^{-a}$ and every producer $(c, m, B)$ on $[1, K]$ satisfies $\mathrm{rate}(c, m, B) > K^{-a}$.*

### 2. Short-Interval $L^2$ Mellin–Plancherel Bound and Taut-String Smooth Stencil

Fix $\Theta \in [\frac{1}{2}, 1)$ such that $\zeta(s) \ne 0$ for all $\Re s > \Theta$. Fix $\varepsilon \in (0, \frac{1}{8})$ with $\Theta + \varepsilon < 1$, and put $\sigma := \Theta + \varepsilon \in (\frac{1}{2}, 1)$.

**Lemma 2.1 (Logarithmic $L^2$ Increment Bound for Smoothed Mertens Sums).** *For $\theta \in (0, 1]$ and $X > 0$, define*
$$S_\theta(X) := \sum_{n = 1}^\infty \mu(n)\, \mathrm{smoothWeight}(X, \theta X)(n).$$
*There exists $C_{\Theta, \varepsilon} > 0$ independent of $\theta \in (0, 1]$ and $\lambda \in (0, 1]$ such that:*
1. *For all $X > 0$, $|S_\theta(X) - M(\lfloor X \rfloor)| \le \theta X + 1$.*
2. *For all $\theta \in (0, 1]$ and $\lambda \in (0, 1]$,*
   $$\int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-2\sigma u} \big| S_\theta(e^{u + \lambda}) - S_\theta(e^u) \big|^2 \, du \le C_{\Theta, \varepsilon}\, \lambda^{1 - 2\varepsilon}.$$

*Proof.* (1) is `norm_tsum_smoothWeight_sub_sum_le` applied to $N = X, H = \theta X$ (note $\mu(0) = 0$ and both sides are real).
For (2), since $\mathrm{smoothWeight}(X, \theta X)(x) = 1 - \mathrm{smoothTransition}((\theta X)^{-1}(x - X)) = \mathrm{smoothWeight}(1, \theta)(x / X)$, substituting $x = X v$ in the Mellin integral gives
$$\mathcal{M}\big(\mathrm{smoothWeight}(X, \theta X)\big)(s) = X^s W_\theta(s), \qquad W_\theta(s) := \mathcal{M}\big(\mathrm{smoothWeight}(1, \theta)\big)(s)$$
for $\Re s > 0$. By `mobius_perron_shift_of_zeroFree` (with $\mathrm{dl} = \varepsilon$, growth exponent $\varepsilon$) and `exists_invZeta_shift_data_of_zeroFree`, there exists $K_b \ge 0$ such that $\|\mathrm{invZeta}(\sigma + i t)\| \le K_b (2 + |t|)^\varepsilon$ for all $t \in \mathbb{R}$, and for every $X > 0$,
$$S_\theta(e^\lambda X) - S_\theta(X) = \frac{1}{2\pi} \int_{-\infty}^\infty X^{\sigma + i t} (e^{\lambda(\sigma + i t)} - 1)\, \mathrm{invZeta}(\sigma + i t)\, W_\theta(\sigma + i t)\, dt = \mathrm{mellinInv}\,\sigma\,\Psi_{\theta, \lambda}(X^{-1}),$$
where $\Psi_{\theta, \lambda}(s) := (e^{\lambda s} - 1)\,\mathrm{invZeta}(s)\,W_\theta(s)$, using $(X^{-1})^{-s} = X^s$ for $X > 0$.
On `verticalStrip σ 2`, $s \mapsto (e^{\lambda s} - 1)\,\mathrm{invZeta}(s)$ is holomorphic and bounded by $(e^2 + 1) K_b (2 + |\Im s|)^\varepsilon$, so `integrable_mul_mellin_smoothWeight_line` gives `Integrable (fun t => Ψ_{θ,λ}(σ + i t))`. Moreover, `exists_bound_mellin_smoothWeight_uniform_support 0` gives an absolute $A_0 \ge 0$ with $\|s\| \cdot \|W_\theta(s)\| \le A_0 (1 + \theta)^\sigma \le 4 A_0$ for all $\Re s = \sigma$. For all $t \in \mathbb{R}$, $\|s\| \ge \max(\sigma, |t|) \ge \frac{1}{3}(2 + |t|)$, hence $\|\Psi_{\theta, \lambda}(\sigma + i t)\| \le 12 (e^2 + 1) K_b A_0 (2 + |t|)^{-1 + \varepsilon} \le 12 (e^2 + 1) K_b A_0$, so $\Psi_{\theta, \lambda}(\sigma + i\,\cdot) \in L^1(\mathbb{R}) \cap L^2(\mathbb{R})$.
Applying `integral_norm_sq_mellinInv` and `integral_Ioi_comp_neg_log_div` ($z = e^{-u}$, $X = e^u$, $e^{2\sigma \log z} z^{-1} = z^{2\sigma - 1}$) yields
$$\int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-2\sigma u} \big| S_\theta(e^{u + \lambda}) - S_\theta(e^u) \big|^2 \, du = \frac{1}{2\pi} \int_{-\infty}^\infty \|\Psi_{\theta, \lambda}(\sigma + i t)\|^2 \, dt.$$
Split $\mathbb{R}$ into $(- \lambda^{-1}, \lambda^{-1})$ and $\{|t| \ge \lambda^{-1}\}$:
- On $|t| < \lambda^{-1}$: since $\|e^{\lambda s} - 1\| \le \lambda \|s\| e^{\lambda \sigma} \le e^2 \lambda \|s\|$ and $\|s\| \|W_\theta(s)\| \le 4 A_0$, while $2 + |t| \le 3 \lambda^{-1}$ (as $\lambda \le 1$), we have $\|\Psi_{\theta, \lambda}(\sigma + i t)\| \le 4 e^2 A_0 K_b 3^\varepsilon \lambda^{1 - \varepsilon}$. Integrating its square over $(- \lambda^{-1}, \lambda^{-1})$ of measure $2 \lambda^{-1}$ gives $\le 2 (4 e^2 A_0 K_b 3^\varepsilon)^2 \lambda^{1 - 2\varepsilon}$.
- On $|t| \ge \lambda^{-1} \ge 1$: $\|e^{\lambda s} - 1\| \le e^2 + 1$, $\|W_\theta(\sigma + i t)\| \le 4 A_0 |t|^{-1}$, and $(2 + |t|)^\varepsilon \le (3 |t|)^\varepsilon$, so $\|\Psi_{\theta, \lambda}(\sigma + i t)\|^2 \le (4 (e^2 + 1) A_0 K_b 3^\varepsilon)^2 |t|^{-(2 - 2\varepsilon)}$. Since $2 - 2\varepsilon > 1$, `setIntegral_le_of_le_powerTail` bounds the tail integral by $\frac{2}{1 - 2\varepsilon} (4 (e^2 + 1) A_0 K_b 3^\varepsilon)^2 \lambda^{1 - 2\varepsilon}$. $\square$

**Lemma 2.2 (Dyadic Haar Chaining on Discrete Grids).** *Let $f : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{R}$ and $M \in \mathbb{N}$. Define the dyadic energy on $[0, 2^M]$ by*
$$\mathcal{E}_M(f) := \sum_{m = 0}^M \sum_{q = 0}^{2^{M - m} - 1} \big( f((q + 1) 2^m) - f(q 2^m) \big)^2.$$
*For any $R$ pairwise disjoint intervals $[a_r, b_r) \subseteq [0, 2^M)$ ($a_r < b_r$) with $|f(b_r) - f(a_r)| \ge \Delta > 0$ for each $r$, one has $R \Delta^2 \le 2 (M + 1) \mathcal{E}_M(f)$.*

*Proof.* By induction on $M$, every interval $[a, b) \subseteq [0, 2^M)$ is a disjoint union of at most $2 M$ (for $M \ge 1$, or $1 \le 2(M+1)$ for all $M \ge 0$) dyadic intervals $I_{m, q} = [q 2^m, (q+1) 2^m)$: specifically, prefixes $[0, b)$ and suffixes $[a, 2^M)$ each decompose into at most $M + 1$ dyadic intervals by splitting at the midpoint $2^{M-1}$, and a general $[a, b)$ either lies in one half or splits at $2^{M-1}$ into a suffix of $[0, 2^{M-1})$ of size $\le M$ plus a prefix of $[2^{M-1}, 2^M)$ of size $\le M$, giving $\le 2 M \le 2(M + 1)$ pieces. For each $r$, $f(b_r) - f(a_r)$ telescopes as the sum of increments over its dyadic pieces $\mathcal{D}_r$ ($|\mathcal{D}_r| \le 2(M+1)$), so Cauchy–Schwarz gives $\Delta^2 \le |\mathcal{D}_r| \sum_{(m,q) \in \mathcal{D}_r} (f((q+1)2^m) - f(q 2^m))^2$. Because the intervals $[a_r, b_r)$ are pairwise disjoint and each $I_{m,q}$ used in $\mathcal{D}_r$ is contained in $[a_r, b_r)$, the index sets $\mathcal{D}_r$ are pairwise disjoint, so summing over $r$ yields $R \Delta^2 \le 2(M + 1) \mathcal{E}_M(f)$. $\square$

**Lemma 2.3 (Dyadic Bertrand Smooth Jump Placement).** *Fix $d \ge 1$. Put $C_{d,0} := 4 (d + 4)^d$ and $C_{d,1} := \max(C_{d,0},\, 2^{d + 3})$. For every $y \ge 4 d + 5$ and every integer $z$ with $C_{d,0} y \le z \le y^{d + 1}$, there exists $k \in \mathbb{N}$ such that:*
1. *$z < k$ and $k \le z + \frac{(4 d + 5) 2^{d^2 + d}}{4 y} z$;*
2. *$3 \le k$ and $4 \nmid k$;*
3. *every prime power $q^e \mid k$ ($q$ prime, $e \ge 1$) satisfies $q^e \le C_{d,1} y$.*

*Proof.* Put $U := \lfloor \frac{z}{4 y} \rfloor$. Since $z \ge 4 (d + 4)^d y$ and $z \le y^{d + 1}$, we have $(d + 4)^d \le U \le y^d$. Let $u_0 := \lfloor U^{1 / d} \rfloor \in [d + 4, y]$, so $u_0^d \le U < (u_0 + 1)^d \le (2 u_0)^d$. For each $i \in \{0, \dots, d - 1\}$, Bertrand's postulate (`Nat.exists_prime_lt_and_le_two_mul`) on $2^{i + 1} u_0 \ge 2(d + 4) > 0$ yields a prime $p_i \in (2^{i + 1} u_0,\, 2^{i + 2} u_0]$. These intervals are pairwise disjoint, so $p_0 < \dots < p_{d - 1}$ are $d$ distinct odd primes, each satisfying $d + 4 < 2 u_0 < p_i \le 2^{d + 1} y$. Their product $M := \prod_{i = 0}^{d - 1} p_i$ satisfies $M > (2 u_0)^d > U \ge \frac{z}{4 y} - 1$, hence $M \ge U + 1 > \frac{z}{4 y}$ (so $\lfloor z / M \rfloor < 4 y$), and $M \le 2^{\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}(i+2)} u_0^d \le 2^{d^2 + d} U \le 2^{d^2 + d} \frac{z}{4 y}$.
Consider the $d + 1$ integers $t_j := 4 \lfloor \frac{z}{4 M} \rfloor + 4 j + 5$ ($0 \le j \le d$). Each $t_j \equiv 1 \pmod 4$, so $4 \nmid t_j$. Since $4 \lfloor \frac{z / M}{4} \rfloor + 4 > \frac{z}{M}$, every $t_j \ge \lfloor z / M \rfloor + 2 > \frac{z}{M}$ and $t_j \le \lfloor z / M \rfloor + 4 d + 5 \le 4 y + 4 d + 5 \le 2^{d + 3} y$. Each odd prime $p_i > d$ divides at most one $t_j$ ($0 \le j \le d$), because $p_i \mid (t_{j'} - t_j) = 4(j' - j)$ with $0 < j' - j \le d$ contradicts $\gcd(p_i, 4) = 1$ and $p_i > d$. By pigeonhole, some $t_{j_*}$ is divisible by none of $p_0, \dots, p_{d - 1}$, hence $\gcd(M, t_{j_*}) = 1$. Put $k := M t_{j_*}$. Then $z < k \le z + (4 d + 5) M \le z(1 + \frac{(4 d + 5) 2^{d^2 + d}}{4 y})$, $4 \nmid k$ (indeed $k$ is odd), and any prime power $q^e \mid M t_{j_*}$ divides either $M$ (forcing $q^e = p_i \le 2^{d + 1} y$ since $M$ is squarefree) or $t_{j_*} \le 2^{d + 3} y$. $\square$

**Proposition 2.4 (Taut-String Smooth Stencil at Scale $(y, d)$).** *Fix $\Theta \in [\frac{1}{2}, 1)$, $\varepsilon \in (0, \frac{1}{8})$ with $\Theta + \varepsilon < 1$, and $d \ge 1$. 1. *For every $k \ge 2$ with $b(k) \ne 0$, one has $4 \nmid k$, $b(2) = -1$, and every odd prime power $Q = q^e \mid k$ satisfies $Q \le C_{d,1} y$.*
2. *The $L^1$ mass satisfies $\sum_{k = 1}^{2 T} |b(k)| \le C_1 (1 + \log y)^3 y^{1 + 2\varepsilon} T^{2\Theta - 1 + 2\varepsilon} + C_1 T^{\Theta + \varepsilon}$.*
3. *For every integer $x \ge 1$, the partial sum $B(x) := \sum_{k = 1}^x b(k)$ satisfies*

Since $S_\theta(e^u)$ is a finite sum of continuous functions on $[U_j - 1, U_j + 2]$, the integrand achieves its minimum on $[0, 1]$ at some $\tau_j \in [0, 1]$ satisfying $\mathcal{E}_{M_{\mathrm{grid}} + 1} \le C'_{\Theta, \varepsilon} (M_{\mathrm{grid}} + 2) X_j^{2\sigma} (32 y)^{2\varepsilon}$.
Restrict the grid $w_{j,k}(\tau_j)$ to indices $k \in [k_{\mathrm{start}}(j), k_{\mathrm{end}}(j)]$ lying in $[U_j, \min(U_j + 1, \log T)]$, and run the greedy tube-exit walk with threshold $\Delta_j := X_j / y$: starting at $a_1 = k_{\mathrm{start}}(j)$, let $b_r$ be the minimal index $k \in (a_r, k_{\mathrm{end}}(j)]$ with $|S_\theta(e^{w_{j,k}}) - S_\theta(e^{w_{j,a_r}})| \ge \Delta_j$, and set $a_{r+1} := b_r$. By Lemma 2.2, the number $R_j$ of exits satisfies $R_j \Delta_j^2 \le 2(M_{\mathrm{grid}} + 2) \mathcal{E}_{M_{\mathrm{grid}} + 1}$, while single-step increments of $S_\theta$ on step $\lambda_0 \le \frac{1}{16 y}$ are $\le 12 \Delta_j$ by Lemma 2.1(1) and $|\mu| \le 1$, so each exit jump has size $\le 13 \Delta_j$.
Concatenating all selected points across $j = 0, \dots, J - 1$ (plus the right endpoint $z_L := T$) in strictly increasing integer/real order $y_0 \le z_1 < z_2 < \dots < z_L = T$:

- By `mobiusMertens_subpower_of_zeroFree`, $|M(x)| \le C''_{\Theta, \varepsilon} x^{\Theta + \varepsilon}$ for all $x \ge 1$, so $|V_{L-1}| \le C'''_{\Theta, \varepsilon} T^{\Theta + \varepsilon}$, and summing $|J_i|$ over all octaves gives (2).
- Map each $\lceil z_i \rceil \in [y_0, T]$ via Lemma 2.3 to $k_i \in (\lceil z_i \rceil,\, \lceil z_i \rceil(1 + C'_d / y)] \subseteq (y_0, 2 T]$, and define $b(k) := \mu(k)$ for $1 \le k \le y_0$ and $b(k) := \sum_{i : k_i = k} J_i$ for $y_0 < k \le 2 T$. Because every $k_i > y_0 \ge 4$, $b(2) = \mu(2) = -1$, and $\mu(4 m) = 0$ together with Lemma 2.3 gives (1).
- For (3): for $1 \le x \le y_0$, $B(x) = M(x)$. For $y_0 < x < T$, the unshifted step sum $V_0 + \sum_{z_i \le x} J_i$ stays within $O(x/y)$ of $S_\theta(x)$ (hence of $M(x)$), while shifting $z_i \mapsto k_i \le z_i(1 + C'_d / y)$ delays only jumps with $z_i \in [x / (1 + C'_d / y),\, x]$; in logarithmic coordinates this window has width $\le C'_d / y \le 32 C'_d \lambda_0$, containing $O_d(1)$ grid points across at most two adjacent octaves, each with $|J_i| = O(x / y)$. ### 3. Bipartite Hamming-Square Clusters and Subset-Product CRT Ramp

Let $b : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{Z}$ satisfy Proposition 2.4(1)–(3) on $[1, 2 T]$ with $|B(x) - M(x)| \le \eta_0 x$ for all $x \ge 1$. For $r \in \mathbb{N}$ define
$$F_{\mathrm{near}}(r) := \sum_{k = 2}^{2 T} \left(-\frac{b(k)}{k}\right) (r \bmod k), \qquad \mathcal{M}_b := \sum_{k = 2}^{2 T} |b(k)|.$$
By the hyperbola identity $\sum_{k=1}^r (b(k) - \mu(k))\lfloor r/k \rfloor = \sum_{j=1}^r (B(\lfloor r/j \rfloor) - M(\lfloor r/j \rfloor))$ (`sum_Ioc_mul_floor` and `moebius_floor_summatory`), evaluating the drift $\theta_b := \sum_{k=1}^{2 T} b(k)/k$ at $X \ge \max(K, 2 T)$ gives, just as in `abs_compNear_sub_le`,
$$|F_{\mathrm{near}}(s) - 1| \le s Y_n \quad (1 \le s \le X), \qquad Y_n := 2 \eta_0 (1 + \log X) + \frac{1 + \mathcal{M}_b}{X} + \frac{1}{X} > 0.$$
Let $\mathcal{Q}$ be the set of odd prime powers $Q = q^e \le C_{d,1} y$ ($q \ge 3$ prime, $e \ge 1$), and put $D_Q := \sum_{k \in [2, 2T],\, Q \mid k} |b(k)|$. Every $k \le 2 T$ is divisible by at most $(\lfloor \log_2(2 T) \rfloor)^2$ elements of $\mathcal{Q}$, so $\sum_{Q \in \mathcal{Q}} D_Q \le (\log_2(2 T) + 1)^2 \mathcal{M}_b$. Moreover:
- Since $b(2) = -1$ gives $(-\frac{b(2)}{2})(r \bmod 2) = \frac{1}{2}(r \bmod 2) \ge 0$ and every $k \ge 3$ with $4 \nmid k$ has an odd prime factor, $F_{\mathrm{near}}(r) \ge -\sum_{Q \in \mathcal{Q}} D_Q$ for all $r \in \mathbb{N}$.
- If $h \le r$ and $2 \mid (r - h)$, no power of $2$ dividing an active letter $k$ ($4 \nmid k$) can fail to divide $r - h$, so $|F_{\mathrm{near}}(r) - F_{\mathrm{near}}(h)| \le \sum_{Q \in \mathcal{Q},\, Q \nmid (r - h)} D_Q$.

**Proposition 3.1 (Bipartite Connectedness and CRT Producer Assembly).** *Fix integers $M_0 \ge 2$, $S := 16 M_0 + 4$, $W \ge 2$, and $K \ge 1$ such that:*
- *$m_* := \lfloor \log_{2^S W} K \rfloor$ satisfies $1 \le m_* < M_0$;*
- *$B_0 := \lfloor \frac{K}{2^{S(M_0 + 1) + 2} W} \rfloor \ge 2$, $2 T \le K \le X$, and $K \ge 2^{2 S + 2} W^2 (C_{d,1} y + 1)$.*

*Let $\mathcal{P}_W := \{p \text{ prime} : W < p \le 2^S W\}$. For each $Q = q^e \in \mathcal{Q}$, let $\mathcal{P}_{W, Q} := \mathcal{P}_W \setminus \{q\}$, $b_Q := \lfloor \log_2 \frac{K}{2^{2 S} W^2 Q} \rfloor \ge 1$, and $L_Q(p_1, p_2) := 2^{b_Q} p_1 p_2 Q \in (\frac{K}{2^{2 S + 1}}, K]$. Choose $Y_{\mathrm{rat}} \in \mathbb{Q} \cap [Y_n, 2 Y_n]$ and set:*
$$C_Q(r) := \frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}_{W, Q}|^2} \sum_{(p_1, p_2) \in \mathcal{P}_{W, Q}^2} \frac{r \bmod L_Q(p_1, p_2)}{L_Q(p_1, p_2)}, \qquad F_{\mathrm{ramp}}(r) := \frac{4 Y_{\mathrm{rat}}}{|\binom{\mathcal{P}_W}{m_*}|} \sum_{A \in \binom{\mathcal{P}_W}{m_*}} \Big(r \bmod \prod_{p \in A} p\Big).$$
*Then clearing denominators of $F_{\mathrm{tot}}(r) := F_{\mathrm{near}}(r) + \sum_{Q \in \mathcal{Q}} \frac{16 M_0 K}{B_0} D_Q\, C_Q(r) + F_{\mathrm{ramp}}(r)$ via `exists_stencil_of_family` produces $(c, m)$ with $\mathrm{IsProducer}\,c\,m\,B_0$, $\mathrm{AlphabetLE}\,c\,K$, and*
$$\mathrm{rate}(c, m, B_0) \le 2 \left( Y_n (1 + \log B_0) + 8 Y_n \log K + \frac{\mathcal{M}_b}{B_0} + \frac{16 M_0 \cdot 2^{2 S + 1} \log K}{B_0} \sum_{Q \in \mathcal{Q}} D_Q \right).$$

*Proof.* By $S$-fold Bertrand's postulate on $(2^i W, 2^{i+1} W]$ ($0 \le i < S$), $|\mathcal{P}_W| \ge S = 16 M_0 + 4$, so $|\mathcal{P}_{W, Q}| \ge 16 M_0 + 3$ and $\binom{\mathcal{P}_W}{m_*}$ is nonempty. For every $A \in \binom{\mathcal{P}_W}{m_*}$, $M_A := \prod_{p \in A} p$ satisfies $B_0 < \frac{K}{2^{S(m_* + 1) + 1} W} < W^{m_*} < M_A \le (2^S W)^{m_*} \le K$, and for every $p_1 \in \mathcal{P}_{W, Q}$, $G_Q(p_1) := 2^{b_Q} p_1 Q > 2^{b_Q} W Q > \frac{K}{2^{2 S + 1} W} > B_0$.
We verify $F_{\mathrm{tot}}(r) \ge 0$ for all $r \in \mathbb{N}$ and $F_{\mathrm{tot}}(r) \ge 1$ for $1 \le r < B_0$:
- **$r = 0$:** $F_{\mathrm{tot}}(0) = 0$.
- **$1 \le r < B_0$:** Since $r < B_0 < M_A$, $r \bmod M_A = r$ for every $A$, so $F_{\mathrm{ramp}}(r) = 4 Y_{\mathrm{rat}} r \ge 4 Y_n r$, while $F_{\mathrm{near}}(r) \ge 1 - r Y_n$ and $C_Q(r) \ge 0$; hence $F_{\mathrm{tot}}(r) \ge 1 + 3 r Y_n \ge 1$.
- **$r \ge B_0$:** For each $Q \in \mathcal{Q}$, call $(p_1, p_2) \in \mathcal{P}_{W, Q}^2$ *small* if $r \bmod L_Q(p_1, p_2) < B_0$, and call $Q$ *aligned* if fewer than $\frac{1}{16 M_0} |\mathcal{P}_{W, Q}|^2$ pairs in $\mathcal{P}_{W, Q}^2$ are non-small.
  - If $Q$ is **not aligned**, at least $\frac{1}{16 M_0} |\mathcal{P}_{W, Q}|^2$ pairs have $\frac{r \bmod L}{L} \ge \frac{B_0}{K}$, so $\frac{16 M_0 K}{B_0} D_Q C_Q(r) \ge D_Q$. If no $Q \in \mathcal{Q}$ is aligned, $\sum_Q \frac{16 M_0 K}{B_0} D_Q C_Q(r) \ge \sum_Q D_Q \ge -F_{\mathrm{near}}(r)$, so $F_{\mathrm{tot}}(r) \ge 0$.
  - If $Q$ **is aligned**, let $\mathcal{R}_Q \subseteq \mathcal{P}_{W, Q}$ be the set of rows $p_1$ with $> \frac{1}{2} |\mathcal{P}_{W, Q}|$ small columns $p_2$. Counting non-small pairs gives $|\mathcal{P}_{W, Q} \setminus \mathcal{R}_Q| \cdot \frac{1}{2}|\mathcal{P}_{W, Q}| < \frac{1}{16 M_0}|\mathcal{P}_{W, Q}|^2$, so $|\mathcal{P}_W \setminus \mathcal{R}_Q| < \frac{1}{8 M_0}|\mathcal{P}_W| + 1$. Whenever $(p_1, p_2)$ is small, $r \bmod L_Q(p_1, p_2) < B_0 < \min(G_Q(p_1), G_Q(p_2))$ and $G_Q(p_i) \mid L_Q(p_1, p_2)$ force $r \bmod G_Q(p_1) = r \bmod L_Q(p_1, p_2) = r \bmod G_Q(p_2)$. Any two rows $p_1, p_1' \in \mathcal{R}_Q$ share a small column $p_2$ by pigeonhole, hence share a single value $h_Q := r \bmod G_Q(p_1) < B_0 \le r$. By `Nat.dvd_sub_mod`, $2^{b_Q} p_1 Q \mid (r - h_Q)$ for all $p_1 \in \mathcal{R}_Q$; in particular $2 \mid (r - h_Q)$ (since $b_Q \ge 1$), $Q \mid (r - h_Q)$, and $p \mid (r - h_Q)$ for all $p \in \mathcal{R}_Q$.
  - For any two aligned $Q_1, Q_2 \in \mathcal{Q}$, $|\mathcal{R}_{Q_1} \cap \mathcal{R}_{Q_2}| > (1 - \frac{1}{4 M_0})|\mathcal{P}_W| - 2 \ge \frac{3}{4}(16 M_0 + 4) - 2 = 12 M_0 + 1 > m_*$, so $\mathcal{R}_{Q_1} \cap \mathcal{R}_{Q_2}$ contains an $m_*$-subset $A$. By `Finset.prod_primes_dvd`, $M_A \mid (r - h_{Q_1})$ and $M_A \mid (r - h_{Q_2})$; since $h_{Q_i} < B_0 < M_A$ and $h_{Q_i} \le r$, $h_{Q_1} = r \bmod M_A = h_{Q_2}$.
  - Fixing an aligned $Q_0$ and setting $h := h_{Q_0} < B_0 \le r$, every $Q \in \mathcal{Q}$ with $Q \nmid (r - h)$ is unaligned, so $|F_{\mathrm{near}}(r) - F_{\mathrm{near}}(h)| \le \sum_{Q \nmid (r - h)} D_Q \le \sum_Q \frac{16 M_0 K}{B_0} D_Q C_Q(r)$. If $h = 0$, $F_{\mathrm{near}}(0) = 0$ gives $F_{\mathrm{tot}}(r) \ge 0$. If $1 \le h < B_0$, the number of $A \in \binom{\mathcal{P}_W}{m_*}$ not contained in $\mathcal{R}_{Q_0}$ is at most $|\mathcal{P}_W \setminus \mathcal{R}_{Q_0}| \binom{|\mathcal{P}_W| - 1}{m_* - 1} = \frac{|\mathcal{P}_W \setminus \mathcal{R}_{Q_0}| m_*}{|\mathcal{P}_W|} \binom{|\mathcal{P}_W|}{m_*} < (\frac{1}{8} + \frac{M_0}{16 M_0 + 4}) \binom{|\mathcal{P}_W|}{m_*} < \frac{1}{2} \binom{|\mathcal{P}_W|}{m_*}$. Every $A \subseteq \mathcal{R}_{Q_0}$ satisfies $M_A \mid (r - h)$, hence $r \bmod M_A = h$, so $F_{\mathrm{ramp}}(r) \ge 2 Y_{\mathrm{rat}} h \ge 2 Y_n h$, and $F_{\mathrm{tot}}(r) \ge F_{\mathrm{near}}(h) + 2 Y_n h \ge 1 + h Y_n \ge 0$.

Finally, evaluating the pronic measure on $F_{\mathrm{near}}$ up to $B_0$ gives $\mathrm{slope}(F_{\mathrm{near}}) \le 1 - \frac{1}{B_0} + Y_n(1 + \log B_0) + \frac{\mathcal{M}_b}{B_0}$, while $\log L_Q(p_1, p_2) \le \log K$ with $L_Q(p_1, p_2) > \frac{K}{2^{2 S + 1}}$ and $\log M_A \le \log K$ give the cluster and ramp slope bounds; `IsProducer.rate_le_of_slope_le` multiplies the excess by $2$. $\square$

### 4. Closing the Full Strip and Question 18

**Theorem 4.1 (Full-Scale Rate from a Zero-Free Half-Plane).** *Let $\Theta \in [\frac{1}{2}, 1)$ satisfy $\forall s \in \mathbb{C},\, \Theta < \Re s \to \zeta(s) \ne 0$. Then for every $a \in (0, 1 - \Theta)$, $\mathrm{HasRate}(K \mapsto K^{-a})$.*

*Proof.* Put $\delta := (1 - \Theta) - a \in (0, 1 - \Theta)$, $a' := (1 - \Theta) - \frac{\delta}{4} > a$, $\varepsilon := \frac{\delta}{64} > 0$, and $\tau_{\exp} := \frac{a'}{1 - \Theta - \varepsilon}$. Since $\varepsilon < \frac{\delta}{8}$ and $1 - \Theta - \varepsilon \le \frac{1}{2}$, we have $\tau_{\exp} = 1 - \frac{\delta / 4 - \varepsilon}{1 - \Theta - \varepsilon} \le 1 - \frac{\delta}{4} < 1$, while $\tau_{\exp} = \frac{a'}{1 - \Theta - \varepsilon} \ge 2 a'$. Choose $d := \lceil \frac{1}{1 - \Theta - \varepsilon} \rceil + 1 \ge 2$, and for large $K$ set $y := \lfloor K^{a'} \rfloor$ and $T := \lfloor \frac{1}{2} K^{\tau_{\exp}} \rfloor$.
1. Since $2 a' \le \tau_{\exp} \le a'(d - 1) < a'(d + 1)$ and $\tau_{\exp} < 1$, for all large $K$ we have $C_{d,0} y \le T \le y^{d + 1}$ and $2 T \le K$, so Proposition 2.4 applies on $[1, 2 T]$.
2. Because $\tau_{\exp}(1 - \Theta - \varepsilon) = a'$, we have $T^{-(1 - \Theta - \varepsilon)} \asymp K^{-a'} \asymp y^{-1}$, giving Mertens tracking error $\eta_0 = O(K^{-a'})$.
3. By the identity $\tau_{\exp} - a' = a'(\frac{1}{1 - \Theta - \varepsilon} - 1) = \tau_{\exp}(\Theta + \varepsilon)$ and $\tau_{\exp} \le 1 - \frac{\delta}{4}$, both $T / y$ and $T^{\Theta + \varepsilon}$ have exponent $\tau_{\exp}(\Theta + \varepsilon) \le (1 - \frac{\delta}{4})\Theta + \varepsilon \le \Theta - \frac{3\delta}{32}$, while $y^{1 + 2\varepsilon} T^{2(\Theta + \varepsilon) - 1}$ has exponent $a'(1 + 2\varepsilon) + \tau_{\exp}(2\Theta + 2\varepsilon - 1) \le (1 - \Theta - \frac{\delta}{4}) + (2\Theta - 1) + 4\varepsilon = \Theta - \frac{3\delta}{16}$. Hence $\mathcal{M}_b = O((1 + \log K)^3 K^{\Theta - \frac{3\delta}{32}})$.
4. Set $\eta := \frac{\delta}{32}$, $M_0 := \lceil \frac{1}{\eta} \rceil + 2$, $W := \lfloor K^\eta \rfloor$, and $X := K^2$. Then $B_0 \asymp K^{1 - \frac{\delta}{32}}$, $2^{2 S + 2} W^2(C_{d,1} y + 1) \asymp K^{1 - \Theta - \frac{3\delta}{16}} \ll K$, $(1 + \log K)^3 \mathcal{M}_b / B_0 = O((\log K)^6 K^{-(1 - \Theta) - \frac{\delta}{16}}) \ll K^{-a}$, and $Y_n \log K = O((\log K)^2 K^{-a - \frac{3\delta}{4}}) \ll K^{-a}$. Proposition 3.1 yields $(c, m, B_0)$ on $[1, K]$ with $\mathrm{rate}(c, m, B_0) \le K^{-a}$ for all sufficiently large $K$. $\square$

**Theorem 4.2 (Unconditional Exact Rate Law and Resolution of Question 18).**
1. *Unconditionally, $\lim_{K \to \infty} \frac{\log \mathrm{bestRate}(K)}{\log K} = \Theta_\zeta - 1$.*
2. *For every $\Theta \in [\frac{1}{2}, 1)$, $(\forall s \in \mathbb{C},\, \Theta < \Re s \to \zeta(s) \ne 0) \iff \forall a \in (0, 1 - \Theta),\, \mathrm{HasRate}(K^{-a})$.*
3. *$\mathrm{RiemannHypothesis} \iff \forall a \in (0, \frac{1}{2}),\, \mathrm{HasRate}(K^{-a})$.*
4. *For every $a_0 \in [0, \frac{1}{2})$ (and in particular $a_0 = \frac{1}{4}$, Question 18),*
   $$\Big(\exists a_* \in \left(a_0, \frac{1}{2}\right),\, \forall^\infty K \in \mathbb{N},\, \forall (c : \mathbb{N} \to_0 \mathbb{Z})\,(m\,B : \mathbb{N}),\, \mathrm{IsProducer}\,c\,m\,B \to \mathrm{AlphabetLE}\,c\,K \to K^{-a_*} < \mathrm{rate}(c, m, B)\Big) \iff \neg\,\mathrm{RiemannHypothesis}.$$

*Proof.*
(2) Forward is Theorem 4.1; converse is `re_le_of_hasRate_rpow_half` applied at $a = \frac{(1 - \Re s) + (1 - \Theta)}{2} \in (0, 1 - \Theta)$ for any hypothetical zero $s$ with $\Theta < \Re s < 1$.
(3) Specialize (2) to $\Theta = \frac{1}{2}$ via `riemannHypothesis_iff_riemannZeta_ne_zero_of_mem_upper_strip` and `riemannZeta_ne_zero_of_one_le_re`.
(1) Fix $\varepsilon > 0$. By Corollary 1.2 applied at $a = 1 - \Theta_\zeta + \varepsilon > 1 - \Theta_\zeta$, eventually $\mathrm{bestRate}(K) \ge K^{\Theta_\zeta - 1 - \varepsilon}$. For the upper bound, if $\Theta_\zeta - 1 + \varepsilon \ge 0$, `hasRate_vk` gives $\mathrm{bestRate}(K) \le 1 \le K^{\Theta_\zeta - 1 + \varepsilon}$ eventually; if $\Theta_\zeta - 1 + \varepsilon < 0$, then $\Theta_\zeta < 1$ and $a := 1 - \Theta_\zeta - \varepsilon \in (0, 1 - \Theta_\zeta)$, so Theorem 4.1 at $\Theta = \Theta_\zeta$ (`riemannZeta_ne_zero_of_zetaZeroAbscissa_lt`) gives $\mathrm{bestRate}(K) \le K^{-a} = K^{\Theta_\zeta - 1 + \varepsilon}$ eventually. Dividing logarithms by $\log K > 0$ ($K \ge 2$) proves (1).
(4) If `RiemannHypothesis` holds, (3) gives $\mathrm{HasRate}(K^{-a_*})$ for every $a_* \in (a_0, \frac{1}{2}) \subseteq (0, \frac{1}{2})$, refuting the eventual strict lower bound. Conversely, if `¬ RiemannHypothesis`, there is $\rho \in \mathbb{C}$ with $\zeta(\rho) = 0$ and $\frac{1}{2} < \Re \rho < 1$; taking $a_* := \frac{\max(a_0, 1 - \Re \rho) + 1/2}{2} \in (a_0, \frac{1}{2})$ gives $\Re \rho > 1 - a_*$, and Lemma 1.1 yields the eventual strict lower bound. $\square$
