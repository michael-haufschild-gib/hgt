## Exact statement

Let `I` be a finite set of physical qubits and let `r >= 1`.  On the
`r`-replica Hilbert--Schmidt operator space `V_r`, define the collective
one-site Pauli commutators

\[
 G_{i,a}^{(r)}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}\sigma_a^{(i,\ell)},
 \qquad
 A_{i,a}^{(r)}=\operatorname{ad}_{G_{i,a}^{(r)}}
 \quad(i\in I,\ a\in\{1,2,3\}).                    \tag{1}
\]

Let

\[
 \mathcal S_r=\bigcap_{i,a}\ker A_{i,a}^{(r)},
 \qquad
 W_r=\mathcal S_r^\perp,
 \qquad
 K_{1,r}=\sum_{i,a}\left(A_{i,a}^{(r)}\right)^2.   \tag{2}
\]

Then `W_r` is invariant under every `A_(i,a)^(r)`, the restriction of
`K_(1,r)` to `W_r` is positive definite, and

\[
 [K_{1,r},A_{i,a}^{(r)}]=0                         \tag{3}
\]

for every `i,a`.  Consequently the positive operator

\[
 R_r=\left(K_{1,r}|_{W_r}\right)^{-1/2}            \tag{4}
\]

commutes with every restricted generator.  The scaled family

\[
 B_{i,a}^{(r)}=R_rA_{i,a}^{(r)}|_{W_r}             \tag{5}
\]

is self-adjoint and exactly bi-tight:

\[
 \boxed{\qquad
 \sum_{i,a}(B_{i,a}^{(r)})^*B_{i,a}^{(r)}
 =\sum_{i,a}B_{i,a}^{(r)}(B_{i,a}^{(r)})^*
 =I_{W_r}.
 \qquad}                                           \tag{6}
\]

Therefore, for every complex linear subspace `U <= W_r`,

\[
 \boxed{\qquad
 \dim_{\mathbb C}\operatorname{span}
   \{A_{i,a}^{(r)}U:i\in I,\ a\in\{1,2,3\}\}
 \ge \dim_{\mathbb C}U.
 \qquad}                                           \tag{7}
\]

In particular, the full family of collective commutators generated by all
Pauli words of weight at most two satisfies the same inequality on every
subspace contained in `W_r`, since it contains the weight-one family.

Equation (7) holds at every finite replica order.  No bound depending on
`r`, no genericity hypothesis, and no spectral or asymptotic input is used.

## Proof

Every `G_(i,a)^(r)` is Hermitian.  Cyclicity of the Hilbert--Schmidt trace
therefore gives

\[
 \langle X,[G_{i,a}^{(r)},Y]\rangle_{\rm HS}
 =\langle[G_{i,a}^{(r)},X],Y\rangle_{\rm HS},       \tag{8}
\]

so every `A_(i,a)^(r)` is self-adjoint.  Operators at distinct physical
sites commute.  At one site the Pauli relations give

\[
 [A_{i,a}^{(r)},A_{i,b}^{(r)}]
 =2\mathrm i\sum_c\varepsilon_{abc}A_{i,c}^{(r)}.  \tag{9}
\]

Put `K_i=sum_a A_(i,a)^2`.  Using
`[XY,Z]=X[Y,Z]+[X,Z]Y`, equation (9), and antisymmetry under interchange of
`a,c`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
 [K_i,A_{i,b}]
 &=\sum_a\bigl(A_{i,a}[A_{i,a},A_{i,b}]
              +[A_{i,a},A_{i,b}]A_{i,a}\bigr)\\
 &=2\mathrm i\sum_{a,c}\varepsilon_{abc}
       (A_{i,a}A_{i,c}+A_{i,c}A_{i,a})=0.          \tag{10}
\end{aligned}
\]

The terms `K_j` for `j != i` commute with `A_(i,b)`, proving (3).
Moreover,

\[
 \langle X,K_{1,r}X\rangle
   =\sum_{i,a}\|A_{i,a}^{(r)}X\|_{\rm HS}^2.       \tag{11}
\]

Thus `ker K_(1,r)=S_r`.  Self-adjointness and (3) show that `S_r` and
`W_r` are invariant under every generator.  The restricted Casimir is
positive definite, so (4) exists and is invertible.  Finite-dimensional
spectral calculus applied to (3) gives
`[R_r,A_(i,a)^(r)]=0`.  Hence every `B_(i,a)^(r)` is self-adjoint and

\[
 \sum_{i,a}(B_{i,a}^{(r)})^2
 =R_r^2K_{1,r}|_{W_r}=I_{W_r},                     \tag{12}
\]

which proves (6).

It remains to prove the dimension statement without hiding an operator-
scaling theorem.  Let `u_1,...,u_d` be an orthonormal basis of `U`, and let
`v_1,...,v_e` be an orthonormal basis of

\[
 U'=\operatorname{span}\{B_{i,a}^{(r)}U:i,a\}.
\]

Using (6), Parseval inside `U'`, self-adjointness, and then Bessel,

\[
\begin{aligned}
 d
 &=\sum_{\alpha,i,a}\|B_{i,a}u_\alpha\|^2\\
 &=\sum_{\alpha,i,a,\beta}
      |\langle v_\beta,B_{i,a}u_\alpha\rangle|^2\\
 &=\sum_{\beta,i,a,\alpha}
      |\langle u_\alpha,B_{i,a}v_\beta\rangle|^2\\
 &\le\sum_{\beta,i,a}\|B_{i,a}v_\beta\|^2=e.     \tag{13}
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore `dim U' >= dim U`.  Finally,

\[
 \operatorname{span}\{B_{i,a}U\}_{i,a}
 =R_r\operatorname{span}\{A_{i,a}U\}_{i,a}.        \tag{14}
\]

Since `R_r` is invertible on `W_r`, the two spans have the same dimension.
This proves (7).

## Consequence for the master frontier

The growing-replica obstruction is not spread over the entire
Hilbert--Schmidt space.  It is confined to subspaces with a nonzero component
in the sitewise singlet algebra `S_r`.  On `W_r`, the weight-one Pauli family
already has exact Hall expansion at every replica order.

For `r=1`, `S_1` is the scalar identity sector, so this recovers and
strictly generalizes the support-block scaling used in
`one-replica-local-pauli-planar-cavity-decay.md`.  For `r=2`, `S_2` is the
tensor product of the local identity/swap planes.  The existing two-replica
edge theorem proves that connected weight-two interactions reduce their
common kernel to the identity and global-swap modes.  What remains is the
stronger Hall law on that singlet sector and a theorem gluing it to (7) for
arbitrary mixed subspaces.

## Hostile audit

- Common-kernel positivity is used only to define an invertible Casimir on
  `W_r`.  Hall expansion follows from the stronger central-Casimir identity
  (12), which the Hermitian-star counterexample does not satisfy.
- The theorem does not prove Hall expansion for arbitrary subspaces of the
  quotient by global replica permutations.  A subspace may mix `S_r` and
  `W_r`; separate inequalities on both summands would not by themselves
  prevent their output spans from overlapping.
- The theorem does not assert that connected weight-two interactions are
  rank non-decreasing on `S_r`.  Their synchronized common kernel is weaker.
- No long-time cavity decay, circuit anti-concentration, complexity growth,
  plateau, or recurrence-time estimate follows from (7) alone.
- This bridge is representation-theoretic infrastructure.  No standalone
  novelty claim is made before external priority review.
