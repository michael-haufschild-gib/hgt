## 1. Definition

Let \(k\) be a field, let \(V\) be a \(k\)-vector space, and order
\(\operatorname{Sub}(V)\) by inclusion. Write

\[
 \mathsf{ExFlag}(V)
 =\{(B,Z)\in\operatorname{Sub}(V)^2:B\subseteq Z\}.
\tag{1}
\]

Give the boundary coordinate the **lower topology** and the cycle coordinate the **Scott
topology**. Equivalently, their respective generating tests are

\[
 N_v^- =\{B:v\notin B\},
 \qquad
 N_v^+ =\{Z:v\in Z\}
 \qquad(v\in V).
\tag{2}
\]

The **provenance-exactness topology** \(\tau_{\rm prov}\) on \(\mathsf{ExFlag}(V)\) is the subspace
topology inherited from

\[
 \operatorname{Sub}(V)_{\rm lower}
 \times
 \operatorname{Sub}(V)_{\rm Scott}.
\tag{3}
\]

For \(v\in V\), define its framed survival locus

\[
 \mathcal U_v
 =\{(B,Z):v\in Z\text{ and }v\notin B\}.
\tag{4}
\]

## 2. Main theorem

[PROVED HERE] [PROVENANCE_EXACTNESS_FLAG_TOPOLOGY]

For every field \(k\) and vector space \(V\), the following hold.

1. The descriptions in (2) generate exactly the lower topology on the boundary coordinate and
   the Scott topology on the cycle coordinate. Hence (3) is the coarsest topology on
   \(\mathsf{ExFlag}(V)\) making every positive cycle test \(v\in Z\) and every negative boundary
   test \(v\notin B\) open.

2. The space \(\mathsf{ExFlag}(V)_{\rm prov}\) is \(T_0\). Its specialization order is

   \[
    (B,Z)\preceq(B',Z')
    \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    B'\subseteq B\subseteq Z\subseteq Z'.
   \tag{5}
   \]

   Thus specialization decreases boundary range and increases cycle space.

3. Every \(\mathcal U_v\) is open, and

   \[
    (B,Z)\in\mathcal U_v
    \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    v\text{ represents a nonzero class in }Z/B.
   \tag{6}
   \]

   If \(V\) is finite-dimensional, then homology dimension is monotone along (5):

   \[
    (B,Z)\preceq(B',Z')
    \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
    \dim(Z/B)\leq\dim(Z'/B').
   \tag{7}
   \]

4. Let

   \[
    \pi_Z:\mathsf{ExFlag}(V)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sub}(V),
    \qquad (B,Z)\longmapsto Z.
   \tag{8}
   \]

   Every invariant, topology, or reporter factoring through \(\pi_Z\) is constant on the whole
   fibre \(\{(B,Z):B\subseteq Z\}\). In particular it cannot decide whether a specified
   \(v\in Z\) is a boundary. If \(B\subsetneq Z\) and \(v\in Z\setminus B\), then
   \(\mathcal U_v\) separates \((B,Z)\) from the falsely exact flag \((Z,Z)\).

5. For a linear map \(f:V\to W\), inverse image defines a continuous map

   \[
    f^*:\mathsf{ExFlag}(W)_{\rm prov}\longrightarrow
    \mathsf{ExFlag}(V)_{\rm prov},
    \qquad
    (B,Z)\longmapsto(f^{-1}B,f^{-1}Z).
   \tag{9}
   \]

   Direct image

   \[
    f_!:(B,Z)\longmapsto(f(B),f(Z))
   \tag{10}
   \]

   is continuous when \(f\) is injective, but is not continuous in general. More precisely, if
   \(k\) is infinite and \(p:k^2\to k\) is a nonzero projection, then \(p_!\) is not continuous.
   Thus source range formation has genuinely different variance from constraint pullback.

## 3. Proof

The lattice \(\operatorname{Sub}(V)\) is algebraic. Its compact elements are exactly the
finite-dimensional subspaces. Indeed, if a finite-dimensional \(K\) is contained in the union of
a directed family of subspaces, a finite basis of \(K\) is contained in finitely many family
members, hence in one common upper member. Conversely, an infinite-dimensional subspace is the
directed union of its finite-dimensional proper subspaces and is therefore not compact.

The lower topology of a poset is generated by complements of principal upper sets. For every
subspace \(K\subseteq V\),

\[
 \operatorname{Sub}(V)\setminus\uparrow K
 =\{B:K\nsubseteq B\}
 =\bigcup_{v\in K}\{B:v\notin B\}.
\tag{11}
\]

Conversely \(N_v^-\) is the complement of \(\uparrow kv\). Therefore the \(N_v^-\) generate
exactly the lower topology.

If \(U\) is Scott-open and \(Z\in U\), write \(Z\) as the directed union of its finite-dimensional
subspaces. Scott inaccessibility gives a finite-dimensional \(K\subseteq Z\) lying in \(U\).
Since \(U\) is upward closed, \(\uparrow K\subseteq U\). If \(v_1,\ldots,v_r\) is a basis of \(K\),

\[
 \uparrow K=\bigcap_{i=1}^r N_{v_i}^+.
\tag{12}
\]

Every \(N_v^+=\uparrow kv\) is Scott-open because \(kv\) is compact. Thus the \(N_v^+\) generate
the Scott topology. Equations (11)--(12) prove item 1.

In lower topology, every open is downward closed. Generating tests show its specialization order
is reverse inclusion: every lower-open neighbourhood of \(B\) contains \(B'\) exactly when
\(B'\subseteq B\). In Scott topology specialization order is inclusion: every Scott-open
neighbourhood of \(Z\) contains \(Z'\) exactly when \(Z\subseteq Z'\). Product and subspace
specialization therefore give (5). If two flags have different cycle coordinates, a test
\(N_v^+\) distinguishes them in one direction; if their boundary coordinates differ, a test
\(N_v^-\) does. Hence the space is \(T_0\), proving item 2.

By (2),

\[
 \mathcal U_v
 =\mathsf{ExFlag}(V)\cap(N_v^-\times N_v^+),
\tag{13}
\]

so it is open. The class of \(v\) belongs to \(Z/B\) precisely when \(v\in Z\), and that class is
zero precisely when \(v\in B\). This proves (6). Under (5), finite-dimensionality gives

\[
 \dim(Z'/B')-\dim(Z/B)
 =\dim(Z'/Z)+\dim(B/B')\geq0,
\tag{14}
\]

which proves (7) and item 3.

Item 4 follows formally from factorization through (8): two flags with the same \(Z\) have the
same image. When \(v\in Z\setminus B\), formula (6) places \((B,Z)\) in \(\mathcal U_v\), while
\((Z,Z)\notin\mathcal U_v\).

For item 5, pull back the generators. For every \(v\in V\),

\[
 (f^*)^{-1}\{(B,Z):v\notin B\}
 =\{(B,Z):f(v)\notin B\},
\qquad
 (f^*)^{-1}\{(B,Z):v\in Z\}
 =\{(B,Z):f(v)\in Z\}.
\tag{15}
\]

These are generating opens, with the evident empty-set interpretation when \(f(v)=0\). Hence
\(f^*\) is continuous.

If \(f\) is injective, every \(w\in f(V)\) has a unique preimage. The inverse image under \(f_!\)
of a boundary nonmembership test or cycle membership test at \(w\) is the corresponding test at
that unique preimage; for \(w\notin f(V)\), the two inverse images are respectively the whole space
and the empty set. Thus \(f_!\) is continuous.

It remains to prove failure for a projection. Let \(K=\ker p\), choose \(1\in k\setminus\{0\}\),
and consider the target open

\[
 \mathcal O=\{(B',Z'):1\notin B'\}.
\tag{16}
\]

Its inverse image under \(p_!\) is

\[
 p_!^{-1}(\mathcal O)=\{(B,Z):B\subseteq K\}.
\tag{17}
\]

The flag \((K,k^2)\) lies in (17). A basic neighbourhood of this flag imposes finitely many
lower-open exclusions on \(B\) and finitely many Scott membership conditions on \(Z\). Keep
\(Z=k^2\). Because \(k\) is infinite, \(k^2\) has infinitely many lines. Choose a line
\(L\neq K\) avoiding the finitely many excluded lines. Then \((L,k^2)\) remains in the basic
neighbourhood, but \(p(L)=k\), so it does not lie in (17). Therefore (17) is not open and
\(p_!\) is not continuous. This completes the proof.

\[
 \boxed{

 \text{exchange geometry}=Z,
 \qquad
 \text{Fourier/spectrum}=Z/B.

\]

This is not a relabelling of a chain complex. Representation object is the *varying flag* with
