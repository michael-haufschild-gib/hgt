## Statement

Let \(A\) be an associative algebra and let
\[
 T,F:\mathbb N_{>0}\longrightarrow A
\]
be multiplicative families:
\[
 T_{mn}=T_mT_n,\qquad F_{mn}=F_mF_n.                                \tag{1}
\]
Set
\[
 \Delta_n=T_n-F_n.                                                  \tag{2}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{\Delta_{mn}=T_m\Delta_n+\Delta_mF_n}.                        \tag{3}
\]

For Deninger's diagonal localization,
\[
 T_n=\tau_{\log n},\qquad F_n=F_n^*,
\]
so `(3)` says that the relation for a composite power belongs to the
two-sided module generated by the relations of its factors.  Induction on
the number of prime factors gives
\[
 \Delta_n\in
 \sum_{p\mid n}A\,\Delta_p\,A.                                      \tag{4}
\]
Thus prime-indexed relations generate every positive-rational suspension
relation after also allowing inverses in the Frobenius colimit.

## Proof

Using `(1)` and `(2)`,
\[
\begin{aligned}
T_m\Delta_n+\Delta_mF_n
 &=T_m(T_n-F_n)+(T_m-F_m)F_n\\
 &=T_mT_n-F_mF_n\\
 &=T_{mn}-F_{mn}\\
 &=\Delta_{mn}.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves `(3)`.  Repeatedly factor \(n\) by a prime and apply `(3)`;
the induction proves `(4)`.  No commutativity between \(T_m\) and \(F_n\)
is needed for `(3)`.

## Consequence and limit

Composite powers add no new **generators** to a hypothetical arithmetic
relation module.  This closes only its algebraic
independence-of-prime-factorization question.

It does not prove that all higher syzygies disappear after analytic
completion.  The equality
\[
\Delta_{mn}
=T_m\Delta_n+\Delta_mF_n
=T_n\Delta_m+\Delta_nF_m
\]
itself supplies prime--prime syzygies when the two multiplicative
families commute internally.  This is why
`DAC_DIAGONAL_ARITHMETIC_LOCALIZATION_DESCENT` was retired: no
source-defined theorem identifies its two-term quotient with Deninger's
coarse invariant sheaf.  The theorem survives as an exact algebraic
no-go diagnostic.
