## Arbitrary-orientation theorem

Fix `H>0`, put `B=C/(H Z)`, and let `G_H^+-` be the multiplicative group generated by nonzero DA
meromorphic functions and finite products

```text
product_j Gamma((epsilon_j s+b_j)/H)^(m_j),
epsilon_j in {+1,-1},  m_j in Z.                           (1)
```

Repeated factors are combined, so write the positive- and negative-orientation exponent vectors
as

```text
p,n in directSum_(omega in B) Z.                           (2)
```

Let

```text
r([b])=[H-b]=[-b] in B                                    (3)
```

and define the **oriented charge**

```text
q=p-r(n).                                                  (4)
```

Then:

1. `q` is a surjective group homomorphism;
2. `q(G)=0` exactly when `G` is differentially algebraic;
3. if `q(G) != 0`, then `G` is hypertranscendental;
4. hence

   ```text
   G_H^+- / (G_H^+- intersect Mer_DA^x)
     ~= directSum_(omega in B) Z;                          (5)
   ```

5. two orientation data `(p,n)` and `(p',n')` have equal charge exactly when their difference is
   creation of a reflected neutral pair:

   ```text
   p-p'=r(g),  n-n'=g                                     (6)
   ```

   for one finitely supported `g`.

Thus negative slopes create no second independent lattice. They are charge-conjugate
antiparticles of the positive-slope sectors.

## Proof

Euler reflection gives, for `z=(-s+b)/H`,

```text
Gamma(z)=pi/(sin(pi z) Gamma(1-z)).                        (7)
```

Therefore

```text
Gamma((-s+b)/H)^m
 = (pi/sin(pi(-s+b)/H))^m
   Gamma((s+H-b)/H)^(-m).                                 (8)
```

The trigonometric factor in `(8)` is nonzero meromorphic and DA: affine sine is a two-term
exponential sum, and the DA meromorphic field is closed under products and inverses.  Every
negative factor is consequently a DA gauge times the inverse positive factor on the reflected
orbit. Applying `(8)` to all negative factors gives

```text
G = A_DA product_(omega in B) Gamma((s+b_omega)/H)^(q_omega), (9)
```

where `q` is `(4)`. The positive-slope orbit-charge phase theorem now says that `(9)` is DA exactly
when `q=0` and HT otherwise. Surjectivity is realized by positive factors. This proves `(1)--(5)`.

For `(6)`, equality

```text
p-r(n)=p'-r(n')                                           (10)
```

is equivalent to

```text
p-p'=r(n-n').                                             (11)
```

Take `g=n-n'`. Conversely `(6)` makes the two sides of `(10)` equal.  The stronger normal form is

```text
(p,n)=(q,0)+(r(n),n),                                     (12)
```

so every datum is its surviving oriented charge plus one neutral reflected pair. This coefficient
theorem is formalized in Lean.

Arbitrary finite products with nonzero commensurable real slopes reduce to `(1)`: Gauss
multiplication first replaces every absolute slope by the common magnitude `1/H`, accumulating
only exponential and constant DA factors; `(8)` then removes all negative orientations. Thus the
same theorem covers the full signed-slope commensurable family.

## Reflection and the exact doubling law

Fix a reflection center `c` and let `J_c G(s)=G(c-s)`. On orbit labels put

```text
rho_c([b])=[H-c-b].                                       (13)
```

This is an involution. Equation `(8)` gives the exact action on the quotient `(5)`:

```text
q(J_c G)=-rho_c(q(G)).                                    (14)
```

Suppose `G` is reflection-symmetric modulo DA, meaning

```text
G(c-s)/G(s) is DA and nonzero.                             (15)
```

Equations `(5)` and `(14)` give

```text
q=-rho_c(q).                                               (16)
```

If `rho_c(omega)=omega`, then `(16)` gives `2q_omega=0`, hence `q_omega=0`. If `q_omega != 0`, its
partner is distinct and

```text
q_(rho_c(omega))=-q_omega != 0.                            (17)
```

Therefore every nonzero symmetric sector has support cardinality at least two. This is sharp: for
every nonfixed `omega` and every `m != 0`,

```text
q=m e_omega-m e_(rho_c(omega))                             (18)
```

is fixed by charge conjugation, is realized by a ratio of two positive-slope Gamma factors, and is
HT. By `(5)` and `(14)` its reflection quotient is DA. Reflection symmetry therefore does not
annihilate the HT phase; it forces exact conjugate doubling.
