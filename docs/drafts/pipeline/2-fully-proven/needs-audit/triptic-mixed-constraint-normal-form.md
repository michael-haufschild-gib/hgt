# Triptic Mixed-Constraint Normal Form

- **ID:** `triptic-mixed-constraint-normal-form`
- **Stage:** 2-fully-proven / needs independent audit
- **Type:** THEOREM (paper) / SUPPORTING CLASSIFICATION
- **Scientific status:** `[BRIDGE]` — the one-orientation normal form is a routine principal-part consequence once the elliptic-gamma atomic-defect theorem is granted; the nonroutine surviving residue is isolated in `triptic-orbit-residue-reciprocity`
- **Statement:** For generic `1,tau,sigma`, every meromorphic `1`-periodic solution of the bare constraint `Delta_tau Delta_sigma F in C` has, modulo differentially algebraic functions, a unique finite constant-coefficient differential-translation normal form generated by the elliptic-gamma logarithmic derivative. The whole quotient class is a free rank-one module over the differential translation algebra, and every member has an exact DA annihilator, translation spectrum, and finite-defect differential-rank formula.

## Medalist lens

The named elliptic gamma function is not the natural object. The natural object is the solution class of the constraint

`Delta_1 F=0`, `Delta_tau Delta_sigma F in C`.

The theorem below says that, after quotienting out every differentially algebraic meromorphic contribution, this class has one atomic generator and no hidden relations. Thus the constraint does not propagate a function by a flow; it fixes a finite differential-translation divisor carried by a single hypertranscendental atom.

## Setup

Let `tau,sigma in H` and assume

`1,tau,sigma`

are linearly independent over `Q`. Put

`H_3=Z+Z tau+Z sigma`, `G=C/H_3`,

and let `pi:C -> G` be the quotient map. Let `delta=d/dz`, and write `T_a` for translation through `a`.

Let

`Psi(z)=delta log Gamma(z;tau,sigma)`

be the additive elliptic-gamma logarithmic derivative. Choose the standard theta logarithmic derivatives so that

`Delta_sigma Psi=lambda_tau`, `Delta_tau lambda_tau=kappa_tau in C*`,

`Delta_tau Psi=lambda_sigma`, `Delta_sigma lambda_sigma=kappa_sigma in C*`.

They are normalized to have residue `1` at their unique pole class on the corresponding elliptic curve. Both `lambda_tau` and `lambda_sigma` are DA over `C(z)`: their derivatives are elliptic functions.

The parent card `elliptic-gamma-rank-three-da-spectrum` proves that `Psi` has the `H_3`-atomic defect property: defects belonging to distinct nonzero classes of `G` are differentially algebraically independent, zero-class defects are DA, and same-class defects differ by DA functions.

Let `Mer_DA` be the field of meromorphic functions on `C` that are differentially algebraic over `C(z)`.

Define the differential translation algebra

`A=C[delta][G]=C[delta] tensor_C C[G]`.

Translations commute with `delta`, so this is an ordinary commutative group algebra over the polynomial ring `C[delta]`. Its elements have finite `G`-support.

For

`D=sum_(g in G) L_g(delta)[g] in A`,

choose lifts `a_g in C` and set

`D Psi=sum_g L_g(delta) Psi(z+a_g)`.

Changing a lift by an element of `H_3` changes this function by a DA function. Hence the class `[D Psi]` in `Mer/Mer_DA` is well defined.

## Differential regular-module theorem `[PROVED HERE]`

The map

`Phi:A -> Mer/Mer_DA`, `D mapsto [D Psi]`,

is injective.

Equivalently, the complete differential-translation orbit of `Psi` is a free cyclic `A`-module modulo DA functions. In particular, no nonzero finite constant-coefficient relation involving arbitrary derivatives and arbitrary translates of `Psi` can land in the DA field.

### Proof

Let

`epsilon:A -> C[delta]`, `epsilon(D)=sum_g L_g(delta)`,

be augmentation.

First suppose `epsilon(D)=0`. Taking `0 in G` as base point gives

`D Psi=sum_(g != 0) L_g(delta)(Psi(z+a_g)-Psi(z))`

modulo DA functions. The defects indexed by the finitely many distinct nonzero `g` are differentially algebraically independent. A nonzero differential-linear combination of differential indeterminates is differentially transcendental: substituting it into any nonzero one-variable differential polynomial produces a nonzero differential polynomial in the independent coordinates. Therefore `D Psi` can be DA only when every `L_g` is zero, hence only when `D=0`.

Now suppose `epsilon(D) != 0` and `D != 0`. The translation stabilizer of a nonzero finite-support labelled divisor `D` in `G` is finite: it acts freely on the finite support. Since `G` is infinite, choose `q in G` with `t_qD != D`. If `D Psi` were DA, then its `q`-translation difference

`(t_q-1)D Psi`

would be DA. But `(t_q-1)D` is nonzero and has zero augmentation, contradicting the first case. Thus `Phi` is injective.

This strictly strengthens the constant group-algebra freeness in `translation-superposition-boundary-calculus`: derivatives are now part of the coefficient ring, not merely operations available after the classification.

## The mixed-constraint class

Define

`Q_(tau,sigma)={F in Mer(C): Delta_1 F=0 and Delta_tau Delta_sigma F in C}`.

It is stable under addition, arbitrary translation, and differentiation. Put

`Qbar_(tau,sigma)=Q_(tau,sigma)/(Q_(tau,sigma) intersect Mer_DA)`.

Every `D Psi` belongs to `Q_(tau,sigma)` modulo a DA correction, and in fact belongs to it exactly: translations preserve the constraint, while differentiation kills its constant right side.

## Universal normal-form theorem `[PROVED HERE]`

The map `Phi` restricts to an isomorphism of `A`-modules

`A isomorphic to Qbar_(tau,sigma)`.

Therefore every `F in Q_(tau,sigma)` has a unique element

`D_F in A`

such that

`F-D_F Psi is DA over C(z)`.

Call `D_F` the **triptic constraint divisor** of `F`.

### Proof of existence

Let

`U=Delta_sigma F`.

Then

`Delta_1 U=0`, `Delta_tau U=c in C`.

Thus the poles of `U` are periodic under the lattice

`Lambda_tau=Z+Z tau`.

There are only finitely many pole classes in a compact fundamental parallelogram.

The function `lambda_tau` has one simple pole of nonzero residue modulo `Lambda_tau`. For every pole class `p` of `U` and every principal-part order `k>=1`, a suitable constant multiple of

`delta^(k-1) lambda_tau(z-p)`

has precisely the required order-`k` principal part at `p`: differentiating the simple pole gives a nonzero pure pole of order `k`. A finite sum

`V=sum_(p,k) c_(p,k) delta^(k-1) lambda_tau(z-p)`

therefore matches every principal part of `U` modulo `Lambda_tau`.

The difference `U-V` is entire, `1`-periodic, and has constant `tau`-increment. Its derivative is an entire elliptic function for `Lambda_tau`, hence constant. Since `U-V` is `1`-periodic, that constant is zero. Therefore

`U=V+b`

for some `b in C`.

Replace each `lambda_tau(z-p)` in `V` by `Delta_sigma Psi(z-p)`. This produces a finite differential-translation operator `D in A` such that

`Delta_sigma(F-D Psi)=b`.

The DA function

`q=(b/kappa_sigma) lambda_sigma`

is `1`-periodic and satisfies `Delta_sigma q=b`. Hence

`W=F-D Psi-q`

has periods `1` and `sigma`. It is elliptic and therefore DA over `C(z)`. Since `q` is DA, this proves

`F-D Psi in Mer_DA`.

### Proof of uniqueness

If both `D` and `D'` represent `F` modulo DA, then `(D-D')Psi` is DA. The differential regular-module theorem gives `D=D'`.

No separate finite-pole hypothesis was used: meromorphy plus the quasi-periodic constraint makes the pole set finite modulo `Lambda_tau` automatically.

## Intrinsic orbit-principal-part formula `[PROVED HERE]`

The constraint divisor can be recovered from `F` without first mentioning elliptic gamma.

Choose representatives `p` of the pole classes of

`U=Delta_sigma F`

on `E_tau=C/Lambda_tau`, and write the principal part at `p` as

`PP_p(U)=sum_(k>=1) a_(p,k)(z-p)^(-k)`.

Normalize the polynomial

`L_p(X)=sum_(k>=1) a_(p,k) X^(k-1)/((-1)^(k-1)(k-1)!)`.

Since `delta^(k-1)lambda_tau(z-p)` has leading principal part

`(-1)^(k-1)(k-1)!(z-p)^(-k)`,

the normal-form proof gives the explicit formula

`D_F=sum_p L_p(delta)[-p+H_3] in C[delta][C/H_3]`.

If different pole classes on `E_tau` belong to the same `sigma`-orbit, their polynomials add. Thus `D_F` is precisely the finite orbit-sum of the labelled principal-part divisor of `Delta_sigma F` under the non-torsion translation by `sigma`.

Changing the fundamental parallelogram adds an element of `Lambda_tau subset H_3` and leaves the formula unchanged. Interchanging `tau` and `sigma` yields the same divisor by uniqueness of the normal form. Hence the invariant is native to the mixed constraint, not to a chosen theta decomposition.

In particular,

`F is DA iff every labelled orbit-sum of PP(Delta_sigma F) vanishes`.

This is a complete, directly readable DA obstruction for the whole class. It is stronger than the ordinary elliptic summability obstruction: it classifies the primitive `F`, not merely whether its quasi-elliptic increment is a rational coboundary.

## Exact DA criterion and hypertranscendence `[PROVED HERE]`

For `F in Q_(tau,sigma)`,

`F is DA over C(z) iff D_F=0`.

Every member with nonzero constraint divisor is hypertranscendental. Thus the quotient does not merely provide a normal form; it separates the DA and HT strata exactly.

## Complete differential-translation annihilator `[PROVED HERE]`

For every `F in Q_(tau,sigma)` and `P in A`,

`P F is DA iff P D_F=0 in A`.

Consequently

`Ann_DA^A(F)=Ann_A(D_F)`.

This includes differential operators, arbitrary finite translation superpositions, and their mixtures. It is stronger than a calculation of a translation spectrum or a list of visible functional equations.

### Proof

Modulo DA functions, `F=D_F Psi`. Hence `PF=(PD_F)Psi` modulo DA. Injectivity of `Phi` gives the equivalence.

## Exact translation spectrum `[PROVED HERE]`

For `D_F != 0`,

`Spec_DA(F)={h in C:Delta_h F is DA}`

is

`pi^(-1)(Stab_G(D_F))`,

where

`Stab_G(D_F)={q in G:t_qD_F=D_F}`.

The stabilizer is finite. Therefore every non-DA function in the mixed-constraint class has a DA spectrum that is a finite extension of the rank-three group `H_3`.

Conversely, torsion-orbit divisors realize every finite subgroup of `G`; hence every finite-index overgroup of `H_3` occurs as the exact spectrum of a member of `Q_(tau,sigma)`.

## Differential boundary-rank theorem `[PROVED HERE]`

Let `F in Q_(tau,sigma)` and let `h_1,...,h_m in C`. Put `q_i=pi(h_i)`. Then

`dtrdeg_(C(z)) C(z)<Delta_(h_1)F,...,Delta_(h_m)F>_delta`

equals

`rank_(C(delta)) {(t_(q_i)-1)D_F : 1<=i<=m}`

inside the vector space

`C(delta) tensor_(C[delta]) A = C(delta)[G]`.

Thus the finite-defect geometry is not merely the constant boundary-vector matroid of a translate divisor. It is the linear matroid over the rational differential-operator field `C(delta)` represented by the differential boundary vectors.

### Proof

Every boundary `(t_q-1)D_F` has zero augmentation. Choose the finitely many nonzero `G`-coordinates occurring in the boundaries. Modulo DA corrections, the corresponding defects of `F` are obtained by applying a matrix over `C[delta]` to a finite tuple of differentially algebraically independent atomic defects of `Psi`.

For a matrix `M` over the Euclidean domain `C[delta]`, the differential transcendence degree of `M y`, where the entries of `y` are differential indeterminates, is `rank_(C(delta)) M`. Indeed, Smith normal form reduces `M`, by invertible row and column operations over `C[delta]`, to a diagonal matrix with `r` nonzero diagonal differential operators. The operations preserve differential transcendence degree. A nonzero constant-coefficient differential operator applied to one differential indeterminate remains differentially transcendental, and outputs arising from distinct coordinates remain differentially independent. Hence the degree is the number `r` of nonzero diagonal entries.

Applying this lemma to the boundary matrix proves the formula. Adjoining the finitely many DA lift corrections does not change differential transcendence degree.

## Constraint-first interpretation

The theorem identifies three layers that are invisible if one treats the elliptic gamma function merely as a special function.

1. **Constraint class.** `Q_(tau,sigma)` is defined without naming gamma.
2. **Atomic carrier.** The quotient by DA functions has one generator, `[Psi]`.
3. **Native invariant.** A function is classified by a finite labelled divisor `D_F` in a differential group algebra; its annihilator, spectrum, and defect matroid are algebraic shadows of that divisor.

The object is therefore fixed by its mixed constraint and finite principal-part data, not propagated by a differential flow.

## Attacks and boundary cases

1. **One-pole collapse.** If `U=Delta_sigma F` has one simple pole, the normal form reduces to a scalar translate of `Psi` plus a DA function. This recovers the collapse in `paired-quasi-elliptic-constraint-rigidity`; that special case is not new.
2. **Multiple poles.** Distinct pole classes and higher orders produce genuinely different elements of `C[delta][G]`, so the full class does not collapse to scalar-affine copies of one function.
3. **DA cancellations.** Principal parts separated by an element of `H_3` can cancel in `D_F`; this correctly detects DA examples such as theta logarithmic derivatives obtained as structural differences of `Psi`.
4. **Dependent periods.** If `1,tau,sigma` are rationally dependent, joint constants and translation cosets change, and the atomic theorem used for injectivity is unavailable.
5. **Meromorphic hypothesis.** The finite principal-part normal form uses meromorphy on `C`. Essential singularities or nonmeromorphic distributions lie outside the class.
6. **Constant coefficients.** `A` uses constant-coefficient differential operators. Allowing arbitrary DA coefficient functions requires a parameterized module theory not proved here.
7. **Orientation.** Interchanging `tau` and `sigma` gives the same quotient normal form. The proof uses one orientation only to choose which theta principal parts to match.

## Parent-theory and literature audit

### Classical ingredients

- theta logarithmic derivatives provide universal principal parts for quasi-elliptic meromorphic functions;
- Mittag--Leffler/Riemann--Roch reasoning on an elliptic curve gives the finite principal-part decomposition;
- elliptic functions are DA;
- Smith normal form over `C[delta]` gives the rank lemma.

These ingredients alone do not prove injectivity, distinguish the DA and HT strata, or compute annihilators. The nonroutine input is the `H_3`-atomic defect theorem for `Psi`.

### Strong adjacent parent: the gamma gerbe

Felder--Henriques--Rossi--Zhu interpret elliptic gamma functions as a meromorphic section of a gerbe on the universal triptic curve and compute its analytic and multiplicative cohomology. Their work explains the rank-three cocycle and is a serious parent-theory threat. It does not state the additive mixed-constraint quotient above, differential-algebraic freeness, a differential group-algebra normal form, exact DA annihilators, or the `C(delta)` boundary-rank formula.

### Other parents checked

- Felder--Varchenko: elliptic-gamma functional, modular, and multiplication identities;
- Ruijsenaars: first-order analytic difference equations and minimal solutions;
- Hardouin--Singer and later elliptic difference Galois theory: differential relations for solutions of fixed operator equations;
- Kato: differential algebraicity for rational periods;
- Nishizawa/Narukawa/Winding: multiple elliptic gamma hierarchies, modular relations, and cone generalizations.

Preliminary exact/equivalent searches through 2026 located no theorem identifying

`Q_(tau,sigma)/(DA)=C[delta][C/H_3]`,

nor the resulting annihilator, spectrum, and differential boundary-rank classification.

The normal form, abstract freeness, and rank deductions are `[BRIDGE]` once atomic defect independence is assumed.
