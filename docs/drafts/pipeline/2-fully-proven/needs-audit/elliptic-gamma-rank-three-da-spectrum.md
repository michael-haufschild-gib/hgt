# Elliptic Gamma Rank-Three DA Translation Spectrum

- **Statement:** For multiplicatively independent elliptic nomes, the elliptic gamma function is hypertranscendental; its additive logarithmic-derivative defects and multiplicative shift quotients have exact DA spectrum equal to the rank-three period group; and the differential transcendence degree of any finite defect or quotient family is the number of distinct nonzero shift cosets.

## Setup

Let `tau, sigma in H` and assume

`1, tau, sigma` are linearly independent over `Q`.

Write

`Lambda_tau = Z + Z tau`, `Lambda_sigma = Z + Z sigma`,

and let `Gamma(z; tau, sigma)` be the additive elliptic gamma function. Its standard functional equations are

`Gamma(z+1)=Gamma(z)`,

`Gamma(z+tau)=theta_sigma(z) Gamma(z)`,

`Gamma(z+sigma)=theta_tau(z) Gamma(z)`.

Put

`Psi(z) = d/dz log Gamma(z; tau, sigma)`

and

`lambda_omega(z)=d/dz log theta_omega(z)`.

Then

`Delta_1 Psi=0`, `Delta_tau Psi=lambda_sigma`, `Delta_sigma Psi=lambda_tau`.

Each `lambda_omega` is DA over `C(z)`: its derivative is `Lambda_omega`-elliptic, and every elliptic function is DA.

## Main theorem `[PROVED HERE]`

Let

`H = Z + Z tau + Z sigma`.

Then

`{h in C : Psi(z+h)-Psi(z) is DA over C(z)} = H`.

Moreover:

1. `Psi` is hypertranscendental over `C(z)`;
2. `Gamma(z; tau, sigma)` is hypertranscendental over `C(z)`;
3. for every `h notin H`, `Psi(z+h)-Psi(z)` is hypertranscendental.

### Strong form: the defect-coset matroid

For arbitrary `h_1,...,h_n in C`, put `d_i=Delta_(h_i) Psi`, and let `m` be the number of distinct nonzero cosets among

`h_1+H,...,h_n+H in C/H`.

Then

`dtrdeg_(C(z)) C(z)<d_1,...,d_n>_delta = m`.

Equivalently, one defect chosen from each distinct nonzero coset is differentially algebraically independent; a defect from the zero coset is DA; and defects belonging to the same coset differ by a DA function after translation. Thus the differential-algebraic dependence matroid of all translation defects is exactly the partition matroid of `C/H` with the zero coset declared algebraic.

The same statements hold for the multiplicative shift quotients

`R_h(z)=Gamma(z+h; tau,sigma)/Gamma(z; tau,sigma)`.

Indeed `delta(R_h)/R_h=Delta_h Psi`. The differential field generated by `R_h` contains its logarithmic derivative, while `R_h` is differentially algebraic over the field generated by that derivative because it satisfies the first-order equation `delta Y=(Delta_h Psi)Y`. Therefore passing between a finite quotient family and its logarithmic-derivative defect family preserves differential transcendence degree. In particular

`R_h is DA over C(z) iff h in H`,

and every off-spectrum quotient `R_h` is hypertranscendental.

This would settle the `r=1` multiplicatively independent case left open in Kato, *Differential Algebraicity of the Multiple Elliptic Gamma Function for a Rational Period* (2021), Remark 1.2.

## Proof

### 1. The structural subgroup is DA

The set of `h` for which `Delta_h Psi` is DA is an additive subgroup: DA meromorphic functions form a field and translation preserves differential algebraicity. The three displayed functional equations therefore give

`H subset Spec_DA(Psi)`.

### 2. A defect is constrained over both elliptic curves

Fix `h in C` and put

`d_h = Delta_h Psi`.

Commuting translations gives

`Delta_sigma d_h = Delta_h lambda_tau =: b_tau,h`,

`Delta_tau d_h = Delta_h lambda_sigma =: b_sigma,h`.

Although `lambda_tau` is only quasi-elliptic, its increment under either generator of `Lambda_tau` is constant. Hence `b_tau,h` is `Lambda_tau`-elliptic. Similarly `b_sigma,h` is `Lambda_sigma`-elliptic.

Let `E_tau` and `E_sigma` be the two elliptic function fields, and let

`K = E_tau E_sigma`

be their compositum inside the field of meromorphic functions on `C`. It is a differential field, is stable under `T_tau` and `T_sigma`, and is DA over `C(z)`.

All elements of `K<d_h>_delta` are 1-periodic. An element fixed by both `T_tau` and `T_sigma` has periods `1,tau,sigma`. Since `H` is nondiscrete, such a meromorphic function is constant. Thus the paired system has no new joint difference constants:

`(K<d_h>_delta)^(T_tau,T_sigma)=C`.

### 3. Paired telescoping forced by differential algebraicity

Assume for contradiction that `d_h` is DA over `C(z)`, hence over `K`.

Apply the multi-automorphism `Sigma-Delta-Pi` Picard--Vessiot theory of Hardouin--Singer to the compatible unipotent system

`T_sigma(y)-y=b_tau,h`,

`T_tau(y)-y=b_sigma,h`.

Concretely, with

`U=[[1,d_h],[0,1]]` and `A_i=[[1,b_i],[0,1]]`,

the equations are `T_i(U)=A_i U`. The differential field `K<d_h>_delta` is a total `Sigma-Delta-Pi` Picard--Vessiot ring: it is generated by `U`, is a field and hence simple, and has the same joint difference constants `C` as `K`.

Because `d_h` is differentially algebraic, the differential Galois group is a proper differential algebraic subgroup of `G_a`. Cassidy's classification of such subgroups gives a nonzero operator

`L in C[delta]`

such that

`g:=L(d_h) in K`.

Equivalently, the **same** `g` satisfies the paired telescoping equations

`T_sigma(g)-g=L(b_tau,h)`,

`T_tau(g)-g=L(b_sigma,h)`.

For completeness, the usual differential-closure hypothesis causes no loss. Base-change from `C` to a differential closure, apply the Hardouin--Singer fixed-field theorem there, and descend. The Galois-group functor of the unipotent system is defined over `C`, so a nonzero linear differential equation cutting out its proper additive subgroup may be chosen in `C[delta]`. Moreover `K<d_h>_delta` and the differential closure are linearly disjoint over `C` because `C` is algebraically closed in the meromorphic function field. Therefore an invariant `L(d_h)` lying in the base-changed `K` already lies in `K`.

### 4. Scalar-extension descent to one elliptic curve

The fields `E_tau` and `E_sigma` are algebraically independent over `C`. Indeed, suppose nonconstant `f in E_tau` and `v in E_sigma` obey `P(f,v)=0`. For a period `omega` of `f`, all functions `v(z+n omega)` are roots of the fixed polynomial `P(f(z),Y)`. Only finitely many roots exist, so two translates coincide and a nonzero multiple of `omega` is a period of `v`. Reversing the roles of the fields shows that their period lattices are commensurable. This contradicts the `Q`-independence of `1,tau,sigma`.

Consequently the multiplication map

`E_tau tensor_C E_sigma -> Mer(C)`

is injective and `K` is the function field of the product of the two elliptic curves. The translation `T_sigma` acts on the first factor and trivially on the second. View `g` as a rational function on this product. Outside a proper closed subset of the second elliptic curve, specialization at a complex point is defined and does not annihilate its denominator. Specializing the first paired equation gives a rational function `g_tau in E_tau` satisfying

`T_sigma(g_tau)-g_tau=L(b_tau,h)`.

### 5. Elliptic orbit residues force the rank-three group

On the elliptic curve `C/Lambda_tau`, the function `lambda_tau` has one simple pole, of residue one. Thus

`b_tau,h=lambda_tau(z+h)-lambda_tau(z)`

has two simple poles with opposite residues, at classes differing by `h`.

Let `N` be the order of the nonzero operator `L`. The order-`N+1` principal parts of `L(b_tau,h)` at those two points are nonzero. If

`h notin Lambda_tau + Z sigma`,

the two poles lie in distinct `T_sigma`-orbits. Each such orbit therefore contains exactly one nonzero order-`N+1` principal part of `L(b_tau,h)`.

For every elliptic `u`, the sum of each fixed-order principal-part coefficient of `T_sigma(u)-u` along a `T_sigma`-orbit is zero: only finitely many points of the orbit are poles on the compact elliptic curve, so the coefficients telescope. Consequently `L(b_tau,h)` cannot equal `T_sigma(g_tau)-g_tau` unless

`h in Lambda_tau + Z sigma = H`.

This contradicts differential algebraicity for every `h notin H` and proves the exact spectrum.

### 5a. Simultaneous defects and the matroid formula

Choose shifts `h_1,...,h_m` representing distinct nonzero cosets of `H`, and suppose their defects are differentially algebraically dependent over `K`. Apply the same multi-operator Picard--Vessiot argument to the direct-sum unipotent system generated by all `d_i`. Its differential Galois group is then a proper differential algebraic subgroup of `G_a^m`. Hence there exist operators `L_i in C[delta]`, not all zero, and `g in K` such that

`g=sum_i L_i(d_i)`.

After applying `T_sigma-1` and specializing the `E_sigma` factor as above, there is `g_tau in E_tau` with

`T_sigma(g_tau)-g_tau = sum_i L_i(b_tau,h_i)`.

Let `N` be the largest order occurring among the `L_i`, and choose an index `i` whose order-`N` coefficient is nonzero. Because the cosets `h_i+H` are nonzero and pairwise distinct, the pole class `-h_i` belongs neither to the orbit of zero nor to the pole orbit belonging to any other `h_j`. At that orbit, the order-`N+1` principal-part sum of the right side consists of one nonzero term. It therefore cannot be a `T_sigma`-coboundary. This contradiction proves differential algebraic independence over `K`.

For a general finite list, each zero-coset defect is DA. If `h_i-h_j in H`, then

`d_i-d_j = Delta_(h_i-h_j) Psi(z+h_j)`

is DA. Hence all defects are differentially algebraic over a set of representatives of the distinct nonzero cosets. Since `K/C(z)` is a differential-algebraic extension, the lower and upper bounds agree over `C(z)`, proving the displayed differential-transcendence-degree formula.

### 6. Hypertranscendence

Choose `h notin H`. The theorem makes `Delta_h Psi` HT. If `Psi` were DA, every translate and difference of translates would be DA, contradiction. Hence `Psi` is HT.

If `Gamma` were DA, its logarithmic derivative `Gamma'/Gamma=Psi` would be DA. Hence `Gamma` is HT as well.

Alternatively, for any `h notin H`, the quotient `Gamma(z+h)/Gamma(z)` is HT by the multiplicative form above; it would be DA if `Gamma` were DA.

## Why the one-operator proof fails

For a single translation, the ambient meromorphic solution field has many nonconstant periodic constants, so the ordinary one-operator Hardouin--Singer criterion cannot be applied directly to the actual elliptic-gamma defect. The paired system is essential: the common periods `1,tau,sigma` form a nondiscrete group, forcing the joint constant field to collapse to `C`.

This is the new mechanism, not the already-known elliptic orbital-residue obstruction by itself.

## Edge cases

1. If `1,tau,sigma` are `Q`-dependent, the joint period group is discrete and the joint-constant collapse fails. This is the multiplicatively dependent boundary, compatible with Kato's DA results for rational periods.
2. If `h in Lambda_tau`, the two poles of `b_tau,h` cancel; this is already inside `H`.
3. If `h in Lambda_tau+Z sigma` but not `Lambda_tau`, the two poles occupy the same orbit and the orbital obstruction vanishes, again exactly as required.
4. The proof uses the logarithmic derivative to convert theta multipliers into additive elliptic constraints. It then returns to `Gamma` only after proving `Psi` HT.
