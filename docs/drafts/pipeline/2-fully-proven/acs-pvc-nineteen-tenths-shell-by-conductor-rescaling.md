# The nineteen-tenths shell by rescaling the conductor

**Paper claim.** Let \(V_n=\lfloor n^{19/10}\rfloor\). On every partial octave \(N\le M\le2N\),
\[
 \left|\sum_{N<n\le M} n^2[T_n(V_n,n)-T_n(2V_n,n)]\right|
 \ll N^3(1+\log(2N)).
\]
The implied constant is independent of the endpoints. Lean verification is pending.

**Arithmetic reduction.** Put \(a(q)=\Lambda(q)-1\). For horizon \(k\), cap \(b\) and lower endpoint \(Y\), replace the capped coprime row by \(\sum_{Y<q\le k} a(q) W(\lfloor k/q\rfloor)\). The uniform cost is \(O((\sqrt k+k/(b+1))(1+\log(2k)))\). Indeed lifting the cofactor cap costs \(O(k\log(2k)/(b+1))\). Restoring proper powers and repeated primes costs \(O(\sqrt k\log(2k))\); the missing first factors at most four cost \(O(\sqrt k)\) by the fixed-factor slice bound. This gives the complete balanced product with inner cutoff one. Its summatory difference from \(a\) is \(O(1+\log(2k))\) at every endpoint through \(k\) by the log-factorial identity. The truncated quotient-pair bound prices this difference by \(O(\sqrt k(1+\log(2k)))\), uniformly in \(Y\). The zero contribution of a unit cofactor is included. Apply this comparison at both original lower endpoints. Since \(k\ll n^2\) and the first spline difference is \(O(n^{-3})\), its price is \(O(1+\log(2n))\) per conductor. **The continuous reading.** Put \(\alpha=19/10,\ Q=N^\alpha,\ J=N^2/Q\), and write \(f\) for the actual compact spline. In the shell \(q\asymp Q\), both ends of its moving horizon lie outside the support for large \(N\). Exact telescoping gives the weighted pair reading \(nV(q/n^2)\), where
\[
 V(h)=\sum_{j\ge0} W(j)[f(jh)-f((j+1)h)].
\]
Only \(O(1/h)\) terms occur for \(h\) small. The periodic difference bound and the eighth forward-difference estimate for \(f\) give \(|V(h)|\ll h^7\) for all real \(0<7h<e^{-8}\). These are the same real mesh estimates used for integer conductors. [Periodic transfer](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExceptionalLayerTheory/SquareConductors/PeriodicQuotientCancellation.lean) · [Eighth difference](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExceptionalLayerTheory/SquareConductors/PiecewiseSmoothnessOfTheDyadicSpline.lean)

Replace the discrete conductor sum for each \(q\) by the integral of \(xV(q/x^2)\) over \(N<x\le M,\ x^\alpha<q\le2x^\alpha\). Replacing \(\lceil q/2\rceil\) by \(q/2\) shifts one conductor endpoint by at most one since \(\alpha>1\). The integrand is \(O(N)\) by boundedness of \(W\) and the total variation of \(f\). Its derivative is \(\sum_j W(j)[g(jh)-g((j+1)h)]\), where \(h=q/x^2\) and \(g(u)=f(u)-2uf'(u)\). Its absolute value is at most \(\|W\|_\infty\int|g'|\), uniformly in \(q,x\). Integrating on unit intervals, including the two truncated endpoints, therefore costs \(O(N)\) per product. Since \(\sum_{q\asymp Q}|a(q)|\ll Q\), the total price is \(O(NQ)\).

**Ordinary-prime cancellation.** Substitute \(x=t\sqrt q\) and switch the finite product sum and integral. The new weight is \(tV(1/t^2)\). At fixed \(t\), the conditions \(N<t\sqrt q\le M\) and \((t\sqrt q)^\alpha<q\le2(t\sqrt q)^\alpha\) intersect in a single half-open product interval within \(q\asymp Q\). This uses \(\alpha<2\) and keeps the actual terminal \(M\). Partial summation of \(\psi(y)-y\ll y^{8/9}\) gives \(|\sum q a(q)|\ll Q^{1+8/9}\) on every such interval. The quoted prime bound uses the project's zero-free strip and its explicit-formula transfer. Here \(t\asymp\sqrt J\), so \(\int t|V(1/t^2)|\,dt\ll J^{-6}\). The integral costs
\[
 Q^{1+8/9}J^{-6}=N^{269/90}.
\]
The finite initial octaves are absorbed by enlarging the constant. Adding the arithmetic and rounding costs yields the paper claim. Products above this single shell remain in the signed prefix. **Whole intervening range.** Let \(H=\lceil(2N)^{19/10}\rceil\) and \(T=N/\sqrt H\). For \(n^{17/9}<q\le n^{19/10}\) all retained products satisfy \(q\le H\). At fixed \(t\), the two different powers again cut out one interval because \(1-(17/9)/2\) and \(1-(19/10)/2\) are positive. The integral vanishes for \(t<T\). On \(t\ge T\), \(t|V(1/t^2)|\ll t^{-13}\), so integrating up to any upper endpoint costs at most \(T^{-12}/12\). The signed integral on the entire band therefore costs
\[
 H^{1+8/9}T^{-12}=N^{-12}H^{7+8/9}\ll N^{269/90}.
\]
All conductor rounding costs together are \(O(NH)\). Charge the two cutoff comparisons once, then sum the cubic-logarithmic partial-octave bounds to obtain the same cost through every terminal. This moves the residual cutoff to \(\lfloor n^{19/10}\rfloor\) on paper. Its upper tail still needs a one-sided bound.

**Lean status.** Lean verified that every rescaled slice is a single integer interval and that its weighted signed Mangoldt mass is uniformly bounded by \(C H^{1+8/9}\), including an additional lower cut. Lean also verified the arithmetic reduction on every conductor fragment for arbitrary moving cutoffs. All six audited declarations use only standard Lean axioms.
