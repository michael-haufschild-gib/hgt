# Opposite excursions on one fixed denominator band

Write $X=e^T$. Under failure of RH, on arbitrarily thin original §2.16 sequences the signed loss and reciprocal abundance can be retained with the same interior interval $(k,L]$, the same sign $s$, and the same choice of exterior flank for every contributing numerator in a test. The arithmetic on the interior is the single function
$$O_{k,L}(H)=\sum_{k<n\le L,\;\lfloor H/n\rfloor\ {\rm odd}}(\mathbf1_{\mathbb P}(n)\log n-1).$$
The exterior interval is either $(\lfloor\sqrt H\rfloor,k]$ throughout or $(L,H]$ throughout. The choices of sign and flank can depend on the test.

**Freezing the interval.** Take $0<r\le\min(1/64,g/16,d/16)$ and $0<\eta\le r$, with $g,d$ from the [abundance theorem](../3-formalized/ecs-abundance-of-state-oriented-numerators.md). For late tests, $\log k\ge(1-r)T$ and $\log L\le(1+2r)T$. Put $h=e^{(-1/2+4r)T}$. For $-T/3\le v\le-h$ every $k<n\le L$ has $T/6<v+\log n<6T$. At $t=v+\log n$ the edge condition is $e^{-t/2}\sqrt t<e^{-v}-1$. For $v\le-1$ the left side is at most one. For $-1<v\le-h$, $t\ge(1-2r)T$ and $\sqrt t\le e^{rT}$ eventually, giving a strict upper bound $h$. Also $H^{1/2+d}<L$ and $H\ge X$ imply $\sqrt H<Le^{-dT}\le k$. The selected interior is therefore exactly $(k,L]$.

**Signed cost.** Translating the finitely many atoms gives the signed displacement density
$$f(v)=m\Phi(v)\,F_{S(v)}(Xe^{-v})/\sqrt{Xe^{-v}},$$
where $F$ is the fractional prime excess and $S(v)$ is the physical active band. On $v\le0$ its absolute value is at most $|m|C_\Phi(1+\log L)LX^{-1/2}e^{2v}$. Outside $[-T/3,-h]$, integration on the negative ray costs at most $|m|C_\Phi(1+\log L)LX^{-1/2}(e^{-2T/3}/2+h)\ll_\Phi e^{8rT}$. This is $o(X^g)$, uniformly over measurable subsets. Thus the original negative contribution persists inside the frozen interval.

**One sign and one flank.** Split the retained displacement set by the sign of $m\Phi(v)$. On a piece retaining half the loss the interior sign is fixed. Within it, separate displacements hitting at least one listed numerator with a qualifying left flank. Each remaining displacement hits a numerator with a qualifying right flank. The hit sets are measurable, since the numerators form a finite list and the binary digits are countable. One piece retains half of the remaining signed loss. The [signed removal bound](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExclusionOfTheCriticalState/Wall/TheWeightedOccupationOfPairedQuotientCells.lean#L421) then gives $L\sum_{H\in N}H^{-1}\gg X^{1/2+g}$ for distinct numerators actually hit there. The opposite factorial estimates hold on every nonempty subset of each admissible terminal block, and both sliding Chebyshev runs persist. Applying the estimates to the singleton block gives a product greater than a constant times $X^{2g}H$ for each $H$. Summing the two signed positive parts against $H^{-2}$ yields a lower bound of order $X^{1/2+3g}/L$ for the arithmetic mixed moment restricted to the joint upper tail. Its explicit summation set includes only numerators where both signed discrepancies exceed the selected threshold.

**Below the upper endpoint.** For $H\le L$ the right flank is empty. The two left-flank ranges form the complete row above $\sqrt H$, whose signed excess has absolute value at most $C\sqrt H$. Thus the interior and opposite left-flank positive parts $P,Q$ satisfy $|P-Q|\le C\sqrt H$. On every finite subfamily where $P\ge B\sqrt H$, the relative error in replacing the mixed moment by the interior second moment is at most $C/B$, by $|PQ-P^2|=P|Q-P|$. In particular this holds throughout $(L/2,L]$ and after a second level restriction on $Q$. At the forced level $B=aX^g$ the relative error is $O(X^{-g})$. A large interior excursion on this range therefore forces the other flank at any threshold lower by $C\sqrt H$.
