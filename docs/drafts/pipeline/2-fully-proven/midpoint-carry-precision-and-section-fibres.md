# Exporting precision in midpoint section maps: carry dichotomy, fibre collisions, and mixed-family prototype

For a finite map $f:X\to Y$ write $F(f)=\max_{y\in Y}|f^{-1}(y)|$ for its largest fibre cardinality. For integer spline divisors $b,a$ and $S(v)=\mathrm{divisorSections}(v)$, any map $f_m:S(mkb)^2\to S(m(kb+a))\times S(m(kb-a))$ satisfies $|S(mkb)|^2\le F(f_m)|S(m(kb+a))||S(m(kb-a))|$, so $-\log F(f_m)/m^2\le\mathrm{midpointExcess}(kb,a,m)$.

## 1. Subquadratic-fibre midpoint receiver and translation rigidity

**Subquadratic-fibre criterion.** Let $b$ satisfy $Q(b)>0$. If for every displacement $a$ and all sufficiently large $k$, every $\varepsilon>0$ admits cofinally many $m$ and maps $f_m:S(mkb)^2\to S(m(kb+a))\times S(m(kb-a))$ with $\log F(f_m)\le\varepsilon m^2$, then $Q(a)\ge0$ for all $a$, which is equivalent to the Riemann Hypothesis. Over the unconditional two-atom prime background $b=e_p\pm e_1$ with $Q(b)>0$, RH is equivalent to this subquadratic-fibre midpoint property.

**Translation-uniformity obstruction.** No single coordinate map $\Psi:\mathbb{Z}^2\to\mathbb{Z}^2$ can serve two translations $k_1<k_2$ with subquadratic fibre cost when the mixed pairing makes the outer log-rate sum smaller than $2k_1^2k_2^2Q(b)$ at the common midpoint $m_1=k_2m$, $m_2=k_1m$, where $S(m_1k_1b)=S(m_2k_2b)=S(k_1k_2mb)$. On a positive ray $a=b$ at translations $k\ge2$ and $2k$, intersecting $\Psi(S(2mkb)^2)\subseteq S(2m(k+1)b)\times S(2m(k-1)b)$ and $\Psi(S(2mkb)^2)\subseteq S(m(2k+1)b)\times S(m(2k-1)b)$ places the image in $S(m(2k+1)b)\times S(2m(k-1)b)$, whose log-cardinality rate $((2k+1)^2+4(k-1)^2)Q(b)=(8k^2-4k+5)Q(b)$ falls short of $8k^2Q(b)$ by $(4k-5)Q(b)>0$, forcing $\log F(\Psi|_{S(2mkb)^2})>\delta(2m)^2$ eventually for every $\delta<\frac{4k-5}{4}Q(b)$. For any displacement $a$ with $B(b,a)\ne0$, choosing $k_2=2k_1$ gives deficit $k_1^2(4k_1|B(b,a)|-5Q(a))>0$ for all large $k_1$, obstructing translation-uniform subquadratic fibres.

## 2. Two-place precision export, polynomial rounding with carry, and joint inverse loss

Write $n=|S(3mb)|$, $L=|S(4mb)|$, $s=|S(2mb)|$ on a ray $b$ with $d=Q(b)>0$, so $\log n/m^2\to9d$, $\log L/m^2\to16d$, $\log s/m^2\to4d$.

1. **Two-place precision versus two-output collisions.** Let $f:\mathrm{Fin}\,n\times\mathrm{Fin}\,n\to\mathrm{Fin}\,L\times\mathrm{Fin}\,s$ be any map (with unrestricted first coordinate $f_1$) having narrow-coordinate precision losses $i,j$ at coprime bases $p,q>1$. Set $r=\lfloor\log_p s\rfloor+1$ and $t=\lfloor\log_q s\rfloor+1$. If $p^{r+i}+q^{t+j}\le n$, two-period constancy (`constant_of_two_periods`) makes $f_2$ constant, so all $n^2$ inputs land in a single slice of size $L$ and $n^2\le F(f)L$. Otherwise $n<p^{r+i}+q^{t+j}\le ps\,p^i+qs\,q^j$. On $S(3mb)^2\to S(4mb)\times S(2mb)$, for every $\delta_1<2d$ and $\delta_2<5d$, eventually every map $f$ satisfies $\delta_1m^2<\log F(f)$ or $\delta_2m^2<\max(i\log p,j\log q)$. Specializing to injections ($F=1$) sharpens the two-place precision lower bound from $i\log p+j\log q>\delta m^2$ ($\delta<d$) to $\max(i\log p,j\log q)>\delta m^2$ ($\delta<5d$). Under `clearsSecond f D`, any map with $\log F(f)\le\delta m^2$ ($\delta<2d$) retains $F(f)L<n^2$, so all but at most one prime in $(\lfloor n/4\rfloor,2\lfloor n/4\rfloor]$ still divide $D$ and $\log\log D/m^2\to9d$.
2. **Polynomial rounding with arbitrary first-output carry.** Suppose $P\in\mathbb{Z}[X_1,X_2]$ is any bivariate integer polynomial, $D\ge1$, $D\cdot f_2(x)+r(x)=P(x_1,x_2)$ with $0\le r(x)<D$, and $f_1(x)\in\mathrm{Fin}\,L$ is an arbitrary function (including any carry of $r(x)$). Since $0\le P(x)<Ds$, $P$ preserves congruences modulo the coprime pair $(Ds,Ds+1)$ with values in $[0,Ds)$. Whenever $2Ds+1\le n$, $P$ is constant on $\mathrm{Fin}\,n\times\mathrm{Fin}\,n$, hence $f_2$ is constant and $n^2\le F(f)L$. On $S(3mb)^2\to S(4mb)\times S(2mb)$, for every $\delta_1<2d$ and $\delta_2<5d$, eventually $\delta_1m^2<\log F(f)$ or $\delta_2m^2<\log D$. Thus every subquadratic-log rounding divisor ($\log D=o(m^2)$) incurs quadratic net fibre loss across both outputs.
3. **Exported carry infects the first output at two coprime places.** Consider the canonical Euclidean carry map $\Phi_M(x_1,x_2)=((x_1\bmod M)+Mx_2,\lfloor x_1/M\rfloor)$ for $M\ge2$. If $\Phi_M(\mathrm{Fin}\,n^2)\subseteq\mathrm{Fin}\,L\times\mathrm{Fin}\,s$ with $n\ge2$, evaluating at $(0,n-1)$ gives $M(n-1)<L$. On the slice $x_2=0$, $\Phi_M(x_1,0)=x_1\bmod M\in\mathrm{Fin}\,M$ is nonconstant ($0\ne1$). If the first output satisfies `firstPrecisionLoss` with losses $(i_1,j_1)$ at coprime $p,q>1$, two-period constancy on $\mathrm{Fin}\,n\to\mathrm{Fin}\,M$ forces $n<p^{\lfloor\log_p M\rfloor+1+i_1}+q^{\lfloor\log_q M\rfloor+1+j_1}\le M(p\,p^{i_1}+q\,q^{j_1})$, hence $n(n-1)<L(p\,p^{i_1}+q\,q^{j_1})$. On $S(3mb)^2\to S(4mb)\times S(2mb)$, $\max(i_1\log p,j_1\log q)>\delta m^2$ eventually for every $\delta<2d$.
4. **Universal joint inverse precision loss at one place.** For any base $p>1$ and any map $f:\mathrm{Fin}\,n^2\to\mathrm{Fin}\,L\times\mathrm{Fin}\,s$, if $sp^{2k}\cdot p\le n$, let $R=\lfloor\log_p n\rfloor$ (so $p^R\le n<p^{R+1}$ and $sp^{2k}<p^R$) and $r=R-k$. Mapping $\mathrm{Fin}\,p^R\times\mathrm{Fin}\,p^R\to\mathrm{Fin}\,p^R\times\mathrm{Fin}\,s$ via $x\mapsto(f_1(x)\bmod p^R,f_2(x))$ yields a fibre $C$ with $|C|s\ge p^R>sp^{2k}$, hence $|C|>p^{2(R-r)}$. Since each residue class modulo $p^r$ in $\mathrm{Fin}\,p^R\times\mathrm{Fin}\,p^R$ has size $p^{2(R-r)}$, $C$ contains $x,y$ incongruent modulo $p^r$ with $f_1(x)\equiv f_1(y)\pmod{p^{r+k}}$ and $f_2(x)=f_2(y)$. On $S(3mb)^2\to S(4mb)\times S(2mb)$, joint $p$-adic recovery across both outputs loses $k\log p>\delta m^2$ eventually for every $\delta<\frac{5}{2}d$.

## 3. Exact carry window and reversible one-place prototype on mixed families

1. **Centered Euclidean carry involution and necessary-and-sufficient window.** For $K\in\mathbb{N}$ and $M=2K+1$, set $q_K(z)=\lfloor(z+K)/M\rfloor$ and $r_K(z)=((z+K)\bmod M)-K\in[-K,K]$. The integral map $\mathcal{I}_K(z_1,z_2)=(r_K(z_1)+Mz_2,q_K(z_1))$ satisfies $\mathcal{I}_K(\mathcal{I}_K(z_1,z_2))=(z_1,z_2)$ and $w_1+Mw_2=z_1+Mz_2$. For $r_0>0$, $\mathcal{I}_K([-r_0,r_0]^2)\subseteq[-r_+,r_+]\times[-r_-,r_-]$ holds iff $r_0\le Mr_-+K$ and $Mr_0+K\le r_+$. Along $(m(kb-a),mkb,m(kb+a))$ with $Q(b)>0$, an odd schedule $M_m=2K_m+1$ with $\log M_m/m^2\to\mu$ embeds $S(mkb)^2\hookrightarrow S(m(kb+a))\times S(m(kb-a))$ eventually whenever $Q(kb)-Q(kb-a)<\mu<Q(kb+a)-Q(kb)$, and cofinal validity requires $Q(kb)-Q(kb-a)\le\mu\le Q(kb+a)-Q(kb)$, hence $Q(a)\ge0$.
2. **Parameter-free one-place block carry on genuinely mixed families.** Define `natBlockCarry : ℕ × ℕ → ℕ × ℕ` recursively on $512=2^9$ blocks by sending $(x_1,x_2)$ with $a=x_1\bmod 512$, $b=x_2\bmod 512$ to $((a/8+64b)+32768\,r_1,(a\bmod 8)+8\,r_2)$ where $(r_1,r_2)=\mathrm{natBlockCarry}(x_1/512,x_2/512)$, and conjugate by the signed bijection $\mathbb{Z}\simeq\mathbb{N}$ ($z\mapsto 2z$ for $z\ge0$, $-2z-1$ for $z<0$) to obtain an explicit parameter-free bijection $\mathcal{B}:\mathbb{Z}^2\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{Z}^2$. It preserves base-$2$ block congruences ($\bmod\,512^t\mapsto\bmod\,32768^t\times\bmod\,8^t$, exporting $6t$ base-$2$ bits per block to the first coordinate) and satisfies $|\mathcal{B}(z)_1|^9\le C_1(|z|+1)^{15}$ and $|\mathcal{B}(z)_2|^9\le C_2(|z|+1)^3$. For the moment threshold $k$ of `positive_integer_window_moments 4` and every $N\ge 2\max(k,1)+2$, `windowMomentKernel N 2 (max k 1)` contains a nonzero $u$; setting $U=\mathrm{pad}(u)$ and $V=\mathrm{dilate}_2(u)$ in $\mathbb{Z}^{2N}$ gives $b=U+V$ with $Q(b)>0$ and $a=3U-V=b+2(U-V)$ genuinely mixed ($\sum_i a_i=0$, strictly positive and negative entries) with $B(b,U-V)=0$ by `integerProbe_dilate`, $Q(3b+a)=16Q(b)+4Q(U-V)>15Q(b)$, and $Q(3b-a)=4Q(b)+4Q(U-V)>3Q(b)$. The single fixed map $\mathcal{B}$ restricts for all large $m$ to an explicit reversible injection $S(3mb)^2\hookrightarrow S(m(3b+a))\times S(m(3b-a))$, as well as $S(3mb)^2\hookrightarrow S(4mb)\times S(2mb)$ on every positive ray $b$.
