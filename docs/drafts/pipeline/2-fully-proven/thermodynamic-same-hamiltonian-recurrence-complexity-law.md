## Frozen conjecture C1

There exist a countable real nearest-neighbour Pauli coefficient field `a`, a bounded self-adjoint
Hamiltonian `H∞` generated by its absolutely summable local terms, and compatible restrictions
`H_L` to the first `2L` qubits such that, for every sufficiently large `L`:

1. `H_L` has simple, maximally projective spectrum;
2. at fixed operator error `10⁻⁴`, its operator-norm two-qubit circuit complexity has a positive
   linear lower envelope throughout a nonempty laboratory window whose endpoint tends to infinity;
3. at fixed operator error `1/10`, its orbit has syndetically recurring intervals excluding every
   continuous two-qubit circuit below `2^(2L)/(64(2L+1))` gates;
4. after each such excursion it has a literal first re-entry into the circuit target, and exact or
   arbitrarily accurate quantum recurrence occurs after every starting time;
5. the finite-volume dynamics converges to the strongly continuous unitary group generated by
   `H∞`, uniformly on compact time intervals.

No clause may use a separately chosen coefficient vector at each `L`.

4. Prove a mobile-tail lower bound: truncate the perturbation before a moving cut.  Arbitrary early

   tail has Duhamel error bounded by time times its absolute norm tail.

   each prefix.  Construct `H∞` as the norm-convergent sum of local Pauli operators and prove compact-

## Paper-proof architecture for C1

### A. One bounded compatible coefficient field

Index the countable path shell by three nonidentity onsite Pauli labels at every qubit and nine
nonidentity Pauli-pair labels at every bond.  Write `c` for the compatible coefficient field of the
nested inverse-square/inverse-fourth diagonal chain after expanding every `P11` term into scalar,
onsite-`Z`, and `ZZ` parts.  Give every shell coordinate `r` a positive radius

`delta(r) = 10^-6 / (1 + position(r))^2`.

For finite prefix `L`, let `Good_L` be the path-shell coefficients whose Hermitian Hamiltonian has
maximally projective spectrum.  The path-shell polynomial genericity theorem makes `Good_L`
residual.  Let `s : R -> (-1,1)` be an order homeomorphism and apply, coordinatewise,

`F_r(x) = c(r) + delta(r) s(x)`.

Every finite product `F_L` is continuous and open, so `F_L^-1(Good_L)` is residual.  Pull these
sets to one countable ambient product through the literal prefix selectors and intersect them.
The ambient residual-atlas theorem supplies one `x`; put `a(r)=F_r(x(r))`.  Then simultaneously:

- every finite prefix belongs to `Good_L`;
- `|a(r)-c(r)| < delta(r)` at every coordinate;
- the perturbation `v=a-c` is absolutely summable;
- every finite Hamiltonian is the literal prefix restriction of this one field.

The finite scalar terms discarded by the Pauli shell are restored from the compatible `P11`
expansion.  Scalar shifts preserve maximal projectivity, so every finite `H_L` is simple and
maximally projective.

### B. Prefix-invariant four-amplitude obstruction

Let `A` be the selected first endpoints of a set of matching pairs.  Suppose a circuit matrix `W`
separates across `A`, while the target has the form

`U = V_prefix tensor D_tail`,

where `V_prefix` is arbitrary unitary and `D_tail` is diagonal.  Choose any unit prefix vector
`alpha` and use `beta=V_prefix alpha` as output vector.  For the four tail basis states obtained by
toggling the two sides of `A`, the four target transition amplitudes are exactly the four diagonal
phases of `D_tail`: the prefix matrix element is

`<beta,V_prefix alpha> = 1`.

Because `W` separates across `A`, the corresponding four bilinear amplitudes obey the determinant
identity `w00*w11=w01*w10`.  Operator-norm distance at most `eta` controls each unit-vector matrix
element by `eta`; unitarity bounds every approximating amplitude by one.  Therefore the existing
four-amplitude substitution argument gives

`|u00*u11-u01*u10| <= 4 eta`.

This is the required basis-free extension of `SeparatesAcross.fourPoint_diagonal`.  It removes all
dependence on the dimension or circuit complexity of the arbitrary prefix.

### C. Mobile truncation with unchanged linear gate count

Fix target error `epsilon`, time `t`, and

`K = floor(t / (256*pi*epsilon))`.

The laboratory assumptions

`2048*pi*epsilon <= t <= epsilon*L`

give `8 <= K` and `8*K <= L`.  Form `H_L^(K)` by keeping the complete perturbed Hamiltonian on the
first `2K` pairs, deleting the one inverse-fourth base bond crossing that cut, deleting all
perturbation terms not wholly contained in the prefix, and retaining the unperturbed nested base on
the remaining tail.  Thus `H_L^(K)` is an arbitrary-prefix tensor factor plus a diagonal tail.

The discarded base bond has norm `(2K+1)^-4`.  Since

`t < 256*pi*epsilon*(K+1)` and `K >= 8`,

its Duhamel contribution is less than `epsilon/2`.  The coordinate radii and the inverse-square
tail estimate give a discarded perturbation norm bounded by twelve copies of a
`10^-6 n^-2` tail; the same time inequality makes its Duhamel contribution less than
`epsilon/2`.  Hence

`||exp(-it H_L) - exp(-it H_L^(K))|| < epsilon`.

If a circuit approximates the full evolution within `epsilon`, it approximates the truncated
evolution within `2*epsilon`.  Assume its two-qubit-gate count is below `K`.  The existing touched-
pair bound leaves more than `2K` untouched matching pairs in the block of `4K` pairs with one-based
indices `4K+1,...,8K`.  The existing subset-sum lemma selects untouched `S` whose inverse-square
phase lies between `3*pi*(2epsilon)` and `5*pi*(2epsilon)`.  All circuit gates lie on one side of
every selected first-endpoint cut, so its matrix separates across that cut.  Section B cancels the
arbitrary prefix.  The inverse-fourth tail changes the selected phase by less than
`(2epsilon)/4`; the existing phase-chord lower bound contradicts the determinant upper bound at
error `2epsilon`.  Therefore

`floor(t/(256*pi*epsilon)) <= C_epsilon^op(exp(-it H_L))`.

At `epsilon=10^-4`, this proves clause 2 on the full window

`2048*pi*10^-4 <= t <= 10^-4 L`.

### D. Late hard intervals, first re-entry, and recurrence

For every prefix, maximal projectivity identifies the completed projective orbit with the full
projective phase torus.  Apply the existing continuous-circuit entropy theorem at

`G_L = 2^(2L)/(64(2L+1))`.

It yields a positive-width hard interval in every bounded-gap late-time window at operator error
`1/10`.  Applying the consecutive-visit theorem to the same orbit gives actual low-circuit visits
on both sides, a literal first re-entry, and arbitrarily late recurrence.  No coefficient is
reselected with `L`.

### E. Infinite-chain completion

The base coefficients and perturbation radii are absolutely summable and every local Pauli term
has norm one.  The infinite series therefore converges in operator norm to a bounded self-adjoint
`H_infinity`.  Embedded prefix Hamiltonians converge to it in operator norm.  The Duhamel estimate

`||exp(-it H_L)-exp(-it H_infinity)|| <= |t| ||H_L-H_infinity||`

gives uniform convergence on every compact time interval.  Conjugation by the limiting unitary
group gives the infinite-volume dynamics on local observables.

Use the following literal representation.  Let `X={0,1}^{N x {0,1}}` and `Hinf=l2(X)`.  Every
finite-support Pauli word acts on the canonical basis by a phase-valued signed permutation,
`T_P delta_x = chi_P(x) delta_(tau_P x)`.  Since `tau_P` is a bijection and `|chi_P|=1`, `T_P`
extends to a unitary; for Hermitian Pauli words it is self-adjoint.  Extend each finite prefix
operator to `Hinf` by the same local signed permutations, acting trivially outside the prefix.
This is a faithful isometric embedding of the finite matrix algebra.

The scalar coefficients, inverse-square matching coefficients, inverse-fourth bridge coefficients,
and `10^-6 n^-2` perturbation envelope are summable after the fixed finite multiplicities three and
nine are included.  Consequently

`Hinf = scalar*I + sum_r a(r) T_r`

converges in operator norm.  Its partial sums are exactly the embedded `H_L`; hence
`||H_L-Hinf|| -> 0`.  Self-adjointness passes to the norm limit.  The power-series exponential in
the Banach algebra of bounded operators supplies a norm-continuous unitary group, and the standard
Duhamel integral gives the compact-time estimate above.  Thus this clause is an operator theorem,
not merely pointwise convergence of configuration energies.

### F. Experimental boundary

Experiments cannot prove the abstract theorem; they can test whether an engineered device realizes
its finite-volume Hamiltonian and falsify claimed low-gate simulators.  A publication claim of
experimental testability will use the constructive witness above: from a proposed sub-`K` circuit,
read its touched pairs, greedily select `S`, prepare the four corresponding branches, and estimate
the four complex transition amplitudes.  Any separated circuit has zero amplitude determinant,
whereas the target has the certified phase chord.  Before this enters the manuscript, add and
formalize (i) a deterministic additive measurement-noise margin and (ii) a finite-shot confidence
bound.  Maximal projectivity itself remains only finitely falsifiable: finite-precision spectroscopy
can exclude bounded-height integer relations, not prove absence of every relation.

For deterministic readout tolerance `xi`, if all four complex amplitudes are estimated within
`xi` and the true amplitudes have modulus at most one, the estimated determinant differs from the
true determinant by at most `4*xi+2*xi^2`: each of the two products costs at most
`2*xi+xi^2`.  Therefore an observed determinant above the separated-circuit threshold plus this
margin falsifies that circuit model.  A Hadamard-test implementation has eight real estimates
(real and imaginary parts of four amplitudes).  With independent outcomes in `[-1,1]`, a two-sided
Hoeffding bound plus the union bound gives failure probability at most
`16*exp(-N*xi^2/4)` when each quadrature uses `N` shots and complex error `xi` is obtained by
quadrature errors `xi/sqrt(2)`.  Equivalently it is enough to take
`N >= 4*xi^-2*log(16/alpha)` for confidence `1-alpha`.  These constants are provisional until the
probability model and inequality are formalized.
