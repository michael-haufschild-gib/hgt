# Projective horizons encode the dual Tits building

- **ID:** `projective-horizon-tits-building`
- **Stage:** 2-fully-proven
- **Type:** PROJECTIVE DIFFERENTIAL INVARIANTS / BUILDING RECONSTRUCTION
- **Scientific status:** `[PROVED ON PAPER] [NOVELTY CANDIDATE] [PHYSICAL-TOPOLOGICAL]`
- **Parents:** `projective-horizon-building-schwarzian-complementarity`,
  `jet-horizon-translation-gauge-classification`
- **Statement:** Positive-derivative horizons of a differential-generic projective curve encode
  the complete incidence matroid of projective hyperplanes. Distinct horizons are complementary,
  while their intersections recover the ranks and joins of the corresponding normal subspaces.

## Set-up

Let `C` be an algebraically closed constant field of characteristic zero, let `V=C^N` with
`N>=2`, and let

```text
X in P(V)
```

be a differential-generic projective point. In one affine chart write

```text
X=[1:x_1:...:x_(N-1)],
F=C<x_1,...,x_(N-1)>.
```

For a projective hyperplane `H=ker(alpha)`, choose a projective transformation taking `H` to the
hyperplane at infinity, write the resulting affine coordinates as `u_H`, and define

```text
mathcal H_H=C<(u_H)'>.                                    (1)
```

All derivatives of order at least one are included in `(1)`.

Let `U_H` be the vector group of projective translations in the affine chart with infinity
hyperplane `H`. Intrinsically,

```text
U_H={I+v tensor alpha : v in ker(alpha)} subset PGL(V).   (2)
```

## Theorem

### 1. Horizon fixed fields

The field `mathcal H_H` is independent of the chosen affine frame with infinity hyperplane `H`,
and

```text
mathcal H_H=F^(U_H(C)).                                   (3)
```

### 2. Pairwise complementarity

For any two distinct hyperplanes `H != K`,

```text
mathcal H_H mathcal H_K=F.                                (4)
```

Thus every two distinct projective derivative horizons jointly reconstruct the entire
differential-generic projective source.

### 3. Span-intersection law

Let `alpha_1,...,alpha_s` be nonzero covectors, put

```text
S=span(alpha_1,...,alpha_s) subset V*,
r=dim S,
W=S^perp subset V,
mathcal I_S=intersection_i mathcal H_(ker alpha_i).        (5)
```

Then `mathcal I_S` depends only on `S`, not on its chosen spanning family, and

```text
mathcal I_S=F^(G_S),                                      (6)
G_S={g in SL(V): g|_W=id}                                 (7)
```

where `(7)` is read through its projective action. For `r>=1`,

```text
dim G_S=rN-1.                                             (8)
```

For nonzero subspaces `S,T subset V*`,

```text
mathcal I_(S+T)=mathcal I_S intersection mathcal I_T.     (9)
```

### 4. Building reconstruction

The assignment

```text
S |-> mathcal I_S                                         (10)
```

is injective and order-reversing on nonzero subspaces of `V*`. It carries span to field
intersection by `(9)`. At any sufficiently high finite jet level `J_k`,

```text
trdeg(J_k / J_k^(G_S))=rN-1,                              (11)
```

so the rank `r` of a collection of hyperplane normals is recovered from its horizon intersection.
Consequently the horizon-subfield poset contains an order-dual copy of the spherical Tits
building of `PGL(V)`, including its full projective dependence matroid.

For `N=3`, three projective lines are concurrent exactly when their three horizon intersection
equals the intersection of any spanning pair among them. Nonconcurrent lines have normal rank
three and a strictly smaller common field. Incidence in the dual projective plane is therefore a
field-theoretic observable.

## Proof

### Choice independence and fixed field

Two projective frames with the same infinity hyperplane differ by an affine transformation

```text
u |-> A u+b,
```

with constant invertible `A`. Their positive derivative tuples differ by `A`, so they generate the
same differential field.

In that affine frame,

```text
F=C(u_i^(j) : 1<=i<N, j>=0),
```

with all jet variables algebraically independent. The group `U_H(C)` translates only the
zeroth-order variables `u_i`; the rational fixed field of this translation action is exactly the
field generated by the variables of order at least one. This proves `(3)`.

### Two-chart reconstruction

Projective covariance reduces two distinct hyperplanes to

```text
H_0={X_0=0},   H_1={X_1=0}.
```

In the first chart use coordinates `x_i=X_i/X_0`. In the second use

```text
u=1/x_1,
v_i=x_i/x_1,  i>=2.
```

The first horizon contains `x_i',x_i'',...`; the second contains `u',u'',...` and
`v_i',v_i'',...`. Projective inversion gives

```text
x_1=2x_1' / [x_1''/x_1' - u''/u'].                       (12)
```

For every transverse coordinate,

```text
x_i=[x_i' x_1-v_i' x_1^2]/x_1'.                          (13)
```

Differential genericity makes the denominators nonzero. Equations `(12)--(13)` place all source
coordinates in the join of the two horizons, proving `(4)`.

### Generated unipotent groups

The intersection in `(5)` is the fixed field of the group generated by the `U_(ker alpha_i)`.
Choose a basis so that independent members of the spanning family are

```text
alpha_i=e_i^*,   1<=i<=r.
```

The group `U_(ker e_i^*)` contains every elementary transvection `I+tE_(ji)` with `j != i`.
Together these generate `SL_r` on the first `r` coordinates and every lower-left shear from their
span to `W`. The resulting block matrices are exactly

```text
[ A  0 ]
[ B  I ],       A in SL_r,  B in Hom(C^r,W),              (14)
```

which is `G_S`. This proves `(6)--(7)`. Its dimension is

```text
(r^2-1)+r(N-r)=rN-1,
```

proving `(8)`. Applying the same generation result to `S+T` proves `(9)`.

### Faithfulness and rank recovery

The prolonged `PGL(V)` action on sufficiently high generic projective jets is generically free:
a generic osculating jet supplies a projective moving frame. Rosenlicht's dimension theorem then
gives `(11)` for every connected subgroup `G_S`. Strict inclusions among the `G_S` therefore give
strict reverse inclusions among their fixed fields. Generic orbit equality would force equality of
the corresponding connected subgroups, so `mathcal I_S=mathcal I_T` implies `S=T`. This proves
injectivity and the building statement.

## Physical interpretation

The theorem produces a literal projective topology of HT information contexts.

- a hyperplane selects an affine observer and its derivative horizon;
- any two distinct observers are complementary and reconstruct the source;
- common information among several observers records exactly their projective incidence rank;
- the deepest intersection is the full projective differential-invariant field.

The proper-subspace poset of `V*` is the spherical building for `PGL_N`, and also the incidence
skeleton underlying finite-dimensional quantum proposition geometry. The theorem realizes this
skeleton inside the HT subfield lattice. It supplies no Hermitian form, orthocomplement, positivity,
Born rule, or quantum state, so the result is not by itself a quantization theorem.

For `N=2`, the building reduces to the already proved projective-line horizon law: distinct
horizons have full join and common Schwarzian field. Higher `N` adds genuine incidence data that
does not exist in the scalar case.

## Parent-theory and novelty gate

- Projective moving frames and rational differential invariants are classical.
- Fixed fields of unipotent radicals and Tits buildings are classical, separately.
- The elementary-group generation in `(14)` is standard linear algebra.
- No checked source identifies affine-chart derivative horizons with the hyperplane building,
  proves the complementarity formula `(12)--(13)`, or recovers projective matroid rank through the
  intersection law `(5)--(11)`.

The combination is marked a novelty candidate pending a specialist literature audit. Its
significance is structural: it recovers an entire incidence geometry, rather than one invariant or
one reconstruction identity.
