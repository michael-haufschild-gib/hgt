# Projective horizons encode the dual Tits building

- **ID:** `projective-horizon-tits-building`
- **Stage:** 2-fully-proven
- **Type:** PROJECTIVE DIFFERENTIAL INVARIANTS / BUILDING RECONSTRUCTION
- **Scientific status:** `[PROVED ON PAPER] [NOVELTY CANDIDATE] [PHYSICAL-TOPOLOGICAL]`
- **Parents:** `projective-horizon-building-schwarzian-complementarity`,
  `jet-horizon-translation-gauge-classification`
- **Statement:** Positive-derivative horizons of a differential-generic projective curve encode
  the complete incidence matroid of projective hyperplanes. Distinct horizons are complementary,
  while their intersections recover the ranks and joins of the corresponding normal subspaces.

## Set-up

Let `C` be an algebraically closed constant field of characteristic zero, let `V=C^N` with
`N>=2`, and let

```text
X in P(V)
```

be a differential-generic projective point. In one affine chart write

```text
X=[1:x_1:...:x_(N-1)],
F=C<x_1,...,x_(N-1)>.
```

For a projective hyperplane `H=ker(alpha)`, choose a projective transformation taking `H` to the
hyperplane at infinity, write the resulting affine coordinates as `u_H`, and define

```text
mathcal H_H=C<(u_H)'>.                                    (1)
```

All derivatives of order at least one are included in `(1)`.

Let `U_H` be the vector group of projective translations in the affine chart with infinity
hyperplane `H`. Intrinsically,

```text
U_H={I+v tensor alpha : v in ker(alpha)} subset PGL(V).   (2)
```

## Theorem

### 1. Horizon fixed fields

The field `mathcal H_H` is independent of the chosen affine frame with infinity hyperplane `H`,
and

```text
mathcal H_H=F^(U_H(C)).                                   (3)
```

### 2. Pairwise complementarity

For any two distinct hyperplanes `H != K`,

```text
mathcal H_H mathcal H_K=F.                                (4)
```

Thus every two distinct projective derivative horizons jointly reconstruct the entire
differential-generic projective source.

### 3. Span-intersection law

Let `alpha_1,...,alpha_s` be nonzero covectors, put

```text
S=span(alpha_1,...,alpha_s) subset V*,
r=dim S,
W=S^perp subset V,
mathcal I_S=intersection_i mathcal H_(ker alpha_i).        (5)
```

Then `mathcal I_S` depends only on `S`, not on its chosen spanning family, and

```text
mathcal I_S=F^(G_S),                                      (6)
G_S={g in SL(V): g|_W=id}                                 (7)
```

where `(7)` is read through its projective action. For `r>=1`,

```text
dim G_S=rN-1.                                             (8)
```

For nonzero subspaces `S,T subset V*`,

```text
mathcal I_(S+T)=mathcal I_S intersection mathcal I_T.     (9)
```

### 4. Building reconstruction

The assignment

```text
S |-> mathcal I_S                                         (10)
```

is injective and order-reversing on nonzero subspaces of `V*`. It carries span to field
intersection by `(9)`. At any sufficiently high finite jet level `J_k`,

```text
trdeg(J_k / J_k^(G_S))=rN-1,                              (11)
```

so the rank `r` of a collection of hyperplane normals is recovered from its horizon intersection.
Consequently the horizon-subfield poset contains an order-dual copy of the spherical Tits
building of `PGL(V)`, including its full projective dependence matroid.

For `N=3`, three projective lines are concurrent exactly when their three horizon intersection
equals the intersection of any spanning pair among them. Nonconcurrent lines have normal rank
three and a strictly smaller common field. Incidence in the dual projective plane is therefore a
field-theoretic observable.

## Proof

### Choice independence and fixed field

Two projective frames with the same infinity hyperplane differ by an affine transformation

```text
u |-> A u+b,
```

with constant invertible `A`. Their positive derivative tuples differ by `A`, so they generate the
same differential field.

In that affine frame,

```text
F=C(u_i^(j) : 1<=i<N, j>=0),
```

with all jet variables algebraically independent. The group `U_H(C)` translates only the
zeroth-order variables `u_i`; the rational fixed field of this translation action is exactly the
field generated by the variables of order at least one. This proves `(3)`.

### Two-chart reconstruction

Projective covariance reduces two distinct hyperplanes to

```text
H_0={X_0=0},   H_1={X_1=0}.
```

In the first chart use coordinates `x_i=X_i/X_0`. In the second use

```text
u=1/x_1,
v_i=x_i/x_1,  i>=2.
```

The first horizon contains `x_i',x_i'',...`; the second contains `u',u'',...` and
`v_i',v_i'',...`. Projective inversion gives

```text
x_1=2x_1' / [x_1''/x_1' - u''/u'].                       (12)
```

For every transverse coordinate,

```text
x_i=[x_i' x_1-v_i' x_1^2]/x_1'.                          (13)
```

Differential genericity makes the denominators nonzero. Equations `(12)--(13)` place all source
coordinates in the join of the two horizons, proving `(4)`.

### Generated unipotent groups

The intersection in `(5)` is the fixed field of the group generated by the `U_(ker alpha_i)`.
Choose a basis so that independent members of the spanning family are

```text
alpha_i=e_i^*,   1<=i<=r.
```

The group `U_(ker e_i^*)` contains every elementary transvection `I+tE_(ji)` with `j != i`.
Together these generate `SL_r` on the first `r` coordinates and every lower-left shear from their
span to `W`. The resulting block matrices are exactly

```text
[ A  0 ]
[ B  I ],       A in SL_r,  B in Hom(C^r,W),              (14)
```

which is `G_S`. This proves `(6)--(7)`. Its dimension is

```text
(r^2-1)+r(N-r)=rN-1,
```

proving `(8)`. Applying the same generation result to `S+T` proves `(9)`.

### Faithfulness and rank recovery

The prolonged `PGL(V)` action on sufficiently high generic projective jets is generically free:
a generic osculating jet supplies a projective moving frame. Rosenlicht's dimension theorem then
gives `(11)` for every connected subgroup `G_S`. Strict inclusions among the `G_S` therefore give
strict reverse inclusions among their fixed fields. Generic orbit equality would force equality of
the corresponding connected subgroups, so `mathcal I_S=mathcal I_T` implies `S=T`. This proves
injectivity and the building statement.

## Physical interpretation

The theorem produces a literal projective topology of HT information contexts.

- a hyperplane selects an affine observer and its derivative horizon;
- any two distinct observers are complementary and reconstruct the source;
- common information among several observers records exactly their projective incidence rank;
- the deepest intersection is the full projective differential-invariant field.

The proper-subspace poset of `V*` is the spherical building for `PGL_N`, and also the incidence
skeleton underlying finite-dimensional quantum proposition geometry. The theorem realizes this
skeleton inside the HT subfield lattice. It supplies no Hermitian form, orthocomplement, positivity,
Born rule, or quantum state, so the result is not by itself a quantization theorem.

For `N=2`, the building reduces to the already proved projective-line horizon law: distinct
horizons have full join and common Schwarzian field. Higher `N` adds genuine incidence data that
does not exist in the scalar case.

## Parent-theory and novelty gate

- Projective moving frames and rational differential invariants are classical.
- Fixed fields of unipotent radicals and Tits buildings are classical, separately.
- The elementary-group generation in `(14)` is standard linear algebra.
- No checked source identifies affine-chart derivative horizons with the hyperplane building,
  proves the complementarity formula `(12)--(13)`, or recovers projective matroid rank through the
  intersection law `(5)--(11)`.

The combination is marked a novelty candidate pending a specialist literature audit. Its
significance is structural: it recovers an entire incidence geometry, rather than one invariant or
one reconstruction identity.

one-parameter orbit law that a density argument would need.  A transvection with row `x` and column
`j` sends `X_(k,l)` to `X_(k,l) - c X_(j,l)` when `k = x` and fixes it otherwise
(`linearJetAction_freeCoordinate_transvection`), so the dependence on the transvection coefficient
is affine linear, and `transvectionSL_mul_same` shows the coefficients compose additively: a
transvection orbit is a one-parameter additive family.

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/ProjectiveHorizonBaseChange.lean` and
`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/ProjectiveHorizonTransvectionDensity.lean` carry out the
density step.  Base change along a field extension of the constants embeds the jet field
(`jetBaseChange`, injective, carrying each jet coordinate to the corresponding one) and the
unimodular group (`slBaseChange`), and `jetBaseChange_smul` shows the two are compatible with the
prolonged action, so an identity of observers over the small field of constants can be read over
any larger one.  The density itself is polynomial: `transvectionOrbitPoly` records the orbit of a
jet polynomial under the one-parameter transvection family as a genuine polynomial in the
transvection coefficient, and `transvectionOrbitPoly_eval` says that evaluating at a constant
returns the substitution by that constant.  Over an infinite field of constants the constants are
therefore dense in the family: `transvectionOrbitPoly_eq_C_of_forall` shows a jet polynomial fixed
by every constant transvection has constant orbit polynomial, and
`transvectionOrbitPoly_cross_of_forall` gives the rational form, that the numerator and denominator
orbit families of a fixed rational observable are proportional as one-parameter families.  Both go
through the finiteness of the root set of a nonzero polynomial over the jet polynomial domain, so
they need no algebraic geometry.

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/ProjectiveHorizonGenericCoefficient.lean` removes the
obstruction that a moving-frame normalisation runs into.  The normalising element of a moving frame
has entries in the jet field, not in the constants, so the shear by it is not an element of the
observer group and invariance under the observer group does not transfer to it directly.
`transvectionSubstAt` is the shear by an arbitrary jet coefficient, `transvectionSubstAt_eq_eval`
identifies it with the orbit polynomial evaluated at that coefficient, and
`transvectionSubstAt_algebraMap` checks that a constant coefficient returns the observer
substitution.  Density then transfers invariance: `transvectionSubstAt_eq_of_forall` and
`transvectionSubstAt_cross_of_forall` show that a jet observable fixed by every constant
transvection of a fixed row and column is fixed by the shear with *any* jet coefficient, and
`horizonField_transvectionSubstAt_cross` states this for a horizon observable of a covector span.
A normalisation with entries in the jet field is therefore legitimate.

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/ProjectiveHorizonFrameInvariants.lean` and
`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/ProjectiveHorizonFrameGeneration.lean` carry out the
moving-frame construction for a span of any rank.  An observer of a span multiplies both the jet
frame and every jet column by the same unimodular block (`linearJetAction_jetFrame`,
`linearJetAction_jetColumn`, block determinant `det_observerBlock`), so the block cancels in the
frame coordinates: `frameCoord_invariant` and `frameCoord_mem_horizonField` show that every frame
coordinate is a horizon observable of the span.  For a direction outside the span an observer
changes its row by a combination of span rows (`linearJetAction_freeCoordinate_notMem`), and the
frame equation says that subtracting the frame reconstruction removes exactly that combination:
`transverseNormal` is the resulting normalised transverse jet and
`transverseNormal_mem_horizonField` shows it too is a horizon observable.

These two families reconstruct everything.  `freeCoordinate_mem_frameAdaptedAdjoin` shows that
every jet coordinate lies in the field generated by the horizon field of the span together with the
jet coordinates at the frame orders -- by the frame equation for a direction in the span, and by
the definition of the normalised transverse jet for a direction outside it -- and
`frameAdaptedGenerators_adjoin_eq_top` concludes that those generate the whole jet field.  The
block has `rN` elements (`card_initialJetBlock`).

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/ProjectiveHorizonFrameCodimension.lean` removes one more.
The jet determinant of the span is itself a horizon observable, so the block coordinates satisfy
the determinant relation over the horizon field, and solving it for one diagonal entry needs the
corresponding cofactor to be invertible.  It is: `adjugate_jetFrame_diag_ne_zero` proves the
diagonal cofactor nonzero by specialising the jet coordinates so that the jet frame becomes the
identity matrix, whereupon the cofactor becomes `1`.  Since a determinant of a matrix with entries
in a subfield stays in that subfield (`det_mem_intermediateField`), every cofactor of the column in
question lies in the field generated by the horizon field and the rest of the block, so
`freeCoordinate_diag_mem_reducedAdjoin` recovers the omitted coordinate and
`reducedFrameGenerators_adjoin_eq_top` shows that the horizon field together with the remaining
`rN-1` coordinates (`card_reducedJetBlockIndex`) already generates the whole jet field.  This is the
codimension `rN-1` of `(11)`, as an upper bound, for a span of any rank.

Two limits are worth stating exactly.  The independence is over the horizon algebra rather than the
horizon field; the two are equivalent, but the field form goes through
`IntermediateField.algebraicIndependent_adjoin_iff`, whose elaboration on this type exceeds a
million heartbeats, so it is not shipped.  And the general-rank statement of `(11)` for `r >= 2`
needs an explicit description of `mathcal I_S` for spans of rank at least two, which the present
model does not provide; that is the one remaining paper-level item on this card.  Neither is represented by an assumption.

## RH impact audit

`[POTENTIAL MULTICHANNEL ORGANISING PRINCIPLE; NOT RH PROGRESS]` If completed-zeta data admit a
source-pure differential-generic projective observation with several natural hyperplane gauges,
their horizon intersections would recover the gauges' incidence matroid and isolate the full
projective-invariant core. No such canonical multichannel projective observation has been proved,
so this currently gives neither a zero-free region nor a critical-line implication.
