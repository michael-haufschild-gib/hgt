## Set-up

Let `C` be an algebraically closed constant field of characteristic zero, let `V=C^N`, `N>=2`,
and let `X in P(V)` be differential-generic. In any affine chart its `N-1` coordinate jets are
algebraically independent over `C`; write their differential field as `F=C<X>`.

For a hyperplane `H=ker(alpha)` and `n>=1`, choose affine coordinates `u_H` whose infinity
hyperplane is `H` and define

```text
mathcal H_(H,n)=C<(u_H)^(n)>.
```

All derivatives of order at least `n` are included. Constant affine changes of `u_H` do not
change this field.

## Theorem: pairwise depth-two collapse

For every two distinct hyperplanes `H != K` and every `n>=2`,

```text
mathcal H_(H,n) intersection mathcal H_(K,n)=C,           (1)
mathcal H_(H,n) mathcal H_(K,n)=F.                        (2)
```

More generally, let `H_i=ker(alpha_i)`, put

```text
S=span(alpha_i) subset V*,
r=dim S,
W=S^perp.
```

If `r>=2`, then for every `n>=2`,

```text
intersection_i mathcal H_(H_i,n)=C.                       (3)
```

The condition is sharp. When `r=1`, every `H_i` is the same hyperplane and the intersection is
that proper depth-`n` horizon field, not `C`.

## Proof of the meet theorem

### 1. Truncated chart gauges

Normalize an `H`-chart by `alpha(X)=1`. The free affine arc has coordinates

```text
u(t)=sum_(k>=0) u_k t^k/k!.
```

The tail field `mathcal H_(H,n)` is the fixed field of independent translations of
`u_0,...,u_(n-1)`. These are ordinary rational-field automorphisms; no claim that they commute
with the source derivation is needed. Intrinsically their infinitesimal modes are

```text
t^k u_H,    0<=k<n,                                      (4)
```

where

```text
u_H={v tensor alpha : v in ker(alpha)} subset sl(V)       (5)
```

is the affine-chart translation radical.

Every common element in `(3)` is therefore annihilated by the modes `(4)` for every selected
hyperplane.

### 2. The generated constant algebra

Choose independent members of the normals as `e_1^*,...,e_r^*` and split

```text
V=Q direct_sum W,    dim Q=r.
```

The constant radicals contain all matrix units `E_(ji)` with `1<=i<=r` and `j!=i`. The units
with `j<=r` generate `sl(Q)`; those with `j>r` are every map from `Q` to `W`. Hence their Lie
algebra is

```text
g_S=sl(Q) semidirect Hom(Q,W)
   ={A in sl(V) : A|_W=0}.                               (6)
```

### 3. Perfectness begins exactly at normal rank two

For `r>=2`, the algebra `(6)` is perfect. Indeed `sl(Q)` is perfect. Choose an invertible
trace-zero `D in sl(Q)`, for example

```text
D=diag(1,...,1,-(r-1)).
```

For any `B in Hom(Q,W)`, the semidirect bracket with `D` gives

```text
[(D,0),(0,-B D^(-1))]=(0,B).                             (7)
```

Thus both summands lie in `[g_S,g_S]`, proving

```text
[g_S,g_S]=g_S.                                            (8)
```

For `r=1`, by contrast, `g_S=Hom(Q,W)` is abelian. This is the algebraic reason a single horizon
does not collapse.

### 4. Depth two generates all current modes

Let `L` be the Lie algebra generated by the degree-zero and degree-one modes supplied by the
selected depth-two horizons. Its degree-zero part contains `g_S` by `(6)`. The degree-one part is
stable under the adjoint action of `g_S` and contains all generating radicals. It therefore
contains the Lie ideal generated by those radicals, which is all of `g_S`. Hence

```text
g_S tensor 1 subset L,
g_S tensor t subset L.                                   (9)
```

Using perfectness,

```text
[g_S tensor t,g_S tensor t^k]
  =[g_S,g_S] tensor t^(k+1)
  =g_S tensor t^(k+1).                                  (10)
```

Induction on `k` now gives

```text
g_S[C[t]] subset L.                                      (11)
```

Every depth `n>=2` contains the same constant and linear modes, so `(11)` holds at every such
depth.

### 5. Polynomial currents erase every finite jet invariant

The connected group with Lie algebra `g_S` has block form

```text
[ A  0 ]
[ B  I ],    A in SL(Q), B in Hom(Q,W).                  (12)
```

It is transitive on

```text
Omega=P(V) minus P(W).                                   (13)
```

Indeed `SL(Q)` is transitive on nonzero vectors of `Q` for `r>=2`, and after matching the
`Q`-component, `B` changes the `W`-component arbitrarily.

Let `f` be a common rational invariant and choose `m` so that `f` depends only on jet
coefficients through order `m`. At a generic arc in `Omega`, the mode `t^m A` fixes all lower
coefficients and changes the `m`th coefficient in the tangent direction `A.X_0`. Transitivity in
`(13)` says these directions span the complete projective tangent space. Since `(11)` annihilates
`f`, every partial derivative of `f` with respect to its highest jet coefficient is zero.
Characteristic zero then makes `f` independent of that coefficient. Descending through
`m,m-1,...,0` leaves `f in C`. This proves `(3)` and therefore `(1)`.

## Proof that the join remains complete

Projective covariance reduces a pair to

```text
H={X_0=0},    K={X_1=0}.
```

Write the first chart as

```text
x=X_1/X_0,    y_a=X_a/X_0,
```

and the second as

```text
b=1/x,        v_a=b y_a.
```

The scalar all-depth inversion theorem reconstructs the complete differential field `C<x>` from
the order-`n` tails of `x` and `b`.

Fix a transverse coordinate `y=y_a`. Once `b` and all its jets are known, the horizon data give
`y^(m)` and `(by)^(m)` for every `m>=n`. For `m=n,...,2n-1`, subtract the already known high-jet
terms to obtain

```text
c_m=sum_(k=0)^(n-1) binom(m,k) b^(m-k) y^(k).             (14)
```

This is an `n` by `n` linear system for `y,...,y^(n-1)`. Its determinant is a nonzero differential
polynomial in `b`: formally setting every displayed jet of `b` to `1` gives the consecutive
Pascal minor

```text
(binom(n+i,j))_(0<=i,j<n),                                (15)
```

whose determinant is `1` by successive row differences and induction. The jets of the
differential-generic `b` are algebraically independent, so the determinant of `(14)` is nonzero.
Cramer's rule recovers every missing jet of `y`. Repeating over all transverse coordinates proves
`(2)`.
