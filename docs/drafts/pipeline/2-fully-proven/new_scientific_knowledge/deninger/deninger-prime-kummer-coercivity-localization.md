## Claim

Let the Tate-normalized positive generator on a prime circle have nonconstant Fourier rates

\[
  \lambda_k=2\pi |k|,\qquad k\in\mathbf Z\setminus\{0\}.
\]

Then all three statements below hold unconditionally.

1. **Uniform local gap.** Every finite nonconstant prime-circle packet satisfies
   \[
      \sum_{k\ne 0}\lambda_k |a_k|^2
      \ge 2\pi\sum_{k\ne 0}|a_k|^2,
   \]
   independently of prime, Fourier aperture, and packet dimension.

2. **Uniform incidence compression.** Let `A` be a positive operator on a finite real Hilbert
   packet `E` satisfying
   \[
      \langle Ax,x\rangle\ge \kappa\|x\|^2
   \]
   and let `L\subset E` be any linearized Milnor-incidence subspace.  With
   `g_L=P_LA|_L`,
   \[
      \|g_Lx\|\ge \kappa\|x\|\qquad(x\in L).
   \]
   In particular, for a Tate-normalized prime packet one may take `\kappa=2\pi`, uniformly in
   `L`.  Pure prime-oscillatory Calderón--conormal intersections therefore have no approximate
   zero modes.  Any compatible completion retaining these compressed operators stays bounded
   below on this sector.

3. **Global Kummer contrast.** The signed prime-log lattice
   \[
      \Lambda_{\log}=\left\{\sum_p m_p\log p:m_p\in\mathbf Z,
      \text{ finite support}\right\}
   \]
   has no positive uniform gap: for every `\varepsilon>0` it contains an `x` with
   `0<|x|<\varepsilon`.

Consequently a nonuniform global derived excess, Green radical, or Route-L `H^1` cannot be
attributed to isolated Tate-normalized prime oscillations.  It must enter through at least one of:

- signed multi-prime Kummer small divisors;
- horizontal residue or coarse/constant modes;
- cross-sector incidence coupling;
- failure of transition maps to intertwine the local contractions.

This is a localization theorem, not construction of global `H^1`.

## Proof

For `k\ne0`, `|k|\ge1`, hence `\lambda_k=2\pi|k|\ge2\pi`.  Multiplication by the nonnegative
coefficient `|a_k|^2` and summation proves (1).  Tate normalization is load-bearing: the original
circle period `\log p` has been absorbed, so the lower bound has no `p`-dependence.

For (2), orthogonal projection does not change pairing against a vector of `L`.  Thus, for
`x\in L`,

\[
  \kappa\|x\|^2
  \le \langle Ax,x\rangle
  =\langle P_LAx,x\rangle
  \le \|P_LAx\|\,\|x\|.
\]

The conclusion is trivial for `x=0`; otherwise divide by `\|x\|`.  This also proves injectivity and
the inverse estimate `\|g_L^{-1}\|\le\kappa^{-1}` on a finite packet.  The estimate is independent
of incidence angle because coercivity is inherited by every compression.

For (3), `\log 2/\log 3` is irrational by unique factorization.  Therefore the additive subgroup
generated by `\log 2` and `\log 3`, hence `\Lambda_{\log}`, is dense in `\mathbf R`.  Every interval
`(0,\varepsilon)` meets it.  Equivalently, no `\kappa>0` can bound the absolute value of every
nonzero signed prime-log frequency from below.
