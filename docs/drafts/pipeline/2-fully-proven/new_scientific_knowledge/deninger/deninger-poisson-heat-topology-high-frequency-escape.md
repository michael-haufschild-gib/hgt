## Exact theorem

Let (H) be a Hilbert space, let (A\geq0) be self-adjoint, and
suppose there are orthonormal vectors (e_n) with

\[
 Ae_n=\lambda_ne_n,
 \qquad \lambda_n\longrightarrow+\infty .                    \tag{1}
\]

For (r>0), define the positive-time Green seminorm

\[
 q_r(x)^2
 =\langle x,Ae^{-2rA}x\rangle .                              \tag{2}
\]

Then

\[
 q_r(e_n)^2=\lambda_ne^{-2r\lambda_n}\longrightarrow0
 \qquad\text{for every fixed }r>0.                            \tag{3}
\]

Consequently (e_n\to0) in the locally convex topology generated by
the family \(\{q_r:r>0\}\), while \(\|e_n\|=1\).  That topology is
strictly weaker than the Hilbert topology.  More generally, it cannot

\[
 \inf_n p(e_n)>0.                                              \tag{4}
\]

### Proof

For fixed (r>0), spectral calculus and `(1)` give the displayed
identity in `(3)`.  Put (u=2r\lambda_n).  Then

\[
 \lambda_ne^{-2r\lambda_n}
 ={1\over2r},u e^{-u}.
\]

Since (u\to+\infty) and (ue^{-u}\to0), `(3)` follows.  Convergence
for every generating seminorm is exactly convergence in the family
topology.  Norm convergence fails because every vector has norm one.
If `(4)` holds and the family topology equalled the source topology,
continuity of (p) would force (p(e_n)\to0), a contradiction.

No compact-resolvent assumption is used; one escaping orthonormal
eigen-sequence suffices.
