## 1. Gaussian Kummer probes

For \(\mu>0\), put
\[
 g_\mu(x)=e^{-\pi\mu^2x^2},\qquad x\in\mathbb R.                \tag{1}
\]
With Meyer's Fourier convention,
\[
 \mathcal Fg_\mu(y)
 =
 \mu^{-1}g_{\mu^{-1}}(y).                                     \tag{2}
\]
For \(\operatorname{Re}s>0\),
\[
 \mathcal Mg_\mu(s)
 :=
 \int_0^\infty g_\mu(x)x^s\,d^\times x
 =
 \frac12\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\mu^{-s}.                         \tag{3}
\]

If \(D_\ell\) is the finite-stage Kummer phase generator of
`DAC_KUMMER_PHASE_POISSON_GREEN_BOUNDARY`, then
\[
 e^{-\pi x^2D_\ell^2}\chi_a
 =
 g_{|\ell(a)|}(x)\chi_a.                                      \tag{4}
\]
Thus `(1)` is a heat vector of the already-constructed source phase
operator.

## 2. Endpoint-corrected core

Let
\[
 f(x)=\sum_{j=1}^N c_jg_{\mu_j}(x).                            \tag{5}
\]
It is even Schwartz and
\[
 f(0)=\sum_jc_j,\qquad
 \mathcal Ff(0)=\sum_j\frac{c_j}{\mu_j}.                       \tag{6}
\]

The Gaussian combination `(5)` belongs to \(H_\cap\) exactly when
\[
 \sum_jc_j=0,\qquad
 \sum_j\frac{c_j}{\mu_j}=0.                                   \tag{7}

These are respectively cone-apex restriction and Kähler/Fourier

### Proof

Equation `(6)` follows from \(g_\mu(0)=1\) and `(2)` at zero.
Meyer's definition of \(H_\cap\) gives the equivalence with `(7)`.

## 3. Power orbit and completed Mellin factor

For \(n\ge1\),
\[
 g_{n\mu}(x)=g_\mu(nx).                                       \tag{8}
\]
Hence the Kummer power orbit \(a\mapsto na\), for which
\(|\ell(na)|=n|\ell(a)|\), is exactly Meyer's dilation orbit.

For \(\operatorname{Re}s>1\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal M\!\left(\sum_{n\ge1}g_\mu(n\,\cdot)\right)(s)
 &=
 \frac12\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\mu^{-s}
 \sum_{n\ge1}n^{-s}\\
 &=
 \frac12\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)\mu^{-s}.                 \tag{9}
\end{aligned}
\]
For every endpoint-corrected `(5)`,
\[
 \mathcal M(Zf)(s)
 =
 \frac12\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)
 \sum_jc_j\mu_j^{-s}.                                         \tag{10}
\]
Poisson summation and `(7)` extend \(Zf\) to \(H_-\); `(10)` then
continues through the all-strip Mellin transform.

### Proof

Equation `(8)` is direct.  On \(\operatorname{Re}s>1\), all terms are
positive in absolute value after replacing coefficients by their
moduli, so Mellin integration and summation may be interchanged.
Equation `(3)` gives `(9)`, and linearity gives `(10)`.
Membership in \(H_-\) is Meyer's Poisson theorem for \(H_\cap\).

## 4. Finite-jet universality of the source core

The exponential polynomials
\[
 P_f(s)=\sum_jc_j\mu_j^{-s}                                    \tag{11}
\]
coming from `(7)` satisfy
\[
 P_f(0)=P_f(1)=0.                                              \tag{12}
\]
Conversely, let \(S\subset\mathbb C\setminus\{0,1\}\) be finite and
fix finite jet data at every point of \(S\).  There is an
endpoint-corrected Gaussian combination whose `(11)` realizes those
jets.

Indeed choose \(\delta>0\) so that the numbers
\[
 e^{-\delta s}\qquad(s\in S\cup\{0,1\})
\]
are distinct.  Hermite interpolation gives a polynomial \(P\) with
zeros at \(1,e^{-\delta}\) and the prescribed transformed jets at
the remaining points.  Expanding
\[
 P(e^{-\delta s})
 =
 \sum_jc_je^{-j\delta s}
\]
gives `(11)` with \(\mu_j=e^{j\delta}\) and satisfies `(12)`.

This universality concerns the source degree-zero domain and its exact
image under \(Z\).  It does not claim that these exact Gaussian
vectors represent nonzero \(H^1\)-classes.
