## The realization theorem `[PROVED HERE]`

its facets. Index an auxiliary positive real support by the primes and put

```text
lambda_p=log p,   epsilon_p=2^(-p),   p prime.               (S0)
```

The numbers `(lambda_p)` are `Q`-linearly independent by unique factorization, and
`sum_p epsilon_p/(1+lambda_p)<infinity`. Choose a bounded positive reweighting `(exp(u_p))` such
that the meromorphic Herglotz function

```text
g(z)=sum_(p prime) epsilon_p exp(u_p)/(lambda_p-z)           (S1)
```

has distinct complex translates

```text

```

that are differentially algebraically independent over `C(z)`.  Such reweightings form a comeager set
by the discrete-support corollary in
`herglotz-full-translation-ht-krein-index-orthogonality.md`.

This source is provably outside every standard finite-order multiple-Gamma hierarchy. The polar
divisor of a finite-order Barnes multiple gamma, multiple sine, or multiple elliptic gamma
logarithmic derivative is contained in a finite-rank affine `Z`-module generated by its finitely
many periods; the same remains true after finitely many translates, products, or quotients.  The
positive residues in (S1) make every `log p` an uncancelled pole, while `{log p:p prime}` has
infinite `Q`-linear rank. Adding or removing finitely many rational poles does not change this obstruction.
Thus `g` is not a disguised finite-order multiple-Gamma logarithmic derivative.  Independently,
the nonpure relation complex below is not the uniform skeleton of any fixed hierarchy order.
For the elliptic hierarchy the finite-rank divisor statement is immediate from Nishizawa's product

A **34** (2001), 7411--7421, DOI `10.1088/0305-4470/34/36/320`.  The Barnes case is the analogous
finite-period Weierstrass product.

## Proof

### Lemma 1: joint facet-orbit freeness

The family

```text
Y={T^nY_A:A in Fac(Delta), n in Z^A}                          (6)
```

is differentially algebraically independent over `D`.

Suppose otherwise.  A differential relation involves only finitely many members of (6) and only
finitely many jets.  Clear its rational coefficient denominators.  The result is a nonzero
polynomial `Q` whose coefficients lie in `C[z_A:A in Fac(Delta)]` and whose differential variables
are the finite jets of

```text
g(z_A+sum_(v in A)n_v omega_v).                              (7)
```

Restrict the product domain to a diagonal affine line

```text
z_A=z+gamma_A.                                               (8)
```

The constants `gamma_A` can be chosen in `Q(i)` to satisfy two conditions simultaneously.  First,
at least one nonzero coefficient of `Q` remains a nonzero polynomial in `z` after (8).  Indeed, for
any chosen nonzero coefficient `c`, the substitutions for which `c(z+gamma_A)_A` vanishes
identically form a proper algebraic subset.  A nonzero polynomial over `C` cannot vanish on the
whole grid `Q(i)^Fac(Delta)`, so this proper subset does not exhaust that grid.  Second, all the
finitely many numbers

```text
gamma_A+sum_(v in A)n_v omega_v                              (9)
```

appearing in the relation are distinct.  For different facets this only excludes finitely many
affine hyperplanes in the `gamma_A`, which likewise cannot exhaust the infinite rational grid;
within one facet, equality in (9) would contradict the `Q`-linear independence of the `omega_v`.
Every number in (9) then lies in `Gamma_Delta`.

Under (8), `delta` becomes `d/dz`.  Because the arguments (9) are distinct, distinct differential
monomials of `Q` remain distinct, and the first choice ensures that the specialized differential
polynomial is nonzero.  The assumed relation therefore specializes, on a sufficiently high upper
half-plane, to a nonzero differential relation over `C(z)` among finitely many distinct
`Gamma_Delta`-translates of `g`.  This contradicts the restricted translation freeness of `g`.
Hence (6) is
jointly differentially algebraically independent over `D`. `QED`

### Lemma 2: the exact operator kernel

For a facet `A`, define

```text
rho_A:R -> S[t_v^(+-1):v in A]
```

by `rho_A(t_v)=t_v` for `v in A` and `rho_A(t_v)=1` for `v notin A`.  Let `rho` be the product of
the `rho_A`.  Then

```text
ker(rho)=I_Delta.                                            (10)
```

In the polynomial ring `S[x_v:v in V]`, the standard facet decomposition is

```text
I_Delta=intersection_(A in Fac(Delta)) (x_v:v notin A).      (11)
```

For completeness, a monomial `x^a` is outside the right side exactly when its support is contained
in some facet, equivalently when its support is a face of `Delta`, equivalently when it is not
divisible by a minimal-nonface monomial.  Since the monomials form an `S`-basis, this proves (11)
coefficient by coefficient without any field hypothesis on `S`.

Now localize by all `t_v=1+x_v`.  Localization is flat and commutes with the finite intersection in
(11).  The localized polynomial ring is `R`, the localized facet quotient sets precisely the
variables outside `A` to zero, and no `1+x_v` becomes zero there.  This gives (10).

For every `P in R`, (1) gives

```text
P(T)F=sum_A rho_A(P)(T)Y_A.                                 (12)
```

If (12) vanishes, Lemma 1 makes the finite collection of translated jets occurring in the sum
algebraically independent over `D`; hence every coefficient is zero and every `rho_A(P)=0`.
The converse is immediate.  Thus

```text
P(T)F=0 iff P in I_Delta.                                   (13)
```

Taking `P=x_J=prod_(v in J)x_v` proves `F_J=0` exactly for nonfaces.  For a face, (12) is a nonzero
constant-coefficient differential-linear combination of members of the free family (6), so it is
differentially transcendental over `D`. `QED`

### Lemma 3: no nonlinear rank loss

Let `X_1,...,X_q` be differential indeterminates over a differential field `K` of characteristic
zero with constant field containing `C`, and let `M` be an `m by q` matrix over `C[delta]`.  If
`Z=M X`, then

```text
dtrdeg_K K<Z_1,...,Z_m>_delta=rank_(C(delta)) M.              (14)
```

Indeed, Smith normal form over the Euclidean domain `C[delta]` gives unimodular matrices `U,V` such
that `UMV` is diagonal with exactly `r=rank_(C(delta))M` nonzero diagonal entries.  Unimodular row
operations replace the generators by differential-polynomially invertible combinations and hence
preserve the generated differential field.  Unimodular column operations are differential
automorphisms of the free differential field `K<X_1,...,X_q>`.  It remains to consider

```text
L_1(delta)X_1,...,L_r(delta)X_r,
```

with every `L_i` nonzero.  If `d_i` is the order of `L_i`, its highest jet is a nonzero constant
multiple of `delta^(d_i)X_i` plus lower jets.  Substitution into a putative differential polynomial
has a unique leading-jet term and cannot vanish.  The displayed elements are therefore jointly
differentially algebraically independent.  This proves (14). `QED`

For the operators `P_1,...,P_m`, collect the finite set of facet translates occurring in all
expressions (12) into a vector `X`.  Lemma 1 makes its coordinates differential indeterminates over
`D`.  The coefficient matrix `M` over `S` has as its `i`-th row the coefficient vector of
`rho(P_i)`.  By (10), the injection

```text
R/I_Delta -> product_A S[t_v^(+-1):v in A]                  (15)
```

identifies the `C(delta)`-rank of those rows with the dimension of the span of the `Pbar_i` in
(5).  Apply (14) to obtain (4).  This completes the realization theorem. `QED`

## Explicit connected nonpure two-thick realization

Take

```text
V={0,1,2,3,4,5}
```

and let the facets be

```text
{0,1}, {1,2}, {2,0},
{0,3,4}, {0,3,5}, {0,4,5}, {3,4,5}.                         (16)
```

This is the boundary of a triangle and the boundary of a tetrahedron joined at the vertex `0`; its
realization is `S^1 wedge S^2`.  It is connected and nonpure because its facets have cardinalities
two and three.

It is two-thick in the exact sense that every nonfacet face `J` has distinct vertices `q,u notin J`
for which both `J union {q}` and `J union {u}` remain faces.  The complete case split is finite and
structural:

- for `J=empty`, take any two vertices in one displayed edge;
- for `J={0},{1},{2}`, take the other two triangle vertices;
- for `J={3},{4},{5}`, take the other two vertices among `{3,4,5}`;
- every remaining nonfacet face is an edge of the tetrahedral boundary; the two vertices of
  `{0,3,4,5}\J` extend it to its two incident triangular facets.

There are no other cases: a face is contained in one of the displayed edges or triangles, so a
nonfacet face has cardinality at most two, and the three cycle edges are already facets.

Its minimal nonfaces are

```text
{1,3}, {1,4}, {1,5}, {2,3}, {2,4}, {2,5},
{0,1,2}, {0,3,4,5}.                                         (17)
```

Indeed, the six pairs are precisely the cross pairs between `{1,2}` and `{3,4,5}`.  If a set has
no cross pair, it lies in `{0,1,2}` or in `{0,3,4,5}`; inside those two vertex sets the only
minimal nonfaces are respectively the full triangle and full tetrahedron.  This proves both that
every set in (17) is minimal and that the list is exhaustive.

Therefore its structural ideal is

```text
I_Delta=(x_1x_3,x_1x_4,x_1x_5,x_2x_3,x_2x_4,x_2x_5,
         x_0x_1x_2,x_0x_3x_4x_5).                           (18)
```

The circuit degrees `2,3,4` and the mixed facet dimensions show directly that this is not the
uniform simplex-skeleton matroid of a multiple-Gamma hierarchy.  Equations (1)--(4) give the
required non-multiple-Gamma analytic realization and its full nonlinear differential matroid.

## Positive law extracted from the former no-go

The theorem replaces the negative sentence "hypertranscendence does not determine a useful
relation geometry" with the following positive universality law:

> **Finite constraint universality.** Maximal complex-translation hypertranscendence is compatible
> with every finite simplicial relation geometry, including every mixture of facet dimensions and
> minimal-circuit degrees.

The prime-log source supplies a second positive law at the carrier level:

> **Finite-jet Zariski fullness.** At every strictly positive prime-log mass configuration, the
> admissible positive cone has full algebraic tangent freedom on every finite translated actual
> differential-jet packet.  Every proper algebraic finite-jet condition can be escaped by an arbitrarily small
> finitely supported mass perturbation without changing the pole carrier or losing positivity.

This statement is proved and formalized in `positive-prime-log-polynomial-jet-escape`.  Its
algebraic and interpolation mechanisms are classical and are classified as a bridge; the
scientifically new candidate on this card remains the realized constraint geometry.

This forces a positive requirement on any RH construction built from HT relation complexes:

> **Arithmetic source-selection requirement.** An RH-capable HT construction must carry a
> distinguished source-sensitive marking that selects its relation complex and its positivity
> orientation.  The marking must survive as extra data beyond the unpointed HT field and abstract
> translation action, and it must discriminate the zeta carrier from the facet-sewn universal
> realizations.

This is a logical consequence, not an RH no-go.  Hypertranscendence supplies capacity; the
arithmetic source must supply selection.  Candidate markings include an Euler-factor residue
orientation, a canonical positive measure or kernel cone, a functional-equation polarization, or a
prime-indexed compatibility class.  A successful construction must prove that its marking is intrinsic and
cannot be changed by the rational gauge freedoms already exhibited in the Herglotz theorem.

Finite-jet Zariski fullness sharpens this requirement.  If the marking is stable under small
positive carrier-preserving mass perturbations, then it cannot be a proper algebraic condition on
any finite jet packet.  Its finite-jet Zariski closure is forced to be the whole packet space.
Therefore an RH-capable marking must be carried by an infinite compatibility layer: a global Euler
product law, a functional-equation polarization across the whole carrier, a boundary kernel cone,
or an inverse-limit prime class.  Positivity and the pole support alone do not provide that layer.

The upper-end carrier now makes the functional-equation option geometric.  Complex translations
preserve one end and commute with differentiation, whereas `s |-> 1-s` exchanges the upper and
lower ends and satisfies the formally proved signed law `R D=-D R` on their meromorphic fields.
Hence an RH-capable polarization must be a two-ended sewing carrying this sign together with the
Euler marking.  The generic HT source fills each one-ended finite differential window; the
arithmetic selector must constrain how the two windows are identified globally.

The result therefore does not furnish a carrier for `zeta`.  It identifies what a carrier theorem
must positively add: a canonical pointing of the otherwise universal constraint geometry.
