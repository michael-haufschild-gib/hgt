## Exact translation-annihilator theorem `[PROVED HERE]`

For every `J` of size `r`, the hierarchy equations give

`product_(j in J) x_j Psi_r=Psi_0(z;tau_(J^c))`,

### Order-one orbit lemma

Fix a two-element parameter set `L={a,b}` and write

`H_L=Z+Z tau_a+Z tau_b`.

Let `J={0,...,r}\L`. If

`0 ne Q in C[t_j^(+-1) : j in J]`,

then

`Q(T)Psi_1(z;tau_L)`

is HT.

If `J` is empty this is a nonzero scalar multiple of the HT function `Psi_1`. Otherwise first suppose the augmentation `Q(1,...,1)` is zero. Expanding `Q` gives a nonzero linear combination of translates whose coefficients sum to zero. Rational independence makes the translate classes distinct modulo `H_L`, so this is a nonzero linear combination of distinct nonzero-coset defects of `Psi_1`. The order-one defect matroid makes it HT.

If the augmentation is nonzero and `Q(T)Psi_1` were DA, choose any `j in J`. Its `tau_j`-difference would be

`x_j Q(T)Psi_1`.

The Laurent polynomial `x_jQ` is nonzero and has augmentation zero, so the preceding case makes this difference HT, contradicting translation-closure of DA functions. This proves the lemma.

`Q=P|_(x_a=x_b=0)`

`x_J=product_(j in J)x_j`

`x_J P(T)Psi_r=P(T)Psi_1(z;tau_L)`.

Modulo DA functions, every term of `P-Q` vanishes: it is divisible by `x_a` or `x_b`, and either operator sends `Psi_1(z;tau_L)` to a theta logarithmic derivative. Hence

`x_J P(T)Psi_r = Q(T)Psi_1(z;tau_L) mod Mer_DA`.

The order-one orbit lemma makes the right side HT. Therefore `x_JP(T)Psi_r` is HT. If `P(T)Psi_r` were DA, applying `x_J` would preserve DA, contradiction. Thus `P notin Ann_DA(Psi_r)`.

This proves the reverse inclusion and the exact annihilator formula.

## Transverse-depth corollary `[PROVED HERE, CONDITIONAL ONLY ON THE ORDER-ONE ATOMIC THEOREM]`

For every `r>=1` and every multi-index `m`,

Equivalently:

1. if `s(m)<r`, then `D_m Psi_r` is hypertranscendental;

In particular, for every `r>=2`, every `N>=0`, and every parameter direction `j`,

`Delta_(tau_j)^N Psi_r`

is HT, while a product of first differences in any `r` distinct parameter directions is DA. Differential-algebraic depth is therefore transverse, not iterative.

## Proof

### 1. `r` distinct directions are sufficient

For distinct indices `j_1,...,j_k`, the hierarchy equations commute and give

`Delta_(tau_(j_1)) ... Delta_(tau_(j_k)) Psi_r=Psi_(r-k)^(j_1,...,j_k)`,

where the superscript means that those parameters are omitted.

If `s(m)>=r`, choose `r` distinct indices in `supp(m)`. Applying one difference in each of them reduces `Psi_r` to `Psi_0`, the logarithmic derivative of a theta function with the single remaining parameter. This is DA. Every remaining factor of `D_m` is a translation difference, and translations and differences preserve differential algebraicity. Hence `D_m Psi_r` is DA.

### 2. Fewer directions cannot suffice

Assume `s=s(m)<r` and put `S=supp(m)`. Choose a set

`K subset {0,...,r}\S`

of size `r-1-s`. Then the complement

`L={0,...,r}\(S union K)`

has exactly two elements.

Suppose for contradiction that `D_m Psi_r` is DA. Applying the additional DA-preserving operator

`product_(k in K) Delta_(tau_k)`

would produce another DA function. Use one difference in every direction of `S union K` to descend through the hierarchy. The resulting function is

`P_S Psi_1(z;tau_L)`,

where

`P_S=product_(j in S) (T_(tau_j)-1)^(m_j-1)`.

Here `Psi_1(z;tau_L)` is the order-one elliptic-gamma logarithmic derivative with the two parameters indexed by `L`.

If every `m_j=1`, including the case `S` is empty, then `P_S=1`; the resulting function is `Psi_1`, which is HT by the order-one theorem. This contradicts DA.

Otherwise expand

`P_S=sum_nu c_nu T_(a_nu)`,

`a_nu=sum_(j in S) nu_j tau_j`, `0<=nu_j<=m_j-1`.

Since at least one factor `T_(tau_j)-1` occurs,

`sum_nu c_nu=P_S(1,...,1)=0`.

Distinct exponent vectors `nu` give distinct cosets

`a_nu + H_L`, `H_L=Z+sum_(ell in L) Z tau_ell`,

because `1,tau_0,...,tau_r` are `Q`-linearly independent. Therefore

`P_S Psi_1=sum_(nu ne 0) c_nu Delta_(a_nu) Psi_1`

is a nonzero constant linear combination of order-one defects from distinct nonzero `H_L`-cosets. The order-one defect-coset matroid says those defects are differentially algebraically independent. Their nonzero linear combination cannot be DA. This is again a contradiction.

Thus `D_m Psi_r` is not DA whenever `s(m)<r`, proving the exact threshold.
