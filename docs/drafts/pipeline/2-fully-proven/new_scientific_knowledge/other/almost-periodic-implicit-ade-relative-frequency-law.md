# Almost-periodic implicit ADE relative-frequency law

- **ID:** `almost-periodic-implicit-ade-relative-frequency-law`
- **Stage:** 2-fully-proven
- **Type:** THEOREM (paper) / GENERAL HYPERTRANSCENDENCE LANDSCAPE
- **Scientific status:** `[PROVED] [CANDIDATE NEW SCIENTIFIC KNOWLEDGE]`
- **Novelty status:** provisional pending independent expert proof and literature review
- **Statement:** A holomorphic uniformly almost-periodic solution of a finite implicit algebraic
  differential equation can add only finitely many rationally independent Bohr frequencies beyond
  the equation's almost-periodic coefficient field, even when the equation is singular and its
  initial-value problem is not globally unique. The same relative finite-rank law holds for the
  solution's zero-divisor distribution.

## Setup

Fix the horizontal strip

```text
S_eta={z in C: |Im z|<eta},  eta>0.                         (1)
```

Let `HAP(S_eta)` denote the holomorphic functions whose real translates are relatively compact for
uniform convergence on every closed substrip. Let

```text
a=(a_alpha)_(alpha in F) in HAP(S_eta)^F                  (2)
```

be a finite coefficient tuple, where `F` is a finite subset of `N^(n+1)`. Define

```text
P_a(z;Y_0,...,Y_n)
  =sum_(alpha in F) a_alpha(z) product_(k=0)^n Y_k^(alpha_k).  (3)
```

Let `f in HAP(S_eta)` satisfy

```text
P_a(z;f(z),f'(z),...,f^(n)(z))=0.                         (4)
```

Write `Sep_a=partial P_a/partial Y_n` and assume the chosen presentation is regular along `f` in
the differential-algebraic sense:

```text
Sep_a(z;f(z),f'(z),...,f^(n)(z)) is not identically zero. (5)
```

Condition `(5)` allows isolated and moving singular times. It does not assume that `(3)` is solved
for `Y_n`, that one real time is regular for every translate limit, or that the implicit equation
has a globally unique initial-value problem.

For an almost-periodic tuple `u`, let `Gamma(u)` be the additive group generated by all of its Bohr
frequencies. Put

```text
Gamma(a,f)=Gamma(a)+Gamma(f).                              (6)
```

## Relative finite-frequency theorem

`[PROVED HERE]` Under `(1)`--`(5)`, there is an integer `m>=1` such that

```text
rank_Q (Gamma(a,f)/Gamma(a)) <= 2m(n+1)<infinity.          (7)
```

Equivalently,

```text
rank_Q (Gamma(f)/(Gamma(f) intersect Gamma(a)))<infinity. (8)
```

If `f` is not identically zero, its almost-periodic zero-divisor distribution satisfies the same
relative finite-rank conclusion.

## 1. Joint hull and coefficient factor

Let `X=X_(a,f)` be the joint real-translation hull of `(a,f)`, and let `A=X_a` be the translation
hull of the coefficient tuple. Bohr hull theory makes `X` and `A` compact abelian groups with dense
real translation orbits. In particular they are minimal translation systems. Projection onto the
coefficient coordinate is a continuous surjective homomorphism

```text
pi:X->A.                                                    (9)
```

For `x=(b,g) in X`, local uniform convergence and Cauchy convergence of derivatives give

```text
P_b(z;g(z),g'(z),...,g^(n)(z))=0.                         (10)
```

Thus every joint translate limit satisfies the corresponding limit equation; it need not satisfy
the original nonautonomous equation with coefficient tuple `a`.

## 2. Minimality removes an everywhere-singular hull fibre

Define

```text
Z={
  (b,g) in X:
  Sep_b(z;g(z),g'(z),...,g^(n)(z)) is identically zero
}.                                                         (11)
```

The set `Z` is closed because all derivatives converge locally uniformly on the hull. It is
translation invariant because translating `(b,g)` translates the separant evaluation. The original
pair `(a,f)` does not belong to `Z` by `(5)`. Minimality of `X` therefore gives

```text
Z=empty.                                                   (12)
```

Every joint hull member consequently has at least one regular real time. A nonzero holomorphic
separant evaluation cannot vanish on the whole real axis without vanishing identically.

For `t in R`, put

```text
U_t={
  (b,g) in X:
  Sep_b(t;g(t),g'(t),...,g^(n)(t))!=0
}.                                                         (13)
```

These open sets cover `X`. Compactness supplies real times `t_1,...,t_m` such that

```text
X=U_(t_1) union ... union U_(t_m).                         (14)
```

This finite regular-sample atlas is the replacement for a globally unique flow.

## 3. Finite observations separate every coefficient fibre

Define

```text
J:X->A x C^(m(n+1)),
J(b,g)=
  (b,(g^(k)(t_j))_(1<=j<=m,0<=k<=n)).                     (15)
```

The map is continuous and injective. Suppose `J(b,g)=J(c,h)`. Then `b=c`. Choose `j` with
`(b,g) in U_(t_j)`. The two order-`n` jets at `t_j` agree, and the common separant value is nonzero.
The analytic implicit-function theorem therefore solves the common nonautonomous relation `(10)`
near that jet as

```text
y^(n)=Phi(z,y,y',...,y^(n-1)).                             (16)
```

Both `g` and `h` lie on this same local branch. Their lower initial jets agree, so uniqueness for
the explicit analytic equation `(16)` gives `g=h` near `t_j`. The identity theorem gives `g=h` on
the whole strip. Hence `(b,g)=(c,h)`.

The coefficient coordinate in `(15)` can have infinite topological dimension. The correct finite
object is therefore not `X`, but the kernel

```text
K=ker(pi).                                                  (17)
```

Restricting the finite jet coordinate of `(15)` to `K` gives a continuous injection

```text
K->C^(m(n+1)).                                              (18)
```

Since `K` is compact, `(18)` is a topological embedding. Consequently

```text
dim K<=2m(n+1).                                             (19)
```

## 4. Relative Pontryagin rank

Pontryagin duality applied to `(9)` gives the exact sequence

```text
0 -> dual(A) -> dual(X) -> dual(K) -> 0.                   (20)
```

The duals of the two Bohr hulls are their frequency groups:

```text
dual(A)=Gamma(a),
dual(X)=Gamma(a,f).                                        (21)
```

For a finite-dimensional compact abelian group, covering dimension equals the rational rank of its
character group. Equations `(19)`--`(21)` give

```text
rank_Q (Gamma(a,f)/Gamma(a))
  =rank_Q dual(K)
  =dim K
  <=2m(n+1),                                               (22)
```

which proves `(7)` and `(8)`.

## 5. Divisor relative-frequency law

Assume `f` is not identically zero. No member `(b,g)` of the minimal joint hull has `g` identically
zero: the set of such members would be a nonempty closed invariant subset of `X`. Hence

```text
Div(g)=(1/(2pi)) Delta log|g|                              (23)
```

is defined for every joint hull member. Local uniform convergence of nonzero holomorphic functions
implies distributional convergence of `(23)`. The map

```text
(b,g) |-> (b,Div(g))                                       (24)
```

is therefore a continuous translation-equivariant observable on `X`. Every Bohr frequency of a
continuous equivariant observable on a compact abelian rotation belongs to `dual(X)`. Hence

```text
Gamma(a,Div(f)) <= Gamma(a,f).                             (25)
```

Passing to the quotient by `Gamma(a)` and using `(22)` gives

```text
rank_Q (
  Gamma(a,Div(f))/Gamma(a)
) <= rank_Q (Gamma(a,f)/Gamma(a))<infinity.                (26)
```

The same conclusion holds for every continuous translation-equivariant observable of the divisor.

## Differential-algebraic corollary

Let `R` be a prescribed differential integral domain contained in `HAP(S_eta)`, and let
`L=Frac(R)`. If `f in HAP(S_eta)` is differentially algebraic over `L`, choose an annihilating
differential polynomial first of minimal order and then of minimal degree in its highest derivative.
Its separant does not vanish identically along `f`: otherwise the separant would be an annihilator
of the same order and smaller highest-derivative degree. After clearing denominators, the equation
has a finite coefficient tuple in `R` and satisfies `(5)`.

Let `Gamma(R)` be the group generated by the Bohr spectra of all elements of `R`. Since the finite
coefficient group is contained in `Gamma(R)`, `(7)` gives

```text
rank_Q (
  (Gamma(R)+Gamma(f))/Gamma(R)
)<infinity.                                                (27)
```

The contrapositive is the relative hypertranscendence law:

> If a holomorphic uniformly almost-periodic function contributes infinitely many rationally
> independent Bohr frequencies beyond a prescribed almost-periodic differential coefficient
> domain, then it is hypertranscendental over that domain's fraction field.

This is full differential transcendence over the stated coefficient field, not merely failure of
an autonomous equation.

## Attacks and boundary cases

1. **Infinite-rank coefficient hull.** The total hull `X` may have infinite dimension. The proof
   uses `K=ker(pi)`, whose finite-dimensional embedding is independent of the size of `A`.
2. **Nonunique implicit equation.** Global uniqueness is never assumed. Equality is compared at one
   of finitely many regular samples and only then reduced locally to an explicit equation.
3. **Singular presentation.** An equation such as `(y'-b)^2=0` has identically vanishing separant
   along its solution, but its minimal relation `y'-b=0` does not. The differential-algebraic
   corollary always uses a minimal relation.
4. **No common regular time.** Compactness produces the finite atlas `(14)`; a common time is not
   required.
5. **Algebraic frequency division.** A relation such as `f^q=a` can add rational divisions of base
   frequencies. These are torsion in the quotient and do not contradict the rational-rank theorem.
6. **Zero solution.** The function-frequency theorem is trivial for `f=0`; the divisor statement is
   asserted only for nonzero `f`.
7. **Merely Besicovitch almost periodic.** The required compact hull can fail, so the theorem is
   deliberately restricted to uniform Bohr almost periodicity.
8. **Gamma/Stirling coefficients.** Vertical translates of the Gamma field are not uniformly almost
   periodic on a fixed strip. The theorem does not settle the noncompact archimedean base.

## HT-landscape and RH-route consequence

Equation `(27)` says that a finite algebraic differential state cannot create an infinite new
frequency module. It can only transport the coefficient module and add finitely many rational
directions.

Therefore any finite-state implementation of a Connes, Hilbert--Polya, de Branges,
Nyman--Beurling, Weil, or Li program that claims to generate the full prime-log module from an
almost-periodic coefficient field missing infinitely many prime directions is impossible, even if
the implementation uses a singular implicit DAE to avoid ordinary flow uniqueness.

This does not exclude the complete named criteria or programs. They may place the infinite prime
module directly in the coefficients, use an infinite-dimensional operator, retain the full divisor
as a constraint, or use the non-almost-periodic Gamma completion. The exact no-go is

```text
finite implicit AP algebraic differential state
  + coefficient module missing infinite prime rank
  => cannot generate the completed prime-frequency carrier.               (28)
```

The theorem is about hypertranscendental functions in general. The RH statement is only its
downstream implementation-level consequence.

## Parent and novelty audit

`[CANDIDATE NEW SCIENTIFIC KNOWLEDGE]`, provisionally.

- Cartwright and O'Brien prove the relative frequency bound for explicit finite-dimensional
  almost-periodic ODEs whose solutions are uniquely determined by initial values. O'Brien's Theorem
  1 gives at most `p-1` frequencies beyond the coefficient module for a system in `R^p`.
- The autonomous minimal-separant finite-sample theorem in this pipeline removes global uniqueness
  for constant coefficients. The present kernel argument extends that mechanism to an
  infinite-rank nonautonomous coefficient hull without bounding its total dimension.
- Preliminary searches did not locate an implicit algebraic-differential or differential-algebraic
  analogue that removes uniqueness and proves the relative rational-rank conclusion.
- Sell's trajectory-space construction turns a nonunique differential equation into a classical
  flow, but it does not retain a finite-dimensional state fibre. Obersnel--Omari show why this is a
  real obstruction: a scalar periodic Caratheodory ODE without uniqueness can have an
  infinite-dimensional set of almost-periodic solutions. Their solutions are assembled by branch
  patching and are not holomorphic minimal-separant solutions. The present theorem isolates exactly
  the analytic regularity that restores finite relative dimension.
- Sacker--Sell skew-product theory obtains finite coverings only under stability, distality, and
  finite-fibre hypotheses. It does not derive finite fibre dimension from an implicit analytic
  relation. Later Smith-reduction work likewise assumes a finite-dimensional determining
  projection supplied by a squeezing property.
- Searches across almost-periodic ODEs and DAEs, differential algebra, analytic implicit equations,
  separants, skew-product extensions, and the papers citing O'Brien did not locate `(7)`, `(22)`,
  `(26)`, or the finite regular-sample mechanism.
- The result passes the significance gate because it closes the singular implicit escape left by
  the classical relative-frequency theorem and yields a coefficient-relative differential
  transcendence criterion, not merely a sharper numerical bound.
- Scientific novelty remains provisional until independent experts check both the proof and the
  older multilingual literature.
