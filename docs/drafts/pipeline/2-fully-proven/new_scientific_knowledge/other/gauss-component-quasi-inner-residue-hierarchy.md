## Theorem

Let `m>=1`, `0<=k<m`, and let `p` be a rational prime. Define the Gauss component

```text
gamma_(m,k)(z)=Gamma(z/(2m)+k/m),
phi_(m,k)(z)=gamma_(m,k)(z)/gamma_(m,k)(1-z),
rho_infinity^(m,k)(z)
 =(pi/m)^(1/(2m)-z/m) phi_(m,k)(z).
```

Let `P` be the Hardy projection and put

```text
H_(m,k,p)=(1-P) M_(rho_infinity^(m,k) rho_p) P.
```

Then `|H_(m,k,p)|^(2m)` belongs to `L^(1,infinity)`, is measurable in the sense of
Connes, and

```text
Tr_omega(|H_(m,k,p)|^(2m))
 = 2 (p-1)^(2m) (p^m-1)^2 / (p^(2m) m^2 log p).    (A)
```

In particular, the critical residue is independent of the Gauss-component index `k`.

For `m=1`, formula (A) is

```text
2 (p-1)^4/(p^2 log p).
```

## 1. Source reduction

The proof of the one-prime decomposition in Connes--Consani applies with
`rho_infinity` replaced by `rho_infinity^(m,k)`. Its only non-trace-class term is

```text
E_(m,k,p)=(1-p)/p W_- C_(m,k,p) W_+*,
C_(m,k,p)=B_p D_(m,k,p) I B_p,
```

where `W_+` and `W_-` are isometries, `I e_n=e_(-n)`, and

```text
D_(m,k,p)e_0=0,
D_(m,k,p)e_n=d_n e_n,
d_n=rho_infinity^(m,k)(2 pi i n/log p).
```

Under Fourier series, `B_p` is multiplication by

```text
b_p(x)=p^((1-x)/2),  0<=x<1.
```

The omitted terms are rapidly smoothing or finite rank. Hence their contribution to the
`2m`-th positive power differs from that of `E_(m,k,p)` by a trace-class operator.

Write `L=log p`, `B=B_p`, `D=D_(m,k,p)`, and `C=C_(m,k,p)`. As in the `m=1`
case,

```text
G=C^2=p B D B^(-2) D* B.                            (1)
```

Thus `|C|^(2m)=G^m`.

## 2. Universal magnitude and phase

Stirling's formula applied to the two gamma factors gives, uniformly for fixed `m,k`,

```text
|rho_infinity^(m,k)(it)|
 =(2 pi/|t|)^(1/(2m)) (1+O(1/|t|)),                (2)
```

and

```text
arg rho_infinity^(m,k)(it)
 =(t/m)(log(t/(2 pi))-1)+c_(m,k)+O(1/t).           (3)
```

The leading terms are independent of `k`. At `t=2 pi n/L`, equations (2)--(3) become

```text
|d_n|^(2m)=L/|n| (1+o(1)),                          (4)

conj(d_(n+j))/conj(d_n)
 =exp(-2 pi i j delta_n)+o(1)                      (5)
```

for every fixed integer `j`, where

```text
delta_n=log(n/L)/(m L) mod 1.                       (6)
```

They also give the global bounds needed below:

```text
|d_n|^2 <= C (1+|n|)^(-1/m),
|d_n|^(-2) <= C |n|^(1/m).                         (7)
```

## 3. Moving-translation lemma

The Fourier coefficients of `b=b_p` satisfy

```text
b_hat(j)=(sqrt(p)-1)/(L/2+2 pi i j),
|b_hat(j)|<=C/(1+|j|).                              (8)
```

Equations (5), (7), and (8) imply

```text
(conj(d_n))^(-1) e_(-n) D* B e_n
 - b(.-delta_n) -> 0 in L^2(R/Z).                  (9)
```

For completeness, after fixing the modes `|j|<=J`, the squared tail is bounded by

```text
C n^(1/m) sum_(|j|>J) 1/(1+j^2)(1+|n+j|)^(-1/m).
```

On `|n+j|>=n/2` this is `O(1/J)`. On `0<|n+j|<n/2`, it is `O(1/n)` for
`m>1` and `O(log(n)/n)` for `m=1`. This proves (9), and the reality relation
`d_(-n)=conj(d_n)` proves the same statement at negative frequencies.

The same proof is uniform for bounded-variation amplitudes with uniformly bounded variation.
The amplitudes generated by finitely many products of translates of `b` and `b^(-1)` form such
a family.

## 4. Iterated principal action

Put `s_p(x)=b_p(x)^2=p^(1-x)` and

```text
a_delta(x)=s_p(x)/s_p(x+delta).
```

Apply (9) first to `D*` and its conjugate version to `D`. Equation (1) gives, for every
bounded-variation amplitude `f`,

```text
G(e_n f)
 =p |d_n|^2 e_n (a_(delta_n) f+o_(L^2)(1)).        (10)
```

The uniform bounded-variation form of the lemma permits iteration a fixed number of times.
Therefore

```text
<G^m e_(+/-n),e_(+/-n)>
 =p^m |d_n|^(2m)
   (integral_0^1 a_(delta_n)(x)^m dx+o(1)).         (11)
```

## 5. Exact two-variable average

The function

```text
A_m(delta)=integral_0^1 a_delta(x)^m dx
```

is continuous and one-periodic. Haar invariance and Fubini give

```text
integral_0^1 A_m(delta) ddelta
 =(integral_0^1 s_p(x)^m dx)
  (integral_0^1 s_p(x)^(-m) dx)
 =(p^m-1)^2/(p^m m^2 L^2).                         (12)
```

Harmonic logarithmic rotation gives, for every continuous one-periodic `f`,

```text
(1/log N) sum_(n<=N) f(delta_n)/n
 -> integral_0^1 f(delta) ddelta.                  (13)
```

This follows by trigonometric approximation: every nonzero Fourier mode reduces to
`sum_(n<=N)n^(-1+i alpha)=O(1)`.

Combining (4), (11), (12), and (13) yields the Fourier-diagonal limit

```text
lim_(N->infinity) (1/log N)
 sum_(0<|n|<=N) <G^m e_n,e_n>
 =2 (p^m-1)^2/(m^2 L).                             (14)
```

## 6. Weak modulation and measurability

Let `V_0=(1+|N|)^(-1)`. Equation (7) gives `|D| in L^(2m,infinity)`. Choose a
real exponent `r>2m` and put `u=1/r`. Every multiplication operator occurring in
the factorization of `G^m` is piecewise smooth with finitely many jumps, hence is bounded on
`H^u(R/Z)` because `u<1/2`. Diagonal phases commute with `V_0`.

Move `V_0^(-u)` leftward through those bounded factors. The resulting factorization has
`2m-1` copies of `|D| in L^(2m,infinity)` and one copy of

```text
|D| V_0^(-u) in L^(q,infinity),
1/q=1/(2m)-1/r.
```

Weak ideal Holder therefore gives

```text
G^m V_0^(-1/r) in L^(r/(r-1),infinity).
```

Thus `G^m` is weakly `V_0`-modulated. The eigenvalue comparison theorem for weakly
modulated operators turns (14) into

```text
lim_(N->infinity) (1/log N) sum_(j<=N) mu_j(G^m)
 =2 (p^m-1)^2/(m^2 L).                             (15)
```

This proves membership and measurability.

## 7. Restore the local-factor coefficient

The isometric source reduction gives

```text
|E_(m,k,p)|^(2m)
 ~ ((p-1)/p)^(2m) G^m
```

up to zero summands. The rapidly smoothing and finite-rank remainder changes the positive
`2m`-th power only by a trace-class operator. Multiplying (15) by `((p-1)/p)^(2m)` proves
formula (A).

### Where the loss was

Routing the moving-translation error through the *normalized* block `S = d_n^(-1) e_(-n) D e_n`

The `m ≥ 2` iteration always has a multiplication operator between the two normalized diagonals —

unattainable; `log_div_le_rpow_div` says exponent `θ` for any `θ < 1` is, and the band-split weight

the reflected-band spike of `S v` has height `n^(1/(2m))/n` against a target `n^(-3/4)`, i.e. it is

### Correction and refinement: the decay class the iteration must carry

The paragraph above assumed that the amplitude class `A/(1+|j|)^θ` is what propagates through the
iteration, with `θ` dropping by a fixed amount per step.  A closer look at `R̃_n` shows that this is
not the right class, and the reason is instructive.

`R̃_n = B ∘ S̃ ∘ B^(-2) ∘ S̃* ∘ B` is built from two kinds of factor:

* `B`, `B^(-2)` — multiplication by a piecewise-exponential *symbol*.  On the Fourier side this is
  convolution with a harmonic kernel, which costs one logarithm, i.e. any fixed power loss
  (`log_div_le_rpow_div`).  This is the effect the exponent ladder was designed for.
* `S̃`, `S̃*` — *Fourier multipliers* with symbol `ρ_j = ‖d_(n+j)‖/‖d_n‖`.  These do not convolve at
  all; they multiply coefficient `j` by `ρ_j`.  But `ρ_j ≍ (n/|n+j|)^(1/(2m))` is `O(1)` in the bulk
  and as large as `(Kn)^(1/(2m))` at `j ≈ -n`.

So a *uniform* bound `A/(1+|j|)^θ` is **not** preserved with a constant `A` independent of `n`: near
`j = -n` the bound degrades by the factor `n^(1/(2m))`, and no drop of the exponent `θ` repairs
that, because the damage is localised at a moving frequency rather than spread over the tail.

The right class is therefore two-piece — a uniform harmonic part plus a spike part carried at the
moving frequency `-n`:

```text
|ĝ(j)| ≤ A/(1+|j|)^θ  +  B · [ 2|n+j| < n ] · (n/|n+j|)^c .
```

Two features make this plausible as the invariant class.  The spike part is supported at distance
`≍ n` from frequency `0`, and only the coefficient at `0` is read at the end
(`⟪e_n, G^m e_n⟫` is `σ_n^m` times the `0`-th coefficient of `R̃_n^m f₀`, because `e_n` is a unitary
multiplier); and a harmonic convolution moves mass from the spike to frequency `0` with the factor
`1/(1+n)`, which is exactly the gain the pass-33 fold uses at `m = 1`.

**Therefore the next step on this card is not a mechanical generalisation of (T).**  It is:

1. fix the two-piece class above and compute how `B`, `B^(-2)`, `S̃`, `S̃*` act on it — the only new
   estimate needed is how a harmonic convolution maps the spike part into the uniform part;
2. prove the one-step estimate `‖(R̃_n - a_(δ_n)) u‖ → 0` for `u` in that class, of which the
   already-proved (T) is the case where `u` has no spike part;
3. run the pass-41 telescope `‖X^r v - Y^r v‖ ≤ Σ_i ‖Y‖^(r-1-i)‖(X-Y)(X^i v)‖` with `X = R̃_n`,
   `Y = a_(δ_n)` — this is the direction that applies the growing operator only to `v`, and it is
   the reason the class has to be invariant under `R̃_n` rather than under the model.

The available `p`-series tail `GaloisForLFunctions.tsum_int_tail_rpow_le` (new, in
`HTGeometry/PowerSeriesTailBound.lean`: `Σ_(|j|>J)(1+|j|)^(-s) ≤ 2(1+J)^(1-s)/(s-1)` for `s > 1`,
by Bernoulli telescoping) is what any exponent below one will need in step 2.

* The naive route — `(1 + |s|) ≤ (1 + |s − r|)(1 + r)`, hence

## Lean status, fifty-sixth pass: section 6 is formalized — the actual operator's singular values

Section 6 of the paper proof routes through the Lord–Sukochev–Zanin eigenvalue comparison theorem
for weakly `V_0`-modulated operators.  That theorem is not in mathlib and is a substantial piece of
noncommutative-integration machinery.  This pass **replaces it by an elementary two-scale
compression argument** and formalizes the replacement in full, so that (15) now holds for the
*actual* operator `G^m` and not only for a diagonal model with the right approximation numbers.

### The elementary substitute for modulation theory

For a positive compact `T` and a finite set `S` of basis indices, write `P_S` for the coordinate
projection and `A = P_S T P_S`.  Then

```text
sum_(n<N) s_n(T) = sum_(n<N) <g_n, T g_n>            (g_n the greedy vectors)
                 = sum_(n<N) <g_n, A g_n> + sum_(n<N) <g_n, (T-A) g_n>
                 <= sum_(n<N) s_n(A) + sqrt(N) (sum_(n<N) ||(T-A) g_n||^2)^(1/2)
                 <= sum_(j in S) <b_j, T b_j> + sqrt(2N) E_S,
```

with `E_S^2 = sum_(j notin S) ||T b_j||^2`.  The first inequality is Ky Fan majorization applied to
the *compression*; the second is Cauchy–Schwarz plus Bessel for the self-adjoint remainder `T-A`,
whose Hilbert–Schmidt mass is at most twice the off-`S` column mass.  Taking `S` the symmetric band
of radius `M = ceil(N^(1+u))` and letting `u -> 0` after `N -> infinity` turns the diagonal
logarithmic mean into the singular-value logarithmic mean, **provided** the columns satisfy
`||T b_n|| <= C_rho |n|^(-1+rho)` for every `rho > 0`.

Nothing here uses `V_0`-modulation, Cesàro operators, or the Calkin correspondence.

### The column decay, and why the class machinery had to be reopened

`G^m e_n = (p |d_n|^2)^m e_n (R_n^m 1)` exactly (this identity was already in the tree), and
multiplication by the exponential monomial is an isometry, so

```text
||G^m e_n|| = (p |d_n|^2)^m ||R_n^m 1||.
```

`|d_n|^(2m) <~ 1/|n|` is the sampled-magnitude bound already owned.  What was missing was
`||R_n^m 1|| <~ (1+|n|)^rho`.  The tied class propagates only to `r = m-1`, because the spike
exponent `c_0 + r/m` must stay below `1 - 1/(2m)` for the *symbol* (convolution) steps.  The last
step is unblocked by the observation that the outermost factor of `R_n = B S B^(-2) S* B` is the
*bounded multiplication operator* `B`: its operator norm `sqrt(p)` suffices, so only the inner four
factors need the class.  Those four impose the weaker constraint `c + 1/(2m) < 1`, which
`c = c_0 + (m-1)/m` satisfies as soon as `c_0 < 1/(2m)`.  The resulting class for the inner part has
bulk exponent `1 - (3m-1) eps` and spike exponent `c_0 + 1`, whose `L^2` mass is
`<~ (1+I)^(2 c_0 + 2(3m-1) eps)` — an arbitrarily small power, since `c_0` and `eps` are free.
