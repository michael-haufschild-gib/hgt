## Setup

Let `C` be algebraically closed of characteristic zero with trivial derivation.  Let `G/C` be a
connected commutative algebraic group, `alpha:G->G` an isogeny whose iterated kernels are Zariski
dense, and `F` a finite group of algebraic automorphisms commuting with `alpha`.  Put `X=G/F`,
and let `R:X->X` be induced by `alpha`.

Write

`V=Lie(G)`, `rho:F->GL(V)`, and `A=d alpha in End_F(V)`.

For a differential-generic `v` in `V`, let `C<V>^F` denote the fixed differential rational
field under the linear `F`-action.

## Theorem A: abstract HT no-hair theorem

**[PROVED HERE]** There is an isomorphism of differential fields with endomorphism

`(Tail(R), R*) isomorphic to (C<V>^F, A*).`                          (1)

Consequently, the abstract tail dynamical system is determined completely by the linear triple

`(V, rho, A).`                                                      (2)

If two bulk triples `(G,alpha,F)` and `(G',alpha',F')` have linearly equivalent data (2), their
abstract HT tails with dynamics are isomorphic even when the algebraic groups are not
isomorphic, not isogenous, or have different moduli and polarizations.

### Proof

The pure-expansion and finite-holonomy theorem gives

`Tail(R)=C<ell_delta(g)>^F`.

The logarithmic derivative `ell_delta(g)` is differential-generic in `V`; its equivariance is

`ell_delta(fg)=rho(f)ell_delta(g)`,

and functoriality under the isogeny is

`ell_delta(alpha(g))=A ell_delta(g)`.

Sending the generic Maurer--Cartan velocity to a differential-generic vector `v` identifies the
tail, the `F`-action, and the induced endomorphism with the right side of (1).  Any linear
equivalence of triples extends to an isomorphism of differential rational fields, carries fixed
fields to fixed fields, and intertwines the endomorphisms.  This proves the theorem.

## Corollary B: abelian varieties have universal multiplication tails

Let `B/C` be any abelian variety of dimension `g` and let `alpha=[m]` with `|m|>1`.  Then

`Tail([m]) isomorphic to C<v_1,...,v_g and all derivatives>`,        (3)

with

`[m]^*(v_j)=m v_j` for every `j`.                                  (4)

The abstract tail is independent of the isomorphism class, isogeny class, period lattice,
endomorphism ring, and polarization of `B`.

### Proof

Torsion points of `m`-power order are Zariski dense in `B`, so the pure-expansion theorem
applies.  Here `F` is trivial, `V` has dimension `g`, and `d[m]=m id_V`.  Equations (3)--(4)
follow from Theorem A.

## Corollary C: all flexible Lattes multiplication tails are abstractly identical

Let `E/C` be any elliptic curve and let `L_(E,m)` be the Lattes map on `E/{+1,-1}` induced by
`[m]`, with `|m|>1`.  Then

`Tail(L_(E,m)) isomorphic to C<u>`,                                 (5)

`L_(E,m)^*(u)=m^2 u`,                                              (6)

independently of the `j`-invariant of `E`.

### Proof

The involution acts on the one-dimensional velocity by `v -> -v`.  Its fixed differential field
is `C<v^2>`: `C<v>/C<v^2>` is the quadratic Galois extension with that involution, because every
derivative of `v` lies in the algebraic extension generated by a root of `X^2-v^2`.  Put
`u=v^2`.  Since `[m]` sends `v` to `mv`, it sends `u` to `m^2u`.

## The reconstruction boundary

The no-hair theorem concerns the **abstract** differential field with its induced endomorphism.
It does not contradict the embedded one-dimensional reconstruction theorem.

For a Lattes map written in a projective coordinate `y`, the tail is embedded as

`C<q_E(y)(y')^2> subset C<y>`,                                     (7)

where the pole divisor of `q_E` is the four-point branch divisor of `E->{P^1}`.  The embedded
field (7) reconstructs that orbifold divisor and hence its cross-ratio and elliptic modulus.
After forgetting the embedding and renaming its generator `u`, all such tails become (5).

Therefore:

- the abstract horizon algebra remembers tangent dimension, finite holonomy, and linear
  coarse-graining;
- the embedding of the horizon algebra into the ambient HT jet field carries global bulk
  placement and moduli.

This distinction is exact, not metaphorical.

## Physical/topological reading

This is interpretation only.  Equation (1) is a mathematical no-hair theorem: infinitely many
inequivalent bulk geometries have the same intrinsic boundary observable algebra.  Global
geometry is recoverable only when the boundary algebra is supplied together with its embedding,
analogous to boundary data plus a choice of how it sits in the full observable theory.

The result rules out an overly strong holographic claim.  The tail is not a complete invariant of
the bulk.  It is a complete invariant of the bulk's surviving tangent representation.

## RH impact audit

This theorem gives a useful no-go result.  An RH construction that uses only the **abstract** tail
of a constant abelian isogeny tower cannot encode zeta-specific arithmetic: the same tail occurs
for every abelian variety of the same dimension with the same tangent action.  An RH-sensitive
mechanism must therefore use at least one of:

1. the embedding of the tail in the ambient source field;
2. the embedded Manin map of a nonconstant group, where Gauss--Manin variation remains visible;
3. arithmetic/Galois structure not visible over the constant field `C`;
4. an additional positivity or spectral structure.

No RH claim follows, but an entire class of abstract-tail-only strategies is ruled out.

The later `abelian-multiplication-tail-manin-horizon` theorem strengthens this point: even a
nonconstant abelian variety has a universal abstract multiplication tail.  Nonconstant variation
helps only through the embedded Manin coordinate, not through the abstract tail field.
