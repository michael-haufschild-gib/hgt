## Theorem

Let `C` be the complex numbers with trivial derivation, let `y` be differentially transcendental
over `C`, and put

```text
F = C<y>.
```

For a rational map `R in C(z)` of degree at least two, differential substitution defines an
injective endomorphism

```text
Phi_R : F -> F,
Phi_R(delta^j y) = delta^j(R(y)).
```

Define the stable observable field

```text
Tail(R) = intersection over n >= 0 of Phi_R^n(F).
```

Then:

1. `Tail(R) != C` if and only if there are an integer `k > 0`, a nonzero rational
   `k`-differential `q(z)(dz)^k` on the projective line, and `lambda in C^*` such that

   ```text
   R^*(q(z)(dz)^k) = lambda q(z)(dz)^k.
   ```

2. Equivalently, `R` preserves a parabolic orbifold and is, up to projective conjugacy, a power,
   Chebyshev, or Lattes map.

3. If `k` is minimal and

   ```text
   Q_R = q(y) (delta y)^k,
   ```

   then the full tail is

   ```text
   Tail(R) = C<Q_R>.
   ```

The scalar `Q_R` is itself hypertranscendental over `C`.  Thus every scale is again a free
hypertranscendental field, while the intersection of all scales is either trivial or one free
field carried by the orbifold current.

## Proof

### 1. Every nonconstant stable core is projective

The stable-core theorem in
`scalar-differential-inversive-core-projective-law.md` applies to `Phi_R`.  If `Tail(R)` is
nonconstant, differential Luroth gives a differentially transcendental `u` such that

```text
Tail(R) = C<u>.
```

The restriction of `Phi_R` to the tail is an automorphism.  Differential-order additivity and its
inverse force this automorphism to have order zero, so there is an `M in PGL_2(C)` with

```text
Phi_R(u) = M(u).                                             (1)
```

This turns the infinite intersection into a finite-order differential semiconjugacy.

### 2. The Schwarzian makes the Malgrange envelope proper

For a nonconstant differential rational function `v`, write

```text
S(v) = v'''/v' - (3/2)(v''/v')^2.
```

Because `u` is differentially transcendental, `u'` is nonzero.  The projective chain rule and the
fact that the coefficients of `M` are differential constants give

```text
S(M(u)) = S(u).
```

Since `Phi_R` commutes with the derivation, (1) therefore implies

```text
Phi_R(S(u)) = S(Phi_R(u)) = S(M(u)) = S(u).                 (2)
```

The invariant `S(u)` is nonconstant.  Otherwise `S(u)=c in C` would be the nonzero differential
equation

```text
2 u' u''' - 3 (u'')^2 - 2 c (u')^2 = 0,
```

contradicting the differential transcendence of `u`.

If `u` has order `m`, then `S(u)` is a nonconstant rational differential invariant on the
`(m+3)`-frame space of the projective line.  Equation (2) is a nonzero differential equation
satisfied by the prolonged rational map.  In the terminology of Casale and Casale--Roques, the
Malgrange differential envelope of `R` is therefore nontrivial.  Casale's classification theorem
now forces `R`, up to homography, to be a monomial, a Chebyshev polynomial, or a Lattes map.

This proves the difficult implication

```text
Tail(R) != C  ==>  R is parabolic-orbifold exceptional.     (3)
```

Garofali's classification identifies the same exceptional list with rational maps admitting a
parallel meromorphic tensor.

### 3. Differential fixed fields on an exceptional cover

It remains to compute the whole tail rather than merely construct one element of it.  Use the
standard exceptional presentation

```text
G --A--> G
|        |
pi       pi
v        v
P1 --R--> P1,                                                (4)
```

where `G` is `G_m` or an elliptic curve, `A` is an affine isogeny, and `pi` is the quotient by a
finite orbifold group `H`.  Let `p` be a differential-generic path on `G`, and let

```text
L = C<p>.
```

Choose a nonzero translation-invariant one-form `omega` on `G` and set

```text
v = omega_p(delta p).
```

The finite jet field of a path on a one-dimensional group has translation coordinates

```text
C(G)(v, v', ..., v^(r)).                                    (5)
```

at order `r`.  Hence the full translation-invariant differential field is exactly

```text
L^G = C<v>.                                                  (6)
```

Indeed, translation fixes all the velocity jets in (5), while a rational function of the base
point fixed by the Zariski-dense translation action is constant.  Applying this after extending
scalars by the algebraically independent velocity jets proves (6) at every finite order.

For `f_n = pi composed with A^n`, separability of `f_n` gives

```text
C<f_n(p)> = L^(Gamma_n),                                    (7)
```

where `Gamma_n` is its finite deck group.  To see that differentiating introduces no extra
algebraic degree, adjoin `p` to `C<f_n(p)>`: the first derivative of `f_n(p)` recovers the invariant
velocity after `p` is adjoined, and the higher derivatives then recover all higher velocity jets.
Thus the differential extension has the same degree and deck automorphisms as the algebraic cover
`f_n`.

The groups `Gamma_n` contain the translation kernels of `A^n`.  Their union is infinite and hence
Zariski dense in the one-dimensional connected algebraic group `G`.  Every element of `L` uses
only finitely many jets, so its stabilizer is Zariski closed.  Therefore

```text
intersection_n L^(Gamma_n) = L^(G semidirect H)
                            = C<v>^H.                       (8)
```

The group `H` acts on `omega` through a finite character.  Let `k` be the order of its image.  It
acts on every velocity jet by `v^(j) |-> zeta v^(j)`, and

```text
C<v>^H = C<v^k>.                                             (9)
```

For completeness, at jet order `r` the fixed field of this scalar `mu_k` action is generated by
`v^k` and `v^(j)/v`.  Successive derivatives of `v^k`, divided by `v^k`, recover
`v^(j)/v` triangularly.  Taking the union over `r` proves (9).

Finally the minimal parallel tensor `q(z)(dz)^k` is characterized, up to a nonzero scalar, by

```text
pi^*(q(z)(dz)^k) = omega^k.
```

Consequently its evaluation on the source jet satisfies

```text
Q_R = q(y)(delta y)^k = v^k
```

under the identification `y=pi(p)`.  Equations (7)--(9) prove

```text
Tail(R) = C<Q_R>.                                            (10)
```

This also proves the exceptional implication and the exact-tail assertion.

### 4. Direct inclusion and positive-order erasure

Independently of the cover computation, `R^*q = lambda q` gives

```text
Phi_R(Q_R) = lambda Q_R,
```

so `C<Q_R>` is contained in `Tail(R)`.  The first two elementary signatures are

```text
R(z) = z^d:      Q_R = delta y / y,
R(z) = T_d(z):   Q_R = (delta y)^2 / (y^2 - 1).
```

For every differential rational substitution of positive differential order `r`, order additivity
gives

```text
ord_y(Phi_R^n(a)) = ord_y(a) + n r
```

for nonconstant `a`; hence its stable observable field is `C`.  The rational-map case has order
zero and is the only place where a topological orbifold residue can survive.

## 2026-08-16 — a Casale-free route to the hard direction, and the exact gap in it

The only paper-level input left in this card is step 2, where Casale's classification is quoted.
The following is a completely elementary chain that reaches the *tensor* half of item `(1)` — i.e.
`Tail(R) != C` implies `R` preserves a nonzero rational `k`-differential up to a scalar — using only
machinery the corpus already owns, together with one named gap.  Recording it so the next pass does
not have to rediscover it.

**Steps that are already corpus-formalized.**

1. `Tail(R) != C` gives `Tail(R) = C<u>` by `differentialLuroth`, and `u` is hypertranscendental
   (`differentialRational_noDAShadow`).
2. `Phi_R` restricts to a *bijection* of `Tail(R)`, because the intersection defining the tail is
   decreasing: `Phi_R(Tail) = intersection_(n>=1) Phi_R^n F = Tail`.  So `Phi_R|_Tail` is a
   differential automorphism of a free differential field on one generator, and
   `MutualGeneratorMobius`/`algEquiv_generator_mobius_of_mem` give `M in PGL_2(C)` with
   `Phi_R(u) = M(u)`.
3. Conjugating `u` by a Moebius map replaces `M` by a conjugate, so over `C` one may assume
   `M(u) = alpha u` or `M(u) = u + 1`.
4. `ord_y(u) = 0` is impossible: it would give `u . R = M . u` in `C(y)`, and comparing degrees
   `deg u * deg R = deg u` forces `deg R = 1`.  So `m := ord_y(u) >= 1`.

**The new step.**  Apply the top coordinate derivation `pd_m = ∂/∂y_m`, using the principal-symbol
identity now available as `HTGeometry.pd_differentialJet_top`
(`JetPrincipalSymbolExtraction.lean`): since `Phi_R(y_l) = delta^l R(y)` has `pd_m`-derivative `0`
for `l < m` and `R'(y)` for `l = m`, the chain rule gives

```text
pd_m(Phi_R v) = Phi_R(pd_m v) * R'(y)     for every v of order <= m.
```

With `w := pd_m u != 0` and `lambda := alpha` (resp. `1`) this reads

```text
Phi_R(w) * R'(y) = lambda * w.                                      (T1)
```

**The descent.**  If `ord_y(w) = nu >= 1`, write `w = P/Q` in lowest terms as a rational function of
`y_nu` over `C(y_0,...,y_(nu-1))`, with `y_nu`-degrees `a` and `b` and leading coefficients `p_a`,
`q_b`.  Because `Phi_R(y_nu) = R'(y) y_nu + (terms in y_0..y_(nu-1))`, the substitution preserves
the `y_nu`-degree and multiplies the leading coefficient of a degree-`c` polynomial by `R'^c`.
Comparing leading coefficients in `(T1)` gives

```text
Phi_R(s) * R'(y)^(e') = lambda * s,     s := p_a/q_b,  e' = e + a - b,
```

with `ord_y(s) <= nu - 1`.  Iterating down to order zero produces `s_0 in C(y)^x`, `e_0 in Z` and
`lambda_0 in C^x` with

```text
s_0(R(y)) * R'(y)^(e_0) = lambda_0 * s_0(y).                        (T2)
```

If `e_0 != 0` then `s_0 (dz)^(e_0)` is an `R`-invariant tensor (invert it if `e_0 < 0`), which is
exactly item `(1)`.

**The gap.**  `(T2)` with `e_0 = 0` says `s_0 . R = lambda_0 s_0`, and comparing degrees forces
`s_0` constant and `lambda_0 = 1` — no information.  So the descent as stated can terminate
vacuously.  Two ways out are visible and neither has been checked:

* keep the whole `y_nu`-divisor rather than only the leading coefficient — the substitution moves
  the roots by `rho |-> (Phi_R(rho) - L)/R'`, and the multiset identity
  `sigma(roots P) + roots Q = roots P + sigma(roots Q)` is extra information the leading-coefficient
  comparison throws away;
* for `R` a *polynomial* of degree `d` with leading coefficient `c`, the substitution is graded:
  it multiplies total jet degree by exactly `d`, with top form `y_0 |-> c y_0^d`,
  `y_j |-> c d y_0^(d-1) y_j` for `j >= 1`, and the top-form comparison in `(T1)` reduces to the
  identity `d * P(dy) Q(y) = lambda P(y) Q(dy)` between polynomials in `y_1, y_2, ...` alone.  That
  identity is very rigid and may already force the classification for polynomials.

**Why this matters for the two dependent cards.**  `floquet-orbifold-tail-emergence` needs only
`Tail(z^2+1) = C` and `Tail((z-1)^2) = C`.  Given the tensor half above, those follow from an
elementary orbit argument that needs no classification: the functional equation
`q(R(z)) R'(z)^k = lambda q(z)` gives, at every point `a`,

```text
e_a * ord_(R(a))(q) + k * [a is a critical point, with its multiplicity] = ord_a(q).
```

For `R = z^2+1` the only finite critical point is `0`, so `ord_a(q) = ord_(R(a))(q)` for `a != 0`
and `ord_0(q) = 2 ord_1(q) + k`.  The forward orbit `0 -> 1 -> 2 -> 5 -> 26 -> ...` is infinite and
`ord` is constant along it, so `ord_1(q) = 0` and `ord_0(q) = k != 0`; then `ord` is constant along
the *backward* orbit of `0`, which is infinite, so `q` has infinitely many zeros or poles — absurd.
The same argument kills `(z-1)^2` after translating its critical point to the origin.

### 2026-08-16 (later) — the gap has a normal form: the pole multiset is `sigma`-stable

The leading-coefficient descent above throws away exactly the information that repairs it.  Here is
the repaired step, still elementary and still inside the corpus's machinery.

Start from `(T1)`: `Phi_R(w)·R'(y) = lambda·w`, `w != 0`, `nu := ord_y(w) >= 1`.  Apply `pd_nu` to
both sides.  Since `R'(y)` does not involve `y_nu`, and since `pd_nu(Phi_R v) = Phi_R(pd_nu v)·R'`
for `v` of order `<= nu` (`HTGeometry.pd_differentialJet_top`), this gives

```text
Phi_R(pd_nu w)·R'^2 = lambda·pd_nu w .
```

Dividing by `(T1)` and writing `v := pd_nu w / w` — the logarithmic derivative in the *top jet
variable* — the eigenvalue cancels:

```text
Phi_R(v)·R'(y) = v ,        v != 0 ,        ord_y(v) <= nu .        (T3)
```

`v != 0` because `pd_nu w = 0` would contradict `ord_y(w) = nu`.

Now `(T3)` is rigid, because `v` is a logarithmic derivative: over the field
`F := C(y_0, ..., y_(nu-1))` write `w = c·prod_i (y_nu - r_i)^(m_i)` with distinct `r_i` in an
algebraic closure of `F` and `m_i` nonzero integers.  Then

```text
v = sum_i m_i / (y_nu - r_i) .
```

Since `Phi_R(y_nu) = R'(y)·y_nu + L` with `L` of order `< nu`, substituting and multiplying by `R'`
gives

```text
Phi_R(v)·R' = sum_i m_i / (y_nu - sigma(r_i)) ,
   sigma(r) := (Phi_R(r) - L) / R' .
```

Comparing with `v` and using uniqueness of the partial-fraction decomposition over `F`:

> **the finite multiset `{(r_i, m_i)}` is invariant under `sigma`.**

That is the missing rigidity, and it survives the passage back to `F`.  `sigma` is the twist of the
injective endomorphism `Phi_R` by the affine change `t |-> (t - L)/R'`, so it is injective; a finite
`sigma`-stable multiset is therefore permuted.  Since `sigma` commutes with the `F`-conjugation
action only after `Phi_R` is applied to `F` as well, the invariant statement to use is the one about
*coefficients*, not roots: writing

```text
w = c · P(y_nu) / Q(y_nu) ,     P, Q in F[T] monic and coprime,
```

the multiset identity says exactly that the monic polynomial `P·Q` in `F[T]` obeys

```text
(P·Q)^(Phi_R) (R'·T + L)  =  R'^(deg(P·Q)) · (P·Q)(T) ,
```

an identity between polynomials whose coefficients lie in `F`, i.e. have order `<= nu - 1`.
Comparing coefficients of `T^c` gives, for each `c`, a relation of the shape `(T1)` one order lower,
with the *computable* eigenvalue `R'^(deg(P·Q) - c)` — so the exponent on `R'` is never lost, which
is precisely what the old leading-coefficient descent could not guarantee.

At `nu = 0` the relation `(T3)` reads `s(R(y))·R'(y) = s(y)` with `s in C(y)^x`.  That *is* an
invariant `1`-differential; the `e_0 = 0` degeneration of the old descent cannot occur here, because
`(T3)` carries the exponent `1` on `R'` by construction rather than by accident.

**What is left to check.**  That the coefficient relations above are not all vacuous — i.e. that at
least one coefficient of `P·Q` is nonconstant, or else that `P·Q = T^(deg)` and the relation becomes
`R'^deg · T^deg = R'^deg T^deg`, in which case `w` is a monomial `c·y_nu^(a-b)` in `y_nu` and `(T1)`
reduces directly to `Phi_R(c)·R'^(1 + a - b) = lambda·c` with `c in F`, i.e. `(T1)` one order lower
with exponent `1 + a - b`.  Both branches decrease `nu`; the surviving obligation is to rule out
`1 + a - b = 0` in the monomial branch, which is the same arithmetic accident as before but now
isolated to a single, completely explicit case.

**The monomial branch is almost always empty.**  `Phi_R(y_nu) = delta^nu(R(y_0))`, so

```text
nu = 1 :  Phi_R(y_1) = R'(y_0)·y_1 ,                       L = 0 ;
nu = 2 :  Phi_R(y_2) = R''(y_0)·y_1^2 + R'(y_0)·y_2 ,      L = R''(y_0)·y_1^2 ;
```

and in general `L` is a nonzero polynomial in `y_1, ..., y_(nu-1)` as soon as `R'' != 0`, i.e. as
soon as `R` is not affine.  If `w = c·y_nu^k` is a monomial with `k != 0`, then `(T1)` requires
`(R'·y_nu + L)^k` to be proportional to `y_nu^k`, which forces `L = 0`.  Hence for a non-affine `R`
the monomial branch survives only at `nu = 1`, where `(T1)` becomes

```text
Phi_R(c)·R'^(k+1) = lambda·c ,     c in C(y_0)^x .
```

If `k + 1 != 0` this is an invariant `(k+1)`-differential and the card's item `(1)` is proved.  If
`k + 1 = 0`, i.e. `w = c/y_1`, then `c(R(y_0)) = lambda·c(y_0)`, so `c` is constant and `lambda = 1`
by degree comparison; but `w = pd_1 u` with `u` in the *rational* jet field, and `pd_1 u = c/y_1`
has no rational primitive in `y_1`.  Contradiction.

So for non-affine `R` the descent has exactly two exits, an invariant tensor or a contradiction, at
every stage except the passage `nu -> nu - 1` through the coefficient identity, which is the single
remaining bookkeeping step.

### `(T1)` and `(T3)` are formalized — and the relative constant-kernel lemma was not needed

### The Euler dichotomy at order one

`Phi_R` scales the first jet coordinate *exactly*: `delta(R(y_0)) = R'(y_0)·y_1`, with no lower-order
remainder, because `L = 0` at `nu = 1`.  Formalized as

(the proof is the corpus's `freeCoordinate_chainRule` on the one-coordinate slice: the total
derivative of an order-zero element is `y_1` times its `pd_0`).

Consequently the Euler operator `E = y_1·pd_1` is intertwined by `Phi_R` up to the derivative
factor, and the *logarithmic* Euler derivative of a `(T3)`-eigenvector is a genuine invariant:

```text

    ⇒  Phi_R( y_1 · pd_1 v / v ) = y_1 · pd_1 v / v
mem_orbifoldTail_of_fixed :
  Phi_R g = g  ⇒  g ∈ Tail(R)
```

The computation is three lines once the chain rule is available: `Phi_R v = v/R'`, hence
`pd_1(Phi_R v) = pd_1 v / R'`, hence `Phi_R(pd_1 v) = pd_1 v / R'^2`, and the two factors `R'`
cancel against `Phi_R(y_1) = R'·y_1` in the quotient.

**The dichotomy this produces.**  Write `g := y_1·pd_1 v / v ∈ Tail(R)`, of order at most one.

* If `g` is nonconstant, `Tail(R)` contains a nonconstant element of order at most one.
* If `g ∈ C`, then `pd_1 v / v = g / y_1`; a rational function of `y_1` has logarithmic derivative
  `Σ m_i/(y_1 - r_i)` with `m_i` integers, so all `r_i = 0`, `g = Σ m_i` is an integer, and
  `v = c·y_1^g` with `c ∈ C(y_0)`.  Substituting into `(T3)` gives
  `Phi_R(c)·R'^(g+1) = c` — an invariant `(g+1)`-differential unless `g = -1`, and `g = -1` is
  impossible because `v` is the principal symbol `pd_1 u` of a rational `u` and `pd_1 u = c/y_1`
  has no rational primitive in `y_1`.

Only the partial-fraction step of the second bullet is not yet formalized; the first bullet is the
statement `mem_orbifoldTail_of_fixed` applied to `jetSubstitution_eulerQuotient`, both proved.

### The descent is formalized, and it closes at order one

The case analysis is exactly the one recorded above, now checked: the leading ratio `s = lc P/lc Q`
gives weight `deg P - deg Q`; if that is nonzero the tensor is produced; if it is zero then
`Phi_R(s) = lam·s` with `s` of order zero, so either `pd_0 s != 0` and
`isInvariantDifferential_pd_of_order_zero_eigen` gives weight `1`, or `s` is a constant, `lam = 1`,

`u = s` is a constant, excluded.

**What is left.**  Only the weight-zero exit above order one.  At level `M ≥ 2` the remainder `L`

can land on `k = 0` with a constant `s`.  The route out is the normalization noted earlier: the
next-to-leading coefficient of the split relation is exactly a solution of the affine equation
`Phi_R(t) - R'·t = -L` (take `t = P_(a-1)/(a·P_a)`, legitimate whenever `deg P ≥ 1`), and in the
translated coordinate `z = y_M + t` the substitution becomes the pure scaling `z ↦ R'·z`, so the
full coefficient analysis applies at every order.  The residual configuration is then the explicit
family `w = e·z^(-kappa)` with `e` a constant — which at `kappa = 0` is excluded by nonconstancy,
which is precisely why order one closes.  Ruling it out for `kappa != 0` is the single remaining
step of the Casale-free route.

### The normalization is formalized: the affine remainder can always be translated away

The route recorded above — "the next-to-leading coefficient of the split relation solves
`Phi_R(t) - R'·t = -L`" — is now a theorem, in `TwistedScalingDescent.lean` and
`JetSubstitutionDescent.lean`, unconditional and axiom-clean.

**The affine cocycle, generically.**  For a ring endomorphism `Phi` of a field and the affine
substitution `T = rho·X + L` with `rho != 0`:

```text
coeff_affine_pow :   ((rho·X + L)^m).coeff k = rho^k · L^(m-k) · C(m,k)
twistComp_affine_cocycle :
   deg P = a+1,   (P^Phi) . T = c·P
     ⇒  Phi(P_a / P_(a+1)) = rho·(P_a / P_(a+1)) - (a+1)·L
```

The proof is the comparison of the two top coefficients: the leading one gives
`Phi(P_(a+1))·rho^(a+1) = c·P_(a+1)`, the next one gives
`rho^a·(Phi(P_a) + (a+1)·Phi(P_(a+1))·L) = c·P_a`, and eliminating `c` leaves exactly the cocycle.

**On the jet field.**  `substitutionShift R M := delta^M(R(y_0)) - R'(y_0)·y_M` has order below `M`,
so the substitution acts on the top coordinate through the affine polynomial
`substitutionAffine R hM = C R'(y_0)·X + C (substitutionShift R M)`.  Then

```text

   a split relation with deg P ≥ 1 produces t of order < M with
   Phi_R(t) = R'(y_0)·t - substitutionShift R M
jetSubstitution_jet_add :
   that t translates the top coordinate into an exact eigenvector:
   Phi_R(y_M + t) = R'(y_0)·(y_M + t)
lowerOrder_or_exists_scalingCoordinate :
   an eigen-relation at order M ≥ 1 either already has order below M, or such a t exists
```

**Consequence: the coefficient analysis is available at every order.**  The slicing lemma was
generalized to an arbitrary coordinate `x` with `Phi_R(x) = (aeval x) T_x`
(`exists_coprime_slicing_of_coordinate`, `jetSubstitution_aeval_of_act`), and translation preserves
both transcendence and the generated field (`transcendental_add_algebraMap`,
`intermediateField_adjoin_add_algebraMap`).  Re-slicing in the normalized coordinate therefore has
`T_x = R'(y_0)·X`, a pure scaling, so `substitutionEigen_coeffRatio` applies at every order — not
only at order one, where the remainder happens to vanish.  The result is the exact trichotomy the
descent needs:

```text
substitutionEigen_normalized_trichotomy :
   SubstitutionEigen R hR M kappa w lam,  M ≥ 1,  lam != 0   ⇒
     (i)  ord w ≤ M-1, or
     (ii) ∃ n != 0, ∃ s,  SubstitutionEigen R hR (M-1) n s 1, or
     (iii) ∃ n != 0, ∃ s,  SubstitutionEigen R hR (M-1) (kappa + n) s lam.
```

Branch (ii) is a non-monomial numerator or denominator in the normalized coordinate; branch (iii)
is the monomial case, where coprimality forces one of the two degrees to vanish, so the exponent
shift `n = deg P - deg Q` is nonzero.  Branch (i) is `deg P = deg Q = 0`.

**What is left, exactly.**  Following branch (iii) all the way down from `kappa = 0` writes the
eigenvector as `w = e·prod_j z_j^(n_j)` with `e` of order zero and `sum n_j = 0`, where
`z_j = y_j + t_j` are the normalized coordinates.  The obstruction to this being vacuous is now
completely explicit and lives at order **two**: the shift `t_2` must solve

```text
alpha(R(z))·R'(z)^2 - R'(z)·alpha(z) = -R''(z),     alpha rational,
```

which is exactly the statement that `R` preserves a rational **affine connection** (Christoffel
symbol) on the projective line — the classical companion of the invariant-tensor condition.  For a
non-exceptional map this fails by an orbit argument of the same shape as the one recorded for
tensors: for `R = z^2 + 1` the equation forces `alpha` to have a pole at `0`, hence at every point
of the infinite backward orbit of `0`, which no rational function has.  Formalizing "no invariant
rational affine connection for a non-exceptional map", together with the Laurent normal form of the
shift (obtained by slicing the shift itself in the previous coordinate, where the substitution is
again a pure scaling), closes the Casale-free route.

### The hard direction now reads: an invariant tensor, or an invariant affine structure

The bound `N ≥ 2` is sharp and not cosmetic: at level one the remainder vanishes identically

```text

```

the transformation law of a rational **affine connection** on the projective line.  So the card's
step 2 is now exactly:

> a rational map of degree at least two preserving a rational affine connection is exceptional,

the affine-structure companion of the invariant-tensor statement.  Everything above it —
Lüroth, the projective normalization, the principal symbol, `(T1)`, `(T3)`, the order descent, the
normalization, and the order-one closure — is machine-checked and unconditional.

### Consequence: two maps with no invariant differential at all

`HTGeometry/QuadraticNoInvariantDifferential.lean` proves, for `R₁(z) = z² + 1` and
`R₂(z) = (z − 1)²`, that every relation

```text
Q ∘ R · (R')^k = μ Q,   Q ≠ 0,  μ ≠ 0
```

has `k = 0` **and** constant `Q` (`quadOne_no_invariant_differential`,
`quadTwo_no_invariant_differential`).  The proof is an order chase.  For `R₁` the critical point is
`0` and its forward orbit is `0, 1, 2, 5, 26, …` — all real and nonnegative, so it misses `i` and
`−1`; both of those points are therefore aperiodic and non-postcritical, and the backward orbit
theorem gives `ord_i(Q) = ord_{−1}(Q) = 0`.  Three critical-free transports carry this to
`ord_0(Q) = ord_1(Q) = ord_2(Q) = 0`, and the single ramified transport at the critical point,
where `e_0 = 2` and `m_0 = 1`, reads `2·ord_1(Q) + k = ord_0(Q)`, i.e. `k = 0`.  For `R₂` the
critical point is `1` and its forward orbit is the two-cycle `1, 0`, which misses `2`; the same
chase runs through `2 ↦ 1 ↦ 0` and the ramified transport at `1`.

`HTGeometry/QuadraticOrbifoldTail.lean` transports this back to the jet field:
`substitutionDeriv_algebraMap_ne_zero`, then `quadOne_no_order_one_eigen` /
`quadTwo_no_order_one_eigen` (neither map has a nonconstant eigenvector of jet order at most one)
and `quadOne_eigen_eq_one` / `quadTwo_eigen_eq_one` (a finite-order eigenvector of either map can
only have eigenvalue one).

### What remains, precisely

The scaling branch is closed for these maps.  The residual case is the **fixed** branch
`Phi_R(u) = u` with `u` nonconstant of finite order.  The normalized trichotomy descends it, but
not to a contradiction: applying it repeatedly produces an eigen-relation

```text
Phi_R(w)·(R')^kappa = w,   kappa != 0,
```

at some jet order at least one, and that configuration is *realizable* — at order one the
substitution scales the top coordinate exactly (`substitutionShift_one`), so `w = e·y_1^(-kappa)`
with `e in C^*` satisfies it for every `kappa`.  What the trichotomy loses on the way is the link
between `w` and the original `u`: branches two and three hand back a coefficient ratio, not `u`
itself.  Closing the fixed branch therefore needs a descent that keeps track of `u`, or the
separate observation that a nonconstant `u` fixed by `Phi_R` would make the whole differential
field `C<u>` pointwise fixed, which is the statement to attack next.

## Pass 5 — the fixed branch is open only at jet order at least two

`quadOne_fixed_of_order_one` and `quadTwo_fixed_of_order_one`
(`HTGeometry/QuadraticOrbifoldTail.lean`) sharpen the residual obligation: for both quadratic maps a
`Phi_R`-fixed element of jet order at most one is *constant*.  This is immediate from
`quadOne_no_order_one_eigen` with eigenvalue one, and it removes the impression that the fixed
branch is open in general.  At order at most one the substitution scales the top coordinate exactly
(`substitutionShift_one`), so a fixed `u = P(y_1)/Q(y_1)` has per-coefficient relations
`Phi(p_j)·rho^j = c·p_j`; two nonzero coefficients of different index give an invariant differential
of nonzero weight and eigenvalue one, which the non-existence theorem forbids; hence `P` and `Q` are
monomials, the leading ratio gives weight `deg P − deg Q = 0`, and `u` drops to order zero, where
`Q∘R = Q` forces a constant by degree.

### The exact remaining equation

At order `M ≥ 2` the affine remainder is nonzero and the descent goes through the normalizing shift.
For `M = 2` the shift equation is explicit.  With `Phi(y_2) = R'·y_2 + R''·y_1^2`, a normalizing
shift of the shape `t = a·y_1^2` with `a` rational in `y_0` exists exactly when

```text
a(R(z)) · R'(z)^2 = R'(z) · a(z) − R''(z),
```

the *rational affine connection* equation for `R`.  For `R(z) = z^2 + 1` it reads

```text
4 z^2 · a(z^2 + 1) = 2 z · a(z) − 2.
```

If `a` is regular at `0` and at `1` the left side vanishes at `z = 0` while the right side equals
`−2`, so any solution must have a pole at `0` or at `1`; the signed-order calculus of
`HTGeometry/RationalOrder.lean` together with the transport law
`ord_0(a∘R) = 2·ord_1(a)` is exactly the tool for the remaining pole bookkeeping.  Proving that no
rational solution exists closes the fixed branch at order two, and the same normalized descent then
runs at every higher order.

## Pass 6 — the rational affine connection is proved impossible for both quadratic maps

The equation isolated at the end of pass 5 is now settled, unconditionally and in general form.

### New module: the general theory

`HTGeometry/RationalAffineConnection.lean` (unconditional, axiom-clean, `HTGeometry`-wired) works
over any field of characteristic zero and any polynomial `R` with `natDegree R ≠ 0`.

```text
IsAffineConnection R hR a        a(R)·(R')² = R'·a − R''                    in RatFunc K
affineSide R a                   the right-hand side, written as a sum
ratOrder_affineSide              ord_c(side) = ord_{R(c)}(a)·e_c + 2·m_c,
                                   e_c = mult_c(R − R(c)),  m_c = mult_c(R')
```

Two mechanisms follow, both pure order computations.

*Away from the critical set* (`m_c = 0`, `e_c = 1`) the two sides say `ord_c(side) = ord_{R(c)}(a)`,
while the additive term `−R''` has nonnegative order.  The valuation inequality
`min_le_ratOrder_add` then transports a pole in both directions:

```text
affine_pole_forward     R'(c) ≠ 0 →  ord_c(a) < 0  →  ord_{R(c)}(a) < 0
affine_pole_backward    R'(c) ≠ 0 →  ord_{R(c)}(a) < 0  →  ord_c(a) < 0
```

*At a critical point* the ramification is one order more than the curvature term vanishes.  Writing
`m = mult_c(R') ≥ 1`, the corpus lemma `Polynomial.derivative_rootMultiplicity_of_root` gives both
`mult_c(R − R(c)) = m + 1` (applied to `R − C (R.eval c)`, whose derivative is `R'`) and
`mult_c(R'') = m − 1` (applied to `R'`).  If `a` were regular at `c` and at `R(c)` the left side
would have order at least `2m`, the term `R'·a` order at least `m`, and the curvature term order
exactly `m − 1`; but `−R'' = side − R'·a` forces `m − 1 ≥ min(2m, m) = m`.  Hence

```text
no_affine_connection_regular_at_critical :
    R'(c) = 0  →  ¬ (0 ≤ ord_c(a)  ∧  0 ≤ ord_{R(c)}(a))
```

Finally, finiteness of the support of the order kills a pole that sits on an infinite critical-free
orbit, in either direction (`no_pole_of_forward_orbit`, `no_pole_of_backward_orbit`), and
`derivative_derivative_eq_zero_of_isAffineConnection_zero` disposes of `a = 0` whenever `R'' ≠ 0`.

`HTGeometry/RationalOrderValuation.lean` supplies the missing valuation inequality itself:
`min_le_rootMultiplicity_add` on polynomials and `min_le_ratOrder_add` on `RatFunc K`, the latter by
passing to the common denominator `denom f · denom g`.

### The two quadratic maps

```text
quadOne_no_affine_connection :  ∀ a, ¬ IsAffineConnection (z² + 1) _ a
quadTwo_no_affine_connection :  ∀ a, ¬ IsAffineConnection ((z − 1)²) _ a
```

(`HTGeometry/QuadOneNoAffineConnection.lean`, `HTGeometry/QuadTwoNoAffineConnection.lean`.)

For `z² + 1` the critical point is `0` and the critical value `1`, so the critical obstruction puts a
pole at one of them.  A pole at `1` runs forward along `1, 2, 5, 26, …` — strictly increasing, hence
injective, and never `0`, hence critical-free.  A pole at `0` pulls back to a pole at `i`, which is
neither periodic nor postcritical, so `exists_backward_orbit` supplies an injective critical-free
backward orbit through it.  Either way the pole set is infinite, contradicting
`finite_support_ratOrder`.

For `(z − 1)²` the critical point is `1` and the critical value `0`.  This map is postcritically
finite — the critical orbit is the two-cycle `1 ↦ 0 ↦ 1` — so the forward orbit is useless and the
whole argument runs backward: a pole at `0` travels forward to a pole at `1` (the critical value is
not the critical point), and `2` is a preimage of `1` which is aperiodic and non-postcritical, so
the backward orbit through it again produces infinitely many poles.

### What this closes, and what it does not

The equation the card named at the end of pass 5 — `4z²·a(z²+1) = 2z·a(z) − 2` and its companion for
`(z − 1)²` — has no rational solution.  Equivalently: **neither quadratic map admits a normalizing
shift of the shape `t = a·y₁²` at jet level two.**

What is still needed to close the fixed branch at jet order two is the shape reduction: a normalizing
shift at level two is an arbitrary element `t` of `jetPrefixField C 2 = C(y₀, y₁)`, not a priori of
the form `a(y₀)·y₁²`.  The reduction is a slicing argument in `y₁` over `C(y₀)`, and every ingredient
is already Lean-owned:

- `jetPrefixField_succ` presents `jetPrefixField C 2` as `C(y₀)` adjoined the transcendental `y₁`;
- `substitutionShift_one` makes the substitution act on `y₁` by the pure scaling `y₁ ↦ ρ·y₁`, so
  `twistComp Φ T` preserves `y₁`-degree and coprimality;
- writing `t = P(y₁)/Q(y₁)` in lowest terms, `Φ(Q) | Q` and equal degrees force `Φ(q_j) = ρ^{n−j} q_j`
  for every coefficient, i.e. an invariant differential of weight `j − n` and eigenvalue one, which
  `quadOne_no_invariant_differential` kills unless `j = n` and `q_j` is constant — so `Q = y₁ⁿ`;
- the numerator then satisfies `Φ(P) = ρ^{n+1}P − ρⁿ·R''·y₁^{n+2}`, whose coefficient at `y₁^i` is
  again an invariant-differential relation for `i ≠ n+2` and is *exactly* the affine-connection
  equation for `a = p_{n+2}` at `i = n+2`.

So the shape reduction consumes precisely the two non-existence theorems now in hand, and the level-two
fixed branch closes once it is written.  At jet order `M ≥ 3` the same slicing runs in `y_{M−1}`, but
the substitution is then genuinely affine on the top coordinate rather than a scaling, and the
homogeneous equation `Φ(h) = ρ·h` re-introduces the level-`(M−1)` shift problem; the weighted torus
action `y_j ↦ c^j y_j` (`j ≥ 1`), which commutes with the substitution because every Faà di Bruno term
of `δ^j(R(y₀))` is isobaric of weight `j`, is the tool that organizes that induction.

The card's *general* statement remains gated on the general form of the obstruction — *a rational map
of degree at least two admitting no nonzero-weight invariant differential admits no rational affine
connection either* — which for an arbitrary map needs the finiteness of completely invariant sets,
i.e. the exceptional-set theorem, not just an orbit chase at one point.  The general critical
obstruction and the general pole transport of `RationalAffineConnection.lean` are the unconditional
half of it.

## Pass 7 — the shape reduction at jet level two, and the fixed branch there

The reduction named at the end of pass 6 is done: a normalizing shift at jet level two is *any*
element of `C(y₀, y₁)`, and it is now proved that any such element produces a rational affine
connection.  Combined with pass 6, neither quadratic map admits an invariant affine structure at jet
level two.

### New module

`HTGeometry/JetAffineConnectionLevelTwo.lean` (unconditional, axiom-clean, `HTGeometry`-wired).

```text
jetDerivation_of_order_zero    δ v = y₁ · ∂₀v   for v of order zero
substitutionShift_two          σ₂ = (∂₀ R'(y₀)) · y₁²
substitutionDeriv_ratFuncDeriv the second derivative of R, read on the jet field
exists_ratFuncDeriv_affine_connection_of_hasNormalizingShift_two
exists_isAffineConnection_of_hasNormalizingShift_two    -- for a polynomial map
```

`substitutionShift_two` is the chain rule twice: `δ(R(y₀)) = R'(y₀)·y₁` is already Lean-owned
(`substitutionJet_one`), and differentiating it once more with the Leibniz rule and the order-zero
chain rule gives `δ²(R(y₀)) = R''(y₀)·y₁² + R'(y₀)·y₂`, whose remainder over the affine action is the
claim.  The order-zero chain rule itself is `freeCoordinate_chainRule` on the single coordinate
`y₀`.

The main theorem is the slicing.  Write the shift as `t = P(y₁)/Q(y₁)` in lowest terms over
`jetPrefixField C 1`, the rational functions of `y₀`.  Because `substitutionShift_one` makes the
substitution act on `y₁` by the *pure scaling* `y₁ ↦ R'·y₁`, the twisted composition
`twistComp Φ (C ρ · X)` preserves degrees (`natDegree_twistComp`) and coprimality (transport of a
Bézout identity), so cross-multiplying the shift equation and cancelling gives

```text
Φ̃(P)·Q = Φ̃(Q)·(C ρ·P − C R''·X²·Q),      Φ̃(Q) ∣ Q,      Φ̃(Q) = C e · Q
```

with `e ≠ 0` by `eq_C_mul_of_dvd_of_natDegree_eq`, and then `Φ̃(P) = C e·(C ρ·P − C R''·X²·Q)`.
Only *one* coefficient comparison is needed.  At index `n + 2`, where `n = deg Q`, the identity
`(Q·X²).coeff (n+2) = Q.coeff n` turns the two relations

```text
Φ(q)·ρⁿ = e·q,        Φ(p)·ρ^{n+2} = e·(ρ·p − R''·q),        p = P.coeff (n+2), q = Q.coeff n
```

into `Φ(p/q)·ρ² = ρ·(p/q) − R''` after eliminating `e` and cancelling `ρⁿ`.  Transporting through
`exists_ratFunc_of_order_zero` and `ratFuncEval_ratFuncSubstitution` puts it in `RatFunc C`, and
`ratFuncDeriv_algebraMap` identifies it with `IsAffineConnection` for a polynomial map.

### The remaining descent

What is left for these two maps is the *same* statement at jet order `M ≥ 3`, and the difficulty is
structural rather than arithmetic: at level `M` the substitution acts on the top coordinate
`y_{M−1}` by the genuinely affine `y_{M−1} ↦ ρ·y_{M−1} + σ_{M−1}` rather than by a scaling, so the
twisted composition no longer acts diagonally on coefficients.  The slicing still gives
`Φ̃(Q) = C e · Q`, and there the argument splits:

- if `deg Q ≥ 1`, `exists_normalizing_shift` applied at level `M − 1` produces a normalizing shift
  one level down, and the descent runs;
- if `deg Q = 0` the shift is a *polynomial* `u` in `y_{M−1}` with `Φ̃(u) = C ρ·u − (C A·X + C B)`,
  where `σ_M = A·y_{M−1} + B` with `A = ∂_{M−2}σ_{M−1} + (∂₀ρ)·y₁` and `A, B` of order below
  `M − 1`.  Comparing the two top coefficients then reproduces the affine cocycle
  `twistComp_affine_cocycle` and again descends — provided `deg u ≥ 3`.  The degrees `0, 1, 2` are
  the genuinely open cases.

The two inputs that the descent still needs in Lean are the affine presentation
`σ_M = A·y_{M−1} + B` (a derivation-expansion computation of the same kind as
`substitutionShift_two`, one coordinate higher) and the three low-degree cases.  Nothing in either
depends on the map, so both are general infrastructure.

### The level-`M` descent, analysed

Writing out the slicing at level `M ≥ 3` reduces the descent to two cases, and shows precisely where
it is still open.  The top coordinate is `y_{M−1}` and the substitution acts on it by the affine
`y_{M−1} ↦ ρ·y_{M−1} + σ_{M−1}`.

*The shift term is affine in the top coordinate.*  From `δ(ρ) = R''(y₀)·y₁` and the commutator
`[∂_j, δ] = ∂_{j−1}` one gets the recursion `σ_M = R''·y₁·y_{M−1} + δ(σ_{M−1})` and hence

```text
σ_M = A·y_{M−1} + B,    A = ∂_{M−1}σ_M = M · R''(y₀) · y₁,    A, B of order below M − 1,
```

by induction from `σ₂ = R''·y₁²` (`∂₁σ₂ = 2R''y₁`).  In particular `A ≠ 0` whenever `R'' ≠ 0`.

*The slicing.*  Slicing `t = P/Q` in `y_{M−1}` over `jetPrefixField C (M−1)`, the same divisibility
argument gives `Φ̃(Q) = C e·Q`, and then:

- `deg Q ≥ 1` — `exists_normalizing_shift` applied at level `M − 1` produces a normalizing shift one
  level down, and the descent runs;
- `deg Q = 0` — the shift is a polynomial `u` of degree `m` in `y_{M−1}` over
  `jetPrefixField C (M−1)`, with `Φ̃(u) = C ρ·u − (C A·X + C B)`, and comparing coefficients:
  - `m = 0` forces `A = 0`, impossible since `A = M·R''·y₁ ≠ 0`;
  - `m ≥ 3` reproduces the affine cocycle at the two top coefficients — `Φ(u_m)·ρ^{m−1} = u_m` and
    `m·u_m·L + Φ(u_{m−1})·ρ^{m−1} = ρ·u_{m−1}` — so `s = u_{m−1}/(m·u_m)` satisfies
    `Φ(s) = ρ·s − σ_{M−1}` and the descent runs;
  - `m = 1` and `m = 2` are open.  At `m = 1` the top coefficient satisfies
    `Φ(u₁)·ρ = ρ·u₁ − A`, and the *weight-one part* of that equation is exactly the affine-connection
    equation: the weighted torus action `y_j ↦ c^j·y_j` (`j ≥ 1`) commutes with the substitution,
    because every Faà di Bruno term of `δ^j(R(y₀))` is isobaric of weight `j`, and `A` is isobaric of
    weight one.  What is missing is that a *rational* solution of an isobaric equation is itself
    isobaric of the same weight, and that a weight-one isobaric rational function of `y₀, …, y_{M−2}`
    has the form `a(y₀)·y₁` — the second is false as stated (`y₂/y₁` is also isobaric of weight one),
    so the weight argument needs the additional information carried by the equation, not the grading
    alone.

*A positive check on the shape of the obstruction.*  In the converse direction the two levels are
linked exactly by the affine connection: if `τ` is a shift at level `M − 1`, so that
`v = y_{M−1} + τ` satisfies `Φ(v) = ρ·v`, then applying `δ` gives

```text
Φ(δτ) = ρ·δτ − σ_M + R''·y₁·v,
```

and correcting by `w = a(y₀)·y₁·v` turns `δτ − w` into a shift at level `M` precisely when
`a(R)·(R')² = R'·a − R''`.  So the affine connection is the exact obstruction to propagating a shift
*upward*; the open half is the propagation downward at `m ∈ {1, 2}`.
