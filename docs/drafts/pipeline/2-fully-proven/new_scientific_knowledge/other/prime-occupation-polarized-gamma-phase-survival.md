## Exact theorem

For a finite set `S` of rational primes, let

```text
M_S={product_(p in S) p^(nu_p) : nu_p in N}
```

be the positive `S`-smooth occupation monoid and put

```text
Z_S(k)=sum_(n in M_S) n^(-k)
      =product_(p in S)(1-p^(-k))^(-1),   k>0.              (1)
```

The sum in `(1)` is finite for every fixed finite `S`, including `k=1`. Define the two entire
opposite-face products

```text
A_S(t)=product_(n in M_S)(1-t/n),
B_S(t)=product_(n in M_S)(1+t/n),                           (2)
```

their unpolarized intensity

```text
I_S(t)=A_S(t)B_S(t),                                       (3)
```

and their tangent-normalized polarized phase

```text
R_S(t)=exp(2t Z_S(1)) A_S(t)/B_S(t).                        (4)
```

Then:

1. `A_S` and `B_S` are entire. If `#S>=2`, each is hypertranscendental over `C(t)`.
2. As `S` tends cofinally through the finite prime subsets,

   ```text
   I_S(t) -> I_infty(t)=sin(pi t)/(pi t)                    (5)
   ```

   normally on `C`.
3. On every compact subset of `|t|<1`,

   ```text
   R_S(t) -> R_infty(t)
          =exp(2 gamma t) Gamma(1+t)/Gamma(1-t).            (6)
   ```

   The right side gives the meromorphic continuation of the limit.
4. `I_infty` is differentially algebraic over `C(t)`, whereas `R_infty` is
   hypertranscendental over the differential field generated by `I_infty`.
5. On `|t|<1`, the complete logarithmic jets split by parity:

   ```text
   log I_S(t)
     =-sum_(j>=1) Z_S(2j)t^(2j)/j,                          (7)

   log R_S(t)
     =-2 sum_(j>=1) Z_S(2j+1)t^(2j+1)/(2j+1).              (8)
   ```

   Cofinal completion replaces every `Z_S(k)`, `k>1`, by `zeta(k)`. Thus the DA intensity
   retains exactly the even zeta tower, while the HT phase retains exactly the odd zeta tower
   from `zeta(3)` onward.
6. The counterterm in `(4)` is canonical among linear exponential gauges. More precisely,

   ```text
   d/dt log(A_S/B_S)|_(t=0)=-2Z_S(1),                       (9)
   ```

   so `exp(c_S t)A_S/B_S` has zero logarithmic tangent at the marked point `t=0` if and only if
   `c_S=2Z_S(1)`.

The raw phases `A_S/B_S` therefore do not have a cofinal limit: their first logarithmic trace is
`-2Z_S(1)`, and `Z_S(1)` diverges cofinally. The polarized completion is not just the regular
function `(6)`. Its source-effective boundary datum is the pair

```text
(R_infty, [2t Z_S(1)]_cofinal),                             (10)
```

where the second coordinate records the odd, reflection-changing first-trace anomaly removed by
the unique tangent normalization.

## Dependency-closed proof

### 1. Finite products and finite-face hypertranscendence

Unique factorization identifies `M_S` with `N^S`. Hence

```text
sum_(n in M_S) 1/n=product_(p in S)(1-p^(-1))^(-1)<infinity.
```

The genus-zero product criterion makes both products in `(2)` entire.

For `q_p=p^(-1)`, `A_S` is the complete backward multiple-`q`-Pochhammer occupation product.
The periods

```text
tau_p=i log(p)/(2pi)
```

have positive imaginary part. Any rational relation among `1` and two distinct `tau_p` would,
after taking real and imaginary parts, give an integral multiplicative relation between distinct
primes. Unique factorization forbids it. Thus every `#S>=2` packet has a rank-three minor, and the
multiple-elliptic-Gamma period-rank theorem makes `A_S` hypertranscendental. Affine reflection
`t -> -t` gives the same conclusion for `B_S`.

### 2. Unpolarized completion

Pairing the opposite faces gives

```text
I_S(t)=product_(n in M_S)(1-t^2/n^2).                      (11)
```

The directed union of the monoids `M_S` is all positive integers. Since

```text
sum_(n>=1) 1/n^2<infinity,
```

the standard normal-product theorem gives cofinal normal convergence to

```text
product_(n>=1)(1-t^2/n^2)=sin(pi t)/(pi t),
```

which is `(5)`. The removable value at `t=0` is `1`.

### 3. Polarized completion and exact odd jet

For `|t|<1`, absolute convergence permits logarithmic expansion:

```text
log(A_S/B_S)
 =sum_(n in M_S)[log(1-t/n)-log(1+t/n)]
 =-2tZ_S(1)
  -2sum_(j>=1) Z_S(2j+1)t^(2j+1)/(2j+1).                  (12)
```

Adding the counterterm in `(4)` proves `(8)`. For every `k>1`,

```text
Z_S(k) -> product_p(1-p^(-k))^(-1)=zeta(k).               (13)
```

Fix `0<r<1`. For `|t|<=r` and `j>=1`,

```text
Z_S(2j+1)<=zeta(2j+1)<=zeta(3),
```

and

```text
sum_(j>=1) r^(2j+1)/(2j+1)<infinity.
```

Dominated convergence in `(8)` gives locally uniform convergence to

```text
exp(-2sum_(j>=1) zeta(2j+1)t^(2j+1)/(2j+1)).              (14)
```

The classical Taylor expansion

```text
log Gamma(1+t)-log Gamma(1-t)
 =-2gamma t
  -2sum_(j>=1) zeta(2j+1)t^(2j+1)/(2j+1)                  (15)
```

identifies `(14)` with `(6)`.

Equivalently, Euler's reciprocal-Gamma product gives the global meromorphic identity

```text
product_(n>=1)
  [exp(2t/n)(1-t/n)/(1+t/n)]
 =exp(2gamma t)Gamma(1+t)/Gamma(1-t).                     (16)
```

Thus `(6)` is not a formal-germ artifact.

### 4. Differential phase separation

The sine carrier in `(5)` satisfies a second-order linear differential equation, so `I_infty` is
differentially algebraic. The reflected quotient `Gamma(1+t)/Gamma(1-t)` is
hypertranscendental by the exact Gamma reflection-orbit theorem. Multiplication by the nonzero
differentially algebraic gauge `exp(2gamma t)` preserves hypertranscendence.

The differential field generated by `I_infty` is a differentially algebraic extension of `C(t)`.
Differential-algebraic base change therefore preserves and reflects hypertranscendence, proving
the stronger relative claim in item 4.

### 5. Canonicity and the first-trace class

Differentiating `(12)` at zero gives `(9)`. Hence the tangent-pointing condition uniquely forces
the counterterm in `(4)`.

The ordinary Euler product at `1` diverges, so `Z_S(1)` is cofinally unbounded. Therefore the raw
phase has no pointed cofinal limit. Reflection `t -> -t` negates its first-trace term, whereas the
intensity product cancels the two terms. This proves that the DA collapse forgets a specific
oriented class rather than hypertranscendence disappearing without a trace. `QED`
