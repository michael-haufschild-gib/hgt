# Inverse-Gamma polygamma principal-part power law

- **ID:** inverse-gamma-polygamma-principal-part-power-law
- **Stage:** 2-fully-proven
- **Type:** THEOREM (paper + formal core)
- **Status:** `[PROVED HERE] [FORMALIZED CORE] [CANDIDATE NEW SCIENTIFIC KNOWLEDGE]`
- **Statement:** At every inverse-Gamma pole of \(\psi\!\circ\!\Gamma\), the complete tower of
  leading principal-part values of \(\psi^{(q)}\!\circ\!\Gamma\) is the factorial-power sequence
  generated by the single digamma-composite residue.

## Values

Fix \(n\geq0\) and a sufficiently large integer \(m\).  Let \(z_{n,m}\) be the distinguished
inverse-Gamma value near \(-n\):
\[
 \Gamma(z_{n,m})=-m.
\]
Put
\[
 \rho_{n,m}
 =\operatorname{Res}_{z=z_{n,m}}\psi(\Gamma z)
 =-\frac1{\Gamma'(z_{n,m})}.
\]
For every \(q\geq0\), define the leading principal-part value
\[
 C_{n,m,q}
 =\lim_{z\to z_{n,m}}
 (z-z_{n,m})^{q+1}\psi^{(q)}(\Gamma z).
\]

## Theorem — factorial-power tower

For every \(q\geq0\),
\[
 \boxed{
 C_{n,m,q}=q!\,\rho_{n,m}^{q+1}.
 }
\]
In particular, \(\psi^{(q)}(\Gamma z)\) has exact pole order \(q+1\) at \(z_{n,m}\).

**Proof.**  At \(w=-m\), the order-\(q\) polygamma has leading principal part
\[
 \psi^{(q)}(w)
 =\frac{(-1)^{q+1}q!}{(w+m)^{q+1}}+	ext{lower pole orders}.
\]
Since
\[
 \Gamma(z)+m
 =\Gamma'(z_{n,m})(z-z_{n,m})+O((z-z_{n,m})^2)
\]
and \(\Gamma'(z_{n,m})\neq0\), composition gives
\[
 C_{n,m,q}
 =\frac{(-1)^{q+1}q!}{\Gamma'(z_{n,m})^{q+1}}
 =q!\left(-\frac1{\Gamma'(z_{n,m})}\right)^{q+1}.
\]
The last factor is \(\rho_{n,m}^{q+1}\). \(\square\)

## Theorem — complete principal-part jet power law

Put \(z_0=z_{n,m}\), \(t=z-z_0\), and define the removable analytic germ
\[
 R_{n,m}(t)
 =-\frac{t}{\Gamma(z_0+t)+m},
 \qquad
 R_{n,m}(0)=-\frac1{\Gamma'(z_0)}=\rho_{n,m}.
\]
Then, as analytic germs at \(t=0\),
\[
 \boxed{
 t^{q+1}\psi^{(q)}(\Gamma(z_0+t))
 \equiv q!R_{n,m}(t)^{q+1}\pmod{t^{q+1}}.
 }
\]
Equivalently, for every \(0\leq j\leq q\),
\[
 \boxed{
 [t^{-(q+1-j)}]\psi^{(q)}(\Gamma(z_0+t))
 =q![t^j]R_{n,m}(t)^{q+1}.
 }
\]
Thus the single analytic germ \(R_{n,m}\) generates the complete principal part of every member
of the outer polygamma tower.

**Proof.**  The Laurent expansion of the digamma function at \(-m\) has the form
\[
 \psi(-m+u)=-u^{-1}+A_m(u),
\]
where \(A_m\) is analytic at zero.  Differentiating \(q\) times gives
\[
 \psi^{(q)}(-m+u)
 =(-1)^{q+1}q!u^{-(q+1)}+A_m^{(q)}(u).
\]
Set \(u(t)=\Gamma(z_0+t)+m\).  Since \(u\) has a simple zero at zero,
\(R_{n,m}(t)=-t/u(t)\) extends analytically.  Multiplication by \(t^{q+1}\) gives
\[
 t^{q+1}\psi^{(q)}(\Gamma(z_0+t))
 =q!R_{n,m}(t)^{q+1}
 +t^{q+1}A_m^{(q)}(u(t)).
\]
The last term vanishes to order at least \(q+1\), proving the germ congruence and all displayed
coefficient identities. \(\square\)

### First subleading coefficient

Write
\[
 d_{n,m}=\Gamma'(z_{n,m}),
 \qquad
 e_{n,m}=\Gamma''(z_{n,m}).
\]
For every \(q\geq1\), let
\[
 B_{n,m,q}
 =[t^{-q}]\psi^{(q)}(\Gamma(z_{n,m}+t)).
\]
Since
\[
 R_{n,m}(t)
 =\rho_{n,m}+\frac{e_{n,m}\rho_{n,m}^2}{2}t+O(t^2),
\]
the complete jet law gives
\[
 \boxed{
 B_{n,m,q}
 =\frac{(q+1)!}{2}\,e_{n,m}\rho_{n,m}^{q+2}.
 }
\]
Equivalently,
\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{B_{n,m,q}}{(q+1)C_{n,m,q}}
 =-\frac{\Gamma''(z_{n,m})}{2\Gamma'(z_{n,m})},
 }
\]
which is independent of \(q\).  The first subleading pole grade therefore adds exactly one scalar
value—the logarithmic curvature of the inner Gamma map—to the residue generator.

## Law 1 — multiplicative semigroup of principal parts

The normalized values
\[
 A_{n,m,q}=\frac{C_{n,m,q}}{q!}
\]
form a geometric power sequence:
\[
 A_{n,m,q}=\rho_{n,m}^{q+1}.
\]
Consequently, for every \(p,q\geq0\),
\[
 \boxed{
 (p+q+1)!\,C_{n,m,p}C_{n,m,q}
 =p!q!\,C_{n,m,p+q+1}.
 }
\]
Thus all all-order principal-part relations are generated by one nonzero scalar value.

## Law 2 — rank-one Hankel and differential-matroid boundary

For every \(r\geq1\),
\[
 \boxed{
 \det\left[
 \frac{C_{n,m,i+j}}{(i+j)!}
 \right]_{0\leq i,j\leq r}=0.
 }
\]
Indeed, the matrix entry is
\(\rho_{n,m}^{i+j+1}=\rho_{n,m}\rho_{n,m}^i\rho_{n,m}^j\), so the matrix has rank one.

This identifies a precise differential-matroid boundary: the leading principal parts of the
entire polygamma-composite tower have rank one, while the full Laurent jets can acquire additional
rank only through the successive Taylor values of \(R_{n,m}\).  Any nonlinear HT determinant
using only the leading pole grade is therefore forced onto this rank-one Veronese locus.  The
first subleading grade is now also classified: after division by \((q+1)C_{n,m,q}\), it is the
single \(q\)-independent Gamma logarithmic-curvature value above.

## Law 3 — analytic interpolation and asymptotic samples

Let \(z_n(x)\) be the analytic inverse-value curve
\[
 \Gamma(z_n(x))=-\frac1x,
\]
and put
\[
 \widehat\rho_n(x)=-\frac1{\Gamma'(z_n(x))}=-x^2z_n'(x).
\]
Then
\[
 \widehat C_{n,q}(x)=q!\widehat\rho_n(x)^{q+1}
\]
is an analytic interpolation whose reciprocal-integer samples are the actual principal-part
values:
\[
 \widehat C_{n,q}(1/m)=C_{n,m,q}.
\]
Using
\[
 \widehat\rho_n(x)
 =r_nx^2-2A_nx^3+O(x^4),
 \qquad
 r_n=\frac{(-1)^n}{n!},
 \qquad
 A_n=\frac{\psi(n+1)}{(n!)^2},
\]
gives
\[
 C_{n,m,q}
 =q!r_n^{q+1}m^{-2(q+1)}
 \left(1-\frac{2(q+1)A_n}{r_nm}+O(m^{-2})\right).
\]

## Hypertranscendence value

The law determines exactly why leading-pole-order arguments alone cannot separate nonlinear
differential monomials in the \(\psi\!\circ\!\Gamma\) HT problem: every outer polygamma leading
coefficient is already a power of the same residue.  The complete jet congruence goes further:
all pole grades are universal power coefficients of \(R_{n,m}\).  The positive next theorem target
is therefore a cross-sample rank theorem for the finite jets of \(R_{n,m}\), rather than another
single-order pole calculation.
