## Proof

### 1. The structural subgroup is DA

The set of `h` for which `Delta_h Psi` is DA is an additive subgroup: DA meromorphic functions form a field and translation preserves differential algebraicity. The three displayed functional equations therefore give

`H subset Spec_DA(Psi)`.

### 2. A defect is constrained over both elliptic curves

Fix `h in C` and put

`d_h = Delta_h Psi`.

Commuting translations gives

`Delta_sigma d_h = Delta_h lambda_tau =: b_tau,h`,

`Delta_tau d_h = Delta_h lambda_sigma =: b_sigma,h`.

Although `lambda_tau` is only quasi-elliptic, its increment under either generator of `Lambda_tau` is constant. Hence `b_tau,h` is `Lambda_tau`-elliptic. Similarly `b_sigma,h` is `Lambda_sigma`-elliptic.

Let `E_tau` and `E_sigma` be the two elliptic function fields, and let

`K = E_tau E_sigma`

be their compositum inside the field of meromorphic functions on `C`. It is a differential field, is stable under `T_tau` and `T_sigma`, and is DA over `C(z)`.

Here “DA over `C(z)`” means that every element of `K` is DA over `C(z)`; the fields are not nested because every element of `K` is 1-periodic whereas `z` is not.

All elements of `K<d_h>_delta` are 1-periodic. An element fixed by both `T_tau` and `T_sigma` has periods `1,tau,sigma`. Since `H` is nondiscrete, such a meromorphic function is constant. Thus the paired system has no new joint difference constants:

`(K<d_h>_delta)^(T_tau,T_sigma)=C`.

### 3. Paired telescoping forced by differential algebraicity

Assume for contradiction that `d_h` is DA over `C(z)`. The implication needed below is valid, but not by field inclusion. Form the compositum `K(z)`. Since `delta(z)=1`, the extension `K(z)/K` is differential algebraic. Since `C(z) subset K(z)`, the function `d_h` is DA over `K(z)`. Transitivity of differential algebraicity therefore makes `d_h` DA over `K`.

We use the following several-translation form of Hardouin--Singer Proposition 3.1.

**Paired telescoping lemma.** Let `k` be a differential field with two commuting automorphisms `rho_1,rho_2`, let their common fixed field be an algebraically closed field `C` on which the derivation is zero, and let `F/k` be a differential field extension stable under both automorphisms with `F^(rho_1,rho_2)=C`. Suppose `y_1,...,y_n in F` obey

`rho_j(y_i)-y_i=a_(j,i) in k`.

If the `y_i` are differentially algebraically dependent over `k`, then there are operators `L_i in C[delta]`, not all zero, and a **single** `g in k` such that

`sum_i L_i(y_i)=g`

and, consequently, for both `j`,

`sum_i L_i(a_(j,i))=rho_j(g)-g`.

Here is the descent proof, since the constant-field point is essential. Let `Ctilde` be a differential closure of `C`. The meromorphic function fields occurring here are regular over `C`, so tensoring with `Ctilde` preserves domains. The standard constant-extension lemma gives

`Frac(F tensor_C Ctilde)^(rho_1,rho_2)=Ctilde`.

For clarity, this remains true for fractions, not only tensors. An invariant fraction uses coefficients from some finitely generated `C`-subalgebra `A subset Ctilde`. At every `C`-specialization of `A` outside the denominator locus it specializes to an element of `F^(rho_1,rho_2)=C`. Write its numerator and denominator in a finite `C`-basis of the coefficient subspace of `F`. Their specializations are proportional on a Zariski-dense set, so every two-by-two coefficient minor vanishes identically. The fraction therefore belongs to `Frac(A)`, and hence to `Ctilde`.

Over `Ctilde`, form the compatible unipotent system for all equations `rho_j(Y_i)-Y_i=a_(j,i)`. In the universal differential solution ring, the evaluation ideal defined by the given `y_i` is a proper invariant differential ideal and contains a nonzero differential relation. Extend it to a maximal invariant differential ideal. The quotient is a several-automorphism parameterized Picard--Vessiot ring in the sense of Hardouin--Singer Appendix 6.2, and its differential Galois group is a proper differential algebraic subgroup of `G_a^n`. Cassidy's classification supplies a nonzero homogeneous linear differential form. The fixed-field theorem then gives operators `Ltilde_i in Ctilde[delta]` and one `gtilde in Frac(k tensor_C Ctilde)` satisfying all telescoping equations simultaneously.

Only finitely many coefficients of `Ctilde` occur in the `Ltilde_i` and in a rational presentation of `gtilde`. Fix those numerator, denominator, and differential-order templates, clear denominators in the simultaneous equations, and normalize one nonzero coefficient of the linear form to one. This is a finite system of polynomial equations and polynomial inequations over `C`: no derivatives of the unknown coefficients occur because the equations contain only `rho_j(g)-g` and the fixed functions `delta^m(a_(j,i))`. It has a solution in `Ctilde`; since `C` is algebraically closed, it has a solution in `C`. Specializing gives `L_i in C[delta]` and `g in k` satisfying both telescoping equations. For the original `y_i`, the difference `sum_i L_i(y_i)-g` is fixed by both automorphisms and hence belongs to `C`; absorbing it into `g` gives the asserted equality.

Apply the lemma with `k=K`, `F=K<d_h>_delta`, `rho_1=T_sigma`, and `rho_2=T_tau`. The common-constant condition was proved in Step 2. For one defect the lemma yields a nonzero

`L in C[delta]`

and a single `g in K` satisfying

`T_sigma(g)-g=L(b_tau,h)`,

`T_tau(g)-g=L(b_sigma,h)`.

### 4. Scalar-extension descent to one elliptic curve

The fields `E_tau` and `E_sigma` are algebraically independent over `C`. Indeed, suppose nonconstant `f in E_tau` and `v in E_sigma` obey `P(f,v)=0`. For a period `omega` of `f`, all functions `v(z+n omega)` are roots of the fixed polynomial `P(f(z),Y)`. Only finitely many roots exist, so two translates coincide and a nonzero multiple of `omega` is a period of `v`. Reversing the roles of the fields shows that their period lattices are commensurable. This contradicts the `Q`-independence of `1,tau,sigma`.

Consequently the multiplication map

`E_tau tensor_C E_sigma -> Mer(C)`

is injective and `K` is the function field of the product of the two elliptic curves. The translation `T_sigma` acts on the first factor and trivially on the second. View `g` as a rational function on this product. Outside a proper closed subset of the second elliptic curve, specialization at a complex point is defined and does not annihilate its denominator. Specializing the first paired equation gives a rational function `g_tau in E_tau` satisfying

`T_sigma(g_tau)-g_tau=L(b_tau,h)`.

### 5. Elliptic pole-orbit boundaries force the rank-three group

On the elliptic curve `C/Lambda_tau`, the function `lambda_tau` has one simple pole, of residue one. Thus

`b_tau,h=lambda_tau(z+h)-lambda_tau(z)`

has two simple poles with opposite residues, at classes differing by `h`.

Let `N` be the order of the nonzero operator `L`. The order-`N+1` principal parts of `L(b_tau,h)` at those two points are nonzero. If

`h notin Lambda_tau + Z sigma`,

the two poles lie in distinct `T_sigma`-orbits. Each such orbit therefore contains exactly one pole
of `L(b_tau,h)`.

No Laurent-coefficient extraction is needed. On a fixed nontorsion orbit, an elliptic meromorphic
function `u` has only finitely many pole indices because the elliptic curve is compact. If this set
is nonempty, its least index makes `T_sigma(u)-u` singular one step before the support, while its
greatest index makes it singular at the final support point: at each boundary one summand has a pole
and the other is regular. The two boundary points are distinct. Hence an elliptic translation
coboundary cannot have exactly one pole on an orbit. Consequently `L(b_tau,h)` cannot equal
`T_sigma(g_tau)-g_tau` unless

`h in Lambda_tau + Z sigma = H`.

This contradicts differential algebraicity for every `h notin H` and proves the exact spectrum.

### 5a. Simultaneous defects and the matroid formula

Choose shifts `h_1,...,h_m` representing distinct nonzero cosets of `H`, and suppose their defects are differentially algebraically dependent over `K`. Apply the same multi-operator Picard--Vessiot argument to the direct-sum unipotent system generated by all `d_i`. Its differential Galois group is then a proper differential algebraic subgroup of `G_a^m`. Hence there exist operators `L_i in C[delta]`, not all zero, and `g in K` such that

`g=sum_i L_i(d_i)`.

After applying `T_sigma-1` and specializing the `E_sigma` factor as above, there is `g_tau in E_tau` with

`T_sigma(g_tau)-g_tau = sum_i L_i(b_tau,h_i)`.

Let `N` be the largest order occurring among the `L_i`, and choose an index `i` whose order-`N`
coefficient is nonzero. Because the cosets `h_i+H` are nonzero and pairwise distinct, the pole class
`-h_i` belongs neither to the orbit of zero nor to the pole orbit belonging to any other `h_j`.
The order-`N+1` term proves that the right side has a pole there, while exact theta pole
classification proves that it has no other pole on that orbit. The coefficient-free pole-support
boundary theorem therefore excludes it from the `T_sigma`-coboundary image. This contradiction
proves differential algebraic independence over `K`.

For a general finite list, each zero-coset defect is DA. If `h_i-h_j in H`, then

`d_i-d_j = Delta_(h_i-h_j) Psi(z+h_j)`

is DA. Hence all defects are differentially algebraic over a set of representatives of the distinct nonzero cosets. Finally `K(z)` is a differential-algebraic extension of both `K` and `C(z)`: over `K` because `delta(z)=1`, and over `C(z)` because the elliptic generators of `K` are DA. Differential-algebraic base extension preserves differential transcendence degree, so the lower and upper bounds agree over `C(z)`, proving the displayed formula.

### 6. Hypertranscendence

Choose `h notin H`. The theorem makes `Delta_h Psi` HT. If `Psi` were DA, every translate and difference of translates would be DA, contradiction. Hence `Psi` is HT.

If `Gamma` were DA, its logarithmic derivative `Gamma'/Gamma=Psi` would be DA. Hence `Gamma` is HT as well.

Alternatively, for any `h notin H`, the quotient `Gamma(z+h)/Gamma(z)` is HT by the multiplicative form above; it would be DA if `Gamma` were DA.

## Why the one-operator proof fails

For a single translation, the ambient meromorphic solution field has many nonconstant periodic constants, so the ordinary one-operator Hardouin--Singer criterion cannot be applied directly to the actual elliptic-gamma defect. The paired system is essential: the common periods `1,tau,sigma` form a nondiscrete group, forcing the joint constant field to collapse to `C`.

This is the new mechanism, not the already-known elliptic orbital-residue obstruction by itself.
