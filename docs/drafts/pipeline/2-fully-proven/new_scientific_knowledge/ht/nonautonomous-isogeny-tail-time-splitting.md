## Setup

Let `C` be algebraically closed of characteristic zero with trivial derivation.  For every
`i >= 0`, let `G_i/C` be a connected commutative algebraic group and let

`a_i : G_i -> G_(i+1)`

be an isogeny.  Define the prefix isogeny

`A_(i,n) = a_(i+n-1) o ... o a_i : G_i -> G_(i+n)`,

with `A_(i,0)=id`.  If `g_i` is a differential-generic point of `G_i`, put
`K_i=C<g_i>`.  Pullback gives injections `A_(i,n)^* : K_(i+n) -> K_i`.  The phase tail is

`T_i = intersection_(n >= 0) A_(i,n)^* K_(i+n).`

Let

`Gamma_(i,n) = ker A_(i,n)(C)`,

`H_i = Zariski closure of union_n Gamma_(i,n) in G_i`,

`Q_i = G_i/H_i`,

and write `pi_i:G_i->Q_i` and `ell_i:G_i->Lie(G_i)` for the quotient and logarithmic
derivative.

## Theorem: nonautonomous Maurer--Cartan time splitting

**[PROVED HERE]** For every phase `i`:

1. **Exact deck closure**

   `T_i = K_i^(H_i(C)).`                                           (1)

2. **Exact position--velocity generators**

   `T_i = C<pi_i(g_i), ell_i(g_i)>.`                               (2)

3. **Canonical transport of the erased and reversible sectors**

   `a_i^(-1)(H_(i+1)) = H_i`,                                     (3)

   so `a_i` induces an algebraic-group isomorphism

   `q_i : Q_i -> Q_(i+1)`.                                        (4)

4. **Flat velocity transport**

   `ell_(i+1)(a_i(g_i)) = d a_i (ell_i(g_i))`,                     (5)

   and `d a_i : Lie(G_i) -> Lie(G_(i+1))` is an isomorphism.

Consequently the persistent positions `Q_i` and the persistent Maurer--Cartan velocities form a
flat local system over discrete time.  In the purely expanding case `H_i=G_i` for every `i`,

`T_i=C<ell_i(g_i)>`,                                               (6)

and

`dtrdeg_C T_i = dim G_i`,                                         (7)

although `T_i intersection C(G_i)=C`.

## Proof

### Step 1: the phase tail is the total deck invariant field

For each finite prefix, `A_(i,n)` is a finite etale quotient by the translation action of
`Gamma_(i,n)`.  As in the autonomous theorem, prolongation preserves this constant deck group,
so

`A_(i,n)^*K_(i+n)=K_i^(Gamma_(i,n)).`

Intersecting over `n` gives invariance under the union of the prefix deck groups.  Every
differential rational function has finite jet order, so invariance under that union is
equivalent to invariance under its Zariski closure `H_i`.  This proves (1).

### Step 2: identify all phase invariants

The differential rational map

`Psi_i(g)=(pi_i(g),ell_i(g))`

has fibres exactly the `H_i(C)`-translation orbits: equality of `pi_i` puts two points in the
same `H_i` coset, while equality of `ell_i` makes their difference a constant point.  The
differential rational quotient theorem therefore gives (2).

### Step 3: prove exact phase transport of topology

Prefix kernels satisfy the identity

`Gamma_(i,n+1)=a_i^(-1)(Gamma_(i+1,n)).`

Taking unions gives

`union_n Gamma_(i,n)=a_i^(-1)(union_n Gamma_(i+1,n)).`

An isogeny in characteristic zero is finite etale and open, so inverse image commutes with the
Zariski closure of this dense union.  This proves (3).  The induced map (4) is an isogeny with
trivial kernel and hence an isomorphism.

### Step 4: transport velocities

Functoriality of the logarithmic derivative gives (5).  The differential of an isogeny in
characteristic zero is a Lie-algebra isomorphism.  Equations (4) and (5) prove flat transport.
The pure-expansion assertions follow from the autonomous position--velocity theorem at each
phase.

## Periodic/Floquet corollary

For a protocol of period `p`, one circuit produces two intrinsic holonomies:

- an algebraic-group automorphism of the reversible quotient `Q_i`;
- a linear automorphism of `Lie(G_i)` carrying the persistent velocity.

The tail is the differential field on which these holonomies act.  The previously proved
two-phase example

`R_1(z)=z^2+1`, `R_2(z)=(z-1)^2`

is the one-dimensional boundary-holonomy instance: use the phase coordinates `z` and `z-1` on
copies of `G_m`.  The transported Maurer--Cartan currents are `dz/z` and `dz/(z-1)`, and the
two-step holonomy is multiplication by four.

Thus two maps that look nonexceptional in one fixed coordinate system can form an exceptional
time protocol because exceptional topology is phase-relative, not attached to either formula in
isolation.

## Relation to the scalar projective-time law

The scalar theorem says that a one-generator phase tail is transported up to `PGL_2(C)` and has
a canonical Schwarzian.  The present theorem resolves the hidden geometry when the protocol
comes from isogenies:

- the `PGL_2` ambiguity is the choice of scalar tail coordinate;
- the intrinsic object underneath it is the finite-holonomy invariant field of a
  Maurer--Cartan velocity;
- time transport is linear on Lie velocity and algebraic on the reversible quotient.

The projective law is therefore the coordinate shadow of the algebraic-group time splitting.

## Physical/topological reading

This is interpretation, not an extra theorem.  Discrete time is not supplied externally as a
flow parameter.  It is the gluing category of phase-dependent constraint quotients.  At each
phase, coarse-graining separates:

- reversible position, which is transported as an algebraic group `Q_i`;
- irreversible position, erased by the dense deck closure `H_i`;
- persistent velocity, transported as a flat Lie-algebra field.

This is a precise topology of time for the constraint tower.  It resembles a Floquet bundle and
a holographic boundary, but makes no claim of quantum mechanics or black-hole physics.

The theorem supplies a strict diagnostic for a time-dependent RH protocol.  A sequence of

### The index trick that made the nonautonomous prefix work

A prefix composite of a *time-dependent* protocol lands in a phase whose index is arithmetic
(`i + n`), and the two natural recursions -- append at the far end, peel at the near end -- disagree
on the index expression (`(i + n) + 1` versus `(i + 1) + n`).  Only the first is definitional, so the
peeling identity, which is the whole content of `(3)`, would be a dependent cast.

Carry the index by the *same* recursion as the composite: `prefixIndex i 0 = i`,
`prefixIndex i (n+1) = prefixIndex (i+1) n`.  Then peeling is definitional and the kernel identity is
one application of `AddMonoidHom.comap_ker`.  Nothing else in the file mentions the target index at
all -- kernels live in the source group -- so no arithmetic on indices ever enters a type.
Monotonicity of the kernels, which naively needs the far-end decomposition, follows instead by
induction on the depth with the phase generalized.

## Half of `hdeg` is free — and the right mathlib lemma
