# Suzuki energy derivative: shifted-range hyperplane repair

**Status:** `[PAPER-PROVED] [NOVELTY-UNVERIFIED] [LEAN-IN-PROGRESS]`

**Primary source:** Masatoshi Suzuki, *Weil's quadratic form via the screw function*,
[arXiv:2606.09096v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09096), Theorem 1.5 and §6.2.

## Claim

Fix `a > 0`.  Let `H(T_a)` be Suzuki's energy Hilbert space, let

\[
  J:C_c^\infty(-a,a)\longrightarrow H(T_a)
\]

be its canonical dense injection, and put `D_a u=i u'` on this core.  The physical embedding
`H(T_a) -> L^2(-a,a)` is continuous.  Define continuous complex-linear functionals

\[
  L_\varepsilon(h)=\int_{-a}^{a}e^{\varepsilon x}(Jh)(x)\,dx,
  \qquad \varepsilon\in\{-1,+1\}.
\]

Then

\[
 \overline{\operatorname{ran}(D_a+\varepsilon i)}^{\,H(T_a)}
   =\ker L_\varepsilon .                                      \tag{1}
\]

Each right-hand side is a closed codimension-one hyperplane.  Consequently

\[
 \ker(D_a^*-\varepsilon i)
   =\operatorname{ran}(D_a+\varepsilon i)^\perp
\]

is one-dimensional (with the usual sign swap between shifted ranges and deficiency spaces), so
`D_a` has deficiency indices `(1,1)`.

This repairs the deficiency-index part of Suzuki's Theorem 1.5 without asserting the unsupported
adjoint-domain regularity used in the published proof.

## Published proof gap

Section 6.2 argues that continuity of

\[
  u\longmapsto \langle D_a u,v\rangle_{T_a}
\]

in the energy norm implies continuity in the `L^2` norm.  The established physical embedding has
the opposite estimate,

\[
  \|u\|_2\le C\|u\|_{T_a}.
\]

It therefore transports `L^2`-continuity to energy-continuity, not energy-continuity to
`L^2`-continuity.  The claimed distributional ODE description of the adjoint domain does not
follow from that estimate.  Proof below never makes this implication.

## Proof of the shifted core ranges

Write

\[
  S_\varepsilon u=(D_a+\varepsilon i)u=i(u'+\varepsilon u).
\]

For `u in C_c^infty(-a,a)`, integration by parts gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
 L_\varepsilon(S_\varepsilon u)
 &=i\int_{-a}^{a}e^{\varepsilon x}(u'(x)+\varepsilon u(x))\,dx\\
 &=i\int_{-a}^{a}(e^{\varepsilon x}u(x))'\,dx=0.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus `ran S_epsilon` is contained in the core kernel of `L_epsilon`.

Conversely, take `g in C_c^infty(-a,a)` with

\[
  \int_{-a}^{a}e^{\varepsilon x}g(x)\,dx=0.
\]

Choose `r<a` with `supp g subset (-r,r)` and define

\[
 u(x)=-i e^{-\varepsilon x}\int_{-r}^{x}e^{\varepsilon t}g(t)\,dt.       \tag{2}
\]

The integrand is smooth.  Its primitive vanishes for `x <= -r`; the zero weighted moment makes it
vanish also for `x >= r`.  Hence `u in C_c^infty(-a,a)`.  Differentiating (2) yields

\[
  u'(x)+\varepsilon u(x)=-i g(x),
  \qquad S_\varepsilon u=g.
\]

Therefore

\[
  \operatorname{ran}S_\varepsilon
   =J(C_c^\infty(-a,a))\cap\ker L_\varepsilon .                \tag{3}
\]

## Density inside the hyperplane

Choose `q_epsilon in C_c^infty(-a,a)` with `L_epsilon(Jq_epsilon)=1`; a nonnegative nonzero bump,
rescaled by its strictly positive weighted integral, supplies one.  Define

\[
 P_\varepsilon h
   =h-L_\varepsilon(h)Jq_\varepsilon .                         \tag{4}
\]

This is a continuous linear projection of `H(T_a)` onto `ker L_epsilon`.  Because `J` has dense
range and `P_epsilon` is continuous and surjective onto that kernel, `P_epsilon J` has dense range
in `ker L_epsilon`.  Moreover

\[
 P_\varepsilon(Ju)=J\bigl(u-L_\varepsilon(Ju)q_\varepsilon\bigr),
\]

and the corrected core vector lies in the kernel.  Hence (3) is dense in `ker L_epsilon`.  Since
the kernel is closed, (1) follows.

## Nontriviality and codimension

The chosen `q_epsilon` proves `L_epsilon` is nonzero.  A nonzero continuous linear functional to
`C` is surjective, and its kernel therefore has quotient dimension one.  Equivalently, (4) gives

\[
 H(T_a)=\ker L_\varepsilon\oplus \mathbb C Jq_\varepsilon.
\]

Thus each shifted-range closure has codimension one.

## Deficiency dimensions

For every densely defined operator `T` on a complex Hilbert space,

\[
  \ker T^*=\operatorname{ran}(T)^\perp .                       \tag{5}
\]

Apply (5) to `T=D_a+\varepsilon i`; equivalently use the standard shifted-adjoint identity.  By
(1), its orthogonal complement is `(ker L_epsilon)^perp`, the one-dimensional line generated by
the Riesz vector of `L_epsilon`.  The two signs give the two deficiency dimensions, both equal to
one.

## Hostile audit

1. **No norm reversal.**  Only continuity of the physical embedding `H(T_a) -> L^2(-a,a)` is used.
2. **No assumed adjoint formula.**  The proof calculates closures of shifted core ranges, then uses
   the general Hilbert-space adjoint/range identity.
3. **No boundary trace on energy vectors.**  Endpoint values occur only for compactly supported
   smooth functions, where they vanish classically.
4. **No formal surrogate.**  `H(T_a)`, its physical embedding, its native `C_c^infty` core, and
   `i d/dx` are the concrete Suzuki objects.
5. **No RH claim.**  This closes the deficiency-index gap in finite-aperture Theorem 1.5.  It does
   not prove the global limit conjecture or RH.
6. **Remaining Theorem 1.5 work.**  Symmetry in the energy inner product, construction of the
   selfadjoint extensions, and identification of their characteristic entire functions remain
   separate obligations until formally closed.
