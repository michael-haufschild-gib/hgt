## 1. The field and the positive law

Let

```text
K_Gamma = C(s)<Gamma>
        = C(s)(Gamma,Gamma',Gamma'',...).                    (1)
```

Write

```text
psi=Gamma'/Gamma,
L=C(s)<psi>=C(s)(psi,psi',psi'',...).                        (2)
```

The first theorem is a projective boundary law.

### Gamma monomial valuation theorem

`[PROVED HERE]` For every nonzero `F in K_Gamma`, there are unique integers

```text
k(F), a(F), b(F) in Z
```

and a unique constant `c(F) in C^x` such that, as `Im(s)->+infinity` with `Re(s)` in
an arbitrary fixed compact interval,

```text
F(s)
  =c(F) Gamma(s)^k(F) s^a(F) (log s)^b(F) (1+o(1)).          (3)
```

The `o(1)` is uniform after adding a variable in any fixed compact subset of a vertical tube.
Here and below `log` is the principal branch in the upper half-plane.

Consequently, for every finite nonzero tuple

```text
A=(F_1,...,F_r) in K_Gamma^r,                               (4)
```

there is an index `j` such that

```text
(F_1(s+z)/F_j(s+z),...,F_r(s+z)/F_j(s+z)) -> (d_1,...,d_r)  (5)
```

locally uniformly in `z` as `Im(s)->+infinity`, where the vector `d in C^r` is constant and
nonzero. Thus every finite coefficient tuple has a single constant point at its upper vertical
projective boundary.

The normalization in `(5)` is allowed to depend holomorphically on the local variable. This is
essential: normalizing only at one base point would leave the rapid factor generated by
`Gamma(s+z)/Gamma(s)`.

### Finite-phase theorem

Let

```text
V_(sigma,eta)={s in C: |Re(s)-sigma|<eta}.                  (6)
```

Let `f` be holomorphic on this vertical strip and uniformly almost periodic under vertical
translation: the family

```text
{f(s+iT):T in R}                                            (7)
```

is relatively compact for **uniform convergence on each closed substrip strictly contained in
`V_(sigma,eta)`**, where the norm is the supremum over the whole (vertically unbounded) closed
substrip. This is the Bohr holomorphic almost-periodic topology, not merely the compact-open
topology.

`[PROVED HERE]` If `f` is differentially algebraic over `K_Gamma`, then

```text
f is differentially algebraic over C,                       (8)

rank_Q Gamma_B(f)<infinity,                                 (9)
```

where `Gamma_B(f)` is the additive group generated by the vertical Bohr frequencies of `f`.
The same finite-rank conclusion holds for every continuous translation-equivariant observable of
the zero-divisor of a nonzero `f`.

Equivalently, the positive carrier law is:

> A recurrent holomorphic object constrained by finitely many Gamma-differential coefficients
> must carry a compact abelian phase carrier of finite covering dimension. Therefore an
> infinite-rank recurrent object must receive its infinite phase carrier from structure beyond the
> finite algebraic-differential extension of the archimedean Gamma field.

The carrier need not be a Lie torus: finite-rank frequency groups can dualize to solenoids. The
invariant forced here is finite covering dimension, equivalently finite rational rank of the
character group.

## 2. The formal Stirling tail cannot disappear

The main issue is cancellation. First-order Stirling asymptotics alone do not prove `(3)`: a
differential rational expression in `psi` and its derivatives could, a priori, cancel to every
algebraic-logarithmic order. The formal shift theorem removes exactly that possibility.

Let `hat(psi)` be the full formal asymptotic expansion

```text
hat(psi)(s)
  =log s-1/(2s)-sum_(n>=1) B_(2n)/(2n s^(2n)),              (10)
```

and put `hat(tau)=D hat(psi) in C((s^(-1)))`. Differentiating the formal digamma shift relation

```text
hat(psi)(s+1)-hat(psi)(s)=1/s                               (11)
```

gives

```text
hat(tau)(s+1)-hat(tau)(s)=-1/s^2.                           (12)
```

Let `rho h(s)=h(s+1)`, `r(s)=-s^(-2)`, and

```text
q(s)=rho(r)/r=s^2/(s+1)^2.
```

Equation `(12)` implies the homogeneous rational shift equation

```text
(rho-q)(rho-1)hat(tau)=0.                                   (13)
```

Adamczewski--Dreyfus--Hardouin, Theorem 1.2 in Case `S_infinity`, takes
`K=C(s)`, `F_0=C((s^(-1)))`, `rho(h)(s)=h(s+1)`, and `D=d/ds`; it proves that a member of `F_0`
satisfying a homogeneous rational linear `rho`-equation belongs to `C(s)` or is
hypertranscendental over `C(s)`. Thus it applies directly to `(13)`.

The series `hat(tau)` is not rational. An elementary pole-orbit proof already suffices. If a
rational `y` satisfied

```text
y(s+1)-y(s)=-1/s^2,                                        (14)
```

its polynomial part would be constant. All poles of its proper rational part would lie in the
integer shift orbit of zero. A maximal integer pole of `y` would force an uncancelled pole of the
left side at that integer, hence would have to be zero. A minimal integer pole would force an
uncancelled pole one step below it, hence would have to be one. These conclusions are incompatible.
Thus `(14)` has no rational solution.

Therefore

```text
hat(tau) is hypertranscendental over C(s).                  (15)
```

If `hat(psi)` were differentially algebraic, then its derivative `hat(tau)` would be
differentially algebraic. Hence

```text
hat(psi) is hypertranscendental over C(s).                  (16)
```

The analytic `psi` is also hypertranscendental. Otherwise `psi` would be differentially algebraic
over `C(s)`, while `Gamma` satisfies the first-order equation

```text
Gamma'=psi Gamma.
```

Transitivity of differential algebraicity would contradict Hoelder's theorem for `Gamma`.

It follows that the assignment

```text
psi |-> hat(psi)                                            (17)
```

extends to an isomorphism from the abstract differential rational field `L` to its formal
Stirling image. In particular, no nonzero element of `L` has the zero formal expansion.

## 3. The algebraic-logarithmic residue of the digamma field

The formal image of `L` lies in

```text
C(log s)((s^(-1))).                                         (18)
```

Every nonzero element of this field has a first nonzero Laurent coefficient in `C(log s)`. Every
nonzero rational function of `log s` has a leading monomial `c(log s)^b`. Consequently every
nonzero `A in L` has unique data

```text
a(A),b(A) in Z, c(A) in C^x
```

such that

```text
A(s)=c(A)s^a(A)(log s)^b(A)(1+o(1))                         (19)
```

uniformly on compact vertical tubes.

To justify transfer from the formal field to analytic asymptotics, use the complete sectorial
Poincare expansion of `psi` and its termwise derivatives. Poincare expansions are closed under
finite sums, products, differentiation, and inversion when the formal leading term is nonzero.
Equation `(16)` guarantees that this leading term exists for every nonzero differential rational
expression. This proves `(19)` without any unproved exclusion of a flat Stirling cancellation.

## 4. Adjoining Gamma creates one exponential valuation direction

The identities

```text
Gamma^(n)/Gamma
  =Bell_n(psi,psi',...,psi^(n-1))                            (20)
```

and their inverse triangular relations give

```text
K_Gamma=L(Gamma).                                           (21)
```

Moreover `Gamma` is transcendental over `L`. Indeed, Hoelder makes `Gamma` a differential
indeterminate over `C(s)`. For every `lambda in C^x`, the assignment

```text
Gamma^(n) |-> lambda Gamma^(n)                              (22)
```

defines a differential automorphism of `C(s)<Gamma>`. It fixes `psi=Gamma'/Gamma` and therefore
fixes `L`. If `Gamma` were algebraic over `L`, it could have only finitely many `L`-conjugates,
contrary to the infinite family `(22)`.

Thus every nonzero `F in K_Gamma` is a rational function of the transcendental element `Gamma`
over `L`. Write

```text
F=P(Gamma)/Q(Gamma),   P,Q in L[X].                         (23)
```

Let `u` and `v` be the smallest powers of `X` with nonzero coefficients in `P` and `Q`. On every
fixed vertical tube,

```text
Gamma(s)->0 exponentially                                  (24)
```

while every quotient of nonzero `L`-coefficients has only algebraic-logarithmic size by `(19)`.
Therefore the lowest Gamma powers dominate:

```text
P(Gamma)~A_u Gamma^u,
Q(Gamma)~B_v Gamma^v.                                      (25)
```

Applying `(19)` to `A_u/B_v` proves `(3)` with `k=u-v`.

Uniqueness follows successively from the exponential separation of distinct Gamma powers, the
power separation of distinct `s` exponents, and the logarithmic separation of distinct `log s`
exponents.

For a finite tuple, order the triples `(k,a,b)` by:

```text
smaller k dominates;
at equal k, larger a dominates;
at equal k,a, larger b dominates.                           (26)
```

Choose a component with dominant triple and divide the tuple by that component. Every ratio tends
to zero or to the ratio of its leading constants. This proves `(5)`.

## 5. Recurrence forces an autonomous equation

Assume `f` is differentially algebraic over `K_Gamma`. After clearing denominators, there is a
finite relation

```text
sum_(alpha in E)
  F_alpha(s) product_(j=0)^n (f^(j)(s))^(alpha_j)=0,         (27)
```

where the nonzero coefficient tuple `(F_alpha)` lies in `K_Gamma`.

Bohr uniform almost periodicity supplies relatively dense common almost periods on any exhaustion
of the strip by closed substrips. A diagonal choice gives positive return times `T_l->+infinity`
such that

```text
f(s+iT_l)->f(s)                                             (28)
```

locally uniformly on the strip. Cauchy's theorem gives the same convergence for every derivative
appearing in `(27)`.

Translate `(27)` by `iT_l`. Choose a dominant coefficient `F_beta` from `(5)` and divide the
translated equation pointwise by `F_beta(s+iT_l)`. For large `l` this coefficient is nonzero on
each fixed compact subset by `(3)`. Equations `(5)` and `(28)` allow passage to the limit and give

```text
sum_(alpha in E)
  d_alpha product_(j=0)^n (f^(j)(s))^(alpha_j)=0,            (29)
```

where `(d_alpha)` is a nonzero constant vector. Hence `f` satisfies a nontrivial autonomous ADE,
proving `(8)`.

The paper theorem
`almost-periodic-implicit-ade-relative-frequency-law.md`, specialized to constant coefficients,
then gives `(9)`, including singular implicit presentations and the divisor-observable statement.
No global initial-value uniqueness is required.

## 6. Hypertranscendence consequences

The contrapositive is the general Gamma-relative frequency criterion:

> If a holomorphic vertically uniformly almost-periodic function has infinite rational Bohr rank,
> then it is hypertranscendental over `C(s)<Gamma>`.

In particular, let

```text
D(s)=sum_(n>=1) a_n exp(-lambda_n s)                         (30)
```

have pairwise distinct real exponents `lambda_n`, nonzero coefficients `a_n`, converge uniformly on
a right half-plane, and have an exponent group of infinite rational rank. Uniqueness of uniformly
convergent Dirichlet expansions identifies the nonzero Bohr spectrum with the `lambda_n`. On every
smaller vertical strip in that half-plane, `D` is vertically uniformly almost periodic and has
infinite Bohr rank. Therefore

```text
D is hypertranscendental over C(s)<Gamma>.                  (31)
```

For `D=zeta`, the frequencies contain `{log p:p prime}`, which are rationally independent by
unique factorization. Thus

```text
zeta is differentially transcendental over C(s)<Gamma>.    (32)
```

Equivalently, `Gamma` and `zeta` are differentially algebraically independent over `C(s)`.

Equation `(32)` is a known theorem and is not claimed as new here. Wang--Yao proved Markus's
conjecture in 2024, and Lue generalized it in 2025. A 2025 paper of Lue--Jiang--Lue separately
studies algebraic independence of Dirichlet series and the Euler Gamma function. The candidate
novelty here is the projective-boundary law `(3)`--`(5)` and its extension `(8)`--`(9)` to arbitrary
holomorphic vertically uniformly almost-periodic functions, not the zeta specialization.

## 7. Positive requirements for RH architectures

This theorem does not classify Connes, Hilbert--Polya, de Branges, Nyman--Beurling, Weil, or Li
criteria as viable or nonviable. It identifies a precise implementation requirement shared by all
of them:

```text
recurrent output with infinite prime-frequency rank
  -> an explicitly supplied infinite prime-frequency carrier
  -> unbounded cofinal phase dimension.                    (33)
```

More precisely:

> Any implementation of these programs that reaches the completed zeta source must carry the
> infinite prime-frequency module explicitly in an infinite-dimensional coefficient, operator,
> determinant, test-function, or divisor object. The archimedean Gamma field supplies the
> completion valuation; a separate arithmetic carrier supplies the prime phase module.

Concretely:

1. **Connes / Hilbert--Polya / de Branges.** The source operator or characteristic determinant must
   already possess an infinite prime-frequency carrier and expose it to the spectral observable.
2. **Nyman--Beurling / Weil positivity.** The cofinal test space must retain unbounded arithmetic
   frequency rank while the completion acts; the cofinal family must therefore have unbounded
   compact-abelian phase dimension.
3. **Li coefficients.** The whole unbounded coefficient hierarchy is the carrier. No finite-order
   replacement is adequate unless it supplies an isomorphic infinite-rank arithmetic phase module.

There is also an exact finite-aperture form. For `sigma>0`, put

```text
Z_(N,sigma)(t)=product_(p<=N) (1-p^(-sigma-it))^(-1).       (34)
```

`[PROVED; BRIDGE]` Its translation hull is the full prime torus `T^(pi(N))`. Indeed, unique
factorization makes `{log p:p<=N}` rationally independent, so the arithmetic translation orbit is
dense in that torus. The torus observable in `(34)` has a nonzero Fourier coefficient in every
coordinate character, hence has trivial translation stabilizer. Therefore any compact abelian
phase carrier producing `(34)` as a continuous equivariant observable must have covering dimension
at least `pi(N)`: the induced factor homomorphism onto the output hull injects the latter's
rank-`pi(N)` character group into the character group of the carrier.

This gives the positive finite-prime growth law:

> A cutoff architecture that retains every prime phase through `p<=N` must transport at least
> `pi(N)` independent phase dimensions. In the reflected critical window
> `epsilon=c/log N`, the universal nonzero overlap profile changes the metric separation but does
> not remove these dimensions. Thus a scalar overlap profile is only a quotient statistic; a viable

> crosses the logarithmic boundary layer.

This is a carrier theorem: every viable finite-prime route must state where its infinite frequency
module lives and prove that the completed constraint preserves it.

## 8. Attacks and boundary cases

1. **Point normalization is false.** `Gamma(s+z)/Gamma(s)` contains a rapid local phase. The theorem
   uses functional projective normalization by one tuple component, so equal Gamma powers cancel
   before the limit.
2. **Flat differential cancellation.** This is the principal attack. It is excluded by the formal
   shift hypertranscendence of `hat(tau)`, not by a finite truncation of Stirling's formula.
3. **Gamma c-points.** Functions such as `1/(Gamma-1)` have nontrivial global pole sets, but on each
   fixed vertical tube they tend to `-1`. The theorem is a vertical projective law, not a global
   pole-confinement claim.
4. **Arbitrary DA functions.** `sin(s exp s)` is DA over `C(s)` and can have dense strip divisors, but
   it is not vertically uniformly almost periodic. The recurrence hypothesis, not finite order or
   zero density, is load-bearing.
5. **Compact-open normality is insufficient.** A bounded holomorphic strip function has a
   compact-open relatively compact translate family by Montel, but need not return to itself.
   Uniform convergence on each whole closed substrip is required and is exactly what supplies the
   Bohr return sequence used in Section 5.
6. **Finite support is not claimed.** Functions such as `exp(2^(-s))` are autonomous DA and have
   infinite Dirichlet support but rank-one frequency group. The theorem concludes finite rational
   rank, exactly as required.
7. **Meromorphic almost-periodic functions.** The stated theorem is holomorphic. Extending it across
   moving poles requires a compact meromorphic-hull topology and a divisor formulation; it is not
   silently included.
8. **Other Gamma orientations.** The proof uses the upper vertical boundary of `Gamma(s)`. Scaled
   archimedean factors `Gamma((s+mu)/2)` admit the same argument after an affine change of variable,
   but simultaneous incommensurable Gamma scales require a separate multi-valuation theorem.

The finite same-scale real-translate extension is now proved in
`finite-real-shift-gamma-projective-boundary-law.md`.  Its orbit-residue injection and real-weight
ordering give the one-point projective boundary for every finite pairwise incongruent translate
family and the translation-saturated prime-log zeta consequence.

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/BohrSpectrum.lean` builds the **Bohr spectrum**.  The pair
`t ↦ (f t, g t)` has a totally bounded orbit as soon as both factors do
(`hasTotallyBoundedOrbit_prod`: a translate is classified by the pair of approximating sample times,
and there are finitely many such pairs), so two almost-periodic functions have a relatively dense set
of *common* almost periods (`exists_common_almostPeriods`) — the step Bohr's definition cannot see and
Bochner's makes immediate.  Hence products of almost-periodic functions are almost periodic
(`isBohrAlmostPeriodic_mul`), and since the character `t ↦ e^{-iλt}` is periodic for `λ ≠ 0` and
constant for `λ = 0` (`isBohrAlmostPeriodic_bohrChar`), the modulated function `f(t)e^{-iλt}` is
almost periodic.  Its mean is the **Bohr coefficient** `bohrCoefficient f λ`, the **spectrum**
`bohrSpectrum f` is the set of frequencies with nonzero coefficient, and the **frequency module**
`bohrFrequencyModule f` is its `ℚ`-span — the object whose rational rank eq. (9) bounds, which until
now did not exist in the tree at all.

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/BohrParseval.lean` makes the almost-periodic functions an
algebra with an inner product and proves **Bessel's inequality**.  The class is closed under sums,
differences, complex scalars, conjugation and finite sums; the window average and the mean are
linear and conjugation-equivariant; and the mean of a character is computed exactly
(`bohrMean_bohrChar`: `M(e^{-iλt}) = 0` for `λ ≠ 0` and `1` for `λ = 0`), which gives orthonormality
of the character system (`bohrMean_bohrExp_mul_conj`).  Subtracting the trigonometric polynomial
`p = ∑_{i∈S} a(i) e^{iit}` from `f` and using `0 ≤ M(|f − p|²)` (`bohrMean_mul_conj_re_nonneg`) with
the three mixed means then yields

`∑_{λ ∈ S} |a(λ)|² ≤ M(|f|²)`  (`bohr_bessel`)

for every finite set `S` of frequencies.  This is the first structural constraint on the Bohr
spectrum: above any positive threshold only finitely many frequencies can occur.

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/KroneckerWeylDensity.lean` proves **Kronecker's approximation
theorem** (`kronecker_approx`), which §7 uses when it asserts that rational independence of

`λ : Fin m → ℝ` linearly independent over `ℚ`, the line `t ↦ (e^{iλ_j t})_j` enters every
`ε`-neighbourhood of every point of the torus.  Mathlib has neither Kronecker nor Weyl
equidistribution.

The proof is by a positive kernel and the Bohr mean.  `cosKernel K x = ((1+cos x)/2)^K` equals
`(1+z)^{2K}/(4^K z^K)` for `z = e^{ix}`, so `ofReal_cosKernel_eq_sum` expands it as a trigonometric
polynomial with Fourier coefficients `binom(2K,r)/4^K`.  Expanding the `m`-fold product along the
line (`Finset.prod_univ_sum`) writes it as a finite combination of characters `e^{i⟨v−K,λ⟩t}`, and
`bohrMean_sum_expChar` keeps only the terms of zero frequency; rational independence forces
`v = (K,…,K)`, so `bohrMean_prod_cosKernel` gives the exact value `(binom(2K,K)/4^K)^m`, which is at
least `(2K+1)^{-m}` by `four_pow_le_succ_mul_centralBinom`.  If the line avoided the `ε`-ball around
`θ`, the same product would be at most `(1 − ε²/4)^K` at every time, and an exponentially small
bound cannot dominate a polynomially small one.

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/GammaSumValuationBoundary.lean` lifts the ordering from single

- **no cancellation** (`polyWeightedSum_eventually_ne_zero`): a sum of graded monomials with a

  formal Stirling series, which is only needed for coefficients outside this algebra;
- the leading behaviour of such a sum is computable — it is that of its dominant monomial;
- `tendsto_polyWeightedSum_ratio`: the projective boundary of two sums is the boundary of their
  dominant monomials, so eq. (5) extends from monomial coefficients to arbitrary elements of the
  graded algebra.

`GammaRationalDatum.one` records the trivial datum, so pure `ℂ[s][ψ]` elements are the special case

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/BohrDerivativeSpectrum.lean` supplies the two spectral
computations any differential-algebraic argument over the Bohr spectrum needs.
`bohrCoefficient_deriv` proves the differentiation rule `a_{f'}(λ) = iλ·a_f(λ)`: integrating by parts
against the character moves the derivative onto `e^{-iλt}` and leaves a boundary term of size `O(1)`,
which the window average kills.  Its corollaries are `bohrMean_deriv_eq_zero` (the mean of a
derivative vanishes) and `bohrSpectrum_deriv_subset` (`spec f' ⊆ spec f`), the closure property that
a relation `∑ d_α ∏_j (f⁽ʲ⁾)^{α_j} = 0` acts on.

Dually, `bohrCoefficient_sum_expChar` computes the coefficients of a trigonometric polynomial to be
its own coefficients, so `bohrSpectrum_sum_expChar_subset` puts its spectrum inside its finite
frequency set and `bohrFrequencyModule_sum_expChar_le` puts its frequency module inside the `ℚ`-span
of that set — whose rank `FrequencyRank.frequencyRank_le_card` already bounds by the number of terms.
This is the concrete instance showing the finite-phase conclusion of eq. (9) is attained rather than
vacuous.

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/LinearOdeBohrSpectrum.lean` proves **eq. (9) in the linear
autonomous case, unconditionally** — the first genuine piece of the finite-phase conclusion itself
rather than of its hypotheses.  Applying the coefficient functional to a relation
`∑_j d_j f⁽ʲ⁾ = 0` with constant `d_j`, and using `a_{f⁽ʲ⁾}(λ) = (iλ)^j a_f(λ)`
(`bohrCoefficient_iterate`, from the differentiation rule), gives

`(∑_j d_j (iλ)^j) · a_f(λ) = 0`   (`linearOde_char_eq_zero`),

so every Bohr frequency of a solution is a characteristic root.  A nonzero characteristic polynomial
has finitely many roots and `λ ↦ iλ` is injective, hence the spectrum is finite
(`bohrSpectrum_finite_of_linearOde`) and the frequency module is finitely generated
(`bohrFrequencyModule_fg_of_linearOde`).  Supporting linearity lemmas — `bohrCoefficient_finsetSum`,
`bohrCoefficient_const_mul`, `bohrCoefficient_of_eq_zero` — are proved here as well.

The nonlinear case is what still needs the topological argument (jet embedding, covering dimension,
Pontryagin duality); the linear case needs none of it.

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/GammaLinearStripDerivative.lean` reads the graded collapse on

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/BohrProductSpectrum.lean` settles how the spectrum behaves
under multiplication in the case where it is exact and elementary — a trigonometric-polynomial
factor.  `bohrCoefficient_mul_expChar` shows modulation translates the coefficient function
(`a_{f·e^{iμt}}(λ) = a_f(λ−μ)`), and `bohrCoefficient_mul_trigPoly` gives the convolution formula
`a_{f·g}(λ) = ∑_i c_i a_f(λ−μ_i)` for `g = ∑_i c_i e^{iμ_i t}`.  Hence
`bohrSpectrum_mul_trigPoly_subset` (`spec(f·g) ⊆ spec f + {μ_i}`) and, most usefully,
`bohrFrequencyModule_mul_trigPoly_le`: multiplying by a trigonometric polynomial enlarges the
frequency module by **at most finitely many generators**, the module of the product lying in
`Γ_B(f) ⊔ span_ℚ{μ_i}`.  This is the multiplicative half of the mechanism behind the finite-rank
conclusion.  The general product rule is now proved outright in
`BohrFrequencyModuleProduct.lean` (see below), so nothing remains open on this branch.

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/BohrMeanInvariance.lean` records the two structural
properties of the mean that any approximation argument runs on.  It is a **contraction** for the
supremum norm (`norm_bohrMean_le`), hence the mean and every Bohr coefficient are `1`-Lipschitz in
the uniform distance (`norm_bohrMean_sub_le`, `norm_bohrCoefficient_sub_le`) — the estimate that will
transfer facts from trigonometric polynomials to their uniform limits.  And it is **translation
invariant** (`bohrMean_comp_add_const`: shifting the window changes the integral only by two end
pieces of bounded size, which the average kills), so translation multiplies each coefficient by the
unimodular factor `e^{iλa}` (`bohrCoefficient_comp_add_const`) and leaves the spectrum, hence the
frequency module, unchanged (`bohrSpectrum_comp_add_const`).

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/BohrConvolution.lean` proves the **Fourier-mode extraction
identity**, the mechanism the Bochner–Fejér construction runs on.  Averaging a translate of `f`
against a character extracts exactly that mode,
`M_u(f(u+t)·e^{-iμu}) = a_f(μ)·e^{iμt}` (`bohrMean_translate_mul_bohrChar`, an immediate consequence
of translation invariance), and against a trigonometric kernel `K = ∑_i c_i e^{-iμ_i u}` the same
computation returns the corresponding weighted partial sum of the Bohr–Fourier series,
`M_u(f(u+t)·K(u)) = ∑_i c_i a_f(μ_i) e^{iμ_i t}` (`bohrMean_translate_mul_trigKernel`).  Every such
convolution is therefore a trigonometric polynomial with exactly the right coefficients
(`bohrCoefficient_trigKernel_convolution`); what remains for the approximation theorem is only the
choice of weights making these polynomials converge uniformly to `f`.  In particular
`bohrMean_translate_mul_trigKernel_eq_zero`: if every Bohr coefficient vanishes then every such
convolution vanishes identically — the uniqueness theorem is exactly the missing approximation step
away.

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/BohrFejerKernel.lean` constructs the **Fejér kernel on an
arithmetic frequency progression** `βℤ`, the summation kernel the Bochner–Fejér approximation runs
on.  Realized as `|D_N|²/(N+1)` with `D_N(u) = ∑_{k≤N} e^{ikβu}`, it is a nonnegative real
(`fejerKernel_eq_ofReal`, `fejerKernel_nonneg`), a trigonometric polynomial with frequencies in `βℤ`
(`fejerKernel_eq_sum`), and has mean exactly one (`bohrMean_fejerKernel` — only the diagonal terms of
the double sum carry zero frequency, and there are `N+1` of them).  Convolving `f` against it
(`bohrMean_translate_mul_fejerKernel`) therefore returns the **Fejér mean of the Bohr–Fourier series
of `f` along `βℤ`**: a genuine trigonometric polynomial whose coefficients are the Bohr coefficients
of `f` at the frequencies `(j−k)β`, weighted by `1/(N+1)`.

What remains for the approximation theorem — and hence for uniqueness, the general product rule and
the nonlinear case of eq. (9) — is the uniform convergence of these Fejér means, together with the
multi-dimensional (rationally independent basis) version of the kernel.

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/BohrFejerConcentration.lean` supplies the third
approximate-identity property, **concentration**.  Summing the geometric series
(`fejerSum_eq_geom`, `expChar_nat_mul`) gives `D_N(u) = (z^{N+1}−1)/(z−1)` with `z = e^{iβu}` of
modulus one, so `‖D_N(u)‖ ≤ 2/‖z−1‖` and
`F_N(u) ≤ 4/((N+1)(2 − 2cos βu))` (`fejerKernel_norm_le`, using
`norm_expChar_sub_one_sq : ‖e^{iβu} − 1‖² = 2 − 2cos βu`).  Off any neighbourhood of the kernel's
peaks, `cos(βu) ≤ 1 − δ`, this is at most `2/((N+1)δ)` (`fejerKernel_norm_le_of_cos_le`), a bound
independent of `u` that tends to zero (`tendsto_fejerBound`).

The Fejér kernel is therefore a genuine approximate identity on the progression `βℤ`: nonnegative,
of mean one, and uniformly small away from its peaks.  The remaining step is the multi-dimensional
kernel over a rationally independent frequency basis, whose peaks are almost periods of `f`, and the
resulting uniform convergence.

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/BohrMultiFejerKernel.lean` builds the **multi-dimensional
Fejér kernel** — the product of one-dimensional kernels over a finite family of base frequencies,
which is the kernel the Bochner–Fejér construction actually uses.  It is a nonnegative real
(`multiFejerKernel_eq_ofReal`, `multiFejerKernel_nonneg`), and `multiFejerKernel_eq_sum` expands it
over `Fintype.piFinset` as a trigonometric polynomial whose frequencies are exactly the integer
combinations `∑_j (a_j − b_j)β_j` with `0 ≤ a_j, b_j ≤ N_j`: its whole spectrum lies in the `ℤ`-span
of the base frequencies.  Hence it is continuous and almost periodic, and
`bohrMean_translate_mul_multiFejerKernel` gives the convolution — the multi-dimensional Fejér mean of
the Bohr–Fourier series of `f`.

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/BohrMultiFejerMean.lean` completes that kernel: it proves
**mean one**.  `multiFejer_diagonal_of_freq_eq_zero` shows that under `ℚ`-linear independence of the
base frequencies an integer combination `∑_j (a_j − b_j)β_j` vanishes only on the diagonal
`a_j = b_j`; identifying the diagonal with the image of the index box under `A ↦ (A,A)` and counting
it with `Fintype.card_piFinset` gives
`bohrMean (multiFejerKernel β N) = (∏_j (N_j+1))·∏_j (N_j+1)⁻¹ = 1` (`bohrMean_multiFejerKernel`).

The multi-dimensional Fejér kernel is therefore an **approximate identity of total mass one** on the
group generated by the base frequencies: nonnegative, mean one, and (coordinatewise) concentrated.

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/BohrMeanTriangle.lean` sets up the convergence step itself.
A kernel of mean one turns the convolution error into a mean of differences,
`M_u(f(u+t)K(u)) − f(t) = M_u((f(u+t) − f(t))·K(u))` (`bohrMean_translate_mul_kernel_sub`), because
`M_u(f(t)K(u)) = f(t)·M(K) = f(t)`.  And the mean obeys the triangle inequality,
`‖M(g)‖ ≤ M(‖g‖)` (`norm_bohrMean_le_re_bohrMean_norm`, since each window average does, with
`isBohrAlmostPeriodic_norm` supplying almost periodicity of `‖g‖`).  Combining them,
`norm_bohrMean_translate_mul_kernel_sub_le` bounds the convolution error of a nonnegative kernel of
mean one by `M_u(‖f(u+t) − f(t)‖·k(u))` — exactly the quantity the concentration of the kernel
controls.  The remaining analytic step is to bound that mean, using that the kernel's peaks are
almost periods of `f`.

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/BohrFejerMass.lean` computes the Fejér kernel's own Bohr
coefficients and thereby its **mass away from the peaks**, measured by the continuous majorant
`1 − cos(βu)` rather than by an indicator (which would leave the almost-periodic class).  Expanding
`F_N` over the square `[0,N]²` gives `bohrCoefficient_fejerKernel`; the fibre `a − b = ±1` is the
image of `range N` under `b ↦ (b+1,b)` (resp. `a ↦ (a,a+1)`), so
`bohrCoefficient_fejerKernel_self` and `bohrCoefficient_fejerKernel_neg` both evaluate to
`N/(N+1)`, and therefore

`M_u( F_N(u)·(1 − cos βu) ) = 1 − N/(N+1) = 1/(N+1)`  (`bohrMean_fejerKernel_mul_one_sub_cos`),

an exact, quantitative concentration statement.

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/BohrHullGroup.lean` constructs the **compact abelian phase
carrier** of the finite-phase law directly, with no dimension theory and no Pontryagin input.  The
translation distance `d_f(s,t) = sup_x ‖f(x+s) − f(x+t)‖` of a bounded continuous `f` is a
translation-invariant pseudometric on the additive group `ℝ` (`BohrLine`,
`BohrLine.dist_add_right`, `dist_neg`, `dist_sub_left`), so subtraction is Lipschitz and the line is
a uniform additive group for it.  Bochner's criterion is exactly total boundedness of that
pseudometric (`BohrLine.totallyBounded_univ`), so the Hausdorff completion

`BohrHull f = UniformSpace.Completion (BohrLine f)`

is a **compact Hausdorff abelian topological group** (`compactSpace_bohrHull`) into which the line
maps by a dense additive homomorphism (`bohrIota_add`, `denseRange_bohrIota`), and `f` itself, being
`1`-Lipschitz for `d_f`, extends to a continuous function `bohrEval` on it with
`bohrEval (ι t) = f t`.  This is the carrier the statement of eq. (9) refers to, now a construction
rather than an assertion.

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/BohrHullCharacter.lean` puts the Bohr frequencies **inside the
character group of that carrier**.  The mode-extraction identity
`M_u(f(u+t)e^{-iμu}) = e^{iμt}c_μ(f)` has a left-hand side that is `1`-Lipschitz in `t` for `d_f`
(`dist_expChar_mul_bohrCoefficient_le`, by `norm_bohrMean_sub_le`), so for every frequency in the
spectrum the exponential `t ↦ e^{iμt}` is Lipschitz for the translation distance
(`uniformContinuous_expChar_bohrLine`) and extends to the hull.  The extension `bohrHullChar` is a
continuous character: multiplicativity (`bohrHullChar_add`) and unit modulus (`norm_bohrHullChar`)
both pass from the dense image of the line.  So `bohrSpectrum f ↪ (BohrHull f)^`, which is the
Pontryagin half of eq. (9) that does not need the duality theorem.  A continuous almost-periodic
function is packaged for this machinery by `toBC`, using that a totally bounded translation orbit
forces boundedness (`exists_norm_le_of_hasTotallyBoundedOrbit`).

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/BohrHullTorusQuotient.lean` proves the **positive carrier
law** for an independent frequency packet, and it needs no duality theorem.  The joint character map
`x ↦ (χ_{λ_j}(x))_j` is continuous on the compact hull, so its range is closed; Kronecker's theorem
(`kronecker_approx`) makes the image of the line dense in the torus; hence the range is the *whole*
torus (`exists_bohrHull_charPacket_eq`, and `…_of_norm_one` with the target given by unit complex
numbers).  So `r` rationally independent frequencies force the phase carrier to cover `𝕋^r` — the
positive direction of eq. (9) and of the §7 prime-torus statement.

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/BohrTorusQuotientHom.lean` packages the torus quotient as a
homomorphism: `bohrHull_torus_surjective_hom` states, for a `ℚ`-independent packet of hull
frequencies, that the joint character map is coordinatewise multiplicative, has unit modulus in each
coordinate, and hits every point of the torus.  This is exactly the object the dimension step
consumes — a continuous surjective homomorphism `BohrHull f ↠ 𝕋^r`.

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/BohrHullPullback.lean` identifies the hull with the space of
uniform translation limits and makes it a source of almost-periodic observables.  A hull point `x` is
described by the function `t ↦ ev (x + ι t)`; for `x = ι s` this is the translate `f(· + s)`, and the
description is *isometric* (`dist_bohrHullBC`), because the hull metric is translation invariant
(`dist_add_bohrIota`) and the evaluation is `1`-Lipschitz (`dist_bohrEval_le`).  In particular hull
points are separated by evaluations (`bohrHull_ext`).  Conversely every continuous `F` on the hull
pulls back to a Bohr almost-periodic function on the line
(`isBohrAlmostPeriodic_comp_bohrIota`): the `ε`-almost periods of `f` are exactly the times at which
`ι` returns close to the origin of the hull.

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/BohrHullFrequency.lean` turns the frequencies into a group and
proves the **observable law** (25).  `IsBohrHullFrequency f μ` says `t ↦ e^{iμt}` is uniformly
continuous for the translation distance, i.e. extends to the hull; this is closed under `0`, `neg`
and `add` (unit modulus makes the product estimate elementary), so the hull frequencies form an
additive subgroup `bohrHullFrequencies f ≤ ℝ` — the character group of the carrier read inside the
line — containing the Bohr spectrum and hence the whole group it generates
(`closure_bohrSpectrum_le_bohrHullFrequencies`).  For a continuous observable `F` of the hull, the
translation distance of `t ↦ F(ι t)` is dominated by that of `f`
(`uniformContinuous_compare_observableBC`), so frequencies transport backwards and

`Γ(F ∘ ι) ⊆ bohrHullFrequencies f`  (`bohrSpectrum_observable_subset_bohrHullFrequencies`),

which is exactly eq. (25): a continuous translation-equivariant observable — the divisor observable
included — creates no phase beyond the carrier's character group.

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/CompactAbelianHaar.lean` and
`…/CompactAbelianConvolution.lean` begin the harmonic analysis of the carrier itself.  The first
normalizes Haar measure on a compact Hausdorff abelian group to a probability measure
(`haarProb`) and records translation and reflection invariance of its integral.  The second proves
the tube-lemma form of uniform continuity — one neighbourhood `V` of `0` with
`‖k(x+v) − k x‖ < ε` for *all* `x` and all `v ∈ V` (`exists_nhds_zero_norm_sub_lt`) — and uses it to
show that convolution with a continuous kernel maps an integrable function to a continuous one with
a modulus of continuity depending only on the kernel and on `‖u‖_{L¹}`
(`norm_convFun_le`, `norm_convFun_sub_le`, `continuous_convFun`).

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/CompactAbelianConvOperator.lean` and
`…/CompactAbelianConvCompact.lean` complete the first, analytic half of the Peter–Weyl argument.
On a probability space `‖u‖_{L¹} ≤ ‖u‖_{L²}` (`integral_norm_le_norm`), so convolution factors as
`L²(G) → C(G) → L²(G)` with `‖k * u‖_∞ ≤ ‖k‖_∞‖u‖₂` (`convOp`).  **That operator is compact**
(`isCompactOperator_convOp`), proved without Arzelà–Ascoli: a finite measurable partition
subordinate to a cover by translates of a small neighbourhood
(`exists_finite_partition_subordinate`, by disjointifying a finite subcover) lets one replace
`k(x − z)` by `k(x − y i)` on each piece, which changes the convolution by at most `ε‖u‖`
(`norm_convC_sub_rebuild_le`) and is a finite-rank operator because it factors through `ℂⁿ`
(`sampleCLM`, `rebuildCLM`, `isCompactOperator_approxOp`).  So the convolution operator is a norm
limit of compact operators.  Compactness is exactly what makes the eigenspaces finite-dimensional,
which is the step that produces characters.

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/CompactAbelianConvSymmetric.lean` supplies the second
hypothesis of the spectral theorem: for a Hermitian kernel, `conj (k (−z)) = k z`, the convolution
operator is **symmetric** (`isSymmetric_convOp`).  Both `⟪k * u, v⟫` and `⟪u, k * v⟫` are the same
double integral over `G × G`, and the two orders of integration agree by Fubini, the integrand being
dominated by `‖k‖_∞ |u| |v|` (`integrable_uncurry_kernel`).  With `isCompactOperator_convOp` this
puts mathlib's spectral theorem for compact self-adjoint operators — eigenspaces spanning, and
finite-dimensional for nonzero eigenvalues — at the disposal of the phase carrier.

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/CompactAbelianTranslation.lean` adds the group action:
translation is an isometry of `L²(G)` (`transOp`, `norm_transOp`, from invariance of Haar measure)
and **commutes with convolution** (`convOp_transOp`, by the substitution `y ↦ y + a` inside the
convolution integral).  Hence every eigenspace of a convolution operator is translation invariant —
finite-dimensional by compactness, and carrying a commuting family of unitaries, which is the exact
situation in which simultaneous diagonalization produces characters.

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/CompactAbelianCharVector.lean` performs that diagonalization
without any spectral theory, by a minimality argument: among the nonzero translation-invariant
subspaces of a nonzero finite-dimensional invariant `W`, take one of least dimension.  For each `a`
the translation has an eigenvalue on it (`ℂ` is algebraically closed), and the eigenspace is again
invariant because translations commute, so minimality forces the translation to be a *scalar*.
Hence `exists_char_vector`: `W` contains a nonzero `w` with `T a w = χ(a)·w` for every `a`.

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/CompactAbelianCharacter.lean` upgrades that scalar function
to a **continuous character**.  An eigenvector of a convolution operator with nonzero eigenvalue
satisfies `w = λ⁻¹(k * w)`, so it has a continuous representative
(`exists_continuous_representative`); the relation `w(x + a) = χ(a)w(x)` then holds *pointwise*
(a.e. equality of continuous functions is equality, Haar having full support), so
`χ(a) = w(x₀ + a)/w(x₀)` at any non-vanishing point — continuous in `a`.  Multiplicativity comes
from composing translations and unit modulus from their being isometries
(`continuous_character_of_char_vector`).

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/CompactAbelianCharSpan.lean`,
`…/CompactAbelianInvariantSplit.lean` and `…/CompactAbelianPeterWeyl.lean` close the argument.
The Hermitian kernels ℂ-span the continuous functions (`f = f₁ + i f₂` with `f₁ = (f + f⋆)/2`,
`f₂ = (f − f⋆)/(2i)`, `f⋆ z = conj (f (−z))`), and continuous functions are dense in `L²`, so a
vector annihilated by every Hermitian convolution is zero (`eq_zero_of_inner_hermitian`,
`inner_toLp_eq_zero_of_convOp_eq_zero`).  Translation is unitary (`inner_transOp`), so the
orthogonal complement of a character line inside an invariant subspace is invariant; peeling off
character lines one at a time shows that if every continuous character is trivial at `g` then
translation by `g` is the identity on every finite-dimensional invariant subspace of an eigenspace
(`transOp_eq_self_of_finite_invariant`).  A vector orthogonal to the fixed space of `T g` is then
orthogonal to every eigenspace of every Hermitian convolution operator, so its image is orthogonal
to *all* eigenspaces and vanishes by the spectral theorem; hence the fixed space is everything and
`T g = 1`, which Urysohn's lemma contradicts unless `g = 0`.  This proves

**`exists_character_ne_one`: the continuous characters of a compact abelian group separate its
points** — Peter–Weyl in the form the phase carrier needs, absent from mathlib and now
unconditional in the tree.

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/CompactAbelianStoneWeierstrass.lean` converts that into
density.  The characters are closed under multiplication and conjugation and contain `1`, so their
linear span is a star-subalgebra of `C(G, ℂ)` (`charSubalgebra`); it separates points by Peter–Weyl,
hence is dense by mathlib's Stone–Weierstrass
(`charSubalgebra_topologicalClosure_eq_top`), and every continuous function on a compact abelian
group is uniformly approximated by a finite linear combination of characters
(`exists_char_combination_approx`).

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/RealCharacterExp.lean` supplies the last identification,
also absent from mathlib: **a continuous multiplicative `ψ : ℝ → ℂ` of modulus one is `t ↦ e^{iλt}`**
(`exists_exp_of_continuous_character`).  The proof is the classical smoothing argument — for small
`a` the integral `A = ∫₀^a ψ` is nonzero and `ψ(t)·A = ∫_t^{t+a} ψ`, so `ψ` is a difference of
primitives, hence differentiable with `ψ' = cψ` for `c = (ψ(a) − 1)/A`; therefore `ψ(t) = e^{ct}`,
and modulus one forces `c` to be purely imaginary.  Together with the previous paragraph this
reduces the Bohr approximation theorem to the transfer along `ι : ℝ → BohrHull f`.

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/BohrApproximation.lean` performs that transfer and thereby
proves **the fundamental theorem of almost-periodic function theory, unconditionally**:

`exists_trig_approx` — for every Bohr almost-periodic `f` and every `ε > 0` there are finitely many
frequencies `λ_j` and coefficients `c_j` with `sup_t |f(t) − ∑_j c_j e^{iλ_j t}| ≤ ε`.

The hull is compact (Bochner) so its characters separate points (Peter–Weyl) and span densely
(Stone–Weierstrass); approximating `bohrEval` and pulling back along `ι` gives the polynomial, and
each character composed with `ι` is an exponential by `exists_exp_of_continuous_character`.

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/BohrHullDual.lean` completes the identification of the dual.
Every continuous character of the hull restricts along `ι` to an exponential, whose frequency is a
hull frequency and whose character is the one already attached to it
(`exists_frequency_of_hull_character`); and distinct frequencies give distinct characters
(`bohrHullChar_injective`).  So

`dual(BohrHull f) = bohrHullFrequencies f`

exactly — eq. (21) of the companion card, now a theorem rather than a citation of Pontryagin
duality.

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/BohrUniqueErgodicity.lean` proves the hull **uniquely
ergodic**: `bohrMean_comp_bohrIota_eq_integral` — for every continuous observable `F` of the hull,

`M_t(F(ι t)) = ∫_X F dHaar`.

A nontrivial character integrates to zero by translation invariance of Haar measure
(`integral_char_eq_zero`), and its pullback `e^{iλt}` has Bohr mean zero for the same reason, so the
two functionals agree on characters (`bohrMean_char_comp_bohrIota`); both are `1`-Lipschitz for the
sup norm, so they agree on the dense span and hence everywhere.  This identifies the Bohr mean —
defined by a limit of window averages on the line — with an honest integral over the compact carrier,
which is what turns positivity statements about `M` into measure-theoretic ones.

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/BohrUniqueness.lean` closes the classical circle.  A
nonnegative almost-periodic function with vanishing mean is identically zero
(`eq_zero_of_bohrMean_eq_zero_of_nonneg`): unique ergodicity turns its mean into a Haar integral,
where a nonnegative continuous integrand of zero integral vanishes almost everywhere — hence
everywhere, Haar measure having full support.  Consequently

**`eq_zero_of_bohrCoefficient_eq_zero`: an almost-periodic function all of whose Bohr coefficients
vanish is zero.**

The proof is the classical one, now available: approximate `f` by a trigonometric polynomial `P`
within `ε`; then `M(f·conj P)` is a finite combination of Bohr coefficients, hence `0`, while
`|M(f·conj f) − M(f·conj P)| ≤ ‖f‖ε`, so `M(|f|²) = 0` and the positivity lemma applies.  With the
approximation theorem this is the uniqueness half of the Bohr–Fourier correspondence.  Two
immediate corollaries are recorded: an almost-periodic function is determined by its Bohr
coefficients (`eq_of_bohrCoefficient_eq`), and a nonzero one has a nonzero coefficient
(`exists_bohrCoefficient_ne_zero`) — i.e. the Bohr spectrum of a nonzero almost-periodic function is
nonempty.

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/BohrTrigPolynomial.lean` isolates the bookkeeping both
Parseval and the product rule need: `bohrCoefficient_trigPoly` computes the Bohr coefficient of
`∑ a_i e^{iμ_i t}` at `λ` as the sum of the `a_i` over the indices with `μ_i = λ` (orthonormality of
the characters for the Bohr mean), with the distinct-frequency form
`bohrCoefficient_trigPoly_of_injective` reading off `a_j` at `μ_j`.

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/BohrTrigMeans.lean` supplies the three mixed means for a
polynomial indexed by a `Finset` of frequencies — so the frequencies are distinct by construction and
no injectivity hypothesis is needed: `bohrMean_mul_conj_trigPoly` pairs the coefficients of `p` with
the Bohr coefficients of `f`, `bohrMean_trigPoly_mul_conj_self` gives `M(|p|²) = ∑|a_λ|²`, and
`bohrMean_sub_trigPoly_mul_conj` assembles them into the expansion of the approximation error

`M(|f − p|²) = M(|f|²) − ∑_λ (conj(a_λ)c_λ(f) + a_λ conj(c_λ(f)) − |a_λ|²)`,

which is the identity Bessel and Parseval both come from — the corpus's `bohr_bessel` proves the
same expansion but hard-wires `a = c_λ(f)`.

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/BohrParsevalUpper.lean` completes **Parseval**, with no
Hilbert-basis machinery.  `exists_trigPoly_approx` restates the approximation theorem with the
polynomial indexed by a finite set of *distinct* frequencies (regrouping the output of
`exists_trig_approx` fibrewise over the image of its frequency map), and
`exists_finset_bohrMean_normSq_le` completes the square in the error expansion:

`M(|f|²) ≤ ∑_{λ∈S} |c_λ(f)|² + ε`  for a suitable finite `S`,

since `M(|f − p|²) ≤ δ²` and `Re(conj(a)c + a conj(c) − |a|²) = |c|² − |a − c|² ≤ |c|²`.  With the
corpus's `bohr_bessel` (`∑_{λ∈S}|c_λ(f)|² ≤ M(|f|²)` for every finite `S`) this is Parseval: the
mean square is the supremum of the finite partial sums of squared Bohr coefficients.

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/BohrCauchySchwarz.lean` adds the last analytic tool:
`bohrMean_sub_smul_mul_conj` expands `M(|h − z k|²)` in `z`, and optimizing the resulting quadratic
gives **Cauchy–Schwarz for the Bohr mean** (`norm_bohrMean_mul_conj_sq_le`),

`‖M(h · conj k)‖² ≤ M(|h|²) · M(|k|²)`.

Taking `z = s·C` with `C = M(h · conj k)` and `s` real keeps every coefficient real, so the
degenerate case is handled by choosing `s` large rather than by a separate argument.

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/BohrSpectralApprox.lean` supplies the other half:
`exists_spectral_mean_square_approx` — for every `δ > 0` there is a finite `S` with

`M(|f − ∑_{λ∈S} c_λ(f) e^{iλt}|²) ≤ δ`.

Replacing the coefficients of a uniform approximant by the true Bohr coefficients can only decrease
the mean-square error (the two expansions differ by `∑_λ |a_λ − c_λ|² ≥ 0`), and the resulting
polynomial is automatically supported on the spectrum, since off-spectrum coefficients vanish by
definition.  This is exactly the form the product rule needs — the approximating frequencies must be
known to lie in the spectrum, which `exists_trig_approx` alone does not give.

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/BohrTrigProduct.lean` supplies the algebraic half:
`trigPoly_mul` expands a product of trigonometric polynomials over the product index set with
frequencies `μ + ν` (via `bohrExp μ · bohrExp ν = bohrExp (μ+ν)`), so
`bohrCoefficient_trigPoly_mul` reads its `λ`-coefficient as `∑_{μ+ν=λ} a_μ b_ν`, and
`bohrCoefficient_trigPoly_mul_eq_zero` records the consequence: the product has no `λ`-mode as soon
as every pair of frequencies summing to `λ` has a vanishing factor.

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/BohrCoefficientBound.lean` turns Cauchy–Schwarz into the
estimate the comparison needs: `norm_bohrCoefficient_mul_sq_le`,

`‖c_λ(u v)‖² ≤ M(|u|²) · M(|v|²)`,

by pairing `u` against `k = conj v · e^{iλt}` — twisting by the character costs nothing since
`M(|k|²) = M(|v|²)`.  Applied to `u = f − p` and to `v = g − q`, a mean-square approximation of the
factors therefore controls every Bohr coefficient of the product.

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/BohrMeanSquareBound.lean` records the two size bounds the
comparison consumes: `bohrMean_mul_conj_re_le_norm_sq` (`M(|f|²) ≤ ‖f‖²`) and
`bohrMean_trigPoly_mul_conj_self_re_le` (`M(|p|²) ≤ M(|f|²)` for the spectral partial sum `p`, i.e.
Bessel's inequality read through the mean square of a trigonometric polynomial).

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/BohrProductRule.lean` assembles these into the **spectral
product rule**, `bohrCoefficient_mul_eq_zero`:

> if every pair `μ + ν = λ` has `c_μ(f) = 0` or `c_ν(g) = 0`, then `c_λ(fg) = 0`.

Splitting `fg − pq = (f − p)g + p(g − q)` for the spectral partial sums `p, q`, each error
coefficient is bounded by `√δ‖g‖` and `‖f‖√δ` through `norm_bohrCoefficient_mul_sq_le`, while
`c_λ(pq) = 0` identically; choosing `δ = (d/2(‖f‖+‖g‖+1))²` with `d = ‖c_λ(fg)‖` contradicts
`d > 0`.  Note the route through the *mean-square* spectral approximation is essential: the uniform
approximant of `exists_trig_approx` has no control on where its frequencies sit, and pruning the
off-spectrum ones costs a factor of the (ε-dependent) number of terms.

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/BohrSpectrumProduct.lean` states the consequence in the form
the differential-algebraic analysis uses: `exists_add_eq_of_mem_bohrSpectrum_mul`
(`spec(fg) ⊆ spec f + spec g`) and `bohrSpectrum_mul_subset_closure` (`spec(fg)` lies in the
additive subgroup generated by the two spectra).  Combined with `BohrDerivativeSpectrum`
(differentiation does not enlarge the spectrum), every polynomial expression in `f` and its
derivatives has all of its frequencies in the group generated by `Γ(f)` — which is the step from
the autonomous relation (8) to the frequency statement (9).

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/IsometricRecurrence.lean` abstracts the recurrence to any

*(a) Peter–Weyl supplies the hypothesis of the corpus's inverse-limit bound.*  `CoveringDimension`

dimension identity reduces to `dim 𝕋^r ≤ r`, with no further group theory.

### The dimension step is now Lean-native: Brouwer built from scratch

The rank-to-dimension half of eq. (9) — `rank_Q dual(K) <= dim K` — was the last classical input
with no mathlib support (mathlib has no covering dimension, no Brouwer fixed point theorem, no
Sperner lemma).  It is now proved in the corpus in a form that bypasses Pontryagin's dimension
theory for compact groups entirely.

`HTGeometry/DeterminantPolynomialIntegral.lean` expands `det (1 + t • N)` into monomials in `t`
(`det_one_add_smul_expand`) and integrates: for a continuous family of matrices on a compact set,
`t ↦ ∫ det (1 + t • N x)` is the evaluation of a single polynomial
(`exists_polynomial_setIntegral_det_one_add_smul`).

`HTGeometry/SmoothNoRetraction.lean` proves **`no_contDiff_retraction_closedBall`**: no map that is
`C¹` on an open neighbourhood of the closed unit ball, sends that ball into the unit sphere, and
fixes the sphere pointwise, can exist.  The proof is Milnor's volume argument: the affine homotopy
`H t x = x + t (ρ x − x)` is injective with positive Jacobian for small `t` (a Lipschitz bound from
the mean value theorem, plus an intermediate-value argument on the determinant), and its image is
the whole ball (inverse function theorem plus connectedness of the open ball, the sphere carrying
zero Haar measure).  So the Jacobian integral equals the volume of the ball for all small `t`,
hence — being a polynomial — at `t = 1`, where the map is the retraction and its differential is
singular everywhere (`det_fderiv_eq_zero_of_mapsTo_sphere`: differentiating `‖ρ‖² = 1` puts the
range of the differential in a hyperplane).  A ball of positive volume would have volume zero.

`HTGeometry/BrouwerFixedPoint.lean` derives **Brouwer's fixed point theorem**.  The differentiable
case builds the ray retraction `ρ x = x + τ(x) u(x)`, `u = (x − f x)/‖x − f x‖`, with `τ` the
positive root of `τ² + 2τ⟨x,u⟩ + ‖x‖² − 1 = 0`; the discriminant is positive on the ball precisely
because `f` maps the ball into itself, and `τ` vanishes on the sphere by Cauchy–Schwarz.  The
continuous case follows by smooth approximation (a partition-of-unity approximation, rescaled back
into the ball) against the displacement lower bound on the compact ball.  `exists_fixedPoint_of_retraction`
transports the fixed point property to any bounded retract, which is how a box gets it.

`HTGeometry/PoincareMiranda.lean` proves **`poincare_miranda`** from Brouwer by clamping the
displacement `x ↦ clamp (x − g x)` to the cube.

`HTGeometry/LipschitzImageNonvalue.lean` supplies the opposite bound: `r` uniformly Lipschitz real
functions on a subset of an `M`-dimensional normed space omit values arbitrarily close to the origin
whenever `M < r`, because a Lipschitz map does not raise Hausdorff dimension
(`exists_norm_lt_notMem_lipschitz_pi_image`).

`HTGeometry/CubeFaceDimensionBound.lean` combines the two into the theorem the finite-phase law
needs, **`le_finrank_of_cube_faces`**: if a compact set `S` in a finite-dimensional normed space
carries `r` pairs of disjoint compact subsets `(A_j, B_j)` and a continuous map of the `r`-cube into
`S` sends the face `x_j = 0` into `A_j` and the face `x_j = 1` into `B_j`, then `r ≤ dim`.  Signed
distance functions to the pairs realise a small omitted value; the face conditions are exactly the
sign hypotheses of Poincaré–Miranda, which forces that value to be attained.

This is the whole content of `rank ≤ dim` for the carrier, and it avoids the two classical detours
the literature route needs — the Eilenberg–Otto separator characterisation of covering dimension,
and Pontryagin's additivity of dimension in a compact group extension.  What replaces them is a
single geometric input on the carrier side: **a continuous `r`-parameter map of the cube into the
carrier whose `j`-th pair of opposite faces lands in `{χ_j = 1}` and `{χ_j = −1}`**.  For the Bohr
hull of a function with `r` rationally independent frequencies that map exists for a structural
reason: the hull is the dual of its (discrete) frequency group `Γ`, a homomorphism `Γ → ℝ^r`
extending `λ_j ↦ e_j` exists because `ℝ^r` is a divisible, hence injective, abelian group, and
dualising it gives a continuous homomorphism `ℝ^r → hull` with `χ_{λ_j}(φ(t)) = e^{i t_j}`.  Note
this does *not* need a continuous section of the carrier over the torus — none exists for a
solenoidal carrier — only the one-parameter subgroups the hull already carries.

### Cauchy transports the return times to every derivative

`HTGeometry/VerticalAlmostPeriodicDerivative.lean` proves the derivative step of eq. (28) in the
strip setting the theorem actually needs.  `IsStripAlmostPeriodicOn F σ r` is uniform almost
periodicity on the closed substrip of half-width `r`; `IsStripAlmostPeriodic F σ R` asks it on every
closed substrip of the open strip of half-width `R` — exactly the topology of eq. (7).  For `F`
holomorphic on that strip, an almost period on the substrip of half-width `r + ρ` is an almost period
of `deriv F` on half-width `r`, with tolerance divided by `ρ`: the translated difference is
holomorphic and bounded on discs of radius `ρ` centred in the narrower strip, so Cauchy's estimate
applies (`norm_deriv_sub_deriv_le_of_ball`, `isStripAlmostPeriodicOn_deriv`).  Rescaling the
tolerance gives **`isStripAlmostPeriodic_deriv`**, and induction gives
**`isStripAlmostPeriodic_iteratedDeriv`** together with holomorphy of every iterate.
`isBohrAlmostPeriodic_line` restricts to the central line, and **`exists_return_sequence_jets`**
produces a *single* sequence of return times along which the function and all its derivatives up to
any fixed order converge to themselves — the "Cauchy's theorem gives the same convergence for every
derivative" step.

The strip is essential, and this is worth recording as a boundary of the statement: if one asks for
almost periodicity uniformly on the whole plane, the translated difference is a bounded entire
function, hence constant by Liouville, and the class collapses to functions with a genuine imaginary
period.  `HTGeometry/StripAlmostPeriodicExpSum.lean` shows the strip class is the intended rich one:
**`isStripAlmostPeriodic_expSum`** proves every finite exponential sum `∑ a_k e^{λ_k z}` with real
frequencies is almost periodic on every strip, so the hypothesis class contains nonconstant functions
with an arbitrary prescribed finite frequency set, of arbitrarily large rational rank.

`HTGeometry/VerticalPhaseRank.lean` packages the restriction (`verticalRestriction`) as a bounded
continuous function and states the finite-phase conclusion in the analytic setting:

> **`rank_frequencies_le_of_vertical`**: for an entire vertically almost periodic `F` whose carrier
> on the line `Re s = σ` admits an injective continuous jet sample into `ℂ^N`, the frequency group
> has rational rank at most `2N`.

### The separation step of the jet embedding

`HTGeometry/ODEJetSeparation.lean` formalizes eq. (16) of the companion card — the step that turns
finitely many observations into an injective map.  `companionField Φ` is the first-order system
attached to an explicit scalar equation `y⁽ⁿ⁾ = Φ(y, …, y⁽ⁿ⁻¹⁾)`; it is Lipschitz whenever `Φ` is,
since every component is either a coordinate projection or `Φ` itself
(`lipschitzWith_companionField`).  The jet vector of a solution solves that system
(`hasDerivAt_jetVec`), so mathlib's uniqueness theorem for Lipschitz ODEs gives

> **`jetVec_eq_of_eq_at`** / **`eq_of_jetVec_eq_at`**: two solutions of the same explicit equation
> whose jets of order `n − 1` agree at a single time have equal jets everywhere, hence are equal.

What remains between this and the injectivity of eq. (15)/(18) is the analytic implicit-function
step — solving the defining relation for the top derivative near a regular jet, where the separant
does not vanish — and the transfer of the strip topology to the hull, so that every hull element is
again a holomorphic solution of the same relation.

### The analytic transfer of eq. (19): the carrier is a family of holomorphic functions

`HTGeometry/StripAlmostPeriodicBounds.lean` supplies the estimates.  A continuous strip almost
periodic function is bounded on each closed substrip — an almost period carries any point into a
compact window where continuity gives the bound (`exists_bound_of_isStripAlmostPeriodicOn`).  For a
holomorphic function this passes to the derivative by Cauchy's estimate on discs inside a slightly
wider substrip (`exists_deriv_bound`), and the mean value inequality on the convex substrip makes the
function Lipschitz there (`exists_lipschitzOnWith_closedStrip`); in particular vertical translation
moves it Lipschitz-continuously in the parameter, uniformly on the substrip
(`norm_translate_sub_le`).

`HTGeometry/StripTranslateCurve.lean` turns those estimates into the carrier construction:
**`exists_strip_curve`** shows the translates `τ ↦ F(· + iτ)` form a *bounded, continuous, Bohr
almost periodic curve* in the Banach space of bounded continuous functions on the substrip.  So the
phase-carrier construction — which the corpus builds for any such curve in a normed space — applies
verbatim in the strip topology of eq. (7), not merely on a line.

`HTGeometry/StripHullHolomorphic.lean` identifies the points of that carrier.  A uniform limit of
translates is holomorphic inside the substrip (`differentiableOn_of_tendstoUniformlyOn`), and each
carrier point is such a limit — the carrier is a completion of the line under the substrip
sup-distance, so its points are limits of sequences of translates and evaluation is continuous.
Hence

> **`exists_holomorphic_of_hull`**: every point of the strip carrier *is* a holomorphic function on
> the open substrip, agreeing with the carrier point's evaluation.

This is the analytic Poincaré transfer the card records as open: the carrier is a compact family of
holomorphic functions on the strip, which is what makes the jet sample of eq. (15) meaningful on it.
What remains for the injectivity of eq. (18) is the identity-theorem step (two carrier points with
equal restriction to the central line coincide) and the propagation of the defining relation to
carrier points.

### Three lines: the line carrier and the strip carrier are the same object

`HTGeometry/StripThreeLinesTransfer.lean` supplies the quantitative half of the transfer, using
mathlib's Hadamard three-line theorem.  Apply it to the difference of two vertical translates on the
half-strip between the central line and an outer line: the difference is bounded by `2M` on the outer
line and by the *line* tolerance `ε` on the central line, so on the line at relative depth `θ`

`‖F(z+is) − F(z+it)‖ ≤ ε^{1−θ} (2M)^{θ}`.

Taking the worse of the two half-strips and the largest depth gives

> **`norm_sub_le_of_line_bound`**: a bound `ε` on the central line plus a bound `M` on the substrip
> of half-width `r'` forces `ε · (2M/ε)^{r/r'}` on the substrip of half-width `r < r'`.

Since `r/r' < 1`, that bound tends to `0` with `ε`, which is

> **`exists_delta_strip_of_line`**: for every strip tolerance there is a line tolerance forcing it.

So the line pseudometric and the substrip pseudometric induce the *same* uniform structure on the
translation parameter, and the phase carrier of the line restriction and the carrier of the strip
translate curve are the same compact group.  This is exactly what the card's §8 attack (5) says
compact-open convergence fails to give and what the Bohr strip topology does give: a jet embedding of
the strip carrier — where jets make sense, the carrier points being holomorphic — is a statement
about the frequency group of the line restriction, where the rank bound lives.

### The carrier solves the equation, and finite jets separate its points

`HTGeometry/StripRelationPropagation.lean` propagates the defining relation.  Locally uniform
convergence of holomorphic functions passes to every iterated derivative
(`tendstoLocallyUniformlyOn_iteratedDeriv`), so a continuous relation among derivatives survives
locally uniform limits (`relation_of_tendstoLocallyUniformlyOn`).  Vertical translates of a solution
of an *autonomous* relation are again solutions, whence **`relation_of_translate_limit`** and, on the
carrier, **`exists_solution_of_hull`**: every carrier point is a holomorphic function on the substrip
satisfying the same relation as `F`.  The carrier is a compact family of solutions of one equation.

`HTGeometry/LineJetTransfer.lean` moves jets between the strip and the line: the `k`-th derivative of
`t ↦ g(σ + it)` is `iᵏ` times the `k`-th complex derivative (`iteratedDeriv_line_restriction`), and —
the strip being convex, hence connected — two holomorphic functions agreeing on the central line agree
on the strip (**`eqOn_of_eqOn_line`**, the identity theorem in the form eq. (16) uses).

`HTGeometry/StripJetSeparation.lean` completes the separation step:

> **`eqOn_of_line_jet_eq`**: if two functions holomorphic on the strip both satisfy
> `y⁽ⁿ⁾ = Φ(y, …, y⁽ⁿ⁻¹⁾)` with `Φ` Lipschitz, and their order-`n` jets agree at a single point of
> the central line, then they agree on the whole strip.

The line restriction solves the same equation with the right-hand side twisted by powers of `i`
(`twistField`), the twist being an isometry so the twisted field stays Lipschitz
(`lipschitzWith_twistField`); Lipschitz ODE uniqueness then equates the restrictions, and the identity
theorem equates the functions.

So eq. (15)–(18) is complete except for one step: solving the defining relation for the top
derivative near a regular jet, i.e. the analytic implicit function theorem where the separant does
not vanish.  Everything else in the chain — the carrier construction in the strip topology, the
holomorphy of carrier points, the equality of the line and strip carriers, the propagation of the
relation, the jet separation, the dimension bound and the rank bound — is proved unconditionally.

### The implicit step, quantitatively

The separation theorems now work on a *region*, which is what a local implicit solution can supply.
`ODEJetSeparation` gained `jetVec_eq_of_eq_at_on` / `eq_of_jetVec_eq_at_on`: uniqueness holds when the
right-hand side is Lipschitz merely on a set through which both jet trajectories run, and
`StripJetSeparation` gained **`eqOn_of_line_jet_eq_on`**, the corresponding statement for holomorphic
solutions on a strip, the twist between complex and line jets being an isometry
(`twistMap`, `dist_twistMap`, `lipschitzOnWith_twistField`).

`HTGeometry/ImplicitLipschitzGraph.lean` supplies the implicit solution in quantitative form, without
invoking an abstract implicit function theorem.  Let `P` be holomorphic on a convex region whose
derivative stays within `C` of a fixed functional `L`.  The mean value inequality against `L` bounds
the increment of `P` minus `L` applied to the increment by `C` times its size; on solutions of `P = 0`
the left side is just `L` applied to the increment, and `L` decomposes into partial derivatives
(`apply_eq_sum_partial`).  Hence

> **`implicit_lipschitz_estimate`**: if `‖∂P/∂y_{j₀}(L)‖ > C`, then for two solutions in the region
> `(‖∂_{j₀}‖ − C) ‖y_{j₀} − z_{j₀}‖ ≤ (C + ∑_{k ≠ j₀} ‖∂_k‖) ∑_{k ≠ j₀} ‖y_k − z_k‖`,

and in particular **`eq_of_erase_eq`**: solutions agreeing in all other coordinates agree in the
distinguished one.  This is exactly the separant condition of eq. (13): near a jet where the separant
does not vanish, the top derivative of a solution is a Lipschitz function of the lower ones.  The
region hypothesis is the honest one — the implicit solution is local, and the separation theorems now
accept exactly that.

### The implicit relation now feeds the separation directly

`HTGeometry/ImplicitLipschitzGraph.lean` was completed to a genuine local implicit function theorem in
the form the ODE argument consumes.  A complex-valued Lipschitz function on a set extends to the whole
space at the cost of a factor two, by extending real and imaginary parts
(**`LipschitzOnWith.extend_complex`**).  A solution set that is a Lipschitz graph over the lower
coordinates is therefore cut out by a globally Lipschitz function
(**`exists_lipschitz_of_graph`**).  Combining with the estimate, and converting the coordinate sum
into the sup distance (`sum_erase_last_le`):

> **`exists_lipschitz_implicit`**: on a convex region where `‖DP − L‖ ≤ C` and the top partial of `L`
> exceeds `C`, there is a globally Lipschitz `Φ` with `y_n = Φ(y_0, …, y_{n−1})` for every solution of
> `P = 0` in the region.

`HTGeometry/StripJetSeparation.lean` then closes eq. (16) for *implicit* relations:

> **`eqOn_of_line_jet_eq_implicit`**: two functions holomorphic on the strip whose `(n+1)`-jets along
> the central line solve the same relation `P = 0` inside such a region, and whose `n`-jets agree at
> one point, agree on the whole strip.

So the separant hypothesis of eq. (13) — the only remaining conditional step — enters exactly where
the card puts it, and produces the injectivity of the finite jet sample on the part of the carrier
where it holds.  What is left of eq. (14)–(15) is the compactness bookkeeping: covering the carrier by
finitely many regular-sample regions and assembling the finitely many local jet coordinates into one
map.

### Eq. (14)–(15) and the constant `2m(n+1)`

`HTGeometry/JetSampleAtlas.lean` closes the sampling bookkeeping.

Cauchy's estimate for derivatives bounds every jet coordinate of the difference of two holomorphic

(**`norm_iteratedDeriv_sub_le`**, with `iteratedDeriv_sub_eqOn` for differences of iterated

> **`rank_le_of_jetAtlas`**: a carrier separated by the jets at `m` sample times with `N` coordinates
> each has `Module.rank ℤ Γ_B(f) ≤ 2 m N`.

With `N = n + 1` that is exactly the card's `rank_Q Γ_B(f) ≤ 2m(n+1)` — eq. (19) and (22) — now
assembled in Lean from the Brouwer-level dimension theory upward.

### The two carriers, joined

`HTGeometry/BohrHullComparison.lean` compares carriers built from different translation distances.

(**`injective_of_hull_maps`**).

(**`dist_strip_lt_of_dist_line_lt`**).  Hence

> **`exists_injective_line_to_strip`**: the carrier of the line restriction maps continuously and
> injectively into the carrier of the strip translates,

and therefore

> **`rank_le_of_strip_jetAtlas`**: a separating jet atlas on the *strip* carrier — where the points
> are holomorphic functions and jets are meaningful — bounds the rational frequency rank of the
> *line* restriction, where the frequency group lives, by `2 m N`.

This is the last structural joint of eq. (15)–(22): jets are taken where they exist, the rank is
read where it is defined, and the three-line theorem certifies that the two carriers are the same

### The capstone: `rank_Q Γ_B(f) ≤ 2m(n+1)`

`HTGeometry/PhaseRankCapstone.lean` assembles the whole chain into one statement.

> **`rank_le_of_regular_jets`**.  Let every point of the phase carrier have a holomorphic

> representative is *regular* at one of finitely many sample times `T`: its full order-`n` jet there
> lies in an open convex region where `‖DP − L‖ ≤ C` and the top partial of `L` exceeds `C`.  Then
> `Module.rank ℤ Γ_B(f) ≤ 2 · |T| · (n+1)`.

The proof is the card's, mechanised: the jet sample `J t x` is continuous by Cauchy's estimate
(`continuous_jet_of_representatives`); if two carrier points have equal jets at a regular time, the
second one's jet is in the same region, so — the region being open — both stay there for nearby times
(`eventually_jet_mem`, `continuous_jet_line`), the local implicit separation applies, the identity
theorem propagates equality to the strip, and `bohrHull_ext` identifies the carrier points; finitely
many sample times cover, so the combined jet sample is a continuous injection into `ℂ^{|T|(n+1)}`; and
the dimension bound reads off the rank.

This is eq. (13)–(22) end to end.  The reduction it rests on is the one the card states: `F` is
almost periodic on the strip, satisfies an autonomous relation, and is regular at finitely many
sample times.  Everything downstream of that — the carrier, its holomorphy, the identification of the
line and strip carriers, the propagation of the relation, the implicit solve, the jet separation, the
finite atlas, the covering dimension of the image and the Pontryagin-free rank bound — is proved
unconditionally, from Brouwer's fixed point theorem upward, all of it built in this corpus.

### Toward the last analytic input of eq. (3): Gamma on a vertical line

The monomial valuation theorem is formalized with one analytic hypothesis, `Γ(σ + it) → 0` as
`t → +∞`.  `HTGeometry/GammaVerticalDecay.lean` starts discharging it without Stirling.

On a vertical line the Gamma values at conjugate points are conjugate (`Gamma_conj_vertical`), so
`‖Γ(σ+it)‖² = Γ(σ+it) Γ(σ−it)`, which the Beta identity converts into

> **`normSq_Gamma_vertical`**: `‖Γ(σ+it)‖² = Γ(2σ) · B(σ+it, σ−it)`.

The Beta integral over the unit interval becomes a Fourier integral along the line under the logistic
substitution `x = e^v/(1+e^v)`, whose basics are established here (`sigmoid_pos`, `sigmoid_lt_one`,
`one_sub_sigmoid`, `hasDerivAt_sigmoid`, `sigmoid_strictMono`, `sigmoid_range = Ioo 0 1`): under it
the integrand is `e^{itv} · (e^v/(1+e^v)²)^σ`, an `L¹` density times a character.  The decay of `Γ` on
vertical lines is then the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma, which mathlib has — that step is next.

### The last analytic input of eq. (3): Gamma decays on vertical lines

`HTGeometry/GammaVerticalDecay.lean` now proves it, without Stirling and without the Beta route.

Substituting `x = e^u` in the Gamma integral turns it into a Fourier integral:

> **`Gamma_vertical_eq_fourier`**: `Γ(σ + it) = ∫_ℝ e^{itu} · e^{σu − e^u} du` for `σ > 0`.

The density `u ↦ e^{σu − e^u}` is integrable — doubly exponentially small at `+∞`, exponentially at
`−∞` — so the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma applies directly, giving

> **`Gamma_vertical_tendsto_zero`**: `Γ(σ + it) → 0` as `t → +∞`, and
> **`Gamma_vertical_tendsto_nhdsWithin_zero`**: the same within `ℂ \ {0}`, since `Γ` has no zeros.

That is exactly the hypothesis `hΓ0` of `gamma_monomial_valuation_residue`, so eq. (3)'s projective
normalization is now unconditional.  The companion bound **`norm_Gamma_le_real_Gamma_re`**
(`‖Γ(s)‖ ≤ Γ(Re s)`, from the integral representation) is also proved; together they were the two
declarations that four Gamma modules of this corpus referenced but which no module supplied, so those
modules now compile.

The squared-modulus route through the Beta integral (`normSq_Gamma_vertical`, plus the logistic
substitution `sigmoid`) is kept as well: it expresses `‖Γ(σ+it)‖²` as `Γ(2σ)` times a Beta integral,
which is the shape the reflection-formula arguments of §4 use.

### Eq. (3) unconditional, and eq. (5) for tuples

With the vertical decay proved, `HTGeometry/GammaMonomialValuationUnconditional.lean` records
**`gamma_monomial_valuation_residue_of_pos`**: on every vertical line of positive abscissa the
projective normalization of a Gamma-field element converges to the residue `R(0)/S(0)` — eq. (3) with
no hypotheses beyond `σ > 0`.

`HTGeometry/GammaTupleProjectiveBoundary.lean` extends this to ratios, which is eq. (5).  Writing two
elements as `Γ^u R(Γ)/(Γ^v S(Γ))`, their ratio is `Γ^k` times a ratio of polynomial values
(`ratio_eventuallyEq`), where `k` is the difference of valuations.  Hence

> **`ratio_tendsto_residue`** — equal valuations: the ratio converges to
> `R_α(0) S_β(0) / (S_α(0) R_β(0))`;
> **`ratio_tendsto_zero`** — strictly larger valuation: the ratio converges to `0`.

Normalizing a finite coefficient tuple by a valuation-minimal member therefore makes every ratio
converge: the tuple has a single constant point at its upper vertical projective boundary, which is
what the recurrence collapse consumes as its `hcoeff` input — and that collapse already has its Bohr
return times proved.

### Eq. (27) → (29) with nothing left open on the coefficient side

`HTGeometry/GammaRationalCollapse.lean` assembles the collapse for the field the card is about.

> **`recurrence_collapse_gamma_rational`**.  Let a finite relation `∑ F_α M_α = 0` have Gamma-rational
> coefficients `F_α = Γ^{u_α} R_α(Γ) / (Γ^{v_α} S_α(Γ))` with `R_α(0), S_α(0) ≠ 0`, let `β` be
> valuation-minimal, and let the monomials be continuous and almost periodic along the line
> `Re s = σ > 0`.  Then there is a constant vector `d` with `∑ d_α M_α(σ) = 0` and `d_β = 1`.

Both analytic inputs are now theorems rather than hypotheses: the coefficient ratios converge by the
tuple projective boundary (itself resting on the vertical decay of `Γ`), and the return times come
from the common Bohr almost periods of the finitely many monomials.  The dominant coefficient is
eventually nonzero because `Γ` does not vanish and `R_β, S_β` do not vanish at the origin.

So the passage from a Gamma-differential relation to an autonomous one — eq. (8) — is complete in
Lean for Gamma-rational coefficients, given only that the solution is almost periodic on the line.

### The monomials need no hypothesis either

`HTGeometry/JetMonomialAlmostPeriodic.lean` removes the last data assumption of the collapse.
Powers (`isBohrAlmostPeriodic_pow`) and finite products (`isBohrAlmostPeriodic_finsetProd`) of
continuous almost periodic functions are almost periodic, by induction on the corpus's product law.
Since the jets of a strip almost periodic holomorphic function restrict to continuous almost periodic
functions on the central line,

> **`isBohrAlmostPeriodic_jetMonomial`**: every monomial `∏_j (F⁽ʲ⁾)^{e_j}` in the jets of a strip
> almost periodic holomorphic `F` is continuous and almost periodic along the line.

Those are exactly the monomials of an algebraic differential relation.  So
`recurrence_collapse_gamma_rational` needs nothing about them beyond the strip almost periodicity of
the solution: eq. (27) → (29) holds for any strip almost periodic holomorphic solution of a
Gamma-rational differential relation, with every analytic input proved.

### Eq. (8): the collapsed relation holds on the whole strip

The limit coefficient vector of the collapse is explicit and does not depend on the line: it is now
the named `gammaRatioLimit p q u v β` (`GammaRationalCollapse`), the residue quotient at the origin on
the valuation-minimal indices and zero elsewhere.  Because it is line-independent, the collapse can be
run on *every* vertical line of the strip at once:

- `isStripAlmostPeriodic_shift` — strip almost periodicity is inherited by every concentric strip
  centred at another interior point, so every interior centre is an admissible collapse line;
- `gamma_rational_autonomous_relation` (`GammaRationalStripCollapse`) — the collapsed relation
  therefore vanishes at every real point of the strip; being holomorphic on a convex strip it vanishes
  identically.

> **Eq. (8) is a theorem**: a strip almost periodic holomorphic solution of a Gamma-rational
> differential relation satisfies the constant-coefficient relation with coefficient vector
> `gammaRatioLimit` at *every* point of the strip.  The solution is differentially algebraic over the
> constants, and the relation is nontrivial (`gammaRatioLimit β = 1`).

### Eq. (9) over the Gamma field

Two further modules turn eq. (8) into the rank bound.

`HTGeometry/JetPolynomialDerivative.lean` makes an algebraic differential relation an analytic object:
`jetPoly d e` is the monomial sum, `jetPolyDeriv d e y` its Fréchet derivative (`hasFDerivAt_jetPoly`),
and `partialCoeff_jetPolyDeriv` computes the partial derivative in each jet coordinate — the quantity
whose dominance drives the separation step.

`HTGeometry/StripSolutionPhaseRank.lean` internalizes all the carrier plumbing.  From the function
alone it builds the bounded almost periodic translate curve, the embedding of the line carrier in the
strip carrier, and the holomorphic representatives solving the same relation, so that

> **`rank_le_of_strip_solution`**: a strip almost periodic holomorphic solution of a relation whose
> solutions are all regular at one of finitely many sample times has
> `rank_ℤ Γ_B(f) ≤ 2 |T| (n+1)`.

Combining the two with eq. (8):

> **`rank_le_of_gamma_rational`** (`HTGeometry/GammaRationalPhaseRank.lean`): for a strip almost
> periodic holomorphic `Φ` satisfying a differential relation with Gamma-rational coefficients,
> `rank_ℤ Γ_B(f) ≤ 2 |T| (n+1)`.

Nothing about the coefficients, the monomials, the carrier, or the transfer is hypothetical any more:
the coefficient limits come from the vertical decay of `Γ`, the monomials are almost periodic because
the jets are, the relation propagates to the carrier by uniform convergence, and the derivative data
of the relation is computed from its own coefficients.  The one remaining input is the regularity
region: a set of jets, valid for every solution at one of the sample times, on which the top partial
derivative of the relation dominates.

### The regularity atlas is a consequence, not an assumption

The atlas was the last hypothesis, and it is now discharged.  Two changes make that possible.

First the atlas API is indexed by an arbitrary type rather than by sample times
(`rank_le_of_jetAtlas`, `rank_le_of_regular_jets`, `rank_le_of_strip_solution`): a finite family of
*(time, region)* pairs is allowed, so several regularity regions may sit over one and the same time.
That is what a covering argument needs, since a compact set of jets is covered by many small balls at
one time, not by one ball at many times.

Then `HTGeometry/NondegenerateSolutionPhaseRank.lean` builds the cover.  Around any jet where the top
partial derivative of the relation is nonzero, continuity of the derivative gives a ball on which it
dominates — an admissible convex regularity region, with `L` the derivative at the centre and
`C` half the modulus of its top partial.  The carrier is compact and the centre jet of the
representative is continuous on it (Cauchy's estimate), so finitely many of those balls cover the
carrier:

> **`rank_finite_of_nondegenerate_solution`**: if the top partial derivative of the relation is
> nonzero at the centre jet of *every* holomorphic solution on the substrip, then
> `rank_ℤ Γ_B(f) < ∞`.

Specialised to the Gamma field (`rank_finite_of_gamma_rational`), with the collapsed relation's own
derivative supplied by `JetPolynomialDerivative`:

> A strip almost periodic holomorphic solution of a differential relation with Gamma-rational
> coefficients, whose collapsed relation is nondegenerate in the top jet along the carrier, has
> **finite rational Bohr frequency rank** — eq. (9), with no atlas, no regularity region, and no
> hypothesis on the coefficients.

What remains on this branch is the nondegeneracy itself: an order-`n` relation that degenerates in
its top jet on the whole carrier is one whose solutions satisfy a lower-order relation, so the
expected route is a minimal-order argument rather than new analysis.

### One nondegenerate point suffices

The nondegeneracy hypothesis has been reduced to a single point of the line.

`HTGeometry/CarrierTranslationEquivariance.lean` supplies the transfer.  The carrier is a topological
group in which the line is dense, so the translates of any one carrier point by the line are dense
(`denseRange_bohrIota_add`), and a continuous function constant along them is constant
(`eq_of_eqOn_translates`).  The representative of a translated carrier point agrees with the
translated representative on the central line, hence on the strip, so the jets of the representatives
are translation equivariant (`iteratedDeriv_rep_translate`), and the representative of the origin has
the jets of the function itself (`iteratedDeriv_rep_zero`).

Degeneracy of the relation at the centre jet is a continuous condition on the carrier.  A carrier
point whose representative were degenerate at *every* time would force degeneracy along a dense set,
hence everywhere — in particular at the function itself.  So
(`HTGeometry/PointNondegeneracyPhaseRank.lean`):

> **`rank_finite_of_nondegenerate_at_point`**: if the top partial derivative of the relation is
> nonzero at a *single* jet of the function, then `rank_ℤ Γ_B(f) < ∞`.

and over the Gamma field, `rank_finite_of_gamma_rational_at_point`: a strip almost periodic
holomorphic solution of a Gamma-rational differential relation whose collapsed relation is
nondegenerate at one point of the line has finite rational Bohr frequency rank.

`HTGeometry/JetPolynomialDescent.lean` starts the removal of even that hypothesis.  The `k`-th
partial derivative of a jet polynomial is again a jet polynomial
(`partialCoeff_jetPolyDeriv_eq`, with coefficients `d α · e α k` and exponent `e α k − 1`), and a jet
polynomial of a holomorphic function is holomorphic, so a jet polynomial relation holding on the
central line holds on the whole strip (`jetPoly_eqOn_zero_of_line`).  A relation degenerate in its top
jet at every point of the line therefore yields the derived relation, of strictly smaller degree in
that jet: the descent that will eliminate the last hypothesis.

### Eq. (9) is now a theorem over the Gamma field

The nondegeneracy hypothesis is gone.  The relation is carried as a multivariate polynomial in the
jets, whose analytic data is computed by `pderiv`:

- `HTGeometry/MvPolynomialJetDerivative.lean` — `hasFDerivAt_mvEval`: the evaluation of a
  multivariate polynomial is Fréchet differentiable with derivative `∑_k (∂_k p)(y) · proj_k`, proved
  by the monomial induction; `partialCoeff_mvJetDeriv` reads off the partial derivatives, and
  `monomialSum_ne_zero` shows a sum of distinct monomials with a nonzero coefficient is a nonzero
  polynomial.
- `HTGeometry/MvPolynomialPDerivDegree.lean` — differentiating strictly drops the degree in that
  variable (`degreeOf_pderiv_lt`), and in characteristic zero a variable with vanishing partial
  derivative does not occur (`degreeOf_eq_zero_of_pderiv_eq_zero`).
- `HTGeometry/MvPolynomialDropLast.lean` — a relation that no longer involves its top jet is a
  relation in one variable fewer, still nonzero (`dropLastPoly_ne_zero`).
- `HTGeometry/ConstantSolutionPhaseRank.lean` — a holomorphic function satisfying a nonzero algebraic
  equation takes values in a finite set, hence is constant on the connected strip; a constant function
  has identically zero translation distance, so its frequency group is trivial and its rank is zero.

The descent (`HTGeometry/PolynomialRelationPhaseRank.lean`) puts these together.  Given any nonzero
polynomial relation satisfied on the strip: if it is nondegenerate in the top jet at some point of the
line, the phase-rank bound applies; otherwise its derivative in the top jet is again a relation, of
strictly smaller degree there; when that derivative vanishes identically the relation drops in order;
and the order-zero case is the constant case.  Hence

> **`rank_finite_of_polynomial_relation`**: a strip almost periodic holomorphic function satisfying
> *any* nonzero polynomial relation in its jets has `rank_ℤ Γ_B(f) < ∞`.

Combined with the collapse (`HTGeometry/GammaRationalFinitePhaseRank.lean`), and with
`gammaRatioLimit_self` giving coefficient `1` at the valuation-minimal index:

> **`rank_finite_of_gamma_rational_relation`** — *eq. (9) over the Gamma field*: a strip almost
> periodic holomorphic solution of a differential relation with Gamma-rational coefficients and
> distinct monomials has finite rational Bohr frequency rank.  Hypotheses: the strip almost
> periodicity of the solution, the Gamma-rational form of the coefficients, valuation minimality of
> the normalizing index, and distinctness of the monomials.  No analytic input is left open.

### The law is not vacuous, and the witness has nonzero rank

`HTGeometry/GammaRationalWitness.lean` inhabits the whole hypothesis class.  For a nonzero real

- is entire and almost periodic on every vertical strip (`isStripAlmostPeriodic_expLine`);
- has jets `Φ^{(k)} = μ^k Φ` (`iteratedDeriv_expLine`), so it solves `Φ' − μ Φ = 0`, a relation whose
  coefficients `1` and `−μ` are Gamma-rational (constant) and whose two monomials are distinct;
- restricts on the line to a bounded continuous function with totally bounded orbit
  (`expLineBC`), so every carrier hypothesis holds.

Hence `rank_finite_expLine`: the law applies and gives a finite bound.  Moreover the frequency `μ` is
a frequency of the hull (`isBohrHullFrequency_expLineBC`: the character is Lipschitz for the
translation distance), so

> **`rank_expLine_pos_and_finite`**: `1 ≤ rank_ℤ Γ_B(f) ≤ K`.

The conclusion is therefore a statement about a nonempty class containing objects of nonzero phase
rank — not an empty or trivial one.

### The divisor clause

`HTGeometry/ObservableFrequencyRank.lean` proves the second half of the finite-phase theorem: the

when the translates of `g` are uniformly close whenever those of `f` are — the precise sense in which

already use.  Then

- every frequency character of `g` is uniformly continuous for the translation distance of `f`

- hence **`rank_finite_of_dominated_gamma_rational`**: every observable dominated by a strip almost

Lipschitz functions of the values are dominated observables (`isDominatedObservable_comp`), so the
clause is not vacuous either.

### Eq. (14): the shift equation has no rational solution

`HTGeometry/RationalShiftNoSolution.lean` proves the elementary pole-orbit step of §2 in a
junk-value-free algebraic form.  Writing a candidate solution as `p/q` and clearing denominators,
`IsShiftInvSqSolution p q` is the polynomial identity `X²(p(X+1)q − p·q(X+1)) = −q·q(X+1)`.  Then:

- the value at `0` forces `q(0) = 0` or `q(1) = 0`, so `q` is nonconstant;
- for coprime `p, q`: every nonzero root of `q` is followed by a root one step up, and every root
  other than `1` is preceded by a root one step down, so the root of largest real part is `0` and the
  root of smallest real part is `1` — impossible;
- the general case descends on a common root, cancelling the factor `(X − a)(X + 1 − a)`.

> **`no_rational_shift_inv_sq`**: no pair `(p, q)` with `q ≠ 0` solves the cleared-denominator shift
> equation — i.e. `y(s+1) − y(s) = −1/s²` has no solution in `ℂ(s)`.

This complements the corpus's meromorphic version
(`GammaProjectiveBoundary.no_finitePole_solution_digammaShiftDerivative`), which assumes the shift
identity at *every* point of `ℂ` — a hypothesis a rational function cannot satisfy at its own poles
under Lean's junk-value convention.  The polynomial form has no such gap.

> `norm_iteratedDeriv_succ_digamma_vertical_le`:
> `‖ψ^{(m+1)}(σ+it)‖ ≤ m! (1/(t−σ/2)² + π/(2(t−σ/2))) / (σ/2)^m`,
> hence **`tendsto_iteratedDeriv_succ_digamma_vertical`**: every positive-order polygamma vanishes
> along vertical lines.

`HTGeometry/DigammaFieldValuation.lean` then reads off the valuation.  With
`L(t) = log t + iπ/2` the corpus's vertical asymptotic gives `ψ/L → 1`
(`tendsto_digamma_div_vertLog`), while all derivative factors vanish.  So a jet monomial normalized
by `L^D` converges — to its tail limit when its digamma degree is exactly `D`, to zero when it is
smaller (`tendsto_monomial_div`) — and summing:

> **`tendsto_jetPoly_digamma_div`**: for a jet polynomial in the digamma tower whose pure monomial
> `ψ^D` occurs with coefficient `d β`, and whose every other monomial either has smaller digamma
> degree or carries a derivative factor,
> `P(ψ, ψ′, …, ψ^{(n)})(σ+it) / L(t)^D → d β`.

That is eq. (19) with `b = D`, `a = 0`, `c = d β`, proved unconditionally for constant coefficients.
What remains for the full statement is the `ℂ(s)`-coefficient case, where the `s^a` factors enter and
the leading monomial is chosen lexicographically in `(degree in ψ, degree in s)`.

- `tendsto_ratio_eval_vertical` — hence on a vertical line, `p(s)·s^{deg q} / (q(s)·s^{deg p})` tends

- **`tendsto_ratCoeff_jetPoly_vertical`** — multiplying by the digamma valuation, for an element

  > `R(s)·P(jets) · s^{deg q} / (s^{deg p} · L(t)^D) → (lead p / lead q) · d β`,

  where `d β` is the coefficient of the pure monomial `ψ^D`.  This is eq. (19) with
  `a = deg p − deg q`, `b = D`, and `c = (lead p / lead q)·d β`, proved unconditionally.

What remains of §3 is the bookkeeping that selects the dominant `(a, b)` pair inside a finite sum of

### Eq. (5) for the digamma field

### The flat-cancellation gap, and the route that closes it

### What is left for the unrestricted eq. (19), and the route to it

### The one remaining generality gap, and the route to it

With §8 closed, every numbered claim of §1–§8 has Lean except one *quantifier*: eq. (3) and eq. (19)
are proved for field elements **presented with their residue data** (a datum `(u,a,dP,b)` and a
nonzero limit `c`), and — since `GammaJetDatumUniqueness.lean` — that presentation is unique when it
exists.  What is not yet Lean is the existential half in the card's own phrasing: *every* nonzero
`A ∈ L` has such a datum, with no presentation supplied.

The obstruction is real and is exactly the one §2–§3 address on paper.  The corpus knows the
*leading* asymptotic of every jet (`tendsto_pow_mul_iteratedDeriv_digamma_vertical`) and knows that
no nonzero jet polynomial over `ℂ(s)` is flat (`not_isVertFlatGerm_digammaJetEval`).  But two
distinct monomials can share a datum — `ψ′ψ‴` and `(ψ″)²` both have `s`-degree `6` and log-degree
`0`, with leading constants `2` and `1` — so a combination such as `ψ′ψ‴ − 2(ψ″)²` cancels at
leading order, and its true datum lies deeper.  Deciding the datum of an *arbitrary* element
therefore needs the asymptotic expansion to arbitrary depth, which is what the paper gets from the
formal Stirling field.

Route (nothing here is blocked, only unbuilt; mathlib has no Euler–Maclaurin and no asymptotic
expansion of the polygamma functions):

1. **Asymptotic scale on a vertical line.**  A predicate for `‖f(t)‖ = O(t^{-k})` on the line, closed
   under sums, products and rational coefficients; the leading-coefficient extraction map.
2. **One order at a time, algebraically.**  For `T_k(s) = ∑_{n≥0}(s+n)^{-k}` (which is the corpus's
   `hasSum_polygamma_series` up to a factorial) the identity
   `(k−1)^{-1}[(s+n)^{-(k-1)} − (s+n+1)^{-(k-1)}] = (s+n)^{-k} + O((s+n)^{-k-1})`
   telescopes: `T_k(s) = ((k−1)s^{k−1})^{-1} + (a sum of the same type one order higher)`.  This is
   Euler–Maclaurin done by rational algebra, with no integrals.
3. **All orders.**  Iterating (2) with the coefficients carried as data gives, for every `K`, an
   expansion `ψ^{(m)}(s) = ∑_{j<K} c_{m,j} s^{-m-j} + O(s^{-m-K})`; differentiation transfers
   expansions between levels by Cauchy's estimate on discs of radius `|s|/2`.
4. **`ψ` itself** carries the `log s` term: integrate the expansion of `ψ′`.
5. **Products and sums.**  The scale is a ring, so every jet polynomial over `ℂ(s)` has an expansion
   to every depth; if *all* coefficients vanished the function would be flat, which
   `not_isVertFlatGerm_digammaJetEval` forbids — so a first nonzero coefficient exists, and that is
   the datum.
6. **Eq. (19) and eq. (3) unquantified**, then the field version by the existing quotient lemmas.

Step 5 is the point of the design: the corpus's flat-elimination theorem replaces the
Adamczewski–Dreyfus–Hardouin input as the injectivity of the expansion map, so no formal shift field
is needed even in the general case.

**Step 1 is built.**  `HTGeometry/LineAsymptoticScale.lean` defines the scale and proves it is a
graded ring: `IsLineBigO σ k f` (`‖f(t)‖·‖σ+it‖^k` bounded) is closed under `add`, `sub`, `mul`
(orders add), `const_mul`, `pow`, and `mono` (a smaller order absorbs a larger one); a positive order
forces `f → 0`.  The two bridges to the corpus's `Tendsto` idiom are
**`tendsto_pow_mul_of_isLineBigO`** — if `f − c·s^{-k}` is one order smaller then `s^k f(s) → c`,
so an expansion coefficient *is* a residue — and its converse `isLineBigO_of_tendsto`.
`isLineBigO_coeff_unique` records that the coefficient is determined.

**Step 1 applied.**  `HTGeometry/PolygammaLineOrders.lean` reads the corpus's leading asymptotics
through the scale: `isLineBigO_iteratedDeriv_succ_digamma` (`ψ^{(m+1)} = O(s^{-(m+1)})`),
`isLineBigO_jet_pow`, and **`isLineBigO_jet_monomial`** — an arbitrary finite monomial
`∏ (ψ^{(i+1)})^{e_i}` decays at its total weight `∑ (i+1)e_i`, which is the grading the datum order
uses.  `#print axioms` clean.

**Step 2 is built, in the form that avoids Euler–Maclaurin entirely.**  Two modules:

- `HTGeometry/ShiftOrbitSumBounds.lean` — the analytic half.  Every orbit point `s+n` has modulus at
  least the ordinate `t` *and* at least its own abscissa `σ+n` (both already in the corpus).
  Splitting the orbit at `n ≈ t` — the near part contributes `≤ (t+1)/t³`, the far part telescopes
  through the corpus's `hasSum_one_div_shift_mul_succ` — gives
  **`tsum_norm_shift_inv_cube_le`** (`∑_n ‖s+n‖^{-3} ≤ 4/t²`) and
  **`tsum_norm_shift_inv_pow_le`** (`∑_n ‖s+n‖^{-(q+3)} ≤ 4/t^{q+2}`).  Each is *one full order
  better* than the trivial term bound, and that gain is what an expansion step consumes.
- `HTGeometry/RisingFactorialTelescope.lean` — the algebraic half.  With
  `risingProd s m = s(s+1)⋯(s+m−1)`, the identity
  **`inv_risingProd_sub`** (`(risingProd s m)^{-1} − (risingProd (s+1) m)^{-1} = m·(risingProd s (m+1))^{-1}`)
  telescopes to the closed form
  **`hasSum_inv_risingProd`**: `∑_{n≥0} 1/((s+n)⋯(s+n+m)) = 1/((m+1)·s(s+1)⋯(s+m))` exactly, for
  every `s` in the right half plane.  These reciprocal rising factorials are the exactly summable
  basis: `(s+n)^{-k}` differs from the rising factorial of the same length by one order, so the
  orbit bound converts the difference into a gained power of `t`.

`#print axioms` clean for both.

**The two halves now meet.**  `HTGeometry/PolygammaFactorialComparison.lean` runs one expansion step
end to end:

- `norm_le_norm_add_nat`, `norm_pow_le_norm_risingProd` — in the right half plane a shift to the
  right never decreases the modulus, so `‖s(s+1)⋯(s+m−1)‖ ≥ ‖s‖^m`;
- **`exists_norm_risingProd_sub_pow_le`** — the leading terms of `x(x+1)⋯(x+m)` and `x^{m+1}` cancel:
  the difference is `O(‖x‖^m)`, by induction on `m` with an explicit constant;
- **`exists_norm_inv_pow_sub_inv_risingProd_le`** — hence the reciprocals differ by *one order*:
  `‖x^{-(m+2)} − (x⋯(x+m+1))^{-1}‖ ≤ C‖x‖^{-(m+3)}`;
- **`exists_norm_polygammaSeries_sub_factorial_le`** — summing over the orbit and applying the orbit
  bound at exponent `m+3`, the polygamma series and the (exactly summable) factorial series differ
  by `C/t^{m+2}`;
- **`exists_norm_iteratedDeriv_digamma_sub_rising_le`** — in the corpus's own normalization,

  `‖ψ^{(m+1)}(σ+it) − (−1)^m (m+1)!/((m+1)·s(s+1)⋯(s+m))‖ ≤ C/t^{m+2}`,

  a *quantitative* leading asymptotic: the jet equals an explicit rational function up to an error
  one full order smaller than the jet itself.  The corpus previously had only the qualitative limit.

`#print axioms` clean.

**The depth induction now has its two engines.**  Iterating the step needs the remainder to be
carried *algebraically*, since only its degree controls the next order.  Two modules do that:

- `HTGeometry/RationalOrderBound.lean` — `exists_norm_polynomial_le` (a polynomial is bounded by its
  degree power outside the unit disc) and **`exists_norm_div_risingProd_le`**: a numerator of degree
  `d` over `x^a·x(x+1)⋯(x+b−1)` decays at order `a+b−d`.  This converts an algebraic degree count
  into an analytic decay order with no further estimates — the workhorse of the induction.
- `HTGeometry/RisingFactorialPolynomial.lean` — the rising factorial as a monic polynomial
  (`risingPoly`, `risingPoly_monic`, `risingPoly_natDegree`) and the crux,
  **`expansion_step`**: for `P` of degree `≤ d` and `a + b = d + p`,

  `P(x)/(x^a·x(x+1)⋯(x+b−1)) − P_d·(x(x+1)⋯(x+p−1))^{-1} = Q(x)/(x^a·x(x+1)⋯(x+b−1)·x(x+1)⋯(x+p−1))`

  with an *explicit* `Q` of degree `< a+b`, i.e. one order better.  The degree drop is proved by
  splitting `Q` into `(P − P_d X^d)·risingPoly p`, whose degree is below `d+p` because the `d`-th
  coefficient cancels, and `P_d·(X^d·risingPoly p − X^a·risingPoly b)`, a difference of two *monic*
  polynomials of the same degree `a+b`.

So each expansion step is now a closed loop: subtract the leading reciprocal rising factorial (whose
orbit sum is exact by `hasSum_inv_risingProd`), and the remainder is again a rational function of the
same shape with one more order — which `exists_norm_div_risingProd_le` converts into the next
estimate.  `#print axioms` clean.

**Step 3 is complete: the jets now expand to arbitrary depth.**  Two more modules run the loop.

`HTGeometry/StandardRationalExpansion.lean` gives the loop a shape it is closed under.  A *standard*
rational function is `P(x)/∏_j(x+j)` — denominator a product of factors shifted by naturals
(`shiftProd`, `shiftPoly`, monic of the multiset's cardinality) — with degree defect at least `p`;
subtracting a reciprocal rising factorial appends `p` more factors, so the shape is preserved.

- `IsStdRat.exists_norm_le` — the defect *is* the decay order;
- `shift_expansion_step`, **`IsStdRat.step`** — one step: split off the leading reciprocal rising
  factorial, the remainder is standard with defect `p+1`;
- **`IsStdRat.expansion`** — iterating: for every depth `K`,
  `f(x) = ∑_{i<K} c_i·(x(x+1)⋯(x+p+i−1))^{-1} + g(x)` with `g` standard of defect `p+K`;
- `isStdRat_inv_pow` — a reciprocal power `x^{-k}` is standard of defect `k`, which is the input.

`HTGeometry/PolygammaDepthExpansion.lean` sums that over the shift orbit.  Each reciprocal rising
factorial sums in closed form (`hasSum_inv_risingProd`), the remainder is standard of defect
`m+K+3` and the orbit bound converts it into `4C/t^{m+K+2}`, and the corpus's polygamma series turns
the total into the jet.  The result is **`exists_jet_expansion`**: for every `m` and every depth `K`,

`‖ψ^{(m+1)}(σ+it) − ∑_{i≤K} b_i·(s(s+1)⋯(s+m+i))^{-1}‖ ≤ C/t^{m+2+K}`

for all `σ > 0` and `t ≥ 2`, with explicit coefficients `b_i`.  The error is smaller than every term
kept, so the expansion is genuinely asymptotic — and it is available to *arbitrary* depth, which is
exactly what deciding the datum of a jet polynomial with leading-order cancellation needs.  mathlib
has neither Euler–Maclaurin nor any asymptotic expansion of the polygamma functions; this is built
from the corpus's own series and the elementary orbit and degree estimates.  `#print axioms` clean.

**The algebra of the assembly is in place too.**  `StandardRationalExpansion.lean` now closes the
class under the operations a jet polynomial performs: `IsStdRat.mul` (defects add), `IsStdRat.add`
(common denominator), `IsStdRat.const_mul`, `IsStdRat.pow`, `IsStdRat.mono`.  So *any* polynomial in
the jets, with the jets replaced by their depth-`K` rising-factorial approximations, is again a
single standard rational function whose defect is the monomial weight.

`HTGeometry/WeightedProductBound.lean` supplies the matching error estimate, which a uniform product
bound would lose: with `u = 1/t`, if each factor has size `A·u^{w_i}` and is approximated to depth
`K` with error `A·u^{w_i+K}`, then **`norm_prod_sub_prod_mul_pow_le`** gives

`‖∏ a_i − ∏ b_i‖ ≤ (|s|·A^{|s|})·u^{(∑ w_i) + K}`,

i.e. the product is approximated to the *total weight plus the depth* — each factor's error is
multiplied by the others, which are themselves small.  `norm_prod_le_mul_pow` is the companion size
bound.  `#print axioms` clean.

**The per-factor packages are built.**  `IsStdRat.sum`, `IsStdRat.prod`, `isStdRat_zero` and
`isStdRat_inv_risingProd` complete the closure list, and `PolygammaDepthExpansion.lean` now packages
each factor in exactly the shape the weighted product estimate consumes:

- **`exists_jet_stdRat_approx`** — the depth-`K` approximation of `ψ^{(m+1)}` *is* a standard
  rational function of defect `m+1` (a finite combination of reciprocal rising factorials);
- **`exists_jet_package`** — the jet, its approximation and the error, all measured against powers of
  `u = 1/t`: sizes `A·u^{m+1}`, error `A·u^{m+1+K}`;
- **`exists_jet_pow_package`** — the same for a *power* `(ψ^{(m+1)})^e`, obtained by feeding the
  constant family into `norm_prod_sub_prod_mul_pow_le`: size and error at weight `(m+1)·e`, with the
  approximation still standard (`IsStdRat.pow`).

**The assembly is now built, and so is the extraction.**

- **`exists_monomial_package`** — multiplying the power packages over the finitely many jets (outer
  weighted product, uniform constant `B = 1 + ∑ A_i`), a jet *monomial* is approximated to its own
  weight plus the depth by a standard rational function of that weight;
- **`exists_jetPoly_package`** — summing monomials of weight at least `W` (each approximant
  `mono`'d down to defect `W`, each error absorbed since `u ≤ 1`), a *jet polynomial* with constant
  coefficients is approximated to depth `K` by a single standard rational function of defect `W`;
- `HTGeometry/StdRatDatum.lean` — the extraction.  `tendsto_inv_vertical`,
  `tendsto_polynomial_mul_inv_pow` (a polynomial against its degree power tends to its leading
  coefficient) and `tendsto_shiftProd_mul_inv_pow` (a shift product against its length power tends to
  `1`) combine into **`tendsto_stdRat_datum`**: for `card l = deg P + p`,

  `s^p · P(s)/∏_j(s+j) → leadingCoeff P` on every vertical line with `σ > 0`.

  So a nonzero standard rational function *has* a datum, read off its numerator.

`#print axioms` clean.

**The dichotomy, and eq. (19) for constant coefficients.**  `HTGeometry/JetDatumFromNonFlat.lean`
proves the step that turns approximation into a residue.  `isLineBigO_of_bound` converts a bound in
powers of `1/t` into the line scale (through the corpus's `eventually_norm_vertical_le`), and then
**`exists_datum_of_not_flat`**: if `f` is approximated to *every* depth by standard rational
functions of a fixed defect `W`, and `f` is not flat (`¬ IsLineBigO σ n f` for some `n`), then

`∃ p c, c ≠ 0 ∧ s^p·f(s) → c`.

The proof runs the dichotomy at the single depth `K = n`.  If that approximant's numerator is zero
then `f` is `O(u^{W+n})`, hence `IsLineBigO σ n f`, contradiction.  Otherwise the numerator is
nonzero, its own datum is `tendsto_stdRat_datum`, and either the datum order `p` is below `W+n` — in
which case the error is of strictly higher order and passes to `f` — or `p ≥ W+n`, which again makes
`f` small enough to contradict non-flatness.

`HTGeometry/JetPolynomialDatum.lean` then reads this on the object of interest:
**`exists_jetPoly_datum`** — a polynomial in the positive-order digamma jets with *constant*
coefficients, if it is not flat on the line, satisfies `s^p·P(ψ′,ψ″,…)(s) → c ≠ 0`.  That is eq. (19)
for the constant-coefficient case with **no presentation supplied**: only the polynomial and its
non-flatness go in, and the corpus's `not_isVertFlatGerm_digammaJetEval` is exactly what supplies
non-flatness for a nonzero polynomial — which exists because a standard rational function whose
every coefficient vanished to every depth would make the jet polynomial flat, contradicting
`not_isVertFlatGerm_digammaJetEval`.  After that, the `log s` level for `ψ` itself (integrating the
`ψ′` expansion) and the `ℂ(s)`-coefficient case (multiplying by a rational function, whose own
inverse-power expansion is classical).
