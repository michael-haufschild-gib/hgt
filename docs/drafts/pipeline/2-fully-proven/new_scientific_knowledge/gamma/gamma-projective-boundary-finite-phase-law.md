- **Statement:** Every finite tuple in the Euler-Gamma differential field has a constant projective

## 1. The field and the positive law

Let

```text
K_Gamma = C(s)<Gamma>
        = C(s)(Gamma,Gamma',Gamma'',...).                    (1)
```

Write

```text
psi=Gamma'/Gamma,
L=C(s)<psi>=C(s)(psi,psi',psi'',...).                        (2)
```

The first theorem is a projective boundary law.

### Gamma monomial valuation theorem

`[PROVED HERE]` For every nonzero `F in K_Gamma`, there are unique integers

```text
k(F), a(F), b(F) in Z
```

and a unique constant `c(F) in C^x` such that, as `Im(s)->+infinity` with `Re(s)` in
an arbitrary fixed compact interval,

```text
F(s)
  =c(F) Gamma(s)^k(F) s^a(F) (log s)^b(F) (1+o(1)).          (3)
```

The `o(1)` is uniform after adding a variable in any fixed compact subset of a vertical tube.
Here and below `log` is the principal branch in the upper half-plane.

Consequently, for every finite nonzero tuple

```text
A=(F_1,...,F_r) in K_Gamma^r,                               (4)
```

there is an index `j` such that

```text
(F_1(s+z)/F_j(s+z),...,F_r(s+z)/F_j(s+z)) -> (d_1,...,d_r)  (5)
```

locally uniformly in `z` as `Im(s)->+infinity`, where the vector `d in C^r` is constant and
nonzero. Thus every finite coefficient tuple has a single constant point at its upper vertical
projective boundary.

The normalization in `(5)` is allowed to depend holomorphically on the local variable. This is
essential: normalizing only at one base point would leave the rapid factor generated by
`Gamma(s+z)/Gamma(s)`.

### Finite-phase theorem

Let

```text
V_(sigma,eta)={s in C: |Re(s)-sigma|<eta}.                  (6)
```

Let `f` be holomorphic on this vertical strip and uniformly almost periodic under vertical
translation: the family

```text
{f(s+iT):T in R}                                            (7)
```

is relatively compact for **uniform convergence on each closed substrip strictly contained in
`V_(sigma,eta)`**, where the norm is the supremum over the whole (vertically unbounded) closed
substrip. This is the Bohr holomorphic almost-periodic topology, not merely the compact-open
topology.

`[PROVED HERE]` If `f` is differentially algebraic over `K_Gamma`, then

```text
f is differentially algebraic over C,                       (8)

rank_Q Gamma_B(f)<infinity,                                 (9)
```

where `Gamma_B(f)` is the additive group generated by the vertical Bohr frequencies of `f`.
The same finite-rank conclusion holds for every continuous translation-equivariant observable of
the zero-divisor of a nonzero `f`.

Equivalently, the positive carrier law is:

> A recurrent holomorphic object constrained by finitely many Gamma-differential coefficients
> must carry a compact abelian phase carrier of finite covering dimension. Therefore an
> infinite-rank recurrent object must receive its infinite phase carrier from structure beyond the
> finite algebraic-differential extension of the archimedean Gamma field.

The carrier need not be a Lie torus: finite-rank frequency groups can dualize to solenoids. The
invariant forced here is finite covering dimension, equivalently finite rational rank of the
character group.

## 2. The formal Stirling tail cannot disappear

The main issue is cancellation. First-order Stirling asymptotics alone do not prove `(3)`: a
differential rational expression in `psi` and its derivatives could, a priori, cancel to every
algebraic-logarithmic order. The formal shift theorem removes exactly that possibility.

Let `hat(psi)` be the full formal asymptotic expansion

```text
hat(psi)(s)
  =log s-1/(2s)-sum_(n>=1) B_(2n)/(2n s^(2n)),              (10)
```

and put `hat(tau)=D hat(psi) in C((s^(-1)))`. Differentiating the formal digamma shift relation

```text
hat(psi)(s+1)-hat(psi)(s)=1/s                               (11)
```

gives

```text
hat(tau)(s+1)-hat(tau)(s)=-1/s^2.                           (12)
```

Let `rho h(s)=h(s+1)`, `r(s)=-s^(-2)`, and

```text
q(s)=rho(r)/r=s^2/(s+1)^2.
```

Equation `(12)` implies the homogeneous rational shift equation

```text
(rho-q)(rho-1)hat(tau)=0.                                   (13)
```

Adamczewski--Dreyfus--Hardouin, Theorem 1.2 in Case `S_infinity`, takes
`K=C(s)`, `F_0=C((s^(-1)))`, `rho(h)(s)=h(s+1)`, and `D=d/ds`; it proves that a member of `F_0`
satisfying a homogeneous rational linear `rho`-equation belongs to `C(s)` or is
hypertranscendental over `C(s)`. Thus it applies directly to `(13)`.

The series `hat(tau)` is not rational. An elementary pole-orbit proof already suffices. If a
rational `y` satisfied

```text
y(s+1)-y(s)=-1/s^2,                                        (14)
```

its polynomial part would be constant. All poles of its proper rational part would lie in the
integer shift orbit of zero. A maximal integer pole of `y` would force an uncancelled pole of the
left side at that integer, hence would have to be zero. A minimal integer pole would force an
uncancelled pole one step below it, hence would have to be one. These conclusions are incompatible.
Thus `(14)` has no rational solution.

Therefore

```text
hat(tau) is hypertranscendental over C(s).                  (15)
```

If `hat(psi)` were differentially algebraic, then its derivative `hat(tau)` would be
differentially algebraic. Hence

```text
hat(psi) is hypertranscendental over C(s).                  (16)
```

The analytic `psi` is also hypertranscendental. Otherwise `psi` would be differentially algebraic
over `C(s)`, while `Gamma` satisfies the first-order equation

```text
Gamma'=psi Gamma.
```

Transitivity of differential algebraicity would contradict Hoelder's theorem for `Gamma`.

It follows that the assignment

```text
psi |-> hat(psi)                                            (17)
```

extends to an isomorphism from the abstract differential rational field `L` to its formal
Stirling image. In particular, no nonzero element of `L` has the zero formal expansion.

## 3. The algebraic-logarithmic residue of the digamma field

The formal image of `L` lies in

```text
C(log s)((s^(-1))).                                         (18)
```

Every nonzero element of this field has a first nonzero Laurent coefficient in `C(log s)`. Every
nonzero rational function of `log s` has a leading monomial `c(log s)^b`. Consequently every
nonzero `A in L` has unique data

```text
a(A),b(A) in Z, c(A) in C^x
```

such that

```text
A(s)=c(A)s^a(A)(log s)^b(A)(1+o(1))                         (19)
```

uniformly on compact vertical tubes.

To justify transfer from the formal field to analytic asymptotics, use the complete sectorial
Poincare expansion of `psi` and its termwise derivatives. Poincare expansions are closed under
finite sums, products, differentiation, and inversion when the formal leading term is nonzero.
Equation `(16)` guarantees that this leading term exists for every nonzero differential rational
expression. This proves `(19)` without any unproved exclusion of a flat Stirling cancellation.

## 4. Adjoining Gamma creates one exponential valuation direction

The identities

```text
Gamma^(n)/Gamma
  =Bell_n(psi,psi',...,psi^(n-1))                            (20)
```

and their inverse triangular relations give

```text
K_Gamma=L(Gamma).                                           (21)
```

Moreover `Gamma` is transcendental over `L`. Indeed, Hoelder makes `Gamma` a differential
indeterminate over `C(s)`. For every `lambda in C^x`, the assignment

```text
Gamma^(n) |-> lambda Gamma^(n)                              (22)
```

defines a differential automorphism of `C(s)<Gamma>`. It fixes `psi=Gamma'/Gamma` and therefore
fixes `L`. If `Gamma` were algebraic over `L`, it could have only finitely many `L`-conjugates,
contrary to the infinite family `(22)`.

Thus every nonzero `F in K_Gamma` is a rational function of the transcendental element `Gamma`
over `L`. Write

```text
F=P(Gamma)/Q(Gamma),   P,Q in L[X].                         (23)
```

Let `u` and `v` be the smallest powers of `X` with nonzero coefficients in `P` and `Q`. On every
fixed vertical tube,

```text
Gamma(s)->0 exponentially                                  (24)
```

while every quotient of nonzero `L`-coefficients has only algebraic-logarithmic size by `(19)`.
Therefore the lowest Gamma powers dominate:

```text
P(Gamma)~A_u Gamma^u,
Q(Gamma)~B_v Gamma^v.                                      (25)
```

Applying `(19)` to `A_u/B_v` proves `(3)` with `k=u-v`.

Uniqueness follows successively from the exponential separation of distinct Gamma powers, the
power separation of distinct `s` exponents, and the logarithmic separation of distinct `log s`
exponents.

For a finite tuple, order the triples `(k,a,b)` by:

```text
smaller k dominates;
at equal k, larger a dominates;
at equal k,a, larger b dominates.                           (26)
```

Choose a component with dominant triple and divide the tuple by that component. Every ratio tends
to zero or to the ratio of its leading constants. This proves `(5)`.

## 5. Recurrence forces an autonomous equation

Assume `f` is differentially algebraic over `K_Gamma`. After clearing denominators, there is a
finite relation

```text
sum_(alpha in E)
  F_alpha(s) product_(j=0)^n (f^(j)(s))^(alpha_j)=0,         (27)
```

where the nonzero coefficient tuple `(F_alpha)` lies in `K_Gamma`.

Bohr uniform almost periodicity supplies relatively dense common almost periods on any exhaustion
of the strip by closed substrips. A diagonal choice gives positive return times `T_l->+infinity`
such that

```text
f(s+iT_l)->f(s)                                             (28)
```

locally uniformly on the strip. Cauchy's theorem gives the same convergence for every derivative
appearing in `(27)`.

Translate `(27)` by `iT_l`. Choose a dominant coefficient `F_beta` from `(5)` and divide the
translated equation pointwise by `F_beta(s+iT_l)`. For large `l` this coefficient is nonzero on
each fixed compact subset by `(3)`. Equations `(5)` and `(28)` allow passage to the limit and give

```text
sum_(alpha in E)
  d_alpha product_(j=0)^n (f^(j)(s))^(alpha_j)=0,            (29)
```

where `(d_alpha)` is a nonzero constant vector. Hence `f` satisfies a nontrivial autonomous ADE,
proving `(8)`.

The paper theorem
`almost-periodic-implicit-ade-relative-frequency-law.md`, specialized to constant coefficients,
then gives `(9)`, including singular implicit presentations and the divisor-observable statement.
No global initial-value uniqueness is required.

## 6. Hypertranscendence consequences

The contrapositive is the general Gamma-relative frequency criterion:

> If a holomorphic vertically uniformly almost-periodic function has infinite rational Bohr rank,
> then it is hypertranscendental over `C(s)<Gamma>`.

In particular, let

```text
D(s)=sum_(n>=1) a_n exp(-lambda_n s)                         (30)
```

have pairwise distinct real exponents `lambda_n`, nonzero coefficients `a_n`, converge uniformly on
a right half-plane, and have an exponent group of infinite rational rank. Uniqueness of uniformly
convergent Dirichlet expansions identifies the nonzero Bohr spectrum with the `lambda_n`. On every
smaller vertical strip in that half-plane, `D` is vertically uniformly almost periodic and has
infinite Bohr rank. Therefore

```text
D is hypertranscendental over C(s)<Gamma>.                  (31)
```

For `D=zeta`, the frequencies contain `{log p:p prime}`, which are rationally independent by
unique factorization. Thus

```text
zeta is differentially transcendental over C(s)<Gamma>.    (32)
```

Equivalently, `Gamma` and `zeta` are differentially algebraically independent over `C(s)`.

Equation `(32)` is a known theorem and is not claimed as new here. Wang--Yao proved Markus's
conjecture in 2024, and Lue generalized it in 2025. A 2025 paper of Lue--Jiang--Lue separately
studies algebraic independence of Dirichlet series and the Euler Gamma function. The candidate
novelty here is the projective-boundary law `(3)`--`(5)` and its extension `(8)`--`(9)` to arbitrary
holomorphic vertically uniformly almost-periodic functions, not the zeta specialization.

## 7. Positive requirements for RH architectures

This theorem does not classify Connes, Hilbert--Polya, de Branges, Nyman--Beurling, Weil, or Li
criteria as viable or nonviable. It identifies a precise implementation requirement shared by all
of them:

```text
recurrent output with infinite prime-frequency rank
  -> an explicitly supplied infinite prime-frequency carrier
  -> unbounded cofinal phase dimension.                    (33)
```

More precisely:

> Any implementation of these programs that reaches the completed zeta source must carry the
> infinite prime-frequency module explicitly in an infinite-dimensional coefficient, operator,
> determinant, test-function, or divisor object. The archimedean Gamma field supplies the
> completion valuation; a separate arithmetic carrier supplies the prime phase module.

Concretely:

1. **Connes / Hilbert--Polya / de Branges.** The source operator or characteristic determinant must
   already possess an infinite prime-frequency carrier and expose it to the spectral observable.
2. **Nyman--Beurling / Weil positivity.** The cofinal test space must retain unbounded arithmetic
   frequency rank while the completion acts; the cofinal family must therefore have unbounded
   compact-abelian phase dimension.
3. **Li coefficients.** The whole unbounded coefficient hierarchy is the carrier. No finite-order
   replacement is adequate unless it supplies an isomorphic infinite-rank arithmetic phase module.

There is also an exact finite-aperture form. For `sigma>0`, put

```text
Z_(N,sigma)(t)=product_(p<=N) (1-p^(-sigma-it))^(-1).       (34)
```

`[PROVED; BRIDGE]` Its translation hull is the full prime torus `T^(pi(N))`. Indeed, unique
factorization makes `{log p:p<=N}` rationally independent, so the arithmetic translation orbit is
dense in that torus. The torus observable in `(34)` has a nonzero Fourier coefficient in every
coordinate character, hence has trivial translation stabilizer. Therefore any compact abelian
phase carrier producing `(34)` as a continuous equivariant observable must have covering dimension
at least `pi(N)`: the induced factor homomorphism onto the output hull injects the latter's
rank-`pi(N)` character group into the character group of the carrier.

This gives the positive finite-prime growth law:

> A cutoff architecture that retains every prime phase through `p<=N` must transport at least
> `pi(N)` independent phase dimensions. In the reflected critical window
> `epsilon=c/log N`, the universal nonzero overlap profile changes the metric separation but does
> not remove these dimensions. Thus a scalar overlap profile is only a quotient statistic; a viable
> cofinal construction must preserve a phase carrier whose dimension diverges with `N` while it
> crosses the logarithmic boundary layer.

This is a carrier theorem: every viable finite-prime route must state where its infinite frequency
module lives and prove that the completed constraint preserves it.

## 8. Attacks and boundary cases

1. **Point normalization is false.** `Gamma(s+z)/Gamma(s)` contains a rapid local phase. The theorem
   uses functional projective normalization by one tuple component, so equal Gamma powers cancel
   before the limit.
2. **Flat differential cancellation.** This is the principal attack. It is excluded by the formal
   shift hypertranscendence of `hat(tau)`, not by a finite truncation of Stirling's formula.
3. **Gamma c-points.** Functions such as `1/(Gamma-1)` have nontrivial global pole sets, but on each
   fixed vertical tube they tend to `-1`. The theorem is a vertical projective law, not a global
   pole-confinement claim.
4. **Arbitrary DA functions.** `sin(s exp s)` is DA over `C(s)` and can have dense strip divisors, but
   it is not vertically uniformly almost periodic. The recurrence hypothesis, not finite order or
   zero density, is load-bearing.
5. **Compact-open normality is insufficient.** A bounded holomorphic strip function has a
   compact-open relatively compact translate family by Montel, but need not return to itself.
   Uniform convergence on each whole closed substrip is required and is exactly what supplies the
   Bohr return sequence used in Section 5.
6. **Finite support is not claimed.** Functions such as `exp(2^(-s))` are autonomous DA and have
   infinite Dirichlet support but rank-one frequency group. The theorem concludes finite rational
   rank, exactly as required.
7. **Meromorphic almost-periodic functions.** The stated theorem is holomorphic. Extending it across
   moving poles requires a compact meromorphic-hull topology and a divisor formulation; it is not
   silently included.
8. **Other Gamma orientations.** The proof uses the upper vertical boundary of `Gamma(s)`. Scaled
   archimedean factors `Gamma((s+mu)/2)` admit the same argument after an affine change of variable,
   but simultaneous incommensurable Gamma scales require a separate multi-valuation theorem.

The finite same-scale real-translate extension is now proved in
`finite-real-shift-gamma-projective-boundary-law.md`.  Its orbit-residue injection and real-weight
ordering give the one-point projective boundary for every finite pairwise incongruent translate
family and the translation-saturated prime-log zeta consequence.
