- **Statement:** Every nonconstant stable core of an injective scalar differential constraint is

## Goal

Classify the static boundary field and residual gauge motion of every scalar persistent
hypertranscendental constraint.  This is a universal theorem about the free scalar differential
field; special functions and RH play no role in its hypotheses.

## Theorem

Let `C` be an algebraically closed constant field of characteristic zero, let `y` be a differential
indeterminate, and put `F=C<y>`.  Let `Phi:F->F` be an injective differential `C`-endomorphism and

```text
T=intersection_(n>=0) Phi^n(F).
```

If `T=C`, the persistent phase is erased.  If `T!=C`, choose a differential Luroth generator `u`
of `T`.  The stable-core projective theorem gives

```text
T=C<u>,
Phi(u)=M(u),  M in PGL_2(C).                              (1)
```

After a Mobius change of generator, exactly one of the following mutually exclusive cases occurs.

### 0. Trivial holonomy

```text
Phi(q)=q,
B=Fix(Phi)=T,
H=1.
```

### 1. Finite multiplicative holonomy

For a unique `N>=2` and a primitive `N`-th root `zeta`,

```text
Phi(q)=zeta q,
B=Fix(Phi)=C<q^N>,
T/B is Picard--Vessiot with group mu_N.                   (2)
```

### 2. Infinite multiplicative holonomy

For `a in C^*` of infinite multiplicative order,

```text
Phi(q)=a q,
J=Dq/q,
B=Fix(Phi)=C<J>,
T=B(q),  Dq=Jq,
T/B is Picard--Vessiot with group G_m.                    (3)
```

### 3. Infinite additive holonomy

```text
Phi(q)=q+1,
P=Dq,
B=Fix(Phi)=C<P>,
T=B(q),  Dq=P,
T/B is Picard--Vessiot with group G_a.                    (4)
```

Equivalently, if `H` is the Zariski closure of the cyclic subgroup generated by the projective
action `M`, then

```text
H in {1, mu_N, G_m, G_a},
B=T^H=Fix(Phi),
T/B is the corresponding rank-one algebraic gauge torsor. (5)
```

In every nontrivial case `B` contains a nonconstant hypertranscendental observable fixed pointwise
by the constraint.

## Proof

### 1. Reduction to one projective transformation

The stable core is a differential intermediate field of `C<y>`.  Differential Luroth gives
`T=C<u>`.  The restriction of `Phi` to `T` is an automorphism.  Differential-order additivity
applied to that automorphism and its inverse forces both generator substitutions to have order
zero, so `Phi(u)=M(u)` for a unique `M in PGL_2(C)`.

### 2. Classification of the cyclic projective closure

Over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, a nonidentity element of `PGL_2` is
either semisimple with two fixed points or unipotent with one fixed point.

- A semisimple element is conjugate to `q |-> a q`.  It has finite order `N` exactly when `a` is a
  root of unity, and otherwise its cyclic subgroup is Zariski dense in `G_m`.
- A nontrivial unipotent element is conjugate to `q |-> q+1`.  Its integer iterates are Zariski
  dense in `G_a` because the characteristic is zero.

This proves the list of possible groups `H`.

### 3. Finite multiplicative branch

If `Phi(q)=zeta q` with `zeta` of exact order `N`, then `q^N` and all its derivatives are fixed.
Thus `C<q^N> subset Fix(Phi)`.  Conversely, `q` satisfies `X^N-q^N` over `C<q^N>`, while the `N`
maps `q |-> xi q`, `xi in mu_N`, are distinct differential automorphisms fixing `C<q^N>`.
Therefore the extension has degree `N`, its Galois group is `mu_N`, and its fixed field is exactly
`C<q^N>`.  It is the Picard--Vessiot extension of

```text
DY=((D(q^N))/(Nq^N))Y.
```

### 4. Infinite multiplicative branch

Put `J=Dq/q`.  Direct differentiation gives `Phi(J)=J`.  Every higher derivative of `q` is
`B_n(J,...,D^(n-1)J)q`, hence

```text
C<q>=C<J>(q).
```

The differential automorphisms over `C<J>` are exactly `q |-> c q`, so this is a `G_m`
Picard--Vessiot torsor.  The powers of `a` are Zariski dense in `G_m`; therefore an element fixed by
`Phi` is fixed by the full group, giving `Fix(Phi)=C<J>`.

### 5. Infinite additive branch

Put `P=Dq`.  Then `Phi(P)=D(q+1)=P`.  Every higher derivative of `q` is a derivative of `P`, so

```text
C<q>=C<P>(q).
```

The automorphisms over `C<P>` are exactly `q |-> q+c`, `c in C`, and form `G_a`.  The integer
translations generated by `Phi` are Zariski dense in `G_a`; hence `Fix(Phi)=C<P>`.  A fundamental
matrix

```text
[1 q]
[0 1]
```

realizes this as the Picard--Vessiot extension of the corresponding triangular linear system.

### 6. Hypertranscendence of the boundary observable

In the multiplicative branch, if `J` were differentially algebraic over `C`, then `Dq=Jq` and
differential-algebraic transitivity would make `q` differentially algebraic.  In the additive
branch, the same conclusion follows from `Dq=P`.  In the finite branch, algebraicity of `q^N` in
the differential sense would make its algebraic root `q` differentially algebraic.  All contradict
the choice of a free differential generator.  Hence every displayed generator of `B` is
hypertranscendental.

## Universal projective envelope

For any branch, the Schwarzian

```text
S(q)=q'''/q'-(3/2)(q''/q')^2
```

is fixed by the whole `PGL_2(C)` gauge group, not merely by `Phi`.  The field `C<S(q)>` is therefore
a universal projective boundary contained in every `B` above.  The exact fixed boundary `B` is
obtained by reducing the projective structure group to the cyclic closure `H` selected by the
constraint.

This gives two complementary invariants:

```text
universal projective boundary: C<S(q)>;
constraint-exact boundary:     B=Fix(Phi)=T^H.
```

The first forgets the chosen constraint element.  The second remembers its holonomy type.

## Clock-character compatibility

Suppose additionally that `Phi` commutes with clock dilation and the projective generator has
positive clock charge.  Clock equivariance forces `Phi(q)=a q`, so only the trivial, finite
multiplicative, and infinite multiplicative branches can occur.  The current `Dq/q` always has unit
clock weight, independently of the generator charge.  Thus the generator charge measures
reconstruction holonomy, while `H` measures constraint holonomy.

## No-go and positive residue

**No-go.**  There is no irreducible scalar infinite-depth dynamics beyond finite cyclic,
multiplicative, or additive gauge motion.  Any proposed scalar persistent mechanism outside these
three algebraic types is impossible.

**Positive theorem.**  Every non-erased scalar constraint has an explicit pointwise-fixed
hypertranscendental boundary field, and the discarded motion is a completely classified
Picard--Vessiot torsor over it.

## Why this is significant

This is a complete universal classification, not an RH-equivalent criterion and not a theorem
about a selected special function.  It answers, in scalar rank, the higher-level question “what
constrains hypertranscendental functions themselves?”:

```text
indefinite algebraic persistence is constrained to be gauge over a static fixed field.
```

It also makes the “thin constraint boundary” precise.  The fixed field still has one full unit of
differential transcendence, but the complete core lies over it by only a finite, multiplicative
one-dimensional, or additive one-dimensional algebraic gauge fibre.

The theorem is strong enough to be the central universal theorem of a standalone scalar paper when
packaged with examples and the rational-orbifold realization theorem.  It is not by itself the
higher-rank Fields-scale classification; that is the gauge-reduction conjecture below.
