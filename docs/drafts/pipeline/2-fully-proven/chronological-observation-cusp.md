## A semigroup already inside the retraction

Let K be the physical cubic section: nonnegative (a,b,c,d,e) with sum one and adjacent products zero. Write R_q=H_(1−q), where H is the audited ring retraction:

`R_q(a,b,c,d,e)=(a+(1−q)c/2,b,qc,d,e+(1−q)c/2)`.

For 0≤q≤1, R_q maps K to K. Direct substitution gives R_q R_r=R_(qr), R_1=id, and R_0=the ring retraction. Thus q=exp(−τ), τ≥0, supplies a continuous semigroup whose clock is the surviving fraction of the middle gap.

Let U be the five-dimensional space of pairing observations already normed in CubicNorm. Its B,C,D coordinates are fixed under pullback T_q f=f∘R_q. The remaining generators restrict on K to

`f=ace`, `v=c²`.

Indeed, the original E generator is Sc(b+c+d); it equals c² because S=1 and c≠0 forces b=d=0. Put w=c³. On the triangle b=d=0, a+e=1−c, so direct expansion gives

`T_q f=q f+q(1−q)v/2−q(1−q²)w/4`,

`T_q v=q²v`, `T_q w=q³w`.

These identities also hold on the rest of K, where c=0 and all three functions vanish.

Therefore W=U+span(w) is invariant. It is the smallest invariant linear space containing U: at q=1/2,

`w=(32/3)((1/2)f+(1/8)v−T_(1/2)f)`.

Any such space must contain w. The extension is strict. A relation Af+Ev+Fw plus a retained observation first forces B=C=D=0 by restriction to the ring. Evaluate the remaining relation at c=1/2, first with (a,e)=(1/4,1/4), then with (a,e)=(1/2,0); their difference forces A=0. The choices c=1 and c=1/2 then force E=F=0. Hence dim W=6.

Diagonalize the decaying part by

`u=f+v/2−w/4=c(1−(a−e)²)/4`, `v=c²`, `w=c³`.

The second identity uses a+e=1−c when c≠0 and is zero on both sides when c=0. The three weights are exactly 1,2,3: T_q u=qu, T_q v=q²v, T_q w=q³w. The other three independent coordinates have weight zero. Precomposition is a contraction for the actual supremum norm because R_q(K)⊆K.

## The observation algebra is a cusp times a line

Define A to be the unital real algebra of functions on K generated by this decaying invariant subspace. Then

`A=ℝ[u,c²,c³] ≅ ℝ[U,V,W]/(W²−V³)`,

with weights deg U=1, deg V=2, deg W=3. The cusp is forced by closure under the retraction; c itself has not been added to the observations.

Here is an injectivity proof, so the presentation is not merely a relation that happens to vanish. The functions u,c are algebraically independent. For each fixed c∈(0,1), the triangular probes allow a−e to vary through [−(1−c),1−c]. Consequently u varies through the nondegenerate interval

`[c(1−(1−c)²)/4,c/4]`.

If a polynomial P(u,c) vanishes on K, regard it as a polynomial in u with polynomial coefficients in c. At each such c it vanishes on an interval, so every coefficient is zero there. Each coefficient therefore vanishes on (0,1) and is the zero polynomial. This uses only the elementary finite-root theorem for nonzero univariate real polynomials.

Now divide an arbitrary polynomial in U,V,W by the monic polynomial W²−V³ in W. The remainder is P(U,V)+WQ(U,V). After substitution it is P(u,c²)+c³Q(u,c²). Distinct monomials have distinct pairs of exponents in u,c: the first part has even c exponent, the second odd exponent at least three. Algebraic independence forces every coefficient to vanish. This proves the exact presentation.

The selected algebra also has a coordinate-free characterization within this concrete geometry. Put L=W∩ker T_0: observations in the smallest invariant space which vanish after the final ring retraction. The exact kernel theorem for the retained readouts gives B=C=D=0 in every element of L. The spectral decomposition above therefore gives L=span(u,v,w), and conversely all three modes lie in L. Consequently A is exactly the unital algebra generated by L. Thus its definition can use the original pairing space and final retraction, without choosing the three eigenmodes as input. ## Normalization has one missing mode in every positive degree

Let B=ℝ[u,c]. This is the integral closure of A in their common fraction field. The element c is integral over A because c²=v, and it lies in Frac A because c=w/v. Thus B is finite and integral over A, and Frac A=Frac B. A polynomial ring over a field is integrally closed. Any element integral over A is also integral over B and hence lies in B, proving the claim.

A has the monomial basis u^j c^k for j≥0 and k∈{0,2,3,4,...}; every k≥2 is generated by 2 and 3. B also contains the monomials with k=1. Hence the graded vector quotient Q=B/A has precisely the basis

`[u^(n−1)c]`, one element in each degree n≥1.

In degree zero both algebras have dimension one. In degree n≥1, dim A_n=n and dim B_n=n+1. Equivalently,

`Hilb_A(z)=(1−z+z²)/(1−z)²`,

`Hilb_B(z)=1/(1−z)²`, `Hilb_Q(z)=z/(1−z)`.

As an A-module, Q is the degree-one shift of ℝ[u]; v and w annihilate it. The flow extends to B by c↦qc and induces multiplication by q^n on Q_n. This spectrum comes from the normalization defect of the actual observation algebra, not from inserting an arithmetic sequence into its definition.

For τ>0 the ordinary graded trace is

`H_Q(τ)=Σ_(n≥1)exp(−nτ)=1/(exp τ−1)`.

`∫_(x>0) exp(−xτ) dx=1/τ`.
