# Profile-monotone and polar monotone Weil cones

- **ID:** weil-profile-monotone-cone
- **Stage:** 2-fully-proven (non-polar case; paper, 2026-10-09, loop row 608)
- **Type:** THEOREM (non-polar profile-monotone cone is RH-equivalent); polar case still CONJECTURE
- **Source:** `3-formalized/weil-cone-order-comparable-rigidity.md`, audit of corollary (D)
- **Statement:** RH ⟺ `W_Λ(Φ) ≥ 0` for every positive-definite compactly supported test `Φ` with `Φ(log n) ≥ 0` (`n ≥ 2`) and `Φ(u)e^{−u/2}` nonincreasing on `[log 2, ∞)`; likewise for the polar monotone cone.

## Goal
Decide whether the profile-monotone subcone of the monotone cone is still RH-equivalent.

## What is known (formal)
- Full cone, monotone `ℓ`-values: RH ⟺ `W_Λ ≥ 0` on the combinations whose `ℓ_n = (Φ(log n)+Φ(−log n))/√n` are nonnegative and nonincreasing (`WeilMonotoneCone.riemannHypothesis_iff_positiveOnMonotoneCone`).
- For even `Φ` the profile condition implies monotone `ℓ`-values, so the profile-monotone cone is a subcone. RH ⇒ positivity on it is immediate.

## Why the duality does not decide it
- The M. Riesz/Farkas alternative for a cone `K` of tests is a test `Φ₁` with `ℓ(Φ₁)` in the cone generated by `ℓ(K)` and `W_Λ(Φ₁) < 0`. Positivity on `K` only covers `Φ₁ ∈ K`; the profile condition is not a condition on `ℓ(Φ)`, so the obstruction set is strictly larger than `K`.
- Polarity forces `∫Φ cosh(u/2) du = 0`; with `Φ ≥ 0` on `|u| ≥ log 2` and `|Φ| ≤ Φ(0)` (positive definiteness), the tail mass `∫_{|u|≥log 2} Φ cosh(u/2)` is at most `O(Φ(0))`. Off-line zeros enter `W_Λ(Φ)` through `∫ Φ(u) cosh(δu) cos(γ₀u) du`, which is then also `O(Φ(0))`; detection would need a near-cancellation of the low-frequency terms.
- The polar monotone cone lacks the Landau step: the duality gives a polar-Weil-positive `lam` with `Σ_{n≤N} lam(n) ≥ ψ(N)`, and the polar cone has no Herglotz continuation of `ξ'/ξ − Φ_lam`.

## Proof of the non-polar case (paper, loop row 608)
**Theorem.** RH ⟺ `W_Λ(Φ) ≥ 0` on the profile-monotone cone. In fact the one-parameter family `Φ_U = Δ_U ⋆ η`, `U → ∞`, suffices. Here:
- `Δ_U(u) = (1 − |u|/U)₊` is the Fejér triangle.
- `η = B_m` is the `m`-fold convolution of `1_{[−a,a]}/(2a)`, with `ma < log 2` and `m ≥ 4` for smoothness.

1. **Cone membership.**
   - `Φ̂_U = Δ̂_U·η̂ ≥ 0`, since `Δ̂_U = 4sin²(zU/2)/(Uz²)` and `η̂ = (sin(at)/(at))^m` with `m` even. So `Φ_U` is positive definite, even, `C^{m−2}`, compactly supported and nonnegative.
   - For `u ≥ ma`, `(Δ_U⋆η)(u) = ∫Δ_U(u−y)η(y)dy` is nonincreasing, because `Δ_U` is nonincreasing on `[0,∞)` and `u − y ≥ 0`.
   - Hence `Φ_U(u)e^{−u/2}` is nonincreasing on `[log 2, ∞)`.
2. **Prime side.** For `n ≥ 2` and `|y| ≤ ma < log 2`, `Δ_U(log n − y) = (U + y − log n)₊/U`. So `Σ_n Λ(n)n^{−½}(Φ_U(log n)+Φ_U(−log n)) = (2/U)(R⋆η)(U)` with `R(V) = Σ_{log n ≤ V}Λ(n)n^{−½}(V − log n)`, and `∫_0^∞ R(V)e^{−sV}dV = −(ζ′/ζ)(s+½)/s²` for `Re s > ½`.
3. **Pole and archimedean sides.**
   - `Φ̂_U(i/2) + Φ̂_U(−i/2) = 2∫Φ_U cosh(u/2) = (32c/U)sinh²(U/4)`, with `c = ∫η(y)cosh(y/2)dy = η̃(½)` (`η` even, so `η⋆cosh(·/2) = c·cosh(·/2)`).
   - The archimedean distribution of `Φ_U` is bounded in `U`: its integrand `(e^{h/2}Φ_U(h) − Φ_U(0))/(2 sinh h)` is bounded near `0` and `O(e^{−h/2})` at `∞`.
4. **Positivity as a one-sided bound.** `W_Λ(Φ_U) ≥ 0` is equivalent to `D(U) := M(U) − (R⋆η)(U) ≥ 0`, where `M(U) = 16c·sinh²(U/4) − (U/2)·arch(Φ_U)`.
5. **Laplace transform of `D`.** `L[D](s) = 4c(1/(s−½) − 2/s + 1/(s+½)) − L[(U/2)arch(Φ_U)](s) + (ζ′/ζ)(s+½)η̃(s)/s²`, with `η̃(s) = (sinh(as)/(as))^m`.
   - At `s = ½` the two simple poles cancel: the prime residue is `4η̃(½) = 4c` (the PNT main term).
   - On `Re s > 0` the only singularities are the poles at `s = ρ − ½` for zeros with `Re ρ > ½`. Their residues are `m_ρη̃(ρ−½)/(ρ−½)² ≠ 0`, because `η̃` vanishes only on the imaginary axis.
6. **Landau.** `D ≥ 0` for large `U` and `L[D]` converges for `Re s > ½`. By Landau's theorem the real abscissa is a singularity. `L[D]` is analytic at every real point `s > 0` (`ρ − ½` is never real), so the abscissa is `≤ 0`. Then `L[D]` is analytic on `Re s > 0`, there is no zero with `Re ρ > ½`, and RH holds.

**Reading.** The card expected that monotone tails cannot oscillate, so detection would need a near-cancellation of low frequencies. That is not needed. Off-line zeros are detected by the kink of the Fejér triangle at `|u| = U` through a one-sided bound on the Riesz mean `R`, i.e. by Landau, not by oscillation. The polar monotone cone remains open: polar tests cannot be nonnegative Fejér-type triangles, since subtracting the pole costs positive definiteness.

## The polar case (heuristic, open)
A polar test has `∫Φ cosh(u/2) = 0`. On the profile-monotone cone `Φ ≥ 0` on `|u| ≥ log 2`, so the tail mass `∫_{|u|≥log 2}Φ cosh(u/2)` must be cancelled by negative values in `|u| < log 2`. Positive definiteness (`|Φ| ≤ Φ(0)`) then makes the tail tiny relative to the central part: a tail of height `h` up to `U` needs a central part of size `≳ h e^{U/2}/U`. The kink at `U` contributes `≈ h e^{δU}/U` from an off-line zero, i.e. `e^{(δ−½)U}` relative to the central scale, which tends to `0`.
**Conjecture P.** The polar profile-monotone cone does not detect off-line zeros. Positivity there is RH-blind; in its strongest form it is unconditional, an exact free half sitting next to the RH-equivalent non-polar cone. If true, this is a sharp boundary: the pole is what lets monotone tests see the zeros (through the cancelled `e^{U/2}` main term). Not proved.
