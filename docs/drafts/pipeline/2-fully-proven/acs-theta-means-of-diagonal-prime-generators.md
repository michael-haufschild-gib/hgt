# Theta averages of the diagonal prime generators

Let $h$ be the real spline trace, $D_n=e_1+2e_{n^2}-e_{(2n+1)^2}$ and $N(u)=\lceil e^{u/2}\rceil$. For each center $T$, assign $x$ the square conductor $N(T+x)$ under the probability density $\Phi(x)/\int\Phi$, where $\Phi$ is Riemann’s positive theta source. Under these weights the mean of $Q(D_n)$ tends to $6h(0)-4h(\log4)$ as $T\to\infty$. The normalized full diagonal surplus after ordinary floor loss has the same limit. The mean of the signed ordinary-prime log generator $\log d_{\rm ord}(nD_n)/n^2$ stays bounded at every sufficiently late center.

For every real $t$, integrating $\Phi(x)h(t+x)$ gives zero. The trace has an absolutely summable exponential expansion in a fixed vertical strip. All absolute exponential moments of $\Phi$ exist, so integration multiplies the coefficient at exponent $z$ by $\Xi(iz)$, which vanishes at every active exponent. This cancels both translates of the continuous long-lag term $4h(u)-2h(u+\log4)$. [Exact theta means of both long lags](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/CountingHolography/Sections/TheThetaMeanOfTheSquareTrace.lean)

Rounding a square root $X\ge1$ up by at most three changes its log by $O(1/X)$. The trace derivative bound gives an error $O(X^{2w-1})$ in the long lag, with $w<1/2$ the zero width. Apply this at $X=e^{u/2}$ and $2e^{u/2}$. The relative log lag converges to $\log4$. Subtracting the two continuous long lags from the integer degree thus leaves a function tending to $6h(0)-4h(\log4)$. [Square-degree formula](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/CountingHolography/Sections/ThreeSquareConductorsWithZeroPoleDegree.lean) · [Width derivative](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/CountingHolography/Sections/UnitMultiplicityOnThePoleHyperplane.lean)

For $T\ge0$ the translated difference, multiplied by $\Phi(x)$, is dominated by $C\Phi(x)e^{|x|/2}$. On $T+x\ge U$ the difference is bounded by convergence. On $T+x<U$ the sample index is at most $N(U)$ and $|T+x|\le|U|+|x|$, so the trace strip bound suffices. Dominated convergence gives the limiting mean. The normalized full surplus with ordinary floor loss removed differs from the signed degree by $o(1)$ and obeys the same argument. The normalized log of the ordinary generator differs from the negative signed degree by a uniformly bounded amount, giving the stated mean bound. The pointwise bound on the ordinary generator remains open.
