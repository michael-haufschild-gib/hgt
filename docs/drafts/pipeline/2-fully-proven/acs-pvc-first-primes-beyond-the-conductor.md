# Removing first primes at most the conductor

Let \(B_{>}(N)\) be the original seventeen-ninths prefix restricted to \(p>n\). On every fragment ending at \(M\), the removed price is \(O(M^3(1+\log(2M)))\). Thus the full one-sided \(11/3\) and \(3+\eta\) targets can both be tested using \(B_{>}\). Each remaining product \(q=pv\) has exactly one prime divisor above \(n\), since \(v\le n<p\). Bounding \(B_{>}\) remains open.

**Proof.** Write \(L\) for the spline horizon, \(f\) for the dyadic spline and \(U=\lfloor n^{17/9}\rfloor\). Eventually \(U\ge\mathrm{productLower}(1,L)\), so a product \(q>U\) first enters at \(k=q\) and remains through \(L\). For \(4<p\le n\) all \(v\le n\) have \(pv\le n^2\le L\). Summing in \(k\) first gives the exact weight
\[
  n^{-1}\{V_{L+1}(pv/n^2)+f(pv/n^2)\}.
\]
The second summand is the returned first endpoint. Since \(W(0)=1\) and \(f(0)=0\), the one-cell mesh is \(V_1=-f\). The numerator is therefore \(V_{L+1}-V_1\), with the same uniform variation estimate on both terms. The cofactor interval is exactly \((\lfloor U/p\rfloor,n]\).

The signed cofactor coefficients \(a_p(v)=\mathbf1_{p\nmid v}\mathrm{innerJump}(1,v)\) have uniformly bounded prefixes. Subtract the multiples of \(p\) from a full prefix and apply the large-prime dilation identity. For \(d=p/n^2\), the mesh \(V_b(vd)\) is bounded and its adjacent differences are \(O(1/v)\) uniformly in \(b\) and \(d\). This follows by comparing \(xV_b((v+1)d/x^2)\) at \(x=1\) and \(x=\sqrt{1+1/v}\) using its bounded conductor derivative. Abel summation on the cofactor interval therefore costs \(O(1+\log(2n))\), including the sample at each end. The ordinary prime mass is at most \(\sum_{q\le n}\Lambda(q)\le7n\). After the \(n^{-1}\) normalization and the exterior \(n^2\), summation proves the fragment bound. The finitely many initial conductors have bounded total cost.

**Removing the entire cofactor cap.** Put \(F_n=\max(n,\lfloor L_n/(n+1)\rfloor)\). The fixed-prime argument charges all \(p\le F_n\) at most \(O(nF_n(1+\log(2n)))\) in the weighted row, because the cofactor interval is always clipped at \(\min(n,\lfloor L_n/p\rfloor)\). Since \(L_n\ll n^2\), this still costs \(O(M^3(1+\log(2M)))\) on every terminal fragment. For every remaining \(p>F_n\) and every actual horizon \(k\le L_n\), one has \(\lfloor k/p\rfloor\le n<p\). Hence the entire inner sum runs over \(\lfloor\max(U,\mathrm{productLower}(1,k))/p\rfloor<v\le\lfloor k/p\rfloor\) with the original signed weights. Its only cofactor restrictions come from the two product endpoints. The upper cofactor cap and coprimality both disappear at the full RH exponent.

**Four adjacent cofactor caps.** Set \(G_V(s)=\sum_{1\le v\le V}g(v)v^{-s}\), where \(g(v)=\mathrm{innerJump}(1,v)\) is the actual coefficient for \(p>n\). The unit stencil has coefficients \((1,c-2,-2c)\) at \((1,2,4)\). Indeed its unit coefficient is one and its reciprocal moment vanishes. For \(V\ge1\),
\[
 G_V(s)=1-A_V(s)-c2^{-s}A_{\lfloor V/2\rfloor}(s),\qquad
 A_V(s)=\sum_{v=1}^V(-1)^{v+1}v^{-s}.
\]
The unit correction accounts for \(g(1)=0\). At every right zero \(\rho\), the cofactor response tends to one. Averaging the four sharp caps \(4K,\ldots,4K+3\) gives \(1+O_\rho(K^{-1-\Re\rho})\). To see the gain, put \(P_j=(A_j+A_{j+1})/2\). The four-cap average equals \(1-(P_{4K}+P_{4K+2})/2-c2^{-\rho}P_{2K}\). At an eta zero, the paired-tail estimate gives \(|P_j|\le\tfrac34|\rho||\rho+1|(j+1)^{-1-\Re\rho}\). This identifies the signed cancellation of the sharp cofactor endpoint on each zero probe. The four-cap estimate has a Lean draft. The sum over ordinary primes at the original moving cut remains open. For a fixed right zero and tolerance \(\varepsilon>0\), choose \(d\ge2\) so that \(|G_j(\rho)-1|<\varepsilon/2\) for all \(j\ge d\). At an actual horizon \(k\), put \(Y=\max(\mathrm{productLower}(1,k),U)\). For \(p>n\), \(d\le n\) and \(dp\le k\), the coprime probe on \(Y<pv\le k,\ v\le n\) has magnitude below \(\varepsilon\) if \(dp\le Y\), and lies within \(\varepsilon\) of one if \(Y<2p\). Indeed its value is \(G_m-G_{\min(m,\lfloor Y/p\rfloor)}\) for \(m=\min(n,\lfloor k/p\rfloor)\), and \(G_0=G_1=0\). The same \(d\) works at every prime and horizon.
