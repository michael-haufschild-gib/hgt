# Research facts and implications from computations

This file is a research synthesis, not a chronology or a proof certificate. Each entry states what object was investigated, what was learned, and what that permits or prevents in a proof attempt. Route names are included for recognition; the conclusion does not depend on knowing an experiment's filename.

**Evidence labels.** **Observation** means finite numerical evidence, including any stated precision checks. **Rejected mechanism** rejects only the specified claim or construction. **Reported analytic result/obstruction** means the existing research records describe an independent paper, symbolic or Lean derivation; this editorial synthesis does not certify that its current Lean dependency chain is unconditional. **Reported finite certificate** is a rigorous numerical bound in the stated finite domain. **Exact candidate** still needs an independent proof. **Paper result; not formalized** explicitly records a remaining Lean gap. Conditional results keep their assumptions explicit. None of these labels makes an unproved physical identification, infinite-limit argument or RH conclusion available as a premise.

**Shared notation.** RH is the Riemann hypothesis. A critical-line ordinate is the imaginary part gamma of a zero 1/2+i*gamma. Lambda(s)=pi^(−s/2)*Gamma(s/2)*zeta(s) is the meromorphic completed zeta function; Xi(t)=s*(s−1)*Lambda(s)/2 at s=1/2+i*t is its entire completion in the height variable. A Gram matrix records pairwise inner products; a Hankel moment matrix has entries depending on the sum of their indices. Positive semidefinite means every quadratic form value is nonnegative. A finite-dimensional Galerkin approximation does not automatically bound the infinite-dimensional minimum from below.

## What finite computations can and cannot establish

- A single exact counterexample can disprove a proposed universal inequality. A finite successful scan cannot establish Riemann hypothesis (RH), positivity at every matrix size, escape control as a cutoff tends to infinity, or a common-zero theorem. Use successful scans to select the next statement to prove, not as that proof.

- [Observation] Numerical sign tests are especially unreliable when spectral points nearly coincide, moment matrices have high rank, or an exponentially small signal is obtained by subtracting large terms. Increasing precision has repeatedly removed apparent negative eigenvalues. A small computed eigenvalue needs a conditioning and precision check before it can reject a mechanism.

- [Research rule] An exact-looking identity suggested by computation becomes an analytic result only after an independent derivation. Some entries below report such derivations; none licenses importing a saved numerical coefficient table as a premise in Lean.

- [Interpretation] Many failed shortcuts kept only diagonal terms, one scalar component, pointwise signs, or a fixed matrix size. The surviving arithmetic expressions often rely on cancellation between the omitted components. Before strengthening a bound, check whether the quantity being bounded still contains the source's mixed terms.

## Reflected theta-source curvature (C5; routes CLG/TTF)

Here Phi is the literal completed-theta source whose cosine transform gives Xi. Reflecting around center u gives the log action q(u,t)=2*log(Phi(u))−log(Phi(u+t))−log(Phi(u−t)); t measures separation. C5 is the route's fifth-root curvature inequality for this reflected source. The symmetry boundary is u=0; coalescence is t=0. In the interpolation entries, M(lambda) is the complete signed compensation margin when higher theta terms are turned on from the first-atom model (lambda=0) to the full source (lambda=1). These are finite source inequalities; even proving them does not by itself complete an RH route.

- [Observation] The fifth-root curvature inequality for the literal completed-theta source, called C5, was nonnegative at all tested admissible points. Its smallest margins occurred when the two reflected evaluation points coalesced; the margin then scaled as the fourth power of their separation. The tested source lay in the negative-elasticity region, so positivity must come from the combined balance, not from assuming elasticity is nonnegative.

- [Observation] Finite sections of the completed-theta Bezoutian kernel were positive through matrix rank 20 after increasing precision. This supports further investigation of that specific kernel. It does not establish positivity at arbitrary rank; the very small eigenvalues make extrapolation particularly unsafe.

- [Observation; rejected extension] The tested completed-theta kernel had no detected total-positivity violation through order four, but a stable violation occurred at order five. Here total positivity means nonnegativity of all ordered minors of the specified order. An argument requiring this kernel to be totally positive at every order, or at order five and above, cannot be justified by its successful low-order tests.

- [Observation] The tested Li coefficients were positive through degree 300, and the tested moment/support matrices built from zeta-zero data were positive through size 12. These are finite checks of positivity conditions related to RH. Clustered zero data made low-precision moment matrices produce false negative signs; neither successful range proves the infinite sequence of inequalities.

- [Rejected mechanism] Positivity of theta-shaped atoms, local log-concavity, endpoint-ratio bounds and the tested Riccati inequality do not together force the C5 curvature inequality. Positive mixtures of only two to four admissible atoms violate it. A proof for the literal theta source therefore needs its particular coefficient relations, or another constraint absent from those mixtures.

- [Observation] For the C5 balance F and reflected log action q, the fixed-action Jacobian J=F_u q_t−F_t q_u was positive in the tested interior. Reflection forces J=0 at the symmetry center u=0. The useful boundary target is therefore the limiting sign of J/u, not strict positivity of J at the symmetry center.

- [Exact candidate] The leading symmetry-center coefficient of the C5 fixed-action Jacobian is controlled by A²D−18A³C+36A²B²−5ABC+4B³, where A,B,C,D are minus the second, fourth, sixth and eighth derivatives of log Phi at the center, respectively. Its modular polynomial reduction has a positive degree-12 Bernstein representation on the physical scale interval. Extending that sign to the larger proposed half-line fails beyond a root near the normalized squared scale X=56.86; the actual modular enclosure is 19.1<X<20. Use that enclosure, not the failed half-line extension.

- [Rejected mechanisms] The desingularized C5 Jacobian J/u is not globally monotone in the center coordinate. The proposed separate signs for its velocity and current contributions also fail, as does separate positivity of an eighth-derivative residual block. A proof must retain signed compensation and distinguish the symmetry boundary, compact interior and large-scale tail.

- [Reported analytic result] The C5 compensation supplied by the first theta atom is positive on the admitted physical tail region. Its coalescence margin first appears at order seven in separation and has a degree-seven polynomial coefficient that is positive after shifting the scale to its lower endpoint. An intermediate polynomial changes sign, so the result does not follow by assigning the same sign to every intermediate term.

- [Observation] Along the tested modular-center trajectory, the C5 balance increased from the coalescence boundary into the far cusp; no stationary point was found. Direct positivity of this derivative is a smaller candidate proof target than a construction that must classify and control every stationary point. The numerical search does not establish global monotonicity.

- [Rejected mechanism] The compensation margin M(lambda), obtained by interpolating from the first theta atom to the complete theta source, is not monotone in the interpolation parameter. Its derivative takes both signs, and an interior value can lie below both endpoint values. Endpoint positivity alone therefore cannot justify this interpolation.

- [Observation; candidate mechanism] The same theta compensation interpolation appears convex in the tested structural tail. A possible sufficient route is M'''(lambda)>0, M''(0)>=0 and M'(0)>=−M(0)/5, with M(0)>0 and 0<=lambda<=1. Together these inequalities would prevent the interpolation from exhausting the positive first-atom margin. No tested counterexample was found; the inequalities for the full source still require analytic proof.

- [Reported reduction; partial evidence] The coalescence limit of the third interpolation derivative reduces to a specific inequality between higher theta likelihood derivatives. One sufficient component has an analytic proof, and the dominant second-theta-atom contribution to the other has a positive degree-nine polynomial certificate. Numerically, higher atoms oppose it only weakly. Generic moment or log-convexity assumptions do not supply the missing full-source bound; the useful task is to control the signed higher-atom remainder.

- [Rejected mechanism] Favorable signs of individual likelihood derivatives and pairwise tangent bounds do not guarantee that the theta interpolation's initial slope fits inside the available first-atom margin. A configuration saturating the adverse bounds violates that budget. Possible surviving information is coupling that prevents simultaneous saturation, the favorable third likelihood channel, and a derived mixed trapezoid inequality; these must remain coupled in any replacement proof.

- [Reported obstruction] Several C5 endpoint-margin constructions fail near coalescence even after adding their proposed reserves. The adaptive construction has a strictly negative fifth derivative at that boundary. These failures identify an omitted cancellation involving the third likelihood channel; they reject the reduced margins, not the complete theta compensation.

- [Reported analytic result] The normalized theta-interpolation curvature balance has an exact decomposition that makes the required curvature threshold explicit. Merely knowing that the curvature contribution is positive does not meet that threshold: its coefficient and the remaining signed terms matter. A replacement argument must compare the complete balance rather than use curvature positivity as a standalone premise.

- [Rejected mechanism] Fixing a positive measure's total mass, support gap and conditional first moment does not force the enlarged-source compensation inequality, even with the tested lower-transport curvature condition. A sufficiently distant atom preserves those constraints while making compensation negative. First-moment control permits an unbounded second moment; additional tail or coupled-moment information is necessary.

- [Reported analytic result; limited scope] A change of coordinates retaining a particular mixed transport quantity yields a curvature coefficient with an exact sum-of-squares decomposition. This proves monotonicity when that mixed coordinate is held fixed. It does not prove monotonicity along the actual theta trajectory, where the coordinate can vary.

- [Rejected mechanism] A single center-independent, damped Fourier correction cannot repair the tested reflected-theta determinant at all centers: different centers require incompatible correction signs. Fast damping also makes the correction disappear inside a known negative rank-three region, while slow damping fails the proposed zero-mass construction. Any surviving correction needs coupled center/frequency information and a bound on the complete signed mass balance.

- [Observation] One fixed even Fourier correction, including both endpoints of its allowed coefficient interval, kept the tested middle-region determinant positive after the stated entry and correction error allowances. This supports that specific compact-region construction. Uniform interval control of the signed errors remains open; sampled corrected positivity is not a certificate for the whole region.

- [Reported analytic result; open connector] The compact even-determinant construction has derived moment identities, exact polynomial/block correspondences and positive lower-order/remainder pieces. The remaining task is to prove that their assembled expression equals the required determinant in the original variables. Generated coefficient tables do not establish that identity or supply missing premises.

## Three-by-three reflected-theta determinant (route S6F)

The tested determinant, often called D1, is the 3-by-3 Hankel determinant formed from the reflected theta-source derivative data. Its sign is needed only on the region reached by the actual source dynamics. A “physical cone” below means a derived enclosure of that trajectory in normalized scale and clock coordinates, not every algebraically possible state.

- [Observation] The actual 3-by-3 Hankel determinant built from reflected theta-source derivatives stayed positive on the tested missing compact trajectory interval. Nearby starting scales can fail later, so positivity is a property to establish for the actual source trajectory, not for every trajectory that starts near the same boundary.

- [Reported analytic enclosure; exact candidates] The actual reflected-theta dynamics have a derived compact enclosure in normalized source coordinates. Exact polynomial calculations suggest positivity on a larger rectangular region containing that enclosure. This separates two tasks: prove the source remains in the enclosure, then prove the determinant inequality there. Neither a finite-order enclosure nor a polynomial sign at this rank produces all-degree Xi positivity.

- [Observation] On the refined region containing the reflected-theta trajectory, the rank-three determinant increased and was convex in the scale variable; it was concave in the squared clock variable, with positive third derivative. These signs suggest a direct boundary-minimum proof. The alternating derivative pattern already fails at order four, so an all-orders complete-monotonicity argument is unavailable.

- [Rejected mechanisms] For the reflected-theta rank-three determinant, global boundary monotonicity, monotonicity along the upper clock boundary, monotonicity of the raw determinant, and the proposed simple factorizations do not provide valid global proofs. A localized geometric argument is needed; the finite determinant's observed positivity survives these failures.

- [Observation; proof target] The tested scale monotonicity and clock concavity reduce the reflected-theta determinant's minimum to its symmetry boundary and one outer one-variable boundary. That outer boundary was decreasing, leaving its endpoint as the final test. This is a candidate small collection of sufficient inequalities; the numerical reduction is not itself their analytic proof.

- [Observation] The projective motion associated with the reflected-theta determinant points inward at the symmetry boundary and had the desired sign on the tested interior boxes. The raw determinant nevertheless rises and then falls, and the first nonzero projective derivatives alternate +,−,+. Favorable inward motion must not be confused with monotonicity of the determinant or positivity of all derivatives.

- [Reported analytic structure] The reflected-theta source dynamics admit three differential operators satisfying the sl₂ commutation relations; physical scale differentiation corresponds to the diagonal, or Cartan, operator. This supplies a concrete algebraic structure for studying finite matrix positivity. It does not by itself establish the required positive inner product or all-rank result.

- [Rejected proof format] Testing each Bernstein coefficient of the reflected-theta derivative matrix for positivity fails, although the assembled matrix appears positive definite. Cancellation between different scale contributions is essential. A matrix or determinant argument may succeed where coefficient-by-coefficient signs cannot.

- [Observation] On the refined physical region, all tested principal minors of the Hankel matrix obtained by differentiating with respect to scale were positive. This suggests strict increase in the Loewner order, meaning that the matrix increment is positive definite. An analytic proof must establish that matrix inequality on the whole region.

- [Rejected all-order extension] The reflected-theta kernel's proposed positive-definite completion fails for a six-point configuration. All tested configurations with at most five points passed. Consequently, a representation as the Fourier transform of a positive measure, which would imply positivity at every finite size, cannot serve this kernel as proposed. Finite-order or direct rank-three arguments remain separate possibilities.

## Weil quadratic forms and spectral receivers

The Weil form combines contributions from primes, the poles of completed zeta and the Gamma factor. “Ground energy” means its least value after normalization of the test function. Restricted forms limit the test function's support; “pole-constrained” additionally removes the directions associated with the poles. A receiver is an observable used to detect spectral points. Positivity of a receiver model does not establish that the arithmetic source produces it.

- [Reported finite certificate] For the restricted Weil quadratic form with the pole constraints imposed, rigorous finite numerical bounds establish positivity through logarithmic support length S=1, where aperture lambda=exp(S/2). Reported lower bounds for the least energy are approximately 0.518 at S=log(2) and 0.0014 at S=1. These certify the stated restricted range, not the unrestricted Weil positivity criterion for RH.

- [Observation] At aperture lambda=sqrt(2), imposing the pole constraints raises the computed least Weil energy from about 0.00133 to 0.547. Numerically resolved positivity persists to about lambda=2.1. No negative value was found up to roughly lambda=5, but the later finite-dimensional eigenvalues are only upper bounds on the true least energy and become extremely small; they cannot certify positivity there.

- [Observation] Removing prime contributions from the pole-constrained Weil form makes it indefinite near aperture lambda=1.8145. Individual omitted prime powers become important shortly after their support thresholds are crossed. Prime contributions themselves have both signs. The arithmetic terms are essential collectively; adding primes cannot be justified as a sequence of positive corrections.

- [Observation] As the allowed support grows, Fourier-transform zeros of both constrained and unconstrained computed Weil ground states approach low zeta-zero ordinates. Pole constraints accelerate this alignment but are not its sole cause. Additional interlacing transform zeros eventually appear, so alignment of a few zeros is not a one-to-one global spectral identification.

- [Observation] In computed compact-support Weil ground states, closely spaced transform zeros can merge even while the lowest energy barely changes. Before merging, the measured location error grows approximately as the inverse cube of the pair separation. A small scalar energy therefore does not ensure separate recovery of nearby zeros or their multiplicities; derivative samples or divided differences are needed to resolve a close pair.

- [Reported analytic result] The combined squared sampling energy at two nearby points decomposes into the squared average response and the squared difference response. As the points merge, the difference channel vanishes quadratically and its rescaled limit measures a derivative norm. This identifies the additional observable needed when ordinary two-point sampling loses multiplicity information.

- [Observation] Computed Weil ground states had monotone autocorrelations and favorable cumulative prime/pole balances after the first contributing atom. The corresponding pointwise completed error changes sign. A proof using these observations must justify the averaging selected by the ground state; it cannot replace a signed oscillatory error by a pointwise positive function.

- [Reported analytic result; open limit] The published finite restricted-Weil matrices are nested principal submatrices of an exact matrix system. The missing result is convergence connecting these source-defined matrices with the proposed infinite-dimensional Laurent/Galerkin operator. Nestedness alone does not justify that identification or transfer finite positivity to the limit.

- [Observation] The tested fixed-pole even/odd energy combinations were positive over their parameter range 0<a<=8. Their normalized margins oscillate around the exact mass 2+gamma−log(4*pi), approximately 0.04619, where gamma is Euler's constant, and the excess margin tends toward zero. A sharp prime-side argument must recover this constant; a uniformly loose positive allowance will not suffice.

- [Observation] The tested arithmetic lag function, evaluated at every integer lag through 20 million, reached only about 40% of the ceiling predicted under RH. This suggests that any conditional recurrence near that ceiling happens much later than accessible samples. Failure to approach the ceiling in this range is not evidence against the conditional recurrence theorem.

- [Observation; conditional analytic obstruction] Truncating prime-power terms in the arithmetic lag kernel causes finite positive-definiteness tests to fail shortly after the first omitted prime-power lag. Separately, an analytic result conditional on RH says that continuum positive definiteness fixes every prime-power location and weight exactly: any nonzero perturbation of one such corner creates a negative high-frequency direction. This conditional rigidity result is not an unconditional proof of RH.

- [Observation] In the tested arithmetic Gram matrices of size N, the smallest eigenvalue behaves approximately as c/N with c around 0.09–0.10; leading eigenvalues follow the tested zeta-ordinate scales N/(1/4+gamma_j²). Here gamma_j is the imaginary part of the j-th sampled critical-line zero. Finite positive-semidefinite tests can miss sufficiently high hypothetical off-line zeros until the matrix is much larger; positive small matrices are weak evidence about the full zero set.

- [Observation] For the Hedenmalm critical feature 2/(1+(z−t)²), extending the receiver from three sampling centers to a complete integer lattice improves the measured capacity threshold only from about 0.3437 to 0.3821. This modest gain does not produce the missing arithmetic source covariance. Further optimization of the same scalar receiver is unlikely to address the main source-identification obstacle.

- [Rejected mechanism] Signed mixtures of scalar separating kernels suppress cross responses only when spectral points remain outside a minimum separation. As that separation shrinks, the coefficient norm and the adverse negative lobe grow rapidly. Such mixtures are not uniform close-pair controls without an additional spacing theorem or a matrix/source cancellation mechanism.

- [Rejected asymptotic inference] Multiplying by a fixed-degree admissible gamma-cell factor can introduce moving off-line zeros, but only in number proportional to height T. Zeta has on the order of T log(T) zeros. These factors can falsify finite-height structural claims; they cannot alone force a positive asymptotic proportion of off-line zeros. A density obstruction needs a perturbation affecting comparably many zeros.

- [Rejected sufficiency claims; finite observations] Positive, strictly log-concave kernels can still have a negative rank-three test. Enforcing functional-equation symmetry on source atoms does not prevent off-axis zeros, and tested finite causal symmetric Volterra operators were indefinite. Conversely, the first two pointwise Laguerre derivative tests can remain positive while close-pair control fails. Positivity, symmetry, causality and low-order derivative signs are individually insufficient substitutes for the missing coupled spectral inequality.

- [Observation] In tests of observables designed to isolate a zero, combining several frequency bands did not conceal substantially more energy than one band with a varying phase frequency. This gives no observed advantage to that particular extra complexity. It is not a uniform lower bound for all possible isolated-zero constructions.

- [Reported bound; numerical refinement] For exponentials with frequency gaps at least g, all sharing decay rate K on a window of length L>2*pi/g, the Gram matrix has an n-independent lower bound exp(−2*K*L)*(L−2*pi/g), where n is the number of exponentials. This follows by comparison with the undamped integral. Finite tests of unequal decay rates did not violate the proposed analogous floor, but do not prove it. A stronger polynomial-scale lower bound when decay rates are small compared with the frequency gap remains conjectural. Do not extrapolate an initial decrease with n into a contradiction of the proved uniform-decay floor.

- [Rejected mechanism] In the barrier representation of the actual Weil form, many jumps at prime-power thresholds are negative. Convex growth between thresholds cannot pay all these losses by itself. Any cumulative positivity argument must include the favorable jumps as well as the negative ones, with their actual signs and sizes.

## Failed carrier and localization shortcuts (C1, prime insertion/JAS, T2)

- [Rejected specific carrier] The tested global-mean scalar construction in route C1 differs from its intended theta quantity by a stable relative margin of about 32%. It cannot be used as an exact identification of that theta source. This rejects the tested mean-based carrier, not every possible construction in route C1; an analytic head/tail separation is the appropriate way to close the rejection.

- [Rejected operator identification] The tested rule for inserting primes integrates to a diagonal similarity transformation that is not unitary. Finite searches found no compatible adjoint-preserving intertwiner between unequal apertures. A surviving operator construction must supply an appropriate inner product, an additional environment/defect, or non-diagonal coupling; algebraic similarity alone does not preserve the required spectral geometry.

- [Observation; exact normalization effect] Mixed prime-character and completed-theta packet Gram matrices remained nonsingular through ten primes, giving matrices of size 1024. Scalar Gamma aperture factors cancel when those Gram matrices are normalized. Consequently, these scalar factors neither improve the normalized conditioning nor select the critical line; that role would require information that survives normalization.

- [Rejected growth control] The tested two-sided reciprocal scalar completion grows on the scale of a primorial, the product of the included primes. No fixed exponential gauge controls that growth. Continuing this construction would require a different topology or an additional non-scalar channel, not a stronger estimate for the same scalar normalization.

- [Rejected estimates] The finite-shell localization route called T2 does not reach its required exponent using the tested interfaces, even after granting the prime-gap exponent 13/25. Generic second/fourth-moment bounds, buffering, physical-slice restrictions and reciprocal-tail repairs did not close the deficit. Improving those estimates without changing the arithmetic localization mechanism has no demonstrated route to the target.

- [Open requirement] A surviving T2 shell argument needs two ingredients together: sufficiently tight localization relative to the target's reciprocal scale, and an arithmetic estimate for the selected cells and excluded set across every paired low shell. Either ingredient alone leaves the tested exponent deficit. Their joint uniform form remains unproved.

## Interacting prime perturbations of Jacobi matrices (route J)

- [Observation with reported scaling result] In route J's Jacobi-matrix model, perturbing one Euler factor at prime p becomes significant around matrix degree n proportional to p, much earlier than the p² scale suggested by keeping only the initial derivative. The rescaled response approaches a profile in n/p with amplitude p^(−1/2). A fixed-degree Taylor approximation therefore cannot be extrapolated to the relevant growing matrix sizes.

- [Reported analytic result] For that one-prime Jacobi scaling, with x=n/p, the limiting response profile is Phi(x)=4*sqrt(2/pi)*(sqrt(x)*cos(4x)−8*x^(3/2)*sin(4x)). Its derivation uses Meixner–Pollaczek/Jacobi matrix elements. This describes a specific growing-degree limit; it is not an independent prime-by-prime approximation to a complete Euler product.

- [Reported analytic result] The Jacobi response to a fixed difference frequency has an explicit oscillatory asymptotic whose amplitude grows like matrix degree to the power 3/2. A two-index scaling statement contains both this regime and the one-prime limit. Keeping both indices is necessary to distinguish a fixed-frequency limit from a prime-dependent frequency tending to zero.

- [Rejected superposition] Two prime perturbations in the Jacobi model do not add independently. For comparable primes, their cross terms dominate the separate effects and numerically match a response at the difference frequency log(p₂/p₁). The interaction between nearby prime frequencies is a leading-order effect, not a small error term.

- [Reported analytic decomposition] The two-mode Jacobi determinant response splits into a difference-frequency term, a sum-frequency term and an explicit finite-truncation defect. Bounds on the underlying matrix entries control that defect. At second order, a finite set of primes contributes only one-prime and two-prime terms; this classification provides a way to retain the leading interactions systematically.

- [Reported expansion; open estimate] The logarithm of the true finite Euler-product determinant has a second-order expansion with an explicit cubic remainder. The available estimate controls individual matrix entries and grows too quickly with matrix size. The missing step is a suitable operator-norm bound uniform in that size; the existence of the finite expansion does not supply it.

- [Interpretation] Assemble the finite Euler product before estimating the Jacobi determinant response, and organize its leading interactions by differences of prime logarithms. The tested independent one-prime layers and fixed-degree Euler-factor derivatives lose the dominant cross terms. This is a constraint on a viable approximation, not a completed infinite-prime limit.

## Signed divisor sums and prime-order energy budgets (CCM-FU)

The signed arithmetic coefficients here are C_d(M)=mu(d)*sum_{k=d}^{M−1}(sqrt(k+1)−sqrt(k))*D(floor(k/d)), where D(q)=log(q!)−sum_{n<=q}tau(n)+2*gamma*q, mu is the Möbius function, tau(n) counts positive divisors, and gamma is Euler's constant. Define the complete sum S(M)=sum_{d<=M}C_d(M) and its diagonal energy Q(M)=sum_{d<=M}C_d(M)². The open comparison S(M)²<=Q(M), together with the reported bound Q(M)<=722*M², would control the complete signed source uniformly after division by M. The experiment does not establish that all-cutoff comparison.

This construction starts from those signed arithmetic divisor coefficients. Processing primes merges groups of terms; the state records which primes have already been processed. Initially the energy is a sum of separate squares, called the diagonal budget. After complete merging the relevant quantity is the square of a signed sum. Intermediate energy and auxiliary charges depend on prime order. The main unresolved task is uniform control of the complete signed sum, rather than contraction of every intermediate group. “Head” denotes the distinguished merged group; “transverse” denotes the remaining groups.

- [Observation; reported diagonal bound] For the literal signed divisor source in CCM-FU, the square of the total signed sum stayed below the sum of individual squared contributions for every cutoff through 2,097,152. The diagonal energy has a derived update identity and bound Q(M)<=722*M². The missing arithmetic statement is that the complete off-diagonal contribution is nonpositive at every cutoff; the finite checks do not prove it.

- [Reported counterexamples] In prime-order regroupings of the signed divisor source, requiring energy to decrease at every step fails already at cutoff 5; requiring every ordering to be nonincreasing fails at cutoff 7. These statements are stronger than staying below the original diagonal budget: a temporary increase can still fit inside that budget. Reject stepwise contraction without discarding the weaker global ceiling question.

- [Observation] Prime-order paths keeping the divisor-source energy below its original diagonal budget were found by exhaustive search through cutoff 32, greedy search through 1024, fixed-late-prime policies through 32768, and joint-prime policies through 8192. These successes concern different finite search procedures and ranges. None gives a policy or energy bound valid for all cutoffs.

- [Observation] On complete processed-prime subset state spaces through cutoff 32, the tested low-energy successor, negative-support and two-step repair rules found admissible continuations. Tested final-overlap states through cutoff 1024 also extended. These are concrete candidate selection rules for the arithmetic source, not theorems for arbitrary signed coefficients or unbounded cutoffs.

- [Rejected selection rules] Uniformly decreasing a geometric certificate, averaging successor allowances, and requiring pointwise monotonicity for the prime-2 successor all fail in the divisor-source state model. Successful selection must use which prime is chosen and the signs of its interactions. A prime-independent averaged allowance loses information needed by the successful finite paths.

- [Reported obstruction and bound] At cutoff 25, the rule demanding both dissipation of all nonempty-group energy and admission by the proposed successor bound has a reachable trapped state. Releasing energy from the distinguished head group can pay the necessary growth elsewhere. A general sharp one-step bound allows the tracked energy to grow by a factor 3/2; demanding contraction excludes a necessary mechanism.

- [Reported obstruction] Keeping every nonempty group of divisor-source terms nonnegative fails from cutoff 21. At least one group must be allowed to carry a moving negative contribution. Derived triangle and mixed-group compensation identities describe how such a contribution can be balanced; universal positivity of each group is the wrong target.

- [Observation; rejected universal policy] A potential equal to remaining-group energy plus alpha times the squared signed head admitted complete decreasing paths through cutoff 32 for 0<=alpha approximately <=0.201. No single fixed alpha guarantees an acceptable next step from every state. Existence of a successful complete path is therefore weaker than a universal greedy rule based on this scalar potential.

- [Rejected termwise sign] Neither binary greatest-common-divisor packets nor individual exact-greatest-common-divisor groups of the source cross terms need be nonpositive. Favorable cancellation can occur between different greatest-common-divisor classes. Proving a sign for each class separately would impose a false requirement.

- [Rejected bound; surviving target] The total positive contribution from exact-greatest-common-divisor groups can exceed the diagonal energy. Yet the complete signed cross sum remained negative at all tested cutoffs. Writing total-sum squared as diagonal + positive cross mass − negative cross mass shows the useful target: bound the difference of the two cross masses, not the positive mass alone.

- [Observation] A flow along edges corresponding to one-prime changes compensated the positive greatest-common-divisor mass in the tested source graphs. The limiting issue is shared capacity: several positive groups may need the same negative neighbors. Balancing total Möbius parity classes ignores that access constraint; a uniform proof would need an appropriate neighborhood or transport-capacity estimate.

- [Rejected selection rules] Greedy rules based on averaged mass allowances, simultaneous steepest descent of coupled groups, and a fixed unit allowance for transverse energy fail as cutoffs grow. Small local variance or average cancellation does not prevent many signed terms from amplifying coherently. A successful rule must control that collective effect.

- [Reported obstruction and identity] Processing an additional retained prime can increase energy in the remaining groups even when the receiving coordinates are small. Helper groups that are inactive in the copied child commute with later transverse costs, so adjusting the head through those helpers cannot reverse the gain. The replacement target is a cumulative signed budget, not independent repairs of every step.

- [Reported obstruction] Processing middle primes in increasing order can exceed the original divisor-source diagonal budget even after the tested fixed scalar reserves are included. A derived nested amplification mechanism defeats every fixed quadratic ceiling in that class of reserve choices. Adaptive prime orders are not ruled out; the obstruction concerns the specified fixed-order and fixed-reserve mechanism.

- [Observation] Fixed small prime cores contain many transitions whose asymptotic energy change is positive, including transitions starting far below the original budget. Thus distance below the budget does not itself predict the next sign. Signed correlations under dilation are a more informative quantity to investigate than local scalar reserve alone.

- [Observation; reported optimization] Two primes chosen jointly can repair a state even when either first move increases energy. The corresponding continuous amplitude optimization has an exact classification, exposing an interval of useful middle/late amplitudes. It remains open whether an unused actual prime lies in the required interval at every stage; continuous feasibility does not establish arithmetic availability.

- [Observation] Coupled signed-head and prime-2 profiles admit tested positive combinations whose net unwanted contribution is zero. A combination indexed by 2,11,15 also cancels the inherited prime-2 debt. These examples motivate cancellation circuits, but neither uniform available capacity nor an exhaustion procedure covering the complete source has been proved.

- [Reported obstruction] Selecting source components independently with probability one half eventually makes the expected squared head exceed the original diagonal budget. The analytic explanation uses zeta-pole behavior. Independent random selection therefore cannot establish the proposed bound merely by averaging; any successful probabilistic selection needs additional dependence or arithmetic structure.

- [Reported analytic result] For the literal divisor source, the unsigned comparison channel has the required orientation and a square-root lower bound independently of the finite sign scans. This provides one controlled part of the construction. It does not establish the unresolved sign of the complete signed off-diagonal energy.

- [Rejected positivity shortcut; analytic survivor] Ordinary decreasing weights, positivity at every prefix and positivity of separate geometric/Laplace components do not prove the signed divisor-source bound. ## Complete reciprocal divisor covariance (EDQ)

These experiments retain interactions between different divisor contributions to the arithmetic source, rather than only their individual squared magnitudes. Each row has an explicit initial part and a corrected remainder; their signs can cancel. The reciprocal flux also retains an infinite time tail and a mean correction. A bound “subpower in N” means O(N^epsilon) for every positive epsilon, with the allowed constant depending on epsilon.

- [Observation] The complete reciprocal divisor flux R(N), with both directed divisor interactions, the mean correction and the infinite time tail retained, was positive at every tested cutoff 2<=N<=512; R(1)=0. Positivity alone would still not give the subpower upper bound required by this route, meaning growth slower than N^epsilon for every epsilon>0. Both the all-cutoff sign and the required growth control remain open.

- [Rejected comparison] The absolute variation across rows of the reciprocal divisor covariance is not always bounded by four times its signed flux. Replacing a row by its Bernoulli mean can miss almost all that variation. The complete finite initial part, nonzero Fourier modes and exterior Möbius signs must remain in the quantity being estimated.

- [Observation] After grouping complete reciprocal-divisor covariance terms by their exact greatest common divisor, the tested total variation stayed below four times the diagonal comparison quantity for both raw and tapered sources. Individual groups take both signs. The observation supports investigating a bound on the complete grouping; it does not prove the constant four uniformly.

- [Rejected decomposition] Estimating the initial polynomial part of a divisor-covariance row and its corrected remainder separately can spend more than the allowed budget even when their signed sum fits. Preserve each complete row's signed initial-part/remainder cancellation before taking absolute values. Separate estimates can manufacture an obstruction absent from the original quantity.

- [Rejected locality] In the reciprocal-divisor covariance, restricting to divisor ratios at most four, to reduced denominators two and three, or to the proposed finite time radius discards essential terms. The omitted long-range contribution can exceed the complete energy and restore its sign. A local model with those truncations cannot identify the complete covariance.

- [Reported analytic bound] The part of the reciprocal-divisor initial sum with reduced denominator at most B is uniformly bounded by B/2. This isolates a concrete missing term: the sum over larger reduced denominators. The finite-denominator bound does not control that complete tail or the final source energy.

- [Observation; failed rate target] Averaging over logarithmically spaced cutoffs reduces the tested error outside the support, but not at the strong rate fixed before the experiment. Existing bounds on omitted tails are also too large to justify full transfer. Averaging has a measured finite benefit, but the proposed quantitative estimate is not supported.

- [Rejected cutoff selector] Choosing the cutoff that minimizes the harmonic initial part does not suppress its complementary tail; in the tested examples roughly half or more of that tail is already in its first block. The exact first-tail expression contains both the harmonic partial sum and the difference of Mertens sums. Minimizing only the harmonic term ignores a leading arithmetic contribution.

- [Rejected surrogate objective] Minimizing the all-greatest-common-divisor initial polynomial does not reliably minimize the complete residue energy. The linear cross term with the unit source can change which finite cutoff is best. A derived subpower bound for the corrected remainder does not automatically control this full energy or its tail.

## Arithmetic approximation and path-dependent tensor responses

The approximation experiments vary finite arithmetic coefficients or average Möbius cutoffs and measure the error against the specified heat/recovery target. The chord experiments are a separate exact model: for ordered endpoints, a chord contributes the sum of path increments between its ends, and a chord pairing contributes the product of those sums. The path budget is the sum of absolute increments. Signed increments permit reversals; nonnegative increments give a monotone path.

- [Observation] Bounded changes to arithmetic coefficients can reduce finite heat-approximation errors by many orders of magnitude. Adjusting nonsquarefree coefficients and coordinating the initial and tail portions both help; nearly optimal finite errors remain possible even when the first quarter of coefficients is fixed to Möbius values. These are finite approximations, not constructions with proved error tending to zero as the fixed prefix grows.

- [Observation; reported necessary condition] Positive averages of truncated Möbius sums can give small finite approximation errors while preserving coefficient signs. For near-identity power damping with exponent epsilon_N at cutoff N, the successful asymptotic mechanism cannot have epsilon_N*log(N) tending to zero: averaging weight must move sufficiently far from the terminal cutoff. Finite small errors alone do not meet that necessary escape condition.

- [Observation] Fixing a growing initial segment of Möbius coefficients increases error when the remaining positive averaging weights must be monotone. Allowing a nonmonotone envelope while preserving coefficient signs removes much of the measured cost. The unresolved issue is convergence of the actual approximation error as the fixed segment grows; relaxed finite fitting is not that convergence proof.

- [Observation] Imposing more exact boundary moments does not monotonically improve the tested arithmetic approximation. Much of the error lies outside the neighborhood described by the matched Taylor derivatives. Matching local derivatives and a harmonic charge therefore does not control the global residual; the missing observable must measure the region where the error remains.

- [Numerical obstruction candidate] Define recovery gaps delta_(m−1) by minimizing max W(r)^m*abs(R(r)) on 0<=r<=1/4 over polynomials R of degree at most m whose coefficient of r^(m−1) equals one, with W(r)=1−sqrt(1−4*r). For gamma_k=(delta_k/delta_0)^2, a canonical degree-17 squared-polynomial functional is numerically negative, stable under independent 140/230-digit computations. All 2080 tested Hausdorff finite differences nevertheless pass. Thus those finite differences do not establish a positive representing measure; an exact negative-square proof remains open. This does not refute the weaker log-convexity question delta_(k+1)^2<=delta_k*delta_(k+2).

- [Paper result; not formalized] For those weighted-polynomial recovery gaps, the associated recovery operator is the weighted shift constructed from consecutive recovery costs. A paper argument proves uniform block growth and identifies its spectrum as the closed disc of radius rho≈0.01736004210, the exponential decay rate of delta_k. The argument does not assume that the squared gaps form a positive moment sequence. This operator result survives the numerical obstruction to that stronger moment interpretation; the paper dependencies and operator result have not been fully formalized.

- [Reported exact counterexample and optimum] For products of chord increments along a path with total variation one, the sum of responses normalized by individual chord capacities can exceed one starting at three chords. With D the three adjacent disjoint chords and R the three nested chords on six ordered endpoints, the combination 27D−R has maximum absolute response 1 for monotone paths but 28/27 when reversals are allowed. Thus path reversal has a genuine advantage that monotone-only optimization misses. The related positive combination 27D+R also has an exactly derived optimum.

- [Reported finite-degree result; open extension] In the cubic chord-response class, one level of the reversal-enclosure construction suffices exactly. Finite searches found no counterexample to analogous shallow stabilization at higher degree. That extension remains conjectural; a proved cubic closure cannot be assumed for arbitrary numbers of chords.

## Operator identifications and the physical BM/HB source

HB is the physical-action-to-extinction route under investigation; these entries concern some of its BM source and operator ingredients. Identification asks whether the complete causal action, with its actual fields, reader and boundary contributions, equals the diagnostic used by the RH argument. Trace norm is the sum of singular values; operator norm is only the largest singular value.

- [Reported obstruction] In the bounded finite-packet operator construction, finite defects either remain nonzero or vanish coherently; a limit in trace norm of zero defects remains zero. Thus this bounded-limit mechanism cannot create the desired nonzero index charge. A singular limiting construction remains a different possibility, but it cannot automatically inherit the bounded construction's Hilbert–Schmidt or trace-norm estimates.

- [Reported obstruction] Subtracting exactly the permitted Gamma contribution leaves a positive tail and index zero. Subtracting more creates a negative direction only by changing the source; it is not a valid counterexample to the original source law. The tested relocation of a distant saddle also has zero trace and no sensitivity to horizontal zero displacement, so it cannot provide the missing selector.

- [Reported analytic result; missing lift] The complete signed scalar combination of prime, pole and Gamma contributions is strictly below one in its prescribed normalization. This scalar cancellation is not a bound on an operator's trace norm and does not preserve an index-one trace automatically. The unresolved task is an operator construction that retains the actual coefficients and satisfies the required trace-norm budget.

- [Reported analytic obstruction] A relative theta metric can have positive curvature with trace one and operator norm 1/3. Because the curvature is positive, its trace norm is still one. - [Observation; missing identification] A positive cyclic exponential-Hankel model has a large cubic positivity margin, mostly supplied by mixed cyclic terms.
