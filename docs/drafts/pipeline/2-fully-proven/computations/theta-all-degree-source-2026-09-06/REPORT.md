# Theta atoms after the logarithmic-moment transform

**Exploratory numerical results, not proof certificates.** These calculations use the actual positive-half-line theta density and no zeta-zero tables. No computed value is used in Lean.

## Question and outcome

Can a fixed, dominant theta-shell prefix supply positivity of every logarithmic Hankel form, with the remaining shells treated as a small perturbation?

The first atom fails at degree **3**. The first two atoms pass degrees 1–11 and fail at degree **12**. Adding the third atom restores the tested degree-12 block. Six-atom blocks are positive through the tested degree 12. This last observation is neither positivity of the infinite source nor an arbitrary-degree result.

| Shell cutoff | Degree | Smallest eigenvalue of scaled Hankel matrix | Monic Schur value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 3 | −4.678667293227019e−5 | −1.548983875995855e−3 |
| 2 | 12 | −2.163911251826798e−18 | −8.741364044713950e−15 |
| 3 | 12 | 6.277005214926623e−19 | 3.327604476874476e−15 |

The third atom is therefore decisive in this test despite its small raw mass. Fixed entrywise relative accuracy in raw moments does not by itself preserve the signs of the small eigenvalues after taking logarithmic derivatives.

## Exact mathematical inputs to the numerical calculation

For shell index `n≥1`, set

`rho_n(x) = n² exp(5x/2 − pi*n²*exp(2x))`.

The Ward representation for the raw moments divided by their common factor `2*pi` is

`W_0,n = 2*n²*exp(−pi*n²) − integral_0^infinity rho_n(x) dx`,

`W_j,n = integral_0^infinity (4*j*x^(2*j−1) − x^(2*j))*rho_n(x) dx`, for `j>0`.

For a chosen shell cutoff, sum these moments over the shells. Write

`c_j = (−1)^j W_j / (W_0*(2*j)!)`.

The logarithmic moments are generated by

`M_k = −2*(k+1)*c_(k+1) − sum_(j=1)^k c_j*M_(k−j)`.

For conditioning, set `r=M_1/M_0` and `s_k=M_k/(M_0*r^k)`. All reported cases have positive `M_0,r`. This scaling is a positive diagonal congruence, so it preserves Hankel inertia. The tested matrix is `H_ij=s_(i+j)`, `0≤i,j≤d`. A monic Schur polynomial is `q=(-H_head^(-1)*column,1)`; the reported value is `q^T H q`.

For the first-atom degree-3 failure, its coefficients in the scaled coordinate are approximately

`q = [-0.30490030719244244, 3.6945150403669984, -4.287399710923789, 1]`.

Full-precision raw moments and witnesses are retained in the JSON files.

## Independent checks and reproducibility

All quadrature and matrix calculations ran on the authorized Runpod, using 16 independent processes and one native linear-algebra thread per process.

1. `atom_hankel.py --degree 6 --precision 90 --atoms 8`: Ward moments integrated in `x`, cutoff `x=6`. Output: `atom-hankel-results.json`.
2. `direct_density.py`: direct positive source-density moments, 160 decimal digits, degree 12, six shells. It changes both the integrand and coordinate: with `y=exp(2x)`, the integrand is `(log(y)/2)^(2j) * n²*y^(1/4)*(2*pi*n²*y−3)*exp(−pi*n²*y)`. Cutoff `y=1000`. Output: `direct-density-results.json`.
3. `atom_hankel.py --degree 12 --precision 220 --atoms 3 --output ward-high-precision-results.json`: independent Ward-form verification of the degree-12 failure. The displayed 45-digit eigenvalues and Schur values agree with the direct-density calculation for cutoffs 1, 2, and 3.

Both coordinate cutoffs are numerical truncations. Agreement between independent formulas and precisions is strong exploratory evidence, not an interval enclosure or a mathematical proof of any eigenvalue sign. In particular, no fixed-shell result is silently transferred to the infinite theta sum.

## Consequences and formal status

The first-atom all-degree positivity conjecture is numerically contradicted, and the two-atom replacement has the same defect at a later degree. An induction using a positive fixed prefix as its all-degree foundation is unsupported. A viable construction must retain the missing tail or control cutoff jointly with degree, including the conditioning of the nonlinear moment transform.

The associated exact analytic result is proved separately in `ThetaFiniteShellFourierBoundary`: for every finite nonempty prefix `P_N`, `t² C_(P_N)(t) → −P_N'(0)<0`, while `t² C_(Phi−P_N)(t) → P_N'(0)>0`. It uses no numerical input. This explains an exact source cancellation lost by truncation, but a negative cosine transform is not itself a negative Hankel witness. The computed Hankel failures and the universal Hankel producer are not formalized by that theorem.

The full-source arbitrary-degree inequality remains open. Further finite positive blocks cannot establish its universal quantifier.
