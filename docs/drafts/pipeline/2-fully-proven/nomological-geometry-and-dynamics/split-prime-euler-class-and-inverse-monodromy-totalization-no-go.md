## 1. Split-prime theorem

Let

\[
 G=\mathbb Q_{>0}^{\times},\qquad
 C_p=\langle p\rangle,\qquad
 G^{(p)}=\{q\in G:v_p(q)=0\}
\tag{1}
\]

for a fixed ordinary prime `p`.

### Theorem 1.1 — fixed-prime scale extension is split

Unique factorization gives canonical isomorphisms

\[
 G\simeq\bigoplus_{\ell\ {m prime}}\mathbb Z,
 \qquad
 G=C_p\times G^{(p)},
 \qquad
 G/C_p\simeq G^{(p)}.
\tag{2}
\]

Consequently the central extension

\[
 1\longrightarrow C_p\simeq\mathbb Z
 \longrightarrow G
 \longrightarrow G/C_p
 \longrightarrow1
\tag{3}
\]

has a canonical group-homomorphic section. Its extension/Euler class is zero:

\[
 e_p=0\in H^2(G/C_p;\mathbb Z),
\tag{4}
\]

where the coefficient action is trivial. For every homomorphism of trivial coefficient modules
`Z -> M`, the pushed-forward class in `H^2(G/C_p;M)` is also zero.

#### Proof

Every `q in G` has a unique finite factorization

\[
 q=\prod_\ell \ell^{v_\ell(q)}.
\]

The valuation vector is therefore an isomorphism from `G` to the direct sum in `(2)`. Splitting
off its `p` coordinate gives `G=C_p times G^(p)`. Every coset modulo `C_p` has a unique
representative of `p`-valuation zero, so the inverse of `G^(p) -> G/C_p` is a homomorphic section
of `(3)`. Split central extensions represent the zero class in `H^2`. Functoriality of group
cohomology sends zero to zero under every coefficient homomorphism. QED.

The same proof works for any finite set of prime-coordinate factors. It is algebraic and also
applies to continuous group cohomology when the groups carry the discrete topology.

### Corollary 1.2 — fixed-prime Euler transgression cannot be the selector

Suppose a crossed-product cyclic-homology construction identifies its periodicity/transgression
operator for the infinite-order element `p` with cap product by the Euler class of `(3)`. Then that
operator is zero for the literal positive-rational scale group. It cannot equal a nonzero
prime-period, relation-kernel, Kummer, or Bockstein calibrator.

#### Proof

Cap product by `e_p` is zero by `(4)`. The corpus prime calibrators named in the conclusion have
already-proved nonzero value on the trace-one ordinary-prime class, so they cannot be this zero
operator. QED.

Ponge's crossed-product theorem is an exact instance: for an abelian group `Gamma=G` and
infinite-order `phi=p`, one has `Gamma_phi=G` and
`bar Gamma_phi=G/<p>`. Under the theorem's additional good-action hypotheses, the periodicity
operator is described by the Euler class of exactly `(3)`, hence vanishes here.

## 2. Inverse-monodromy totalization theorem

A parachain complex has operators satisfying

\[
 b^2=B^2=0,
 \qquad bB+Bb=1-T,
\tag{5}
\]

and its para-`S` differential has

\[
 d^2=(1-T)S.
\tag{6}
\]

A cylindrical two-direction object requires horizontal and vertical monodromies with

\[
 T_hT_v=1,
\tag{7}
\]

so the total object becomes an honest mixed complex.

### Theorem 2.1 — total cyclicity does not select trivial factors

Condition `(7)` does not imply `T_h=1` or `T_v=1`. For every unit `lambda` in the coefficient
field,

\[
 T_h=\lambda\,\mathrm{id},
 \qquad
 T_v=\lambda^{-1}\,\mathrm{id}
\tag{8}
\]

satisfies `(7)`. Therefore a construction that pairs the physical mate gain with its inverse can
produce an honest total mixed complex for every nontrivial gain. It cancels monodromy by design;
it does not prove the individual monodromy is one.

#### Proof

The product in `(8)` is the identity for every nonzero `lambda`. If `lambda != 1`, neither factor
is the identity although the total cylindrical condition holds. Thus total cyclicity cannot imply
factorwise cyclicity. QED.

At a Riemann divisor point one may set `lambda=delta_n(rho)`. The model then totalizes equally
well for `Re(rho)=1/2` and `Re(rho) != 1/2`. It cannot prove RH.

Getzler's normalization criterion is compatible with this boundary: a paracyclic module becomes
cyclic only when the cyclic projection acts as the identity on the normalized complex.
Normalization does not manufacture this equality.
