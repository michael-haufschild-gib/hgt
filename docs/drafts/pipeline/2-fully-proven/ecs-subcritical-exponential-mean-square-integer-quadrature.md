# Exponential mean square of the integer quadrature error

For $0\le\lambda<1/2$, the critical-state integer quadrature error $e^{\lambda t}(N(t)-Ae^{t/2})$ belongs to $L^2(\mathbb R)$.

Set $s=1/2-\lambda+i\xi$, $\Psi_\lambda(u)=e^{\lambda u}\Phi(u)$ and $H_\lambda(v)=e^{(\lambda-1/2)v}(\lfloor e^v\rfloor-e^v)$. The function $H_\lambda$ is integrable at both ends. Its Fourier transform is the fractional-part Mellin integral $\zeta(s)/s$. As distributions the weighted lattice-minus-continuum measure is $(\partial_v+1/2-\lambda)H_\lambda$. The resulting Parseval identity is
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}e^{2\lambda t}|N(t)-Ae^{t/2}|^2dt
 =\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}|\zeta(1/2-\lambda+i\xi)\,\mathcal L\Phi(\lambda-i\xi)|^2d\xi .
\]
The tilted state has $\|\Psi_\lambda-\Psi_\lambda(\cdot+h)\|_2^2=O(h/\log(1/h))$ by the wall law, boundedness of the weight on the negative ray and an $O(h)$ weight increment in norm. A dyadic Fourier annulus at height $Y$ then has energy $O(1/(Y\log Y))$, by testing the increment at $h=1/Y$ in angular frequency, where $|1-e^{ih\xi}|$ has a fixed positive lower bound on that annulus. Hence every squared Fourier weight $(1+|\xi|)^{2r}$ is integrable for $r<1/2$. Zeta convexity gives growth exponent $1/4+\lambda/2+\varepsilon<1/2$. The physical primitive removes the need to differentiate $\Phi$. Write $D=N-Ae^{t/2}$. Fubini gives
\[
 B(t)=e^{(\lambda-1/2)t}\int_{-\infty}^te^{u/2}D(u)du=(H_\lambda*\Psi_\lambda)(t).
\]
Indeed, a substitution in each integer atom leaves the number $\lfloor e^{t-v}\rfloor$ of contributing indices. Exponential confinement dominates the absolute integrals. Thus $B$ is continuously differentiable and $B'+(1/2-\lambda)B=e^{\lambda t}D$. Both convolution factors are integrable and $B\in L^2$. The zeta multiplier estimate gives $\xi\widehat B\in L^2$. Plancherel bounds the $L^2$ norms of the difference quotients of $B$ uniformly, since $|e^{ih\xi}-1|\le |h\xi|$. Fatou at $h\downarrow0$ then puts $B'$ in $L^2$. For fixed $\lambda<1/2$ take a second tilt $\lambda<\mu<1/2$. Cauchy–Schwarz against $e^{-(\mu-\lambda)t}$ on $t>0$ now puts $e^{\lambda t}D$ in $L^1$ there. On $t<0$ the confined atom sum gives $N(t)=O(e^{2t})$, so the negative ray is absolutely integrable as well. The differential identity gives $B'\in L^1$. Classical Fourier integration by parts then gives $\widehat {e^{\lambda t}D}=\zeta(s)\widehat{\Psi_\lambda}$ pointwise, and Plancherel gives the stated equality. **Growing sieve and actual variance.** For a finite prime set $P$, the translated error recursion costs at most $\prod_{p\in P}(1+p^{\lambda-1/2})/(1-p^{-1})\le 4^{|P|}$ in weighted $L^2$. Hence the cost is $e^{o(T)}$ uniformly over every family with $|P|=o(T)$, at arbitrary prime sizes. This includes all subsets of the primes up to $T$ by Chebyshev's upper bound. Let $E$ be the ordinary-prime wave minus its polar term. For $d>\Theta-1/2$, $e^{-dt}E\in L^2(0,\infty)$: take the empty set of removed modes in the [ordinary-prime square-mean theorem](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExclusionOfTheCriticalState/Modes/UnheatedZeroModesBeyondTheWall.lean), using $-\Re z_c\le\Theta-1/2$ for every centered zero. Write $E_P$ for the ordinary-prime wave with $P$ removed, minus the normalized integer reference sieved by the same $P$, and $G=E-E_P$. Each deleted prime $p$ contributes a translate of $e^{\lambda u}\Phi(u)$ with coefficient $(\log p)p^{\lambda-1/2}=O_\lambda(1)$. Consequently $\|e^{\lambda t}G\|_2\ll_\lambda 4^{|P|}+|P|$. For $0\le r<1-\Theta$ choose $\Theta-1/2<d<\lambda-r$ and $\lambda<1/2$. Cauchy–Schwarz on the positive ray gives
\[
 \int_0^\infty e^{rt}|E_P^2-E^2|
 \le 2\|e^{-dt}E\|_2\|e^{\lambda t}G\|_2+\|e^{\lambda t}G\|_2^2
 \ll_r 16^{|P|}.
\]
Thus the cumulative absolute variance error on $[U,\infty)$ is $O_r(16^{|P|}e^{-rU})$. For every family of $o(T)$ removed primes of arbitrary size, and $\eta>0$, it vanishes exponentially even on the entire ray $[\eta T,\infty)$. In particular the unsieved integer-centered and polar-centered squared prime waves differ by an exponentially integrable function. The positive width follows from the [unconditional zero-abscissa bound](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExclusionOfTheCriticalState/Decay/TheLeftTraceUpToTheRateOfTheZeros.lean).

For the larger sieve $p\le T^2$, choose $\lambda=0$. Its $L^2$ multiplier is at most the same $\prod_{p\in P}(1-p^{-1/2})^{-1}=e^{o(T)}$ as in the [quadratic-horizon sieve](../3-formalized/ecs-growing-sieve-for-integer-centering.md). The reverse triangle inequality therefore bounds the difference of the two **square roots of the integrated variances** by $e^{o(T)}$. On every scale where either $L^2(0,T)$ norm exceeds $e^{\delta T}$ for a fixed $\delta>0$, the ratio of the squared integrals tends to one, uniformly in the sieve. Deleting the true primes in this larger set contributes only $O(T^2)$ to the $L^2$ norm by the single-prime estimate.
