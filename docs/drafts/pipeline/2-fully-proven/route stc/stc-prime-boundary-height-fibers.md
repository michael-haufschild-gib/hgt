## Source and theorem

Let H be complex ell² over primes. For a,v>0 and t real, define the literal depth-zero source

    x_(a,t,v)(p) = v (log p) p^(-(1+a v)/2) exp(i t log p).

With the inner product linear in its first argument and c=(a+b)/2, the exact Gram entry is

    <x_(a,t,v),x_(b,s,v)>
      = v² sum_p (log p)² p^(-1-cv) exp(i(t-s)log p).          (1)

**Spectral-fibre boundary theorem.** For fixed a,b>0 and fixed t,s,

    lim_(v→0+) <x_(a,t,v),x_(b,s,v)>
      = 4/(a+b)² if t=s, and 0 if t≠s.                       (2)

If t(v)-s(v)=κv, or more generally (t(v)-s(v))/v→κ, the limit is

    1/(c-iκ)².                                               (3)

Distinct fixed heights separate; height differences of order v stay coherent. For each v>0 the height dependence is continuous in H, but the limiting Gram is discontinuous at t=s. Boundary and height-coalescence limits do not commute.

The pre-Hilbert space generated by formal height symbols with Gram (2) completes to

    direct_sum_(t∈ℝ) L²((0,∞), r dr),                          (4)

where (a,t) maps to exp(-a r/2) in the t summand. This boundary is nonseparable. It cannot be realized as nonzero strong limits at every real height through asymptotically Gram-preserving maps into one fixed separable Hilbert space.

## Proof

For Re z>0 write F(z)=sum_p (log p)² p^(-1-z). It is holomorphic by absolute local uniform convergence. Twice differentiating the Euler product gives

    F(z) = (log ζ(1+z))'' - E(1+z),
    E(w) = sum_p sum_(m≥2) m(log p)² p^(-mw).                 (5)

The prime-power series E converges locally normally on Re w>1/2, including all local derivatives: on compact subsets its terms are dominated by convergent sums of m^j(log p)^(j+2)p^(-mσ), σ>1/2. Near w=1 write ζ(w)=h(w)/(w-1), where h is holomorphic and h(1)=1. Thus

    F(z)=z^(-2)+O(1) as z→0 from Re z>0.                     (6)

At w=1-iω for fixed ω≠0, ζ has neither a pole nor a zero, by its unconditional zero-free line Re w=1. A local holomorphic log ζ exists there, so (5) remains bounded when z→-iω from the right. Substitute z=cv-i(t-s) into (1) to obtain (2), and z=v(c-iκ) into (6) to obtain (3). Continuity gives the varying-ratio version.

For (4), the same-height kernel in (2) is the Gram of exp(-a r/2) in L²(r dr). The exponential span is dense by Laplace-transform uniqueness, as proved in stc-euler-prime-boundary-tangent-space.md. Different heights are orthogonal. Uncountably many nonzero mutually orthogonal vectors make the completion nonseparable; no separable Hilbert space can realize those simultaneous Gram-preserving nonzero limits.

## Canonical singular reader and exact scope

On the dense domain of y∈H for which the sum converges absolutely, define the endpoint reader at height τ by

    Λ_τ(y) = sum_p (log p) p^(-1/2) exp(-iτ log p) y(p).

It is not closable. Put z_(a,t,v)=v x_(a,t,v). Then

    ||z_(a,t,v)||² = v⁴ F(a v) → 0,
    Λ_τ(z_(a,t,v)) = v² F(a v/2-i(t-τ))
      → 4/a² if t=τ, and 0 if t≠τ.                          (7)

For t=τ the vectors approach zero in H while their reader values approach 4/a². A fixed reader misses every distinct fixed height, while a phase-matched reader gives the same universal charge at every height. The singularity alone supplies no Xi-zero detector. It supplies a precise distributional boundary charge to couple to zero-specific data.

At fixed nonzero horizontal grade g, the normalized compact channel v² P_(b_(v+2|g|)) vanishes in operator norm, as shown in stc-euler-prime-boundary-tangent-space.md. Thus the one-level singular charge also erases the magnitude of fixed grade. A viable STC/RHist transport must keep finite-v height coherence and recover a grade-dependent finite part while establishing an Xi-zero-specific relation. Equations (2)–(7) do not exclude more singular, height-dependent, or finite-part transports and do not prove RH.
