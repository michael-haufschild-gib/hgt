## 1. Two-term law algebra

Let

\[
 V_{-1}\xrightarrow{\mu_1}V_0
\tag{ST.1}
\]

be a finite-dimensional real two-term `L_infinity` algebra. Its only possibly nonzero operations
are

\[
 \mu_1:V_{-1}\to V_0,
 \quad
 \mu_2:V_0\wedge V_0\to V_0,
 \quad
 \mu_2:V_0\otimes V_{-1}\to V_{-1},
 \quad
 \mu_3:\Lambda^3V_0\to V_{-1}.
\tag{ST.2}
\]

Put

\[
 K=\ker\mu_1,
 \qquad
 \overline{\mathfrak g}=V_0/\operatorname{im}\mu_1.
\tag{ST.3}
\]

### Lemma 1.1 — the homotopy kernel is an honest module

The operation `mu_2` induces a Lie algebra structure on `overline mathfrak g` and a representation

\[
 \overline\rho:\overline{\mathfrak g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(K).
\tag{ST.4}
\]

#### Proof

The Jacobiator of `mu_2` on `V_0` is `mu_1mu_3`, so it vanishes in the quotient by
`im mu_1`. Hence the quotient bracket is Lie. The compatibility identity

\[
 \mu_1\mu_2(w,k)=\mu_2(w,\mu_1k)
\]

shows that `V_0` preserves `K`. The identity with two degree-zero inputs and one `k in K` says that
their commutator action equals the action of their `mu_2` bracket; its `mu_3` correction contains
`mu_1k` and vanishes. Finally,

\[
 \mu_2(\mu_1v,k)=\mu_2(v,\mu_1k)=0,
\]

so `im mu_1` acts trivially. The action therefore descends to `(ST.4)`. QED.

## 2. Semistrict 2-connection

Let `M` be a connected smooth manifold. A local semistrict 2-connection is

\[
 A\in\Omega^1(M;V_0),
 \qquad
 B\in\Omega^2(M;V_{-1}).
\tag{ST.5}
\]

Define

\[
 \mathcal F
 =dA+\frac12\mu_2(A,A)-\mu_1(B),
\tag{ST.6}
\]

and

\[
 H_3
 =dB+\mu_2(A,B)-\frac1{3!}\mu_3(A,A,A).
\tag{ST.7}
\]

Assume fake flatness, `mathcal F=0`. On the trivial bundle `M times K`, define

\[
 \nabla^K=d+\overline\rho([A]).
\tag{ST.8}
\]

The same construction glues on a semistrict principal 2-bundle because its transition maps preserve
the two-term operations and their homology module.

## 3. Semistrict kernel-reduction theorem

### Theorem 3.1 — ternary curvature reduces to a closed flat-local-system form

The connection `nabla^K` is flat, and

\[
 \boxed{
 H_3\in\Omega^3(M;K),
 \qquad
 d_{\nabla^K}H_3=0.}
\tag{ST.9}
\]

Thus the genuine ternary bracket `mu_3` changes the formula for higher curvature but does not destroy
its canonical reduction to the flat homotopy-kernel local system.

#### Proof

By Lemma 1.1, `overline rho` is an honest representation. Its curvature is therefore

\[
 R_{\nabla^K}
 =\overline\rho\left(
 dA+\frac12\mu_2(A,A)
 \right).
\tag{ST.10}
\]

Fake flatness identifies the argument with `mu_1(B)`, whose action on `K` is zero by Lemma 1.1.
Hence `R_{nabla^K}=0`.

For the remaining assertions, form the total `L_infinity` algebra of differential forms and the
total degree-one field `alpha=A-B`. Its Maurer--Cartan curvature is

\[
 \operatorname{Curv}(\alpha)=\mathcal F-H_3
\tag{ST.11}
\]

with the sign convention `(ST.6)`--`(ST.7)`. Every uncurved `L_infinity` algebra satisfies the
formal Bianchi identity

\[
 \sum_{j\geq0}\frac1{j!}
 \widetilde\mu_{j+1}
 \left(\alpha^{\otimes j},\operatorname{Curv}(\alpha)\right)=0.
\tag{ST.12}
\]

Under fake flatness, `(ST.11)` is `-H_3`. Splitting `(ST.12)` by internal and form degree leaves
exactly

\[
 \mu_1(H_3)=0,
 \qquad
 dH_3+\mu_2(A,H_3)=0.
\tag{ST.13}
\]

Terms `mu_2(B,H_3)` have internal degree `-2` and vanish in a two-term algebra; terms involving
`mu_3` and `H_3` also vanish because `mu_3` accepts only degree-zero inputs. Equations `(ST.13)` say
precisely that `H_3` is `K`-valued and `d_{nabla^K}`-closed. QED.

## 4. Higher-period Ambrose--Singer consequence

Fix `x in M`. Define

\[
 W_x(H_3)=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}
 \left\{
 \tau_\gamma^{-1}H_{3,y}(u,v,w):\gamma:x\to y
 \right\}\subseteq K_x.
\tag{ST.14}
\]

Let `P_x(H_3)` be the additive group generated by all based integrals over smooth 3-simplices, and
let `mathfrak p_x(H_3)` be the Lie algebra of the identity component of its closure in `K_x`.

### Corollary 4.1 — transported ternary curvature generates the lifted period algebra

\[
 \boxed{\mathfrak p_x(H_3)=W_x(H_3).}
\tag{ST.15}
\]

If `M`, `A`, and `B` are real analytic, then

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak p_x(H_3)
 =\operatorname{span}\{(\nabla^K)^jH_3|_x:j\geq0\}.}
\tag{ST.16}
\]

#### Proof

Theorem 3.1 places `H_3` in a finite-dimensional flat local system. Every simplex integral is a
Riemann integral of transported pointwise values, giving `mathfrak p_x subseteq W_x`. Integrals over
small 3-parallelotopes satisfy

\[
 \int_{\sigma_s}^{x}H_3
 =s^3\tau_\gamma^{-1}H_{3,y}(u,v,w)+o(s^3),
\]

so every generator of `W_x` is tangent to the identity component of the period closure. This proves
`(ST.15)`. In the analytic case, flatly trivialize `K` on the universal cover. Modulo the span of all
jets at a lift of `x`, every Taylor coefficient vanishes; analytic continuation then forces every
transported value into that jet span, proving `(ST.16)`. QED.

## 5. Exact advance and remaining boundary

The strict crossed-module theorem is not the end of kernel reduction. The mechanism survives a
nonzero Jacobiator `mu_3` because the complete `L_infinity` Bianchi identity, not an ordinary Lie
Bianchi identity, is the correct generator of the coefficient equations.

What remains open is no longer the semistrict Lie 2 sector. The first unproved field case requires a
complex of length at least three, where:

1. several higher homology degrees coexist;
2. curvature can transfer between them through nonzero higher operations;
3. the fixed-degree kernel is not the whole nonlinear holonomy carrier;
4. higher Stokes subdivision must recover the coupled multigraded tangent algebra.
