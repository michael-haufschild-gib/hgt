## 1. Observer fibrations

Let

\[
 p:X\longrightarrow Y
\tag{OHP.1}
\]

be a pointed Kan fibration, or a Serre fibration of sufficiently well-pointed spaces. Interpret
`X` as a space of sourced laws and histories, `Y` as an observer space, and let `x in X` map to
`y in Y`. Let

\[
 F=p^{-1}(y)
\tag{OHP.2}
\]

be the pointed homotopy fibre.

The standard long exact homotopy sequence contains

\[
 \pi_1(X,x)\longrightarrow\pi_1(Y,y)
 \xrightarrow{\partial}\pi_0(F)
 \longrightarrow\pi_0(X).
\tag{OHP.3}
\]

## 2. Closed observer, open lift

### Theorem 2.1 — holonomy boundary criterion

Let `gamma` be a loop in `Y` based at `y`, and lift it from `x` to a path `tilde gamma` in `X`.
Then the endpoint `tilde gamma(1)` lies in `F`, and

\[
 \boxed{
 \partial[\gamma]=[\widetilde\gamma(1)]\in\pi_0(F).}
\tag{OHP.4}
\]

The lifted path can be closed by a path inside the fibre if and only if `partial[gamma]` is the
base component of `F`. Thus a closed observer loop has a genuinely open law lift exactly when its
connecting class is nontrivial.

### Proof

This is the defining path-lifting construction of the connecting map in the long exact sequence of
a fibration. Exactness says that the loop lies in the image of `pi_1(X)` precisely when its
endpoint component in the fibre is trivial. `[PROVED HERE]`

### Corollary 2.2 — principal holonomy form

If `p:X->Y` is a principal `G`-bundle with a specified path-lifting rule, there is a unique
`h_gamma in G` satisfying

\[
 \widetilde\gamma(1)=x\cdot h_\gamma.
\tag{OHP.5}
\]

For the `n`-fold concatenation of the same loop,

\[
 h_{\gamma^n}=h_\gamma^n.
\tag{OHP.6}
\]

Consequently the observer can return after every loop while the marked source state advances
through the powers of one vertical holonomy element.

No curvature hypothesis is used. Flat bundles can have nontrivial holonomy.

## 3. Cost is additional constitutive data

Let `c:G->[0,infinity]` be a marked vertical endpoint cost with `c(1)=0`. Assume

\[
 c(h_\gamma^n)\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{OHP.7}
\]

This holds, for example, when the cyclic subgroup generated by `h_gamma` is discrete, `c` is proper
on it, and `h_gamma` has infinite order.

### Theorem 3.1 — recurrence with divergent lifted cost

Under `(OHP.7)`, the repeated observer loop is exactly recurrent in `Y`, while the endpoint cost of
its lifted histories diverges:

\[
 \boxed{p(\widetilde{\gamma^n}(1))=y,
 \qquad c(h_{\gamma^n})\to\infty.}
\tag{OHP.8}
\]

### Proof

Each concatenation is a loop in `Y`. Equation `(OHP.6)` identifies its lifted endpoint, and
`(OHP.7)` gives divergence. `[PROVED HERE]`

The cost conclusion is not contained in recurrence or topology. It requires the independently
declared constitutive cost `c`.

## 4. Prolongation-rank horizon

Let `V_n` be finite-dimensional source-law tangent spaces at prolongation order `n`, and let

\[
 d_n:V_n\longrightarrow W_n
\tag{OHP.9}
\]

be the tangent maps of an observer family. Suppose

\[
 \sup_n\dim W_n\leq d<\infty.
\tag{OHP.10}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 — finite observer horizon

If `dim V_n>d`, then `d_n` has nonzero kernel. In particular, if

\[
 \dim V_n\longrightarrow\infty,
\tag{OHP.11}
\]

no fixed finite-rank observer family can be infinitesimally conservative at every prolongation
order.

For finitely many observers `d_(i,n):V_n->W_(i,n)` with

\[
 \sup_n\sum_i\dim W_{i,n}\leq d,
\tag{OHP.12}
\]

their product map also has nonzero kernel whenever `dim V_n>d`.

### Proof

Rank-nullity gives

\[
 \dim\ker d_n\geq\dim V_n-\dim W_n.
\]

Apply the same argument to the product map into `direct-sum_i W_(i,n)`. `[PROVED HERE]`

### Corollary 4.2 — exact HT scope

Whenever a specified differential-algebraic source has a proved unbounded Kolchin/prolongation
rank sequence `dim V_n`, Theorem 4.1 forces loss in every specified fixed finite-rank observer.

The converse is false: observer loss does not imply hypertranscendence, and unbounded prolongation
rank does not imply noncompact observed dynamics.

## 5. Formal-law specialization

Let `mathcal L` and `mathcal O` be formal moduli problems and let

\[
 q:\mathcal L\longrightarrow\mathcal O
\tag{OHP.13}
\]

be an observer morphism. Replace it by a fibration without changing its homotopy fibre. Then:

1. Theorem 2.1 measures observer-loop holonomy by the component boundary in the formal law fibre.
2. Theorem 4.1 measures infinitesimal observer horizons using the tangent complexes of the
   prolongation tower.
3. A complete theory must retain both. Local tangent curvature does not determine global
   holonomy, and global recurrence does not determine tangent conservativity.

This gives Nomological Homotopy Geometry two independent but compatible invariants:

\[
 \boxed{
 \text{local prolongation kernel}
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \text{global holonomy boundary}.}
\tag{OHP.14}
\]

## 6. Hostile countermodels

1. **Recurrence need not open the lift.** For `p=id_Y`, every observer loop lifts closed and the
   connecting map is zero.
2. **Open lift need not have growing cost.** A nontrivial finite holonomy element has bounded
   powers. Divergence requires `(OHP.7)`.
3. **Flatness does not kill holonomy.** A flat bundle associated to a nontrivial representation of
   `pi_1(Y)` has zero local curvature and nontrivial boundary holonomy.
4. **HT does not force observed noncompactness.** An unbounded sequence `V_n` can map to the zero
   point observer, whose orbit is compact.
5. **Noncompactness does not imply HT.** An infinite-dimensional topological vector space can be
   noncompact while carrying no differential source or prolongation tower.
6. **Finite rank loss need not be permanent under parallel refinement.** Adding observers with
   genuinely new tangent coordinates can make the product injective; serial observers cannot
   repair an already-lost direction.
7. **Holonomy is not curvature by definition.** Local minimal curvature and global connecting
   holonomy can vary independently.

## 7. Field consequence

The theorem replaces two false universal slogans by exact statements:

\[
 \boxed{
 \text{closed observer + open lift}
 \iff
 \text{nonzero homotopy-fibre boundary},}
\tag{OHP.15}
\]

and

\[
 \boxed{
 \text{unbounded source prolongation rank}
 \Longrightarrow
 \text{finite-observer tangent horizon}.}
\tag{OHP.16}
\]

Neither statement identifies hypertranscendence with noncompactness or recurrence with complexity.
Those physical or analytic identifications require a source realization and constitutive cost.
