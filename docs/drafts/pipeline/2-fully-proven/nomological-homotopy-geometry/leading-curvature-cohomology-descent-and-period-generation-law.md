## 1. Total law connection

Let `L=direct_sum_{j<=0}L_j` be a finite-dimensional nilpotent `L_infinity` algebra over
`mathbb R`, with operations `ell_n` and unary differential `ell_1`. Let `M` be a connected smooth
manifold. A total degree-one law connection is

\[
 a=a_1+a_2+a_3+\cdots,
 \qquad
 a_p\in\Omega^p(M;L_{1-p}).
\tag{LC.1}
\]

Extend the operations to `Omega(M) tensor L` with Koszul signs. Its total curvature is

\[
 \operatorname{Curv}(a)
 =\sum_{n\geq1}\frac1{n!}\widetilde\ell_n(a,\ldots,a)
 =\sum_{q\geq2}F_q,
\tag{LC.2}
\]

where

\[
 F_q\in\Omega^q(M;L_{2-q}).
\tag{LC.3}
\]

The `L_infinity` identities give the universal Bianchi equation

\[
 \sum_{n\geq0}\frac1{n!}
 \widetilde\ell_{n+1}
 \left(a^{\otimes n},\operatorname{Curv}(a)\right)=0.
\tag{LC.4}
\]

## 2. Internal homology connection

Let

\[

\tag{LC.5}
\]

The binary operation induced on internal homology makes `H_0(L,ell_1)` a Lie algebra and every
`H_j(L,ell_1)` a module over it. The one-form `a_1` therefore defines a connection

\[
 \nabla^{\mathcal H}=d+[\ell_2(a_1,-)]

### Lemma 2.1 — fake flatness makes internal homology flat

If `F_2=0`, then

\[
 \boxed{(\nabla^{\mathcal H})^2=0.}
\tag{LC.7}
\]

#### Proof

The `L_infinity` identities imply that `ell_2` descends to a genuine graded Lie bracket on internal
homology. Hence the curvature of `(LC.6)` is the homology action of

\[

\tag{LC.8}
\]

The form-degree-two component `F_2` differs from `(LC.8)` by the `ell_1`-boundary contributed by
`a_2`. Therefore `(LC.8)` has zero internal-homology class when `F_2=0`, and its action on
`mathcal H` vanishes. QED.

## 3. Leading curvature theorem

Assume that, for some `r>=3`,

\[
 F_2=F_3=\cdots=F_{r-1}=0.
\tag{LC.9}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 — first surviving curvature descends and closes

The component `F_r` is an `ell_1`-cycle and determines a covariantly closed form

\[
 \boxed{
 [F_r]\in
 \Omega^r\left(M;\mathcal H_{2-r}\right),
 \qquad
 d_{\nabla^{\mathcal H}}[F_r]=0.}
\tag{LC.10}
\]

#### Proof

Take the form-degree-`r` part of `(LC.4)`. Every term containing a positive-form component of `a`
raises form degree, and every curvature component below `F_r` vanishes by `(LC.9)`. The only
remaining term is

\[
 \ell_1(F_r)=0.
\tag{LC.11}
\]

Thus `F_r` defines an internal-homology class.

Now take form degree `r+1` in `(LC.4)`. Terms involving `F_q` with `q<r` again vanish. A term with
two or more positive-form components of `a` has form degree at least `r+2`. Hence

\[
 dF_r+\ell_2(a_1,F_r)+\ell_1(F_{r+1})=0.
\tag{LC.12}
\]

Passing to internal homology kills the last term. By Lemma 2.1, the remaining operator is the flat
connection `(LC.6)`. This proves `(LC.10)`. QED.
