# The dual divisor stencil through the square-root product window

**PVC · Paper theorem.** For logarithmic $R$, $p>X^{10^{-6}}$, $L+1\ge\sqrt X$ and $U\le X^{1/2+10^{-9}}$, the sum over all $j\ge1$, $L<pj\le U$, of $\log(p)\beta_j B_R(\lfloor X/(pj)\rfloor)$ costs $O(M_R\|\beta\|_\infty(1+\log X)^3\sqrt X)$. Here $B_R(a)=-\mathrm{factorialPrefix}(R,a)-\tfrac12\sum_e c_e$ and $M_R$ is the stencil mass. Any larger prime cutoff is allowed.

**Proof.** For a divisor $e\le2R$, test $\{X/(epj)\}-1/2$. The [short factor estimate](../3-formalized/pvc-short-factors-in-the-dual-prime-window.md) handles $j\le X^{10^{-6}}$. For larger $j$, use the [product-window estimate](../3-formalized/pvc-centered-fractional-parts-across-a-product-window.md) with separated coefficients $\log(p)\mathbf1_{p\ \rm prime}$ and $\beta_j$. The same $H=\lfloor X^{1/5000000}\rfloor$ works for every $e\le X^{1/25000000}$ when $X^{1/25000000}\ge64$. The two factor intervals contribute two logarithms and the prime coefficient one. Finally $\lfloor\lfloor X/(pj)\rfloor/e\rfloor=\lfloor X/(epj)\rfloor$. The zero reciprocal moment therefore gives $B_R(\lfloor X/(pj)\rfloor)=\sum_e c_e(\{X/(epj)\}-1/2)$ exactly. Taking the finite coefficient mass proves the estimate.
