# Full square sections on long blocks in every logarithmic window

For every $0\le\beta<1$ there are $B\in\mathbf N$ and $\gamma>0$ such that every sufficiently late $T$ admits an integer $n>0$ with $|2\log n-T|\le18\log(T+2)$. For every integer $n\le d\le n+n^\beta$ and every $m\ge1$ on that same block, the ordinary generator is at most $B^{m^2}$ and the full original lattice of $mD_d$ has at least $2\lfloor e^{\gamma(d^2+m^2)}\rfloor+1$ sections.

Under RH the signed degree $Q(D_d)$ is uniformly positive at all late conductors. Off RH let $w>0$ be the zero width and take $a=\max(0,w+(\beta-1)/2)<w$. Select a zero with shifted real part greater than $a$. A positive theta deformation retains its quartet in the spline trace and kills every other mode outside a fixed finite set. For $L(u)=4h(u)-2h(u+\log4)$, with $h$ the real spline trace, the filter on $-L$ multiplies each retained coefficient by $4^z-2$. The selected mode survives because $\Re z<1/2$. The finite spectrum is conjugate symmetric and contains no real frequency. If $v$ is its greatest real exponent, its filtered sum has an excursion below $-\eta e^{vt}$ in every interval $[T,T+C]$ for large $T$, with $v>a$. If $L(u)\le H e^{au}$ throughout $|u-T|\le16\log(T+2)$, the positive filter applied to $H e^{au}-L(u)$ at that excursion is at least $-O(e^{-T})$. This follows from its logarithmic moment bound and the strip envelope. Its exact value is at most $H e^{at}\int e^{ax}k(x)\,dx-\eta e^{vt}$, a contradiction for late $T$. Thus every such window contains a peak above every prescribed multiple of $e^{au}$. [Formal peak theorem](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/CountingHolography/Sections/SquareTracePeaksInEveryLogarithmicWindow.lean#L86)

Round $X=e^{u/2}$ up to $n$. For $n\le d\le n+n^\beta$ the root displacements $d-X$ and $(2d+1)-2X$ are $O(X^\beta)$. The two long log displacements are $O(X^{\beta-1})$ and the width derivative bound costs $O(X^{2w})$. The short lag stays bounded, so $L(u)-Q(D_d)\le C e^{au}$ throughout the block. A peak above $(C+1)e^{au}$ leaves degree at least one simultaneously on all its conductors. Under either zero alternative the common reserve pays the full radius at every positive multiplicity, and the ordinary floor saving gives the common power bound. The pointwise upper bound at each prescribed conductor remains open.
