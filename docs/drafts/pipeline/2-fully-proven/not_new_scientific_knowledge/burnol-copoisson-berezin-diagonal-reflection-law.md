## Setup

Let `L_b` be Burnol's extended cosine-Sonine space, written in its completed-Mellin
point-evaluable realization.  Let `C_b` be the closed subspace generated by compactly supported
co-Poisson sources and put

```text
Y_b=C_b^perp,    E_b=orthogonal projection onto Y_b.
```

For a regular point `w`, let `k_w^b` be the continuous evaluation vector of `L_b` and define the
quotient evaluation vector

```text
y_w^b=E_b k_w^b.                                         (1)
```

This definition is source-pure: `E_b` is determined by the closed co-Poisson source space and
`k_w^b` is determined by completed-Mellin evaluation.  It does not choose a zeta zero.

For a bounded operator `T` on `Y_b`, define its unnormalized co-Poisson Berezin diagonal by

```text
Ber_b(T)(w)=<y_w^b,T y_w^b>.                              (2)
```

Where the indicated comparison norm is nonzero, one may instead use

```text
D_(a,b)(T)(w)=Ber_b(T)(w)/||k_w^a||^2.                   (3)
```

Equation `(3)` is the diagonal reader requested as `(K2)` in the parent card.

## Canonicality and faithfulness

The closed subspace `Y_b` is itself a reproducing-kernel Hilbert space.  Indeed, for `f in Y_b`,

```text
<f,y_w^b>=<f,E_b k_w^b>=<f,k_w^b>=f(w).                  (4)
```

Consequently the vectors `y_w^b`, as `w` ranges over any open regularity domain, have dense span
in `Y_b`: a vector orthogonal to all of them represents a meromorphic function vanishing on an
open set and is therefore zero.

For every `w`, the map `T |-> Ber_b(T)(w)` is a positive linear functional on positive operators.
The family is faithful on the positive cone:

```text
T>=0 and Ber_b(T)(w)=0 for every regular w  ->  T=0.      (5)
```

To prove `(5)`, write `T=S^*S`.  Equation `(2)` gives `S y_w^b=0` for every `w`, and density gives
`S=0`.  For self-adjoint `T`, vanishing of the diagonal on an open set also determines the full
sesquianalytic kernel by polarization.  Thus no zero-indexed basis or conditional expectation
onto a divisor-built diagonal algebra is needed.  The construction is a Berezin transform, not a
claim that such a divisor diagonal algebra already exists.

## Exact Fourier-reflection value law

Let `F` be the cosine Fourier involution and write `w#=1-conj(w)`.  Burnol's completed-Mellin
functional equation gives, in the Hermitian Riesz-vector convention,

```text
F k_w^b=k_(w#)^b.                                        (6)
```

The co-Poisson source space is Fourier invariant, hence `F E_b=E_b F` and

```text
F y_w^b=y_(w#)^b.                                        (7)
```

If `T` is Fourier odd, `F T F=-T`, then

```text
Ber_b(T)(w#)=-Ber_b(T)(w).                               (8)
```

Thus `(8)` is the usual FE--Schwarz reflection across the critical line.  It is an exact source
value relation, not a consequence of RH.

## The finite-holonomy diagonal

Fix `r>1`, put `b=a/r<=1`, and let

```text
H_(a,r)=U_r Pi_a U_r^*-U_r^* Pi_a U_r                   (9)
```

be the bilateral aperture holonomy from the parent card.  It is Fourier odd, so its source scalar

```text
h_(a,r)(w)=Ber_b(H_(a,r))(w)                             (10)
```

satisfies `(8)`.

Let `rho` be a nontrivial zeta zero, with multiplicity retained.  Every co-Poisson source Mellin
transform vanishes at `rho` to the required order.  Therefore its evaluation vectors lie in
`C_b^perp`, and

```text
y_rho^b=k_rho^b.                                         (11)
```

The bilateral aperture-defect identity now gives the exact normalized value

```text
h_(a,r)(rho)
 =(r^(2 Re(rho)-1)-r^(1-2 Re(rho))) ||k_rho^a||^2,

D_(a,b)(H_(a,r))(rho)
 =r^(2 Re(rho)-1)-r^(1-2 Re(rho))
 =2 sinh(2 log(r)(Re(rho)-1/2)).                         (12)
```

Off the de Branges symmetry axis the zero evaluator is nonzero, so `(12)` has the strict sign of
`Re(rho)-1/2`.

## Top-down consequence

The full quotient-order condition from the parent card is stronger than necessary.  It imposed
off-diagonal relations between distinct zero fibres.  Equations `(1)`--`(12)` replace it by one
scalar source reader:

```text
source theorem forcing h_(a,r)(rho)<=0 on every right-half
co-Poisson annihilator character rho
  -> no zeta zero with Re(rho)>1/2 by (12)
  -> no off-critical zero by functional-equation reflection
  -> RH.                                                  (13)
```

The phrase `co-Poisson annihilator character` can be expressed without an enumeration: it means a
point at which every vector in the explicitly generated closed co-Poisson source space vanishes.
Burnol's theorem identifies these characters, including multiplicity jets, with the zeta divisor.

The gain in `(13)` is exact but limited.  Constructing the Berezin reader does not prove its sign
on the annihilator spectrum.  A proof of that sign from the explicit co-Poisson sums would be the
remaining RH-bearing source theorem.  Replacing it by the assertion that every zeta zero has the
desired value would merely restate RH and is not an acceptable provider.

## Co-Poisson ideal factorization

There is a sharper source decomposition before specializing to a zero.  Let `Lambda_b(w)` denote
the completed Mellin multiplier in the co-Poisson formula.  Away from the two trivial boundary
points its zero divisor is the nontrivial zeta divisor, with multiplicity.  Let `P_C=1-E_b`.

The vector `P_C k_w^b` is the evaluation kernel of the closed co-Poisson subspace itself.  Every
co-Poisson Mellin transform is divisible by `Lambda_b`.  Hence, at a zero of `Lambda_b` of order
`m`, this vector-valued anti-analytic kernel and its first `m-1` parameter derivatives vanish.
Local Banach-valued analytic division therefore gives

```text
P_C k_w^b=conj(Lambda_b(w)) c_w^b                       (14)
```

for a locally anti-analytic source vector `c_w^b`.  In particular,

```text
y_w^b=k_w^b-conj(Lambda_b(w)) c_w^b.                    (15)
```

For a self-adjoint bounded `T`, expanding `(2)` yields

```text
Ber_b(T)(w)
 =<k_w^b,T k_w^b>
  -2 Re <P_C k_w^b,T k_w^b>
  +<P_C k_w^b,T P_C k_w^b>.                             (16)
```

Thus the difference between the quotient and ambient diagonals belongs locally to the
real-analytic ideal generated by `Lambda_b(w)` and `conj(Lambda_b(w))`; the quadratic term is
divisible by `|Lambda_b(w)|^2`.

For the finite holonomy, the ambient bilateral aperture law holds at every regular evaluation
point, not only on the divisor.  Combining it with `(16)` gives

```text
h_(a,r)(w)
 =2 sinh(2 log(r)(Re(w)-1/2)) ||k_w^a||^2+R_(a,r)(w),    (17)

R_(a,r) in (Lambda_b,conj(Lambda_b)).                    (18)
```

Equations `(17)`--`(18)` are the exact `constraint x orientation` splitting for this route.  The
co-Poisson constraint can alter the scalar reader away from its annihilator, but its correction
vanishes on that annihilator.  Formula `(12)` is the resulting fibre value.

This factorization is stronger than merely observing that `E_b k_rho^b=k_rho^b`: it exposes the
precise source ideal in which every quotient correction lives.  It still does not supply a sign
or a vanishing theorem for `h_(a,r)`.

## Relation to the existing source diagonals

The continued prime--Gamma one-Cauchy diagonal and the Hedenmalm Hermitian diagonal are source
functions on ordinary continued-Cauchy points.  The present reader is different: it first
compresses a finite aperture holonomy by the closed co-Poisson constraint and only then takes its
reproducing-kernel diagonal.  All three readers detect the same horizontal orientation on an
actual zero fibre, but no corpus theorem identifies them away from the divisor.

The quotient-to-ambient comparison is discharged by `(17)`.  The exact remaining value-relation
question is therefore

```text
compare the explicit ambient aperture-orientation term in (17),
or its scale derivative at r=1,
with the continued prime--Gamma Cauchy diagonal at the same source point. (19)
```

An identity of the form `(19)` with a source-controlled sign would transfer an existing
prime--Gamma producer directly to `(13)`.  No such comparison is currently proved.
