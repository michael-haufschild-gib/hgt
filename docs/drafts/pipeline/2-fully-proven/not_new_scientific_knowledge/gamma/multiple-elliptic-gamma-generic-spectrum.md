## Setup

For `r>=1`, let

`tau=(tau_0,...,tau_r) in H^(r+1)`

and suppose

`1,tau_0,...,tau_r`

are linearly independent over `Q`. Let `G_r(z;tau)` be Nishizawa's multiple elliptic gamma function and put

`Psi_r(z;tau)=delta log G_r(z;tau)`.

For `0<=j<=r`, write `tau^(j)` for the tuple with `tau_j` omitted and

`Psi_(r-1)^(j)(z)=Psi_(r-1)(z;tau^(j))`.

The defining functional equations are

`G_r(z+1;tau)=G_r(z;tau)`,

`G_r(z+tau_j;tau)=G_(r-1)(z;tau^(j)) G_r(z;tau)`.

After logarithmic differentiation,

`Delta_1 Psi_r=0`,

`Delta_(tau_j) Psi_r=Psi_(r-1)^(j)`.

## General spectrum-descent constraint

Without any independence hypothesis, the functional hierarchy forces

`Spec_DA(Psi_r) subset intersection_(j=0)^r Spec_DA(Psi_(r-1)^(j))`.

Indeed, if `Delta_h Psi_r` is DA, then each of its translated differences

`Delta_(tau_j)Delta_h Psi_r=Delta_h Psi_(r-1)^(j)`

is DA. This inclusion is the hierarchy-level constraint. The generic theorem below is the case where the right side can be evaluated exactly and collapses to the literal period group.

## Main theorem `[PROVED HERE, CONDITIONAL ONLY AT ORDER ONE]`

### Order one

For `r=1`, put

`H_1=Z+Z tau_0+Z tau_1`.

Then

`Spec_DA(Psi_1)=H_1`.

This is the theorem proved in `elliptic-gamma-rank-three-da-spectrum`.

### Every higher order

For every `r>=2`,

`Spec_DA(Psi_r)={h in C : Delta_h Psi_r is DA over C(z)}=Z`.

Consequently:

1. `Psi_r` is hypertranscendental for every `r>=1`;
2. `G_r` is hypertranscendental for every `r>=1`;
3. for `r>=2`, every nonintegral defect `Delta_h Psi_r` is hypertranscendental;
4. for `r>=2`,

   `G_r(z+h;tau)/G_r(z;tau) is DA iff h in Z`,

   and every nonintegral quotient is hypertranscendental.

Thus the higher hierarchy does not inherit the rank generated by its quasi-period parameters. At order one, theta logarithmic derivatives are DA and the two parameter shifts enter the DA spectrum. At order two and above, every parameter shift produces a lower multiple elliptic gamma logarithmic derivative, which is HT, so all parameter directions leave the DA spectrum.

## Proof

### 1. Order two is the intersection step

Let `r=2` and fix `h in C`. Put

`d_h=Delta_h Psi_2`.

Assume `d_h` is DA. Translation preserves differential algebraicity, so for each `j=0,1,2`,

`Delta_(tau_j)d_h`

is DA. Commuting translations with the functional equation gives

`Delta_(tau_j)d_h=Delta_h Psi_1^(j)`.

The order-one exact-spectrum theorem applied to the two periods remaining after deleting `tau_j` yields

`h in H_j:=Z+sum_(k ne j) Z tau_k`.

Therefore

`h in H_0 intersect H_1 intersect H_2`.

Rational independence makes this intersection exactly `Z`. Explicitly, equality of an `H_0` representation and an `H_1` representation forces the coefficients of `tau_0` and `tau_1` to vanish and leaves `h in Z+Z tau_2`; intersecting with `H_2` then kills the `tau_2` coefficient.

Hence `Spec_DA(Psi_2) subset Z`. The reverse inclusion follows from 1-periodicity, so

`Spec_DA(Psi_2)=Z`.

### 2. Higher orders propagate the collapse

Assume `r>=3` and that the theorem holds at order `r-1` for every rationally independent parameter tuple. If `d_h=Delta_h Psi_r` is DA, then for any `j`,

`Delta_(tau_j)d_h=Delta_h Psi_(r-1)^(j)`

is DA. The tuple `1,tau_0,...,hat(tau_j),...,tau_r` remains `Q`-linearly independent, and `r-1>=2`; the induction hypothesis therefore gives `h in Z`.

Again 1-periodicity supplies the converse. Thus `Spec_DA(Psi_r)=Z` for all `r>=2`.

### 3. Hypertranscendence and quotients

For every order, the exact spectrum is a proper subgroup of `C`. Choose a shift outside it. If `Psi_r` were DA, every translate and every translation defect would be DA, contradiction. Hence `Psi_r` is HT.

If `G_r` were DA, its logarithmic derivative `Psi_r` would be DA. Hence `G_r` is HT.

Finally, for

`R_h=G_r(z+h)/G_r(z)`,

`delta R_h/R_h=Delta_h Psi_r`.

The quotient is DA exactly when its logarithmic derivative is DA: one implication is logarithmic differentiation, and the other follows from the first-order equation `delta Y=(Delta_h Psi_r)Y` over a DA extension of `C(z)`. The quotient classification follows from the exact spectrum.

## Scientific meaning: a hierarchy phase transition

The hierarchy is often read as a generative tower because every parameter shift of `G_r` produces `G_(r-1)`. Differential algebra sees the opposite:

- at order zero, theta data are DA;
- at order one, the theta constraints enlarge the DA defect spectrum to rank three;
- at order two, the three lower rank-three spectra intersect only in `Z`;
- at every higher order, the lower HT constraint already forces the spectrum to remain `Z`.

The functional equations therefore cease to be DA propagation rules at exactly the first genuinely multiple level. They become constraints whose increments are themselves hypertranscendental.

The spectrum-descent inclusion remains valid on partially dependent parameter loci and gives a reusable upper bound there, even though the exact lower spectra on those loci are not yet classified.

The separate card `multiple-elliptic-gamma-transverse-constraint-depth` computes the full constant-coefficient parameter-translation annihilator of `Psi_r` modulo DA functions: it is the squarefree degree-`r` ideal generated by products of `r` distinct parameter differences. This classifies arbitrary cancellations, implies that a mixed monomial is DA exactly when it uses at least `r` distinct directions regardless of total order, and classifies every finite integer product or quotient of structural translates of `G_r`.
