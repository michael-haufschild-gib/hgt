# Integral saturation of the universal Hankel determinantal ideal

- **ID:** `deninger-hazewinkel-integral-saturation`
- **Stage:** 2-fully-proven
- **Type:** THEOREM / exact literature frontier
- **Route:** L — rational-Witt source foundation
- **Scientific status:** `[PROVED HERE] [LITERATURE THEOREM] [NOT NEW SCIENTIFIC KNOWLEDGE] [NOT AN RH PROOF]`
- **Primary source:** Hazewinkel, *Operations in the K-theory of endomorphisms* (1983),
  Appendix A.2--A.3.
- **Target:** For every positive `n`, multiplication by the leading determinant `D_n` is
  injective on `Z[X_0,X_1,...]/J_n`; equivalently

  ```text
  (J_n : D_n) = J_n.
  ```

  Together with the formalized localized-coordinate domain theorem, this makes `J_n` prime for
  every positive stage.

## Why this is the exact remaining theorem

Corpus now proves:

1. all Hankel `(n+1)`-minors are integral combinations of fixed-row minors;
2. Sylvester exchange gives `D_n J_n ⊆ K_n`, where `K_n` is generated by special minors;
3. `D_n` is prime in the integral infinite-variable polynomial ring (or a unit at degenerate
   sizes);
4. after inverting the class of `D_n`, the stage quotient is explicitly isomorphic to a
   localization of a finite integer polynomial ring and hence is a domain.

Thus no existence, field-descent, or fixed-row-generation gap remains. The only missing map is
injectivity into that localization, exactly the saturation statement above.

## Conjecture / failure loop

### C1 — prime pivot alone forces saturation: failed

`D` prime in the ambient polynomial ring and `D∉J` do not imply `(J:D)=J`. Example:
`J=(D X)` has `D X∈J` but `X∉J`. Leading-determinant primeness is necessary only for the final
coefficient division in Hazewinkel's proof.

### C2 — localized quotient domain forces saturation: failed

Localization kills `D`-torsion. Therefore a localized domain can coexist with a non-saturated
original quotient; using localized integrality to infer injectivity is circular.

### C3 — triangular recurrences alone force saturation: failed

Each special minor has shape

```text
m_q = D_n X_(q+n-1) + p_q(lower variables).
```

This clears high variables after multiplying by powers of `D_n`, but does not by itself remove
the resulting power. One still needs independence of products of the tail special minors on the
prime divisor `D_n=0`.

### C4 — triangular clearing plus tail-leading-monomial separation: proved on paper below

This is Hazewinkel's exact mechanism. After clearing high coefficient variables, choose an
expression with minimal power of `D_n`, group powers of the first special minor into low
coefficients, restrict to `D_n=0`, and use distinct lexicographic leading monomials of the remaining
special-minor products. The coefficients vanish on a dense open of the irreducible divisor
`V(D_n)`, hence are divisible by `D_n`, contradicting minimality. For formalization, the
topological density sentence is replaced by the stronger algebraic observation that `E_n g_J`
vanishes on all of `V(D_n)`.

- [x] **S3 — grouping law.** Group all occurrences of `m_n`; each resulting coefficient `g_J`

- [x] **S4 — tail leading monomial.** Let `E_n` be the cofactor obtained from the leading Hankel

  `D_n=0` and `E_n≠0`, products `m_J` have pairwise distinct lexicographic
  leading monomials

  ```text
  (-E_n)^|J| · ∏_{j∈J} a_(n+j-1).
  ```

## Dependency-closed paper proof of S1--S6

Write `a_0=1` and `a_r=X_(r-1)` for `r>0`. For `n≥2`, set

```text
D = det(a_(i+j))_(0≤i,j<n),
m_s = det(a_(i+c_j))_(0≤i≤n),   c=(0,1,...,n-1,s),  s≥n.
```

Every `m_s` is one of the special fixed-row minors, and the special ideal is generated by these
minors. Products are indexed by finite multisets of integers `s≥n`.

### S1 — two leading cofactors

Expand `m_s` down its last column. The bottom entry is `a_(s+n)` and its cofactor is exactly `D`,
so

```text
m_s = D a_(s+n) + p_s,
```

where every entry contributing to `p_s` is among the first `n` Hankel columns or the first `n`
entries of the last column. For `s≥n`, all those indices are `<s+n`. Thus `p_s` uses only
`a_1,...,a_(s+n-1)`.

For `s>n`, expose one more term. Let

```text
E = det(a_(r_i+j))_(0≤i,j<n),   r=(0,1,...,n-2,n).
```

The entry `a_(s+n-1)` occurs only in row `n-1` of the last column, so

```text
m_s = D a_(s+n) - E a_(s+n-1) + q_s,             (1)
```

where `q_s` uses no variable above `a_(s+n-2)`.

The polynomial `E` is nonzero modulo `(D)`. Indeed, expansion in `a_(2n-1)` gives

```text
E = D_(n-1) a_(2n-1) + lower,
```

whereas `D` is independent of `a_(2n-1)`. If `D|E`, coefficient comparison in that variable
would give `D|D_(n-1)`. But `a_(2n-2)` occurs in `D` with nonzero coefficient `D_(n-1)` and does
not occur in `D_(n-1)`; variable-support preservation in a domain rules this out. Therefore

```text
D ∤ E.                                               (2)
```

### S2 — well-founded clearing, without a global rewrite loop

Call a coefficient polynomial *low* if it uses only `a_1,...,a_(2n-1)`. For a monomial `u`, list
its exponents at `a_r`, `r≥2n`, from the largest occurring index downward and order these finite
lists lexicographically. This is well founded because each monomial has finite support.

We prove by induction on that profile: for every monomial `u`, there are `k`, finitely many low
polynomials `b_I`, and finite multisets `I` of special-minor indices such that

```text
D^k u = Σ_I b_I m_I.                                (3)
```

If `u` is low, take `k=0` and the empty product. Otherwise choose its largest high factor `a_r`
and write `u=u'a_r`. Applying S1 with `s=r-n` gives

```text
D u = u' m_(r-n) - u' p_(r-n).
```

Every monomial in the second term has a strictly smaller high profile; the first term has one
fewer occurrence of `a_r` in its coefficient. Apply induction to all finitely many resulting
monomials and raise their finitely many powers of `D` to a common maximum. Since `D` is low, this
does not reintroduce a high variable. Linearity extends (3) to every coefficient polynomial.

Now assume `DP∈J_n`. Formalized Sylvester containment gives `D J_n⊆K_n`, hence
`D²P∈K_n`. Membership in the span defining `K_n` is a finite sum `Σ_s c_s m_s`. Apply (3) to each
of the finitely many `c_s` and synchronize exponents. This yields

```text
D^k P = Σ_I f_I m_I,                                (4)
```

where every `f_I` is low and every multiset `I` is nonempty.

### S3 — isolate the boundary minor

Choose `k` minimal among representations (4). In every multiset `I`, separate all occurrences of
the index `n`; call the remaining multiset `J`, whose entries are strictly greater than `n`.
Grouping equal `J` gives

```text
D^k P = Σ_J g_J m_J.                                (5)
```

Because `m_n=D a_(2n)+p_n(a_1,...,a_(2n-1))`, each `g_J` belongs to

```text
Z[a_1,...,a_(2n-1), D a_(2n)].                      (6)
```

Let `h_J` be its reduction modulo `D`; equivalently, replace every positive power of
`D a_(2n)` by zero. Then `g_J-h_J` is visibly divisible by `D`, and `h_J` is a polynomial in the
finite low variable set.

### S4 — symbolic tail separation

Pass temporarily to an algebraic closure of `Q` and fix low coordinates `x` satisfying
`D(x)=0` and `E(x)≠0`. For every `s>n`, equation (1) says that, as a polynomial in tail variables
`a_(2n),a_(2n+1),...`, the lexicographic leading term of `m_s(x)` is

```text
-E(x) a_(s+n-1).
```

Consequently the leading term of `m_J(x)` is

```text
(-E(x))^|J| ∏_(s∈J) a_(s+n-1).
```

Distinct finite multisets `J` give distinct monomials. Hence the finitely many `m_J(x)` occurring
in (5) are linearly independent over the ground field. Reducing (5) modulo `D` therefore forces

```text
h_J(x)=0                                             (7)
```

for every `J` and every such `x`.

### S5 — Nullstellensatz without a density formalization

For every point `x` of `V(D)`, either `E(x)=0`, in which case `(E h_J)(x)=0` tautologically, or
`E(x)≠0`, in which case (7) gives the same conclusion. Thus `E h_J` vanishes on all of `V(D)`.

All involved polynomials use finitely many variables. Apply mathlib's finite-variable
Nullstellensatz with base field `Q` and algebraic closure `Qbar`:

```text
E h_J ∈ radical((D)) = (D).
```

The last equality uses primeness of `D` after scalar extension, already proved by the Schur
determinant argument. By (2), prime cancellation gives `D|h_J` over `Q`; (6) then gives `D|g_J`
over `Q`.

### S6 — return to integers and lower the exponent

The integral `D` is primitive. The corpus Gauss-contraction theorem
`MvPolynomialPrimitive.dvd_of_fraction_map_dvd` therefore contracts each rational divisibility
`D|g_J` to `Z`. Write `g_J=D b_J`.

If `J` is nonempty, `b_Jm_J` still contains a special-minor factor. If `J` is empty, construction
of the grouping shows `g_∅=m_n c`. Also `D∤m_n`: specialize `a_0=1`, `a_(n+1)=1`, and all other
`a_r` with `1≤r≤2n` to zero. The leading `n×n` determinant has a zero row, while the full
`(n+1)×(n+1)` Hankel determinant `m_n` is block diagonal with `1` and a reversal permutation, so
it is a unit. Since `D` is prime, `D|m_nc` and `D∤m_n` imply `D|c`; hence `b_∅` also retains an
`m_n` factor.

Substituting `g_J=Db_J` into (5) and cancelling nonzero `D` in the ambient integer polynomial
domain produces an admissible representation with exponent `k-1`, contradicting minimality if
`k>0`. Thus `k=0`, and the ungrouped form (4), whose products all contain a special minor, gives
`P∈K_n⊆J_n`. Therefore `(J_n:D_n)=J_n` for `n≥2`. Stage `n=1` is already prime in the corpus and
is saturated separately.
