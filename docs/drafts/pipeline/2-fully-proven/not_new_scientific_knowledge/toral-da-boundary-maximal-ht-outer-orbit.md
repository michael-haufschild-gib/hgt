# Toral DA boundary of a maximal-HT outer translation orbit

- **ID:** `toral-da-boundary-maximal-ht-outer-orbit`
- **Stage:** 2-fully-proven
- **Type:** THEOREM (paper) — `[PROVED HERE] [BRIDGE] [NOT NEW SCIENTIFIC KNOWLEDGE]`
- **Lane:** general hypertranscendence; translation hulls; almost-periodic boundary dynamics
- **Statement:** For every `d>=1`, an invertible outer function on the upper half-plane has a
  differentially algebraically independent real-translation family at every algebraic power, while
  the compact-open boundary of each power orbit is a `d`-torus consisting entirely of
  differentially algebraic functions.

## Goal

The one-point parabolic-collapse theorem shows that maximal translation HT can converge to one DA
boundary point.  The present theorem asks whether that rank collapse is forced to be zero
dimensional.

It is not.  The topology and dimension of the translation-limit hull can be changed without losing
one differential transcendence degree at any finite orbit packet.  The protecting mechanism is a
constant-coefficient differential filter which annihilates the added quasiperiodic carrier and
exposes the original free orbit.

## Collapsing maximal-HT input

Let `H={z in C:Im z>0}` and `D=d/dz`.  The theorem
`maximal-ht-parabolic-orbit-collapse.md`, transported from the disk to `H`, supplies an analytic
function `S` such that:

1. `S,S^(-1) in H^infinity(H)` and `S` is outer;
2. the family `{S(z+t):t in R}` is differentially algebraically independent over `C(z)`;
3. for

   `c=exp(-1)`,

   one has `S(z+t)->c` locally uniformly on `H` as `t->+infinity` and as `t->-infinity`;
4. `S(z+t)->c` also as `Im z->infinity`, locally uniformly in bounded real `t`.

For completeness, `S` is the upper-half-plane form of

`exp((m-i)/(m+i))`,

where `m` is the finite positive atomic Stieltjes transform with maximally free translation family.
The last assertion follows directly from

`|m(x+i y)|<=mu(R)/y`.

## Differential-filter protection lemma `[PROVED HERE]`

Let `K` be a differential field of characteristic zero, let `x_1,...,x_r` be differentially
algebraically independent over `K`, and let

`0!=L in C[D]`

be a constant-coefficient differential operator.  Then

`Lx_1,...,Lx_r`

are differentially algebraically independent over `K`.

### Proof

Put

`E=K<Lx_1,...,Lx_r>_D`,

`F=K<x_1,...,x_r>_D`.

For every `j`, the element `x_j` is differentially algebraic over `E`, because it satisfies the
nonzero differential equation

`L(Y)-Lx_j=0`.

Hence `F/E` is a differentially algebraic extension.  Differential transcendence degree is
unchanged under such an extension, so

`dtrdeg_K E=dtrdeg_K F=r`.

Thus the filtered tuple is differentially algebraically independent. `QED`

## Toral construction

Fix `d>=1`.  Choose positive real numbers

`omega_1,...,omega_d`

which are linearly independent over `Q`, and choose nonzero complex coefficients

`a_1,...,a_d`.

Put

`A(z)=sum_(j=1)^d a_j exp(i omega_j z)`,

`M=sum_j |a_j|`.

Since `omega_j>0`, one has `|A(z)|<=M` on `H`.  Choose

`0<epsilon<c/M`

and define

`G(z)=S(z)+epsilon A(z)`.                                     (1)

For real `t`, write

`G_t(z)=G(z+t)`.

## Maximal HT and outer invertibility `[PROVED HERE]`

The function `G` and its inverse belong to `H^infinity(H)`, `G` is outer, and every finite family
of distinct real translates of `G` is differentially algebraically independent over `C(z)`.

### Proof

The bounds on `S` from its Cayley--exponential construction give

`c<|S(z)|<exp(1)`.

Therefore

`|G(z)|>=c-epsilon M>0`,

`|G(z)|<=exp(1)+epsilon M`.

Thus `G,G^(-1) in H^infinity(H)`.  An invertible `H^infinity` function has no nonconstant inner
factor, so `G` is outer.

Let

`L=prod_(j=1)^d (D-i omega_j)`.

This nonzero constant-coefficient operator annihilates every translate of `A`.  Hence

`L(G_t)=L(S(z+t))`.                                           (2)

For distinct `t_1,...,t_r`, the tuple `(S(z+t_k))_(k=1)^r` is differentially algebraically
independent.  The filter lemma makes `(L(S(z+t_k)))_(k=1)^r` differentially algebraically
independent.  By (2), this tuple lies in the differential field generated by
`G_(t_1),...,G_(t_r)`.  That field therefore has differential transcendence degree at least `r`,
and it cannot exceed `r` because it is generated by `r` differential elements.  Thus the
`G_(t_k)` are differentially algebraically independent. `QED`

## All-power toral-boundary theorem `[PROVED HERE]`

For `N>=1`, define

`O_N={G_t^N:t in R}`.

For `lambda=(lambda_1,...,lambda_d) in T^d`, put

`b_lambda(z)=c+epsilon sum_(j=1)^d a_j lambda_j exp(i omega_j z)`,       (3)

and

`B_N={b_lambda^N:lambda in T^d}`.                              (4)

Then:

1. every finite set of distinct elements of `O_N` is differentially algebraically independent;
2. every element of `B_N` is differentially algebraic over `C(z)`;
3. `B_N` is homeomorphic to `T^d` in the compact-open topology of `Hol(H)`;
4. the full compact-open closure is the disjoint union

   `closure(O_N)=O_N disjoint_union B_N`;                      (5)

5. `B_N` is exactly the cluster set of `G_t^N` as `|t|->infinity`.

### Proof: algebraic freedom at every power

For distinct parameters `t_1,...,t_r`, each `G_(t_j)` is algebraic over its `N`-th power.  If the
powers were differentially algebraically dependent, adjoining these algebraic roots could not
raise differential transcendence degree to `r`, contradicting the preceding theorem.  This proves
(1).

### Proof: the boundary is DA

Every `b_lambda` is an exponential polynomial plus a constant and satisfies the homogeneous linear
differential equation

`D prod_(j=1)^d (D-i omega_j) b_lambda=0`.

It is therefore differentially algebraic, as is its power.  This proves (2).

### Proof: torus topology

The estimate `|b_lambda(z)|>=c-epsilon M>0` holds uniformly in `lambda`, so all quotients below are
analytic on `H`.

The map

`beta_N:T^d -> Hol(H)`, `lambda |-> b_lambda^N`,

is continuous.  It is injective.  Indeed, if `b_lambda^N=b_mu^N`, then the analytic quotient
`b_lambda/b_mu` takes values in the finite set of `N`-th roots of unity, hence is constant.  As
`Im z->infinity`, both numerator and denominator tend to the same nonzero constant `c`, so that
constant is `1`.  Linear independence of the distinct exponentials then gives `lambda=mu`.

A continuous injection from compact `T^d` to the Hausdorff space `Hol(H)` is a homeomorphism onto
its image.  This proves (3).

### Proof: closure and cluster set

Write

`theta(t)=(exp(i omega_1 t),...,exp(i omega_d t)) in T^d`.

Kronecker's theorem and rational independence of the frequencies say that `theta(R)` is dense in
`T^d`; every point of the torus is approached along an unbounded sequence of positive parameters.
Moreover,

`G_t(z)=S(z+t)+epsilon sum_j a_j theta_j(t) exp(i omega_j z)`.  (6)

If `|t_n|->infinity` and `theta(t_n)->lambda`, then the collapse of `S` and (6) give

`G_(t_n)^N -> b_lambda^N`

locally uniformly.  Thus every point of `B_N` is an infinite-parameter cluster point.  Conversely,
compactness of `T^d` lets every unbounded parameter sequence pass to a phase-convergent subsequence,
and every resulting limit has the form (4).

If a parameter sequence is bounded, it has a convergent subsequence `t_n->t`, and continuity gives
the orbit limit `G_t^N`.  These two alternatives prove the set equality (5) and compactness of the
closure.

The union is disjoint.  If `G_t^N=b_lambda^N`, the quotient argument above, now using
`G_t(z)->c` and `b_lambda(z)->c` as `Im z->infinity`, gives `G_t=b_lambda`.  Equation (6) would then
express the HT function `S(z+t)` as an exponential polynomial plus a constant, making it
differentially algebraic.  This contradicts translation HT of `S`. `QED`

## Law extracted

**Toral differential-rank boundary law.**  For every finite dimension `d`, maximal differential
freedom of a continuous translation orbit is compatible with a `d`-dimensional compact boundary on
which differential transcendence degree collapses identically to zero.  This remains true after
every algebraic power normalization and inside the invertible outer cone.

The law is stronger than non-universality.  It shows that maximal translation HT determines neither:

- whether the translation hull has one boundary point or a positive-dimensional boundary;
- the dimension of that boundary;
- the quasiperiodic frequency group;
- the Haar/phase dynamics on the boundary;
- the differential-algebraic type of orbit-limit functions.

The differential-filter lemma isolates the mechanism: a DA summand may carry arbitrary finite
quasiperiodic phase geometry while a differential operator deletes that summand and certifies that
the remaining carrier still has maximal HT freedom.

## Exact RH-route consequence

The theorem rules out the implementation claim

> maximal translation hypertranscendence, even for all algebraic powers and an invertible outer
> carrier, determines the Bohr hull, phase torus, limit distribution, recurrence geometry, or
> correlation-driving quasiperiodic data needed by a zeta universality or zero-statistics argument.

This applies to HT-only implementations of:

1. **Voronin--Bagchi:** strong recurrence and the distinguished zeta limit distribution are not
   consequences of maximal HT; the same algebraic freedom coexists with boundary tori of every
   finite dimension consisting only of DA limits.
2. **Montgomery--Odlyzko / GUE heuristics:** the phase-frequency group and its Haar distribution are
   extra data.  Maximal HT does not select the prime-log torus, the explicit-formula measure, or the
   resulting correlation kernel.
3. **Bohr--Courant value distribution:** algebraic freedom does not determine the translation hull
   supporting the value distribution.

The full classical results and conjectures are not refuted.  They retain the actual vertical zeta
orbit, Euler frequencies, arithmetic probability measure, and explicit formula.  The theorem
excludes deriving those structures from translation HT alone.

## Attacks and boundaries

1. **The torus is not hidden in the HT carrier.**  It is carried by a DA exponential polynomial and
   can be removed exactly by `L`.
2. **The construction is not merely pointwise HT.**  Every finite tuple of distinct real translates
   is differentially algebraically independent, and this survives every positive power.
3. **The whole boundary is DA.**  The degeneration is not one exceptional limit function; it is a
   compact manifold of arbitrary finite dimension.
4. **Only finite-dimensional tori are proved.**  An infinite Bohr compactification would require an
   infinite-order annihilator, which is not available in ordinary differential algebra.
5. **No Euler normalization is preserved.**  The added exponential polynomial changes the pointed
   analytic carrier.  This is precisely why the theorem is an HT-only no-go rather than a
   deformation theorem for zeta.

Bohr almost-periodic theory already identifies finite exponential-frequency hulls with tori.
Composition-operator theory studies hypercyclicity, recurrence, spectra, and orbital limit points of

is available, adding a finite DA quasiperiodic summand is a routine differentially algebraic
extension argument; the explicit differential filter merely makes that elementary invariance
visible.  Bohr theory then supplies the finite torus.  The exact packaging was not located, but the
mechanism is immediately generated from its parents.  This card is therefore a structural bridge,
not new scientific knowledge.
