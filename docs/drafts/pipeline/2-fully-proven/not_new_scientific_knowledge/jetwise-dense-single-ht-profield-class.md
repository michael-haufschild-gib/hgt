# Jetwise density of one symmetric HT translation-profield class

- **ID:** `jetwise-dense-single-ht-profield-class`
- **Stage:** 2-fully-proven
- **Type:** THEOREM (paper) — `[PROVED HERE] [NOVELTY UNVERIFIED]`
- **Lane:** general hypertranscendence; compact-open topology; continuous-selector no-go
- **Statement:** One explicit zero-free FE/Schwarz-symmetric hypertranscendental entire carrier has
  a symmetric-polynomial orbit which is dense, even in every compatible finite-jet fiber, while
  every orbit member generates exactly the same translation-differential field at every power.

## Definitions

Let `D=d/ds`.  For an entire function `f` and an integer `r>=1`, write

`T_r(f)=C(s)(D^n(f(s+a)^r): a in C, n>=0)`.

Thus `(T_r(f))_(r>=1)` is the complete positive-power translation-differential profield of `f`.

Let `X` be the real Frechet space, with the compact-open topology, of entire functions satisfying

`f(1-s)=f(s)`,

`f(conj(s))=conj(f(s))`.

Equivalently, after `w=s-1/2`, the elements of `X` are the real-even entire functions.

## The zero-free symmetric HT carrier `[PROVED HERE]`

Put

`h(s)=1/Gamma(s)`,

`q(s)=h(s)+h(1-s)`,

`U(s)=exp(q(s))`.

Then `U` is entire, zero-free, belongs to `X`, and is hypertranscendental over `C(s)`.

### Proof

The reciprocal gamma function `h` is entire.  The two displayed symmetries of `q` and `U` follow
from conjugation for `Gamma` and from interchanging `s` and `1-s`.

Euler reflection gives

`1/Gamma(1-s)=Gamma(s) sin(pi s)/pi`.

Consequently `Gamma(s)` satisfies

`sin(pi s) Gamma(s)^2-pi q(s) Gamma(s)+pi=0`.                 (1)

If `q` were differentially algebraic over `C(s)`, then (1) would make `Gamma` algebraic over a
differentially algebraic extension of `C(s)`.  Differential algebraicity is transitive, so `Gamma`
would be differentially algebraic over `C(s)`, contradicting Holder's theorem.  Hence `q` is
hypertranscendental.

If `U=exp(q)` were differentially algebraic, then

`q'=U'/U`

would be differentially algebraic.  But `q` satisfies the first-order differential equation
`Y'-q'=0` over the differential field generated by `q'`.  Transitivity would again make `q`
differentially algebraic, a contradiction.  Thus `U` is hypertranscendental. `QED`

## Exact profield orbit `[PROVED HERE]`

Let

`P(s)=R((s-1/2)^2)`, `0!=R in R[t]`.

Then `PU` belongs to `X`, is hypertranscendental, and for every `r>=1`,

`T_r(PU)=T_r(U)`.                                             (2)

### Proof

For every translation parameter `a`,

`(P(s+a)U(s+a))^r=P(s+a)^r U(s+a)^r`,

and the nonzero factor `P(s+a)^r` and its inverse lie in `C(s)`.  Hence the order-zero translated
generators on each side of (2) generate one another; closing under `D` gives equality.  At `r=1`,
if `PU` were differentially algebraic, division by `P in C(s)^*` would make `U` differentially
algebraic. `QED`

## Dense-class theorem `[PROVED HERE]`

The orbit

`O_U={P U: 0!=P(s)=R((s-1/2)^2), R in R[t]}`

is dense in `X` for the compact-open topology.  Every member of this dense set is
hypertranscendental and all members have the same complete profield `(T_r(U))_(r>=1)`.

### Proof

Fix `F in X`.  Since `U` is zero-free, `g=F/U` is entire and belongs to `X`.  In the centered
coordinate `w=s-1/2`, its Taylor expansion contains only even powers and has real coefficients.
Thus

`g(s)=G((s-1/2)^2)`

for an entire function `G` with real Taylor coefficients.  The real Taylor polynomials `R_n` of
`G` converge compact-open to `G`.  Therefore

`R_n((s-1/2)^2) U(s) -> F(s)`

compact-open.  Zero approximants, if any, may be replaced by arbitrarily small nonzero constant
perturbations.  The exact profield and HT assertions follow from (2). `QED`

## Jetwise dense-class theorem `[PROVED HERE]`

Let `A subset C` be finite and invariant under `s |-> 1-s` and conjugation.  Let the nonnegative
depth `m(a)` be invariant under the same actions.  Fix any symmetry-compatible jet table

`c_(a,j)`, `a in A`, `0<=j<=m(a)`,

and let `X_c subset X` be its realization fiber.  If `X_c` is nonempty, then

`O_U intersect X_c`

is dense in `X_c`.  In particular, exact finite normalization does not destroy the density of the
single profield class.

### Proof

Fix `F in X_c` and again put `g=F/U in X`.  Ordinary Hermite interpolation, applied at the full
symmetry-invariant set `A` through the larger depths `2m(a)+1`, gives a polynomial with the same
jets as `g`.  Averaging it under

`p(s) |-> p(1-s)`, `p(s) |-> overline(p(conj(s)))`

gives a real-even polynomial `I` retaining those jets, because the jet table of `g` is compatible
with both symmetries.

Set

`S(s)=prod_(a in A) (s-a)^(2(m(a)+1))`.

The polynomial `S` is real-even, and the enhanced interpolation depth makes

`v=(g-I)/S`

an entire member of `X`.  Approximate `v` compact-open by real-even polynomials `V_n` and put

`P_n=I+S V_n`.

Then `P_n` is real-even, `P_n->g` compact-open, and `P_n-g` vanishes to order at least
`2(m(a)+1)` at every `a`.  Hence

`P_n U-F=U(P_n-g)`

has zero derivatives through order `m(a)` at each `a`, while `P_n U->F` compact-open.  If an
approximant is the zero polynomial, add a sufficiently small nonzero real multiple of `S`; this
preserves every required jet and convergence.  Thus all approximants lie in `O_U intersect X_c`.
`QED`

## Continuous profield-selector law `[PROVED HERE]`

Let `Y` be Hausdorff and let

`Phi:X_c -> Y`

be compact-open continuous.  Suppose `Phi(f)=Phi(g)` whenever

`T_r(f)=T_r(g)` for every `r>=1`.

Then `Phi` is constant on `X_c`.

Indeed, `Phi` is constant on `O_U intersect X_c` by exact profield equality, and that set is dense
by the jetwise theorem.  Continuity and the Hausdorff property force the same value at every limit
point.

The same conclusion holds with no passport (`A` empty), so every compact-open-continuous invariant
of the complete translation-differential profield is constant on all of `X`.

## Order barrier `[PROVED HERE]`

The infinite-order cost is unavoidable.  A zero-free entire function of finite order is, by
Hadamard factorization, `exp(Q)` for a polynomial `Q`; it is therefore differentially algebraic.
Hence no zero-free hypertranscendental entire function can have finite order.

This barrier is why the dense-class theorem does not address a route whose hypotheses genuinely
retain order one or a fixed Hadamard genus.  The order-one remote-quartet theorem is the separate
finite-probe obstruction in that cage.

## Exact RH-route consequence

This theorem excludes the following implementation pattern, not the named RH routes in full:

1. replace the completed zeta carrier by its complete HT translation-differential profield;
2. retain at most a compatible finite normalization passport;
3. feed that data into a construction continuous for compact-open convergence;
4. claim that the output nontrivially selects an RH-valid carrier inside `X_c`.

Any such output is constant on the whole symmetric jet fiber.  This covers compact-open-continuous
feature maps, finite local phase/modulus packages, continuous finite-rank operator surrogates, and
limit arguments whose only rigid input is the profield plus finitely many jets.

It does **not** exclude:

- the full Speiser, Li, Weil, de Branges, Hilbert--Polya, or Nyman--Beurling equivalences;
- a discontinuous global-divisor selector;
- a construction retaining the Euler product, Dirichlet coefficients, or another arithmetic datum
  not determined by the profield;
- fixed order-one or fixed-genus arguments;
- a complete Hermite--Biehler inequality, complete spectral measure, or full test-function cone.

The law says that compact-open continuity plus even the complete positive-power
translation-differential profield cannot be the missing selector.  A viable route must retain a
global datum which is absent from this equivalence class or cross a genuinely discontinuous
boundary.
