# Atomic increments force the full primitive translation orbit

- **ID:** `atomic-increment-full-orbit-codimension-law`
- **Stage:** 2-fully-proven
- **Type:** THEOREM (paper) — `[PROVED HERE] [BRIDGE] [NOT NEW SCIENTIFIC KNOWLEDGE]`
- **Statement:** Let a non-torsion translation `T_a` act on a differential function field.  If the
  complete translation orbit of one increment `(T_a-1)u`, modulo the base field, is
  differentially algebraically independent, then the complete translation orbit of the primitive
  `u` is differentially algebraically independent.  One atomic increment direction recovers all
  transverse coset base modes.  The proof is a codimension-amplification law, not an integration
  formula.

## General setup

Let `G` be an abelian translation group, let `a in G` have infinite order, and let `K` be a
differential field stable under every translation `T_g`, `g in G`.  Translations commute with the
derivation `D`.

Let `u` belong to a differential field extension carrying the same translations.  Suppose there
is `r in K` such that

`x=(T_a-1)u-r`.

Assume the **atomic increment-orbit hypothesis**:

> For every finite set of distinct `g_1,...,g_q in G`, the functions
> `T_(g_1)x,...,T_(g_q)x` are differentially algebraically independent over `K`.

Equivalently, the full translation orbit `{T_gx:g in G}` is a differential transcendence basis for
the differential field it generates.

## Codimension-amplification theorem `[PROVED HERE]`

Under these hypotheses, for every finite set of distinct `s_1,...,s_m in G`,

`dtrdeg_K K<T_(s_1)u,...,T_(s_m)u>_D=m`.                   (1)

Thus the full translation orbit `{T_su:s in G}` is differentially algebraically independent over
`K`.

### Step 1: assume a missing dimension

Fix a finite set `S={s_1,...,s_m}` and put

`r_S=dtrdeg_K K<(T_su)_(s in S)>_D`.

Suppose `r_S<m`.  Let `C` be the number of cosets of the cyclic subgroup `<a>` met by `S`.

Because `a` has infinite order and `S` is finite, choose a positive integer `Q` so large that the
sets

`S, S+Qa, S+2Qa, ...`

are pairwise disjoint and, inside each `<a>`-coset, successive copies occur in separated order.
For a positive integer `N`, put

`S_N=union_(ell=0)^(N-1)(S+ell Qa)`.

Translation preserves `K` and differential transcendence degree.  Every block `S+ell Qa` therefore
has differential transcendence degree `r_S`.  Subadditivity gives the upper bound

`dtrdeg_K K<(T_tu)_(t in S_N)>_D <= N r_S`.                (2)

### Step 2: the increment forest inside the repeated copies

The set `S_N` has `Nm` vertices and still meets exactly `C` cosets modulo `<a>`.  In each coset,
order its finitely many vertices along the integer `a`-coordinate and join consecutive vertices.
The resulting graph is a forest with `C` components and therefore has

`Nm-C` edges.                                               (3)

If an edge joins `v` to `v+ka`, iteration of the defining increment equation gives

`T_(v+ka)u-T_vu`

`=sum_(j=0)^(k-1) T_(v+ja)x + element of K`                (4)

for `k>0`, with the analogous negative sum for `k<0`.

For the consecutive-edge forest, the coefficient vectors in (4) are supported on disjoint
intervals of the `a`-edge set inside each coset.  Hence the `Nm-C` coefficient vectors are linearly
independent; equivalently, applying the boundary map sends them to the independent forest-edge
boundaries.

The functions `T_gx` are differentially algebraically independent by hypothesis.  Independent
constant linear combinations of differential indeterminates remain differentially algebraically
independent, and adding elements of `K` changes no differential transcendence degree.  Therefore
the `Nm-C` edge differences in (4) are differentially algebraically independent over `K`.

Every edge difference belongs to the differential field generated by the vertex values
`(T_tu)_(t in S_N)`.  Hence

`Nm-C <= dtrdeg_K K<(T_tu)_(t in S_N)>_D`.                 (5)

### Step 3: amplify the contradiction

Combining (2) and (5) gives

`Nm-C <= N r_S`.

If `r_S<=m-1`, this implies `N<=C`.  Choosing `N>C` is a contradiction.  Therefore `r_S=m`, proving
(1).

The mechanism is the law:

> A fixed missing differential dimension repeats once per translated copy, but a finite number of
> cyclic cosets can absorb only the fixed boundary loss `C`.  Repetition amplifies any hidden base
> relation past the increment forest's exact rank budget.

## Complete finite-superposition matroid `[PROVED HERE]`

Let `A=K^T[D][G]` denote the finite-support differential group algebra with coefficients in the
joint translation-constant subfield `K^T`; it is enough for the application to take complex
constant coefficients.  For finite operators `L_1,...,L_q in A`, write `L_ju` for the associated
finite differential-translation superpositions.

The orbit independence (1), together with the standard differential-linear rank lemma, gives

`dtrdeg_K K<L_1u,...,L_qu>_D`

`=dim_(Frac(K^T[D])) span{L_1,...,L_q}`.                   (6)

Thus every nonlinear differential-algebraic dependence among finite superpositions is already
forced by a differential-linear dependence among their operator divisors.  There are no hidden
nonlinear relations.

## Why this is stronger than the atomic-defect bridge

The earlier bridge-edge theorem assumes that all defects `(T_g-1)u`, for every translation class
`g`, already form the atomic differential matroid.  The present theorem assumes much less: only
the translates of one fixed increment `(T_a-1)u-r` are known to be independent.  Defects joining
different cosets modulo `<a>` are not assumed controlled at all.

The repeated-copy forest recovers those transverse base modes automatically.  This is the new
content: one direction of atomic constraint data forces the entire translation representation to
be free.

## Riemann xi specialization `[PROVED FROM THE PRIME-FEATURE PARENT]`

Take

`G=C`, `a=2`, `K=F_s`, `u=xi'/xi`,

where `F_s` is Nagoshi's differential field of meromorphic functions of Nevanlinna characteristic
`o(r)`.  It contains `C(s)` and is stable under complex translations and differentiation.

The formalized source-stripping identity is

`(T_2-1)u-r=F`,                                             (7)

where

`r(s)=1/(s+2)+1/(s+1)-1/(s-1)`

and

`F(s)=sum_(n>=2) Lambda(n)(1-n^(-2))n^(-s)`.               (8)

The series is supported on prime powers and every prime coefficient is nonzero.  The fully proved
prime-feature polarization theorem in `prime-power-dirichlet-complex-jet-independence.md` shows
that all finite families of distinct complex translates of `F`, with arbitrary derivative jets,
are algebraically independent over `F_s`.  This is exactly the atomic increment-orbit hypothesis.

The general theorem therefore gives:

**Completed-logarithmic-orbit theorem.** For arbitrary distinct complex numbers
`h_1,...,h_m`, the functions

`(xi'/xi)(s+h_1),...,(xi'/xi)(s+h_m)`

are differentially algebraically independent over `F_s`, hence over `C(s)`.

In particular, `xi'/xi` satisfies no nontrivial algebraic differential-difference equation using
finitely many arbitrary complex shifts.  The shifts need not lie on a lattice.  For finite
constant-coefficient differential-translation superpositions, equation (6) computes the complete
differential-algebraic matroid.

## Exact RH-route consequences

The specialization excludes any proposed implementation whose completed logarithmic carrier is
required to close under a finite algebraic differential state law involving arbitrary complex shifts.
The law may be nonlinear and may use arbitrary finite derivative orders.  This strictly contains
the earlier finite linear-stencil no-go.

It applies to concrete finite-state implementations inside:

1. Li/Keiper schemes using finitely many complex-shifted completed logarithmic jets;
2. finite-dimensional Hilbert--Polya or Connes resolvent traces with a claimed algebraic
   differential shift closure;
3. finite canonical/de Branges transfer systems closing the completed carrier in finitely many
   shifted differential coordinates;
4. finite-state explicit-formula or Weil readers replacing fresh prime input by a fixed nonlinear
   algebraic shift recurrence;
5. Nyman--Beurling reductions only when they first factor the completed carrier through such a
   finite complex-shift differential state system.

It does not exclude the full Li, Hilbert--Polya, Connes, de Branges, Weil, explicit-formula, or
Nyman--Beurling routes.  Those routes can retain genuinely infinite-dimensional information and do
not require a finite algebraic state law for `xi'/xi`.

## Attack as false

1. **Torsion is fatal.** If `a` has finite order, far disjoint copies along `<a>` do not exist and
   the theorem can fail.
2. **One increment being HT is insufficient.** The full translate orbit of the increment must be
   differentially algebraically independent.  Binary HT alone cannot support the forest rank.
3. **Finite group or finite orbit is insufficient.** Codimension amplification needs arbitrarily
   many disjoint translated copies.
4. **Complex xi shifts require the stronger parent.** Nagoshi alone supplies real shifts.  The
   prime-feature polarization theorem supplies the nonlinear arbitrary-complex increment orbit.
5. **No categorical RH-route claim.** Only implementations containing the stated finite closure
   are excluded.

## Novelty and significance gate

### Significance `[PASS]`

The theorem converts one atomic constraint direction into a complete nonlinear translation-orbit
matroid.  In the xi specialization it transfers the prime-feature theorem to every finite complex
shift configuration, a materially larger class than holonomic or linear filters.

### Novelty `[FAIL]`

The exact finite inequality `Nm-C<=Nr` was not found in the searched literature, and
Hardouin--Singer's additive theorem works over a field already containing the increments rather
than over the smaller field used here.  Nevertheless, the qualitative implication is the standard
maximal-transformal-differential-dimension principle applied to the inclusion

`K<(T_a-1)u>_(D,G) subset K<u>_(D,G)`.

The repeated-copy forest is an elementary finite witness for that monotonicity, not a new
scientific mechanism.  Under the project's routine-parent test the abstract law is therefore a
bridge.  Its completed-xi consequence inherits whatever novelty survives in the separate
prime-feature polarization theorem; it is not independently new.
