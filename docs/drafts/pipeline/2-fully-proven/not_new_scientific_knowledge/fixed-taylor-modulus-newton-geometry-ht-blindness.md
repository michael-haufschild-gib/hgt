- **Statement:** Every admissible infinite Taylor-modulus profile has a hypertranscendental phase realization. In particular, one DA entire function and one HT entire function can have identical coefficient moduli, maximum-term function, logarithmic Newton envelope, active indices, and plateau widths.

## Goal

Separate the data carried by logarithmic Newton geometry from the data carried by an algebraic differential equation. Newton polygons see only coefficient magnitudes. Hypertranscendence can instead be carried entirely by coefficient phases.

This is a general statement about entire HT functions. Its RH relevance is only the downstream exclusion of implementations that try to infer DA versus HT from maximum-term or coefficient-magnitude geometry alone.

## Theorem 1: every modulus profile has an HT realization `[PROVED HERE]`

Let `(rho_n)_(n>=0)` be nonnegative real numbers with infinite support and

`limsup_(n->infinity) rho_n^(1/n)=0`.                                      `(1)`

Then there are unit complex numbers `u_n`, for every `n` in the support, such that

`F(z)=sum_(n>=0) rho_n u_n z^n`                                            `(2)`

is an entire function hypertranscendental over `C(z)`.

The coefficient magnitudes of `F` are exactly `rho_n`. Consequently its maximum-term function

`mu_F(r)=max_n rho_n r^n`,                                                   `(3)`

its logarithmic Newton envelope, all active indices, all transition radii, and all plateau widths are prescribed by `(rho_n)`.

### Proof

Let `S={n:rho_n>0}` and let

`K=Q(rho_n:n in S)`.

The field `K` is countable. Choose the `u_n` inductively on the unit circle so that the family `(u_n)_(n in S)` is algebraically independent over `K`. At every finite stage the algebraic closure of the field generated by the previous choices is countable, while the unit circle is uncountable, so the induction continues.

The coefficients `a_n=rho_nu_n`, `n in S`, are algebraically independent over `K`: a polynomial relation among finitely many `a_n` becomes a polynomial relation among the corresponding `u_n` after the nonzero scalings `X_n |-> rho_nX_n`, whose coefficients remain in `K`.

Condition (1) gives infinite radius of convergence, so (2) is entire.

Suppose that `F` were DA over `C(z)`. Put

`G(s)=F(exp(-s))=sum_(n>=0) a_n exp(-ns)`.                                  `(4)`

Composition with `exp(-s)` carries a differential equation for `F` to one for `G` over the differential field `C(s,exp(s))`. Since `exp(s)` is DA over `C(s)`, differential-algebraic transitivity would make `G` DA over `C(s)`.

Ostrowski's finite-basis theorem for convergent Dirichlet series then puts all coefficients `a_n` in a field generated over `Q` by finitely many complex numbers. Such a field has finite transcendence degree over `Q`. This contradicts the algebraic independence of the infinite family `(a_n)_(n in S)`. Hence `F` is HT. `square`

## Theorem 2: one exact Newton profile supports both DA and HT `[PROVED HERE]`

There are entire functions `F_DA,F_HT` such that:

1. `F_DA` is DA and `F_HT` is HT over `C(z)`;
2. every Taylor coefficient is nonzero and

   `|[z^n]F_DA|=|[z^n]F_HT|=1/n!` for every `n>=0`;

3. the two functions have identical maximum-term functions, logarithmic Newton envelopes, active-index sequences, transition radii, and plateau widths;
4. both have order one and exponential type one.

### Proof

Take

`F_DA(z)=exp(z)`.

It satisfies `F_DA'-F_DA=0`. Apply Theorem 1 to `rho_n=1/n!` and call the resulting phase-twisted series `F_HT`.

The equality of all coefficient moduli gives item 2 and every Newton-geometric assertion in item 3. The coefficient formulas for order and type give item 4. `square`

For this profile the Newton lines are

`ell_n(t)=nt-log(n!)`,

and the transition between `n` and `n+1` occurs at `t=log(n+1)`. The DA and HT realizations therefore have literally the same complete logarithmic Newton diagram; only the coefficient phases differ.

## Consequences for the super-Gaussian attack

1. **Newton trapping cannot imply DA or recurrence.** A fixed algebraic locus for exponent gaps and normalized magnitudes has no access to phase transcendence.
2. **Newton geometry alone cannot supply the coefficient-floor theorem.** The DA hypothesis must enter through exact complex coefficient identities or an equivalent phase-sensitive consequence. Convex geometry and maximum-term asymptotics can organize those identities but cannot replace them. This does not rule out a proof that combines `(SG)` with such ADE-specific information.
3. **The algebraically independent super-Gaussian example is HT.** Taking `rho_n=exp(-n^3)` gives an HT entire series with every Newton transition adjacent and every plateau width asymptotic to `6n`, after bounded phase perturbations of the logarithmic data are removed by choosing unit phases. Thus exceptionally simple Newton geometry is compatible with genuine hypertranscendence.

The last item should not be confused with a counterexample to the super-Gaussian DA coefficient-floor conjecture: it lies on the predicted HT side.

## Exact RH-route scope

This theorem excludes only universal implementations of heat, Wiman--Valiron, or coefficient-Newton routes whose proposed DA/HT decision, across all entire functions, factors through Taylor coefficient magnitudes, maximum terms, active indices, transition radii, or plateau widths. The profile `1/n!` already has both a DA and an HT realization, so no universal classifier can factor through those data alone.

It does not refute the restricted super-Gaussian conjecture: it produces an HT realization of every super-Gaussian profile, but it does not produce a DA realization of such a profile. A proof of the restricted conjecture must still use what an assumed ADE forces beyond the shared modulus data.

It does not exclude any complete RH criterion, any method using the exact complex coefficients or Euler source, or any argument that genuinely evaluates a differential polynomial rather than only its tropical magnitude shadow.
