- **Statement:** The absolute-Euler boundary densities of the completed logarithmic derivative at

## Setup

Put

```text
X(w)=xi(1/2+w),
L(w)=X'(w)/X(w).                                           (1)
```

For `a>1/2`, let

```text
h_a(t)=Re L(a+it).                                         (2)
```

This is positive on the absolute-Euler line.  For nodes `w_j` with `Re(w_j)>a` and coefficients
`c_j`, denote the deficit in the Euler-safe Cauchy-loss inequality by

```text
O_a(c,w)
 =integral_R h_a(t)|sum_j c_j/(w_j-a-it)|^2 dt

  -2a integral_R h_a(t) integral_0^infinity
       |sum_j c_j exp(-w_j x)/(w_j-a-it)|^2 dx dt.         (3)
```

The parent theorem proves algebraically that

```text
O_a(c,w)
 =pi sum_(j,k)c_j conjugate(c_k)
       [L(w_j)+conjugate(L(w_k))]
       /(w_j+conjugate(w_k)).                              (4)
```

In particular `(4)` is already independent of `a`.  The purpose here is to identify the exact
source-side mechanism behind that independence.

## Tail-Laplace conjugation

Define

```text
g_a(y)=sum_j c_j exp(-(w_j-a)y),
f(y)=exp(-ay)g_a(y)=sum_j c_j exp(-w_j y).                 (5)
```

The elementary Laplace identities

```text
sum_j c_j/(w_j-a-it)
 =integral_0^infinity exp(ity)g_a(y)dy,                    (6)

sum_j c_j exp(-w_j x)/(w_j-a-it)
 =exp(-(a+it)x) integral_x^infinity exp(ity)g_a(y)dy       (7)
```

are exact.  Expanding the squares in `(3)`, interchanging the absolutely convergent integrals,
and integrating first in `x` gives

```text
2a integral_0^min(y,z) exp(-2ax)dx
 =1-exp(-2a min(y,z)).                                    (8)
```

Therefore the subtraction in `(3)` retains precisely the complementary factor

```text
exp(-2a min(y,z))
 =exp(-ay)exp(-az)exp(a|y-z|).                            (9)
```

Use the Fourier convention

```text
r_a(u)=(1/(2pi)) integral_R h_a(t)exp(itu)dt               (10)
```

in the tempered-distribution sense.  Equations `(5)`--`(10)` yield the exact finite-exponential
quadratic identity

```text
O_a(c,w)
 =2pi integral_(0,infinity) integral_(0,infinity)
      f(y)conjugate(f(z)) exp(a|y-z|)r_a(y-z) dy dz.       (11)
```

Equation `(11)` is first understood through its left side, which is an ordinary convergent
integral.  This avoids an illegitimate unrestricted multiplication of a tempered distribution by
the nonsmooth exponentially growing function `exp(a|u|)` at `u=0`.

## Poisson stationarity

Let `b>a>1/2`.  The function `Re L` is positive harmonic on `Re(w)>1/2`.  Its logarithmic growth
excludes a linear Herglotz mass at infinity.  Applying the half-plane Poisson representation first
on the line `Re(w)=a` and then on `Re(w)=b` gives

```text
h_b=P_(b-a)*h_a,                                          (12)
```

where

```text
P_c(t)=c/(pi(c^2+t^2)).                                   (13)
```

Fourier transformation of `(12)` gives the distributional semigroup law

```text
r_b(u)=exp(-(b-a)|u|)r_a(u).                              (14)
```

Consequently, on every autocorrelation generated by the finite exponential span in `(5)`,

```text
exp(b|u|)r_b(u)=exp(a|u|)r_a(u).                          (15)
```

The equality in `(15)` is an equality of the induced quadratic forms, defined by `(11)`, rather
than a claim that both raw products exist on the full Schwartz dual.  Denote this common form by

```text
W_Xi(f)=O_a(c,w)/(2pi).                                   (16)
```

It is independent of every safe offset for which the chosen nodes lie in the corresponding
half-plane.  Thus the family `h_a` is a forward Poisson orbit, while the Cauchy-loss subtraction
performs exactly the inverse conjugation that recovers the stationary critical form `W_Xi`.

## Prime weights lose the safe offset exactly

On `Re(s)=1/2+a>1`, the prime part of `(2)` is

```text
-sum_(n>=2) Lambda(n)n^(-1/2-a) cos(t log n).              (17)
```

Its contribution to `r_a` consists of the two atoms at `u=+-log n`, each with weight

```text
-(1/2)Lambda(n)n^(-1/2-a).                                (18)
```

The inverse-Poisson factor in `(11)` evaluates there to

```text
exp(a|+-log n|)=n^a.                                      (19)
```

Multiplying `(18)` by `(19)` leaves the offset-free critical weights

```text
-(1/2)Lambda(n)n^(-1/2).                                  (20)
```

The pole, normalization, and Gamma terms undergo the same Poisson transport.  More invariantly,
equations `(4)` and the two-channel resolvent--Weil identity identify the whole stationary form
`W_Xi`, including its contact term at `u=0`, with the completed Weil distribution restricted to
the finite exponential/resolvent test span.  No separate product of distributions is needed for
this identification.

## Exact RH chain

Combining `(3)`, `(4)`, and the complete-detector theorem gives, for any one `a>1/2`,

```text
O_a(c,w)>=0 for every finite safe node/coefficient system

  iff W_Xi is nonnegative on the finite exponential span

  iff the critical logarithmic-derivative kernel is positive

  iff RH.                                                  (21)
```

The content of the theorem is not a new equivalent formulation in `(21)`.  It is the value
relation `(11)`--`(15)`: all apparently different absolute-Euler source problems are the same
stationary constraint in diagonal coordinates.

## Positive research consequence

The offset `a` is not a tunable small parameter.  Taking `a` farther into absolute convergence
makes `h_a` smoother and its prime coefficients smaller, but the tail-Laplace loss simultaneously
applies the exact inverse factor `exp(a|u|)`.  The two effects cancel prime power by prime power.

Accordingly, a proof attempt should not seek a favorable large-`a` perturbation.  It must prove an
orientation theorem that survives de-Poissonization, for example a source factorization of the
stationary form `W_Xi`.  This is a positive uniqueness statement:

```text
every Euler-safe Cauchy-loss criterion is one congruent slice
of the unique completed critical Weil constraint.         (22)
```

This also connects the safe Cauchy-loss route to the existing complete Weil and central Hankel
interfaces without asserting that their missing positivity has been supplied.

## Distributional guardrail

The boundary density `h_a(t)` has logarithmic growth and is not integrable.  Hence `r_a` in `(10)`
is a tempered distribution, not an ordinary function.  The expression `exp(a|u|)r_a(u)` must not
be declared globally without choosing a test space and treating the contact term at `u=0`.
Equations `(3)`, `(11)`, and `(16)` provide the canonical rigorous definition on the finite
exponential span needed by the RH consumer.  Any extension to a larger completed source space
requires a separate continuity theorem.
