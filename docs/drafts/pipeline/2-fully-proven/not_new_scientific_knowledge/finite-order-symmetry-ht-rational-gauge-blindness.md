## Carrier theorem `[PROVED HERE]`

With `w=s-1/2` and real `alpha>0` satisfying `2 alpha-1 notin Z`, set

`C_alpha(s)=1/(Gamma(alpha+i w) Gamma(alpha-i w))`.

Then:

1. `C_alpha` is entire of order exactly one;
2. its zeros are the simple points `1/2 +- i(n+alpha)`, `n>=0`, all on the critical line;
3. `C_alpha(1-s)=C_alpha(s)` and
   `C_alpha(conj s)=conj(C_alpha(s))`;
4. `C_alpha` is hypertranscendental over `C(s)`.

### Proof

The reciprocal gamma function is entire of order one and has simple zeros at the nonpositive
integers.  Therefore the displayed product is entire of order at most one and has exactly the zeros
in item 2.  Their exponent of convergence is one, so the order is at least one and hence exactly
one.  Replacing `w` by `-w` swaps the factors, proving the functional equation.  Since `alpha` is
real and `Gamma(conj z)=conj(Gamma(z))`, conjugating also swaps the two factors, proving Schwarz
reality.

Translate by `i`.  The gamma recurrence gives

`C_alpha(s+i)=A_alpha(s) C_alpha(s)`,

where

`A_alpha(s)=(alpha+i w-1)/(alpha-i w)`.                         (1)

The multiplier has one simple zero at `w=i(alpha-1)` and one simple pole at `w=-i alpha`.  These
points belong to the same orbit under `w |-> w+i` exactly when `2 alpha-1 in Z`.  By hypothesis they
belong to distinct shift orbits, so the divisor of `A_alpha` has nonzero degree on each of two
orbits.  It is not a constant times a rational shift coboundary.

The rank-one rational-shift hypertranscendence criterion of Hardouin--Singer now applies to (1): a
nonzero meromorphic solution for a non-coboundary rational multiplier is differentially
transcendental over `C(s)`.  Hence `C_alpha` is hypertranscendental. `QED`

## Finite-order same-field opposite-divisor theorem `[PROVED HERE]`

Let `G` be the four-element group generated by `s |-> 1-s` and complex conjugation.  Let `A subset
C` be any finite `G`-invariant set and let `nu:A -> N` be invariant.  There exists a nonzero
polynomial `P` such that

1. `P(1-s)=P(s)` and `P(conj s)=conj(P(s))`;
2. `(P-1)^(j)(a)=0` for every `a in A` and `0<=j<nu(a)`;
3. `P` has a zero off the critical line.

Consequently, with

`F_crit=C_alpha`, `F_off=P C_alpha`,

the following all hold:

1. both functions are entire, order-one, and hypertranscendental;
2. both obey the functional equation and Schwarz reality;
3. their derivatives agree through order `nu(a)-1` at every `a in A`;
4. the divisor of `F_crit` lies on the critical line, while `F_off` has an off-line zero;
5. their complete differential-translation fields are not merely isomorphic but equal:

   `C(s)(D^n T_h F_crit:h in C,n>=0)`

   ` = C(s)(D^n T_h F_off:h in C,n>=0)`;                       (2)

6. their Nevanlinna characteristics differ by only `O(log r)`, and their zero counts differ by a
   bounded amount.  Thus they have the same order and the same leading coarse growth and zero-density
   data.

### Proof

Set

`H(s)=prod_(a in A)(s-a)^(2 nu(a))`.

The even exponents and `G`-invariance give `H(1-s)=H(s)` and
`H(conj s)=conj(H(s))`.  Choose a point `rho` off the critical line and outside `A`, with a free
`G`-orbit, and choose a second free orbit disjoint from it.  Equivariant finite Hermite
interpolation supplies a symmetric Schwarz-real polynomial `q` satisfying

`q(rho)=-1/H(rho)`

and vanishing at a representative of the second orbit.  Put `P=1+Hq`.  Then `P` is not the zero
polynomial, has the two symmetries, agrees with `1` to the required orders at `A`, and vanishes at
`rho`.  This proves the polynomial assertion.

The carrier theorem gives all claims about `F_crit`.  A polynomial factor cannot increase the order
past one, and it cannot cancel any carrier zero, so `F_off` also has order exactly one.  If `F_off`
were differentially algebraic, division by the nonzero rational function `P` would make `C_alpha`
differentially algebraic, a contradiction.  The symmetry and passport statements follow from the
corresponding properties of `P`, and `C_alpha(rho)!=0` because `rho` is off the critical line.

For every `h in C`,

`T_h F_off(s)=P(s+h) T_h F_crit(s)`.

The coefficient `P(s+h)` is a nonzero element of the base field `C(s)` and hence invertible there.
Each translated carrier therefore belongs to the field generated by the translated `F_off`, and
conversely.  Closure under `D` proves (2), and the same identity respects every translation.  Finally,
standard Nevanlinna product inequalities applied in both directions to the rational factor `P`
give

`T(r,F_off)=T(r,F_crit)+O(log r)`.

The polynomial adds only finitely many zeros, so the unintegrated zero-count difference is bounded.
`QED`

## Exact route consequence

Finite order does not repair differential-translation divisor blindness.  Even after adding the
scalar functional equation, Schwarz reality, an arbitrary finite analytic passport, order one, and
leading coarse growth/zero-density data, the complete differential-translation field cannot decide
whether all zeros lie on the critical line.

This excludes any implementation of a Li, Weil, Hilbert--Polya, Connes, de Branges, or
Nyman--Beurling route whose proposed selector is asserted to follow only from that package.  It does
not exclude the complete named criteria: their exact positivity forms, canonical-product data,
Hilbert norms, spectral measures, or Euler normalization distinguish information not retained in
