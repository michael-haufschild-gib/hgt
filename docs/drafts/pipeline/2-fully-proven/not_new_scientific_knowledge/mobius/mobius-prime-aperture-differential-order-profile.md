- **Statement:** The squared norm of the normalized Möbius channel carried by prime coordinates
  `p<=N` has an exact Euler-product expression.  In the critical scaling
  `N=exp(c/(2omega))`, its retained norm fraction tends to `exp(-E_1(c))`, where `E_1` is the

## 1. Prime-supported channel mass

For `omega>0`, put

```text
sigma=1+2omega.                                           (1)
```

The normalized squared-coordinate law of the Möbius channel is

```text
P_omega{d}
 =mu(d)^2d^(-sigma)
   /sum_(n>=1)mu(n)^2n^(-sigma).                          (2)
```

Let

```text
M_omega(N)
 =P_omega{every prime divisor of d is <=N}.              (3)
```

Then

```text
M_omega(N)=product_(p>N)(1+p^(-1-2omega))^(-1).          (4)
```

### Proof

The squarefree Dirichlet series has the Euler product

```text
sum_(d>=1)mu(d)^2d^(-sigma)=product_p(1+p^(-sigma)).     (5)
```

Restricting to integers whose prime divisors are at most `N` replaces `(5)` by the finite product
over `p<=N`.  Dividing by `(5)` gives `(4)`.

For every nonzero Hilbert-space input `f`, `(4)` is exactly the fraction of
`||C_omega f||^2` lying in divisor channels generated by the finite prime set `{p:p<=N}`.  Thus it
measures the actual finite-prime state retained by the orthogonal Möbius analysis, not merely a
counting density of integers.

## 2. Critical prime-aperture theorem

For fixed `c>0`, let

```text
N_omega=exp(c/(2omega)).                                  (6)
```

Define

```text
E_1(c)=integral_c^infinity e^(-u)du/u.                   (7)
```

Then

```text
M_omega(N_omega)->exp(-E_1(c))                           (8)
```

as `omega` decreases to zero.

### Proof

Taking logarithms in `(4)` gives

```text
log M_omega(N)
 =-sum_(p>N)log(1+p^(-1-2omega)).                        (9)
```

The quadratic remainder is negligible at `(6)`:

```text
0<=sum_(p>N_omega)
  (p^(-1-2omega)-log(1+p^(-1-2omega)))
 <=sum_(n>N_omega)n^(-2)->0.                             (10)
```

It remains to evaluate the prime sum.  The prime number theorem and partial summation give

```text
sum_(p>N_omega)p^(-1-2omega)
 ~ integral_(N_omega)^infinity x^(-1-2omega)dx/log x.    (11)
```

The approximation is valid in this joint limit because `N_omega->infinity`; the PNT relative
error is uniformly small for every `x>=N_omega`.  With the substitution

```text
u=2omega log x,                                          (12)
```

the integral in `(11)` becomes exactly

```text
integral_c^infinity e^(-u)du/u=E_1(c).                   (13)
```

Equations `(9)`--`(13)` yield

```text
log M_omega(N_omega)->-E_1(c),                           (14)
```

which proves `(8)`.

## 3. Quantile law

The function

```text
Q(c)=exp(-E_1(c))                                        (15)
```

is continuous and strictly increasing from zero to one.  Therefore for every desired retained
fraction `q in (0,1)` there is a unique `c_q>0` satisfying

```text
exp(-E_1(c_q))=q.                                        (16)
```

A prime cutoff

```text
N_omega(q)=exp(c_q/(2omega))                             (17)
```

then retains asymptotic channel fraction `q`.

This is sharper than the divisor-size law `2omega log d=>Exp(1)`.  A divisor cutoff and a prime
support cutoff are different carriers: `(8)` computes the latter exactly.

Full mass transport has the equivalent cofinal condition

```text
M_omega(N_omega)->1
  iff 2omega log N_omega->infinity.                       (18)
```

For the forward implication, if `2omega log N_omega` had a bounded subsequence, monotonicity and
`(8)` would bound the retained mass away from one on that subsequence.  Conversely, if the scaled
cutoff tends to infinity, comparison with every fixed `c` and then `c->infinity` gives full mass.

## 4. Exact scalar differential-order cost

Let

```text
S_(N_omega)={p prime:p<=N_omega}.                         (19)
```

Every first-order-faithful rational scalar readout of these Euler coordinates has exact
differential aperture

```text
ap=#S_(N_omega)=pi(N_omega),                             (20)
```

both over `C` and over `C(s)<Gamma>`.  The prime number theorem and `(17)` give

```text
ap_q(omega)
 ~ (2omega/c_q)exp(c_q/(2omega))                         (21)
```

for retention fraction `q`.

Thus the critical channel has a quantitative state-price curve:

```text
retained norm fraction q
  <-> scaled prime aperture c_q
  <-> scalar differential order
      (2omega/c_q)exp(c_q/(2omega)).                      (22)
```

For full mass transport, `(18)` and the PNT imply

```text
omega log ap(omega)->infinity.                           (23)
```

No fixed-order, polynomial-order, or `exp(O(1/omega))` family with bounded exponent constant can
retain the complete critical Möbius channel.  A successful construction must instead expose the
same state through a genuinely infinite-dimensional or global carrier.

## 5. Hilbert--Pólya consequence

At every positive shift, the normalized Möbius analysis is contractive.  The theorem identifies
the exact number of independent prime coordinates which a finite approximation must preserve
before the complementary floor--tail incidence and archimedean boundary renormalization are
applied.

Accordingly, a finite-prime pole-to-divisor transfer retaining fraction `q` of the normalized
source must possess at least one of the following equivalent resources:

1. a first-order-faithful scalar differential aperture asymptotic to `(21)`;
2. that many visible prime coordinates in a growing operator, determinant, or coefficient state;
3. an infinite-dimensional/global carrier whose finite-prime compressions reproduce `(8)`.

Gamma completion and rational determinant formation cannot lower `(20)`.  They may only move the
coordinates between scalar syntax and exposed state.  This is an exact positive carrier law, not a
claim that finite-prime Hilbert--Pólya approximation is impossible.

The remaining RH-bearing theorem is still the nonexpansive archimedean covariance on this carrier.
Equation `(8)` now supplies its mandatory cutoff scaling: a contraction verified on smaller prime
apertures does not control the desired fraction of the critical source norm.
