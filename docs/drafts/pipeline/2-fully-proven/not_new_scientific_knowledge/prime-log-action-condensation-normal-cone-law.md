## Discovery


They are the same law in two charts.  At fixed \(s\), the shell is a complete exponential

## 1. Complete fixed-coordinate action expansion

For \(m\geq1\), put
\[
 B_m(s)={1\over\zeta(ms)},
 \qquad
 H_m(s)=B_{m+1}(s)-B_m(s)
 ={1\over\zeta((m+1)s)}-{1\over\zeta(ms)}.
\tag{1}
\]
If \(\sigma=\Re s>0\) and \(m\sigma>1\), absolute convergence of the reciprocal-zeta Dirichlet
series gives
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 H_m(s)
 =
 \sum_{n\geq2}
 c_n(s)e^{-ms\log n},
 \qquad
 c_n(s)=\mu(n)(n^{-s}-1).
 \quad}
\tag{2}
\]
For \(0<\Re s<1\), \(c_n(s)\ne0\) exactly when \(n\) is squarefree and \(n\geq2\).

Equation `(2)` is exact, not a formal transseries.  It equips the depth tail with the ordered
action spectrum
\[
 {\cal A}_{\rm sf}
 =
 \{\log n:n\geq2,\ n\ {\rm squarefree}\}.
\tag{3}
\]

## 2. Every action coefficient is recoverable

`[PAPER-PROVED] [PRIME_LOG_DEPTH_ACTION_EXTRACTION]`

Fix \(s\) with \(\Re s>0\).  For every integer \(N\geq2\),
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \lim_{m\to\infty}
 N^{ms}
 \left(
 H_m(s)
 -
 \sum_{2\leq n<N}c_n(s)n^{-ms}
 \right)
 =
 c_N(s).
 \quad}
\tag{4}
\]

### Proof

After inserting `(2)`, the left side of `(4)` is
\[
 c_N(s)
 +
 \sum_{n>N}c_n(s)(N/n)^{ms}.
\tag{5}
\]
Since \(|c_n(s)|\leq2\), it is enough to prove
\[
 \sum_{n>N}(N/n)^{m\sigma}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{6}
\]
For \(a=m\sigma>1\), the integral test gives
\[
 \sum_{n>N}(N/n)^a
 \leq
 \left({N\over N+1}\right)^a
 +
 N^a\int_{N+1}^\infty x^{-a}\,dx
\tag{7}
\]
and the right side tends to zero as \(a\to\infty\).  This proves `(4)`. `QED`

Consequently no finite leading-action truncation is the complete source.  Repeated asymptotic
deflation recovers every squarefree integer coefficient and hence every prime generator.

## 3. Diagonal normal blow-up condenses all actions

Let
\[
 \varepsilon_m={1\over m+1},
 \qquad
 s=\varepsilon_m u.
\tag{8}
\]
Each individual action in `(2)` changes scale:
\[
 e^{-ms\log n}
 =
 n^{-(1-\varepsilon_m)u}
 \longrightarrow n^{-u}.
\tag{9}
\]
The coefficient simultaneously satisfies
\[
 {c_n(\varepsilon_m u)\over\varepsilon_m}
 =
 \mu(n){n^{-\varepsilon_m u}-1\over\varepsilon_m}
 \longrightarrow
 -u\,\mu(n)\log n.
\tag{10}
\]
For \(\Re u>1\), dominated convergence in the Dirichlet series gives
\[
 {H_m(\varepsilon_m u)\over\varepsilon_m}
 \longrightarrow
 -u\sum_{n\geq2}\mu(n)\log n\,n^{-u}
 =
 u\left({1\over\zeta}\right)'(u).
\tag{11}
\]

The branch-free normalized form is sharper.  Since
\[
 B_{m+1}(\varepsilon_m u)={1\over\zeta(u)},
\qquad
 B_m(\varepsilon_m u)={1\over\zeta((1-\varepsilon_m)u)},
\tag{12}
\]
one has
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 {1\over\varepsilon_m}
 {H_m(\varepsilon_m u)\over B_{m+1}(\varepsilon_m u)}
 =
 {1\over\varepsilon_m}
 \left(
 1-{\zeta(u)\over\zeta((1-\varepsilon_m)u)}
 \right)
 \longrightarrow
 -u{\zeta'(u)\over\zeta(u)}.
 \quad}
\tag{13}
\]

Thus the Euler logarithmic connection is the normalized condensation current of the complete
maximum-exponent action spectrum.

## 4. Universal analytic normal-cone law

The mechanism does not depend on zeta.  Let \(F\) be holomorphic and nonvanishing near a compact
set containing \(u\) and \((1-\varepsilon)u\) for small \(\varepsilon\).  Put
\[
 B_m^F(s)={1\over F(ms)},
 \qquad
 H_m^F(s)=B_{m+1}^F(s)-B_m^F(s).
\tag{14}
\]
Then locally uniformly,
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 {1\over\varepsilon_m}
 {H_m^F(\varepsilon_m u)\over B_{m+1}^F(\varepsilon_m u)}
 \longrightarrow
 -u{F'(u)\over F(u)}.
 \quad}
\tag{15}
\]

Indeed the left side equals
\[
 {1\over\varepsilon_m}
 \left(
 1-{F(u)\over F((1-\varepsilon_m)u)}
 \right),
\tag{16}
\]
and `(15)` is the first-order Taylor formula.

For \(F=\Xi\), equation `(15)` is the completed depth connection

\tag{18}

Combining the endpoint-zero and endpoint-one normal cones produces the centered residue
