## Statement

Let the two-reading clock involution be `Jz=1-z` and define the multiplicative reflection
coordinates

\[
 P(z)=\Gamma(z)\Gamma(1-z),\qquad
 R(z)=\frac{\Gamma(z)}{\Gamma(1-z)}.
\]

Then

\[
 P(Jz)=P(z),\qquad R(Jz)=R(z)^{-1},                       \tag{1}
\]

and Euler's reflection formula gives

\[
 P(z)=\frac{\pi}{\sin \pi z}.                            \tag{2}
\]

Thus the invariant clock channel is differentially algebraic, while the anti-invariant
multiplicative reading `R` is hypertranscendental.  Moreover the reflected Gamma pair is algebraic
over the differential field generated by `R` and the elementary invariant `P`:

\[
 \Gamma(z)^2=P(z)R(z),\qquad
 \Gamma(1-z)^2=P(z)/R(z).                                \tag{3}
\]

Consequently `R` carries the complete differential-transcendence content of the two reflected
Gamma readings; the invariant product carries none.

## Proof

Equations `(1)` are immediate and `(2)` is Euler's reflection formula.  Since sine is
differentially algebraic and the meromorphic DA elements form a differential field, `P` is DA.

Assume that `R` were DA.  Then `P R=Gamma^2` would be DA.  The function `Gamma` is algebraic over
that differential field, as a root of `Y^2-P R`; algebraic extensions of DA differential fields
remain DA.  This contradicts Hölder's theorem.  Hence `R` is hypertranscendental.  Equations `(3)`
give the final field statement.

Equivalently, away from poles and zeros,

\[
 (\log R)'=\psi(z)+\psi(1-z),
\]

whereas the reflection constraint fixes the difference

\[
 \psi(1-z)-\psi(z)=\pi\cot(\pi z).
\]

The sum is therefore the unclosed observable and the difference is the elementary constraint.
