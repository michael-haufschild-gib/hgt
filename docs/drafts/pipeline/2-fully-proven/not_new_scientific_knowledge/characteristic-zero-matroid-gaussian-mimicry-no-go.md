## Goal

Decide whether the underlying finite matroid of connected-correlator dependencies can itself be the new physical invariant. It cannot. In characteristic zero, algebraic matroid independence has a linear model after extending the coefficient field, and a finite linear model can be specialized to complex coefficients and realized by Gaussian observables.

## Theorem `[PROVED HERE FROM PARENT THEOREMS]`

Let `K` be a subfield of `C`, let `L/K` be a characteristic-zero field extension, and let `E={x_1,...,x_n} ⊂ L` be finite. Let `M_E` be its algebraic matroid over `K`. Then:

1. `M_E` has a linear representation over a characteristic-zero extension field of `K`.
2. When `K=C`, `M_E` has a linear representation by a finite vector family `v_1,...,v_n ∈ C^r`.
3. If `g` is a nondegenerate standard complex Gaussian vector in `C^r` and

   ```text
   X_i = <v_i,g>,
   ```

   both the linear-dependence matroid and the algebraic matroid over `C` of the Gaussian linear forms `{X_i}` are exactly `M_E`.

The same conclusion applies to every finite minor of `M_E`, in particular to a restriction after contraction by a finite Gaussian/face base.

## Proof

### Algebraic-to-linear conversion

Ingleton's characteristic-zero theorem converts an algebraic representation into a linear representation over a suitable extension field. In modern language, choose a transcendence basis and map each algebraic representative `x_i` to its Kähler differential `d x_i`; separability in characteristic zero makes algebraic independence of a finite subset equivalent to linear independence of the corresponding differentials over the function field. Hence the vectors `d x_i` represent `M_E` linearly.

Algebraic matroids are closed under contraction and restriction. Equivalently, contraction by a base packet replaces the coefficient field by the field generated by that packet, after algebraic closure, and retains characteristic zero. Therefore the Wick-contracted finite interaction matroid remains algebraic and falls under the same linearization theorem.

### Specialization to complex coefficients

For `K=C`, the finitely many entries of the representing matrix lie in the fraction field of some finitely generated integral `C`-algebra `A`. After localizing `A` at the product of the finitely many minors required to be nonzero, the resulting finite-type `C`-algebra is nonzero. It therefore has a closed point, whose residue field is `C` by the weak Nullstellensatz. Evaluation at that point sends every identically zero minor to zero and preserves every required nonzero minor. The specialized complex matrix consequently represents exactly the same matroid.

### Gaussian mimic

Let the specialized columns be `v_i ∈ C^r`. For a nondegenerate Gaussian vector `g`, every deterministic linear relation among the random variables `X_i=<v_i,g>` is exactly a linear relation among the columns `v_i`: a coefficient vector `a` gives `sum_i a_i X_i=0` almost surely exactly when `sum_i a_i v_i=0`. Moreover a finite family of linear forms on `C^r` is algebraically independent exactly when it is linearly independent: after a linear change of coordinates, an independent family is a coordinate family. Thus the Gaussian linear observables have precisely the algebraic matroid `M_E`. ∎

## Consequence

The following proposed interaction test is false:

```text
nontrivial or exotic ordinary matroid of connected correlators
  implies genuinely nonlinear or non-Gaussian QFT interaction.
```

At every finite window, the same independent sets and circuits can occur in a linear Gaussian model. The combinatorial matroid does not retain the circuit equations, their coefficients, the original coefficient field, correlation order, derivative grading, scaling, phase, positivity, or directed persistence.

The positive successor must retain at least:

1. **decorated circuits:** the actual circuit polynomials or differential ideals, not only their supports;
2. **sector grading:** which labels are one-, two-, or higher connected reporters;
3. **vertical grading:** derivative order and the pro-jet structure;
4. **base data:** the face-complete Wick field and admissible counterterm class;
5. **valuation:** scaling/decay orders and RG asymptotics;
6. **phase and period closure:** information invisible to a plain chirotope;
7. **directed persistence:** survival under every cofinal source/base refinement.

An ordinary matroid remains a useful skeleton and supports exact contraction laws, but it cannot be the complete physical field.
