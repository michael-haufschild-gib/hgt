## Classification theorem `[PROVED HERE] [BRIDGE]`

Let `F : C^2 -> C` be entire and write `F_t(z)=F(t,z)`. Then

`DA(F) = {t in C : F_t is differentially algebraic over C(z)}`

is either all of `C` or a countable subset of `C`.

Conversely, for every countable set `A subset C`, there is an entire function

`G : C^2 -> C`

such that:

1. `G(t,z) != 0` for every `(t,z) in C^2`;
2. `G(t,z+1)=G(t,z)` for every `(t,z)`;
3. `G_t` is DA over `C(z)` exactly when `t in A`;
4. `G_t` is HT over `C(z)` for every `t notin A`.

Thus the alternatives are exact: the DA-fiber locus is either the whole parameter plane or an arbitrary countable set. In particular the exceptional set may be finite, empty, dense, nonclosed, or have arbitrary finite accumulation patterns.

## Proof of the upper bound `[SEARCH]`

Ostrowski's Theorem 13 states precisely the needed one-parameter rigidity: if one analytic fiber of an analytic two-variable function satisfies no algebraic differential equation in the fiber variable, then at most countably many analytic fibers satisfy such an equation. Applied to an entire `F(t,z)`, one HT fiber therefore makes `DA(F)` countable. If no HT fiber exists, every fiber is DA and `DA(F)=C`.

This is not a new theorem. It is the classical half of the classification.

## Construction of the sharp converse

### Vanishing polynomials

First suppose `A={a_1,a_2,...}` is countably infinite, enumerated without repetition, and put

`P_n(t) = product_(1 <= j <= n) (t-a_j)`.

If `A={a_1,...,a_N}` is finite and nonempty, instead put

`P_n(t) = product_(1 <= j <= min(n,N)) (t-a_j)`.

For `A` empty, put `P_n=1`. In every case,

- if `t in A`, then `P_n(t)=0` for every sufficiently large `n`;
- if `t notin A`, then `P_n(t)!=0` for every `n`.

Let `K=Q(A)`, the subfield of `C` generated by `A`. It is countable. Put

`M_n = max_(|t| <= n) |P_n(t)|`.

Choose inductively nonzero complex numbers `c_n`, algebraically independent over `K`, satisfying

`|c_n| <= 2^(-n) exp(-(n!)^2)/(1+M_n)`.

This is possible without probability: after finitely many choices, the algebraic closure in `C` of the countable field `K(c_1,...,c_(n-1))` is countable, whereas every punctured disk is uncountable.

Define

`Phi(t,z) = sum_(n>=1) c_n P_n(t) exp(2 pi i n! z)`.

### Joint entire convergence

Fix `R>0`. For `|t|,|z|<=R` and `n>=R`, the `n`th summand has modulus at most

`2^(-n) exp(-(n!)^2 + 2 pi R n!)`.

This majorant is summable. Hence the series converges locally uniformly on `C^2`; `Phi` is jointly entire. Every summand is `1`-periodic in `z`, so `Phi` is also `1`-periodic in `z`.

### Fibers on `A`

If `t=a_j`, all terms with sufficiently large index vanish. Thus `Phi_t` is a finite exponential polynomial. It satisfies a constant-coefficient linear differential equation, hence is D-finite and DA over `C(z)`.

### Fibers off `A`

Fix `t notin A` and write

`b_n = c_n P_n(t)`.

All `b_n` are nonzero. We claim that `(b_n)` has no finite basis in Ostrowski's sense. Suppose instead that there were finitely many complex numbers `kappa_1,...,kappa_r` such that every `b_n` belonged to `Q(kappa_1,...,kappa_r)`. Since `P_n(t)` belongs to `K(t)^x`, every

`c_n = b_n/P_n(t)`

would belong to `K(t,kappa_1,...,kappa_r)`. Therefore

`trdeg_(K(t)) K(t,c_1,c_2,...) <= r`.

This is impossible. The `c_n` are algebraically independent over `K`, so adjoining the single element `t` can reduce their infinite transcendence degree by at most one; equivalently, if the displayed transcendence degree were finite, the tower formula would make `trdeg_K K(t,c_1,c_2,...)` finite, although this field contains the infinite-transcendence-degree field `K(c_1,c_2,...)`.

Under the affine change `s=-2 pi i z`, the fiber becomes the convergent Dirichlet series

`Phi_t(-s/(2 pi i)) = sum_(n>=1) b_n exp(-n! s)`.

Ostrowski's Theorem 8 says that a convergent Dirichlet series whose coefficients have no finite basis satisfies no algebraic differential equation. Differential algebraicity is invariant under a nonzero affine change of the independent variable. Hence `Phi_t` is HT over `C(z)`.

We have proved

`{t : Phi_t is DA} = A`.

### Zero-free periodic strengthening

Set

`G(t,z)=exp(Phi(t,z))`.

Then `G` is jointly entire, zero-free, and `1`-periodic in `z`.

If `t in A`, then `Phi_t` is DA. The equation

`G_t' = Phi_t' G_t`

makes `G_t` DA over the differential field generated by the DA function `Phi_t`; differential-algebraic transitivity makes `G_t` DA over `C(z)`.

If `t notin A` and `G_t` were DA, its logarithmic derivative

`G_t'/G_t = Phi_t'`

would be DA. Then `Phi_t` would be DA as an antiderivative of a DA function, contradicting the preceding paragraph. Therefore `G_t` is HT exactly off `A`. This completes the proof. `square`

## Degenerate and boundary cases

1. `A=emptyset`: take `P_n=1`; the family is independent of `t` and every fiber is HT.
2. `A` finite: the stabilized finite product makes precisely those fibers truncate.
3. `A` dense countable: joint holomorphy does not force DA status to be locally constant; ADE complexity escapes along every accumulation.
4. `A=C`: a constant zero family supplies the all-DA alternative. This case cannot be included in the countable construction because `C` is uncountable.
5. The construction gives a common empty divisor and a common period `1`, but not a common *minimal* period: a truncated fiber may have additional periods.

## Holomorphic-deformation no-transition corollary

Let `F(t,z)` be jointly entire and suppose at least one fiber is HT. Then the set of DA fibers contains no nontrivial real interval, no complex arc, and no subset of `C` of positive planar measure. In particular there is no threshold `lambda` for which

`F_t is DA  iff  t is real and t >= lambda`.

The first sentence follows because every interval, arc, or positive-measure subset is uncountable, whereas Ostrowski makes the DA locus countable. The sharp realization proves the opposite boundary: after intervals are excluded, no residual topological ordering remains, because every countable pattern is possible.

Consequently a holomorphic deformation with an HT anchor cannot use DA status itself as a phase variable. It cannot identify DA fibers with the de Bruijn-Newman real-zero half-line, a positivity half-line, or any other interval-valued RH phase. Nor can it recover a distinguished threshold from isolated DA times without additional structure: those times can be prescribed arbitrarily by a zero-free periodic entire family.

## Exact scope for RH routes

The theorem does **not** say that de Bruijn-Newman, Hilbert-Polya, Connes, Nyman-Beurling, de Branges, Weil positivity, Li's criterion, or any other complete criterion cannot prove RH.

It rules out only implementations whose decisive information factors through the following data of a holomorphic deformation:

- which parameter fibers are DA rather than HT;
- whether that DA set is isolated, accumulating, dense, or nonclosed;
- the common absence of zeros of the represented auxiliary family;
- the common period `1`.

Those data admit arbitrary countable DA phase diagrams. Consequently a countable set of algebraically simplifying times, even a dense one, cannot by itself select an arithmetic transition or distinguish an RH-relevant deformation. A route must use structure absent from this theorem, such as its exact PDE or semigroup, positivity, norm geometry, spectral self-adjointness, prime-source coefficients, or the zeta functional equation.

Route-by-route, the proved exclusion is:

- **de Bruijn-Newman:** no jointly holomorphic HT-anchored implementation can equate its DA phase with the real-zero half-line or use DA status alone to locate the Newman threshold;
- **Hilbert-Polya / Connes:** no jointly holomorphic determinant or trace family with an HT fiber can acquire DA status on an open spectral or scaling phase; self-adjointness, positivity, and the actual spectrum remain outside the theorem;
- **de Branges / Weil positivity:** the theorem excludes only an added holomorphic deformation whose positive phase is claimed to coincide with DA status; it does not touch the original positivity criteria;
- **Nyman-Beurling / Li:** the theorem excludes only auxiliary holomorphic parameter families that try to select the critical parameter from their DA fibers; it does not touch norm closure or coefficient positivity themselves.
