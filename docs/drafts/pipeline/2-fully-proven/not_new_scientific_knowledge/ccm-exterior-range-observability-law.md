## Abstract range lemma

Let `H` be a Hilbert space and let `D:H->H` be bounded, self-adjoint, and injective. For `g in H`,
the following are equivalent:

```text
g in range D,                                               (1)

there exists C<infinity such that
  |<g,h>|<=C||Dh|| for every h in H.                        (2)
```

Moreover, if `g=Dx`, then the least constant in `(2)` is exactly `||x||`.

### Proof

If `g=Dx`, self-adjointness gives

```text
|<g,h>|=|<x,Dh>|<=||x||||Dh||.                             (3)
```

Conversely, define a functional on `range D` by

```text
ell(Dh)=<g,h>.                                              (4)
```

Injectivity makes `(4)` well-defined, and `(2)` makes it bounded. Extend it continuously to the
closure of `range D`, and then by zero on its orthogonal complement. Riesz representation gives an
`x in H` such that

```text
<g,h>=ell(Dh)=<x,Dh>=<Dx,h>                                (5)
```

for every `h`, so `g=Dx`. Since `D` is injective, the preimage is unique. Taking the supremum over
`h` in `(3)` after identifying `range D` with its dense closure gives the norm identity. `QED`

## CCM specialization

Fix an aperture `a>0`. Let

```text
I_a=(-a,a),
E_a=R minus [-a,a],
R_a=m_a(D),       m_a=1/c_a>0,
D_(a,E)=1_(E_a)R_a1_(E_a).                                (6)
```

The preceding graph-norm theorem proves that `D_(a,E)` is positive and injective with dense
nonclosed range. For an interior trace `f in L2(I_a)`, define its exterior forcing

```text
g_a(f)=-1_(E_a)R_a1_(I_a)f.                               (7)
```

Then the exterior support equation

```text
D_(a,E)v_E=g_a(f)                                         (8)
```

has a solution exactly when

```text
C_a(f):=
 sup_(0!=h in L2(E_a))
 |<f,1_(I_a)R_a1_(E_a)h>| / ||1_(E_a)R_a1_(E_a)h||
 <infinity.                                                (9)
```

When `(9)` holds, the exterior datum is unique and

```text
||v_E||=C_a(f).                                           (10)
```

### Proof

For exterior `h`, self-adjointness of `R_a` gives

```text
<g_a(f),h>=-<f,1_(I_a)R_a1_(E_a)h>.                       (11)
```

Apply the abstract lemma to `D_(a,E)` and `g_a(f)`. Equations `(9)`--`(10)` follow immediately.
`QED`

## Exact finite-input Evans reduction

Let `F_a` be the finite-dimensional characteristic trace space generated by the real zeros of the
corrected aperture symbol. Define

```text
F_a^adm={f in F_a:C_a(f)<infinity}.                       (12)
```

This is a linear subspace. For `f in F_a^adm`, let `v_E(f)` be the unique solution of `(8)`, put

```text
v(f)=f+v_E(f),
u(f)=R_a v(f).                                            (13)
```

Then `u(f)` has support in `[-a,a]`. Conversely, every characteristic pair with supported `u`
whose transformed trace `v=c_a(D)u` belongs to raw `L2` arises in this way. Thus support matching
in the raw-`L2` characteristic category is completely equivalent to the observability condition
`(9)`.

Because the bounded characteristic factor

```text
b_a(xi)=p_a(xi)/(1+xi^2)^(d_a/2)                         (14)
```

is nonlocal, `p_a(D)f=0` is only the local necessary condition obtained after clearing its
denominator. The complete Evans test on `F_a^adm` still consists of

```text
1_(I_a)b_a(D)v(f)=0,
integral_(I_a)u(f)=0,
(G_a u(f))(0)=0,
(G_a u(f))(a/2)=0.                                       (15)
```

Equations `(9)` and `(15)` are the exact finite-input/infinite-boundary formulation in that
category. The boundary state is no longer unspecified: it is the unique unbounded reconstruction
selected by `(9)`. Applying this reduction to every original `L2` screw null vector additionally

required `L2` regularity.
