## 1. Antisymmetric half-density value

For `n>=1`, let

```text
f_n(x)=thetaCuspSourceTerm x n,
A_n(z)=integral_(0,infinity) exp(z*x)*f_n(x) dx.
```

The Ward--Laplace anchors proved in the parent card are

```text
A_n( 1/2)=2*pi*n^2*exp(-pi*n^2),
A_n(-1/2)=(2*pi*n^2-1)*exp(-pi*n^2).                   (1)
```

Their sum gave the even half-density budget.  Their difference gives the independent
antisymmetric value relation

```text
2*integral_(0,infinity) sinh(x/2)*f_n(x) dx
  =exp(-pi*n^2).                                       (2)
```

Put

```text
M_(k,n)=integral_(0,infinity) x^k*f_n(x) dx.
```

All terms are nonnegative, so monotone convergence in the power series for `sinh` gives

```text
sum_(j>=0) M_(2*j+1,n)/(4^j*(2*j+1)!)
  =exp(-pi*n^2).                                       (3)
```

Together with the even budget

```text
2*sum_(j>=0) M_(2*j,n)/(4^j*(2*j)!)
  =(4*pi*n^2-1)*exp(-pi*n^2),                          (4)
```

this identifies both parity sectors of the source atom at the same reflected value pair.

Equivalently, if `nu_n` is the probability measure whose unnormalized density is
`cosh(x/2)*f_n(x)`, then

```text
E_(nu_n)[tanh(X/2)]=1/(4*pi*n^2-1).                    (5)
```

Equation `(5)` is a bounded orientation-sensitive value relation.  It is not inferred from a Xi
zero and contains no real-rootedness assumption.

## 3. A source-specific curvature floor

For the first source atom put `t=pi*exp(2*x)`.  Up to a positive constant,

Let `V=-log(f_1)`.  Direct differentiation gives


Set `y=2*t-3`.  Since `x>=0` and `pi>3`, one has `y>=3`.  The exact factorization

## 4. Half-line slope-curvature identity

Let `f` be a positive `C^2` density on `(0,infinity)` with finite moments of all orders and enough
decay that the following integrations by parts have no boundary term.  Write

```text
M_k=integral_(0,infinity) x^k*f(x) dx,
V=-log(f),
R(x)=V'(x)/x,
E(x)=R'(x).
```

For `m>=1` define the normalized Turan defect

```text
D_m=M_(2*m)^2
    -(2*m-1)/(2*m+1)*M_(2*m-2)*M_(2*m+2).              (14)
```

Then

```text
2*(2*m+1)*D_m
 = integral integral
     u^(2*m)*v^(2*m)*(v^2-u^2)*f(u)*f(v)
       *(integral_u^v E(t) dt) du dv.                  (15)
```

### Proof

Since `f'=-x*R*f`, integration by parts gives

```text
integral x^(2*m)*R(x)*f(x) dx=(2*m-1)*M_(2*m-2),
integral x^(2*m+2)*R(x)*f(x) dx=(2*m+1)*M_(2*m).       (16)
```

Replace the inner integral in `(15)` by `R(v)-R(u)`, expand the four separated terms, and apply
`(16)`.  The result is

```text
2*((2*m+1)*M_(2*m)^2-(2*m-1)*M_(2*m-2)*M_(2*m+2)),
```

which is exactly the left side of `(15)`.

This is the half-line form of the classical Csordas--Varga slope-curvature identity.  The proof
above records the boundary assumptions needed here rather than importing even-kernel hypotheses.

## 5. Boundary pole and odd determinant

For `f=f_1`, define the boundary score

```text
s_0=(log f_1)'(0)=9/2-2*pi+6/(2*pi-3).                 (17)
```

It is positive.  Indeed

```text
s_0=(-8*pi^2+30*pi-15)/(2*(2*pi-3)),
```

and the standard bounds `3<pi<22/7`, together with monotonicity of the numerator on that interval,
give a lower numerator bound `13/49`.

Because `V'(0)=-s_0`, the slope curvature splits exactly as

```text
R(x)=-s_0/x+R_reg(x),
E(x)=s_0/x^2+E_reg(x).                                 (18)
```

Substitution of the pole term into `(15)` gives

```text
D_m=P_m+G_m,                                           (19)

P_m=s_0/(2*m+1)
    *(M_(2*m-1)*M_(2*m+2)-M_(2*m)*M_(2*m+1)),          (20)
```

where `G_m` is the same double integral as `(15)`, divided by `2*(2*m+1)`, with `E_reg` in place
of `E`.  Moment-ratio monotonicity for a positive measure proves `P_m>=0`.

Thus the half-line boundary is favorable, not an uncontrolled defect.  Moreover all odd moments
appearing in `(20)` belong to the exact budget `(3)`.  The only non-value-theoretic remainder is
the regular curvature functional `G_m`.

## 6. One-seam curvature and positive crossing windows

The regular curvature in `(18)` has an exact one-seam sign law.  From `(11)`, with `y=2*t-3`,

```text
V'''(x)=4*(y+3)*(y^3-6*y-36)/y^3.                     (21)
```

The cubic `q(y)=y^3-6*y-36` is strictly increasing for `y>=3`, is negative at the initial value
`y(0)=2*pi-3`, and is positive at `y=4`.  For the middle assertion, use
`y(0)<23/7` and

```text
q(23/7)=-6943/343<0.
```

Hence `V'''` changes sign exactly once, from negative to positive.  Put

```text
N(x)=x*V''(x)-(V'(x)-V'(0)).                           (22)
```

Then `N(0)=0`, `N'(x)=x*V'''(x)`, and `(11)` gives `N(x)->infinity`.  Therefore there is a unique
`c>0` such that

```text
E_reg(x)=N(x)/x^2 < 0  for 0<x<c,
E_reg(c)=0,
E_reg(x)>0            for x>c.                        (23)
```

Thus the regular source curvature has one orientation seam; it is not an arbitrary signed error.

There is also an exact one-dimensional form of `G_m`.  Put `p=2*m` and define

```text
L_k(t)=integral_0^t x^k*f(x) dx,
U_k(t)=integral_t^infinity x^k*f(x) dx,
K_m(t)=L_p(t)*U_(p+2)(t)-L_(p+2)(t)*U_p(t).            (24)
```

Equivalently,

```text
K_m(t)=integral_(0<u<t<v)
         u^p*v^p*(v^2-u^2)*f(u)*f(v) du dv,            (25)
```

so `K_m(t)>0` for `t>0`.  Fubini in `(15)` gives

```text
G_m=1/(2*m+1)*integral_0^infinity E_reg(t)*K_m(t) dt.  (26)
```

Moreover direct differentiation of `(24)` collapses to

```text
K_m'(t)=t^(2*m)*f(t)*(M_(2*m+2)-t^2*M_(2*m)).          (27)
```

Hence `K_m` is strictly increasing up to

```text
tau_m=sqrt(M_(2*m+2)/M_(2*m))
```

and strictly decreasing afterwards.  Combining `(23)` and `(26)` yields the exact seam balance

```text
(2*m+1)*G_m
 = integral_c^infinity E_reg(t)*K_m(t) dt
   -integral_0^c (-E_reg(t))*K_m(t) dt.                (28)
```

The two open orders therefore differ only in which positive unimodal window, `K_1` or `K_2`,
tests the same signed one-seam curvature.  Ordinary moment log-convexity also gives

```text
tau_1<=tau_2,                                           (28a)
```

because `M_4^2<=M_2*M_6`.  Thus the second window peak is shifted weakly to the right of the first;
the unresolved issue is the quantitative balance across `c`, not the order of the windows.

## 7. Exact form of the two open gates

Return to `M_(2*j)=a_j/2` for the first atom and put




The lower source gate



The damped-tail theorem gives `H/W<8/2997<1/14`, so the coefficient on the right of `(23)` is

Combining `(19)`--`(20)` with `(29)`--`(31)`, the remaining low block is precisely


Both inequalities concern the same explicit function


with `V'` given by `(11)`.  This is the narrowed source problem.

## 9. Literature boundary

Csordas and Varga proved the classical slope-curvature representation and unconditional Turan
inequalities for the Riemann kernel.  Csordas and Dimitrov showed that an additional concavity of a
secondary curvature determinant implies double Turan inequalities; for the Riemann kernel they
state that secondary concavity as a problem/conjecture, not a theorem.  Therefore those papers
motivate `(15)` but do not discharge `(32)`--`(33)`.
