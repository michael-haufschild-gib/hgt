- **Statement:** The global-sign direction collapsed by the signed prime-zeta differential field

## Constraint

The raw signed prime-zeta field has the projective tail quotient

```text
epsilon ~ delta
  iff delta = epsilon eventually or delta = -epsilon eventually.
```

This quotient forgets a global sign.  Euler exponentiation is nonlinear and sees that sign.  The
object to determine is therefore not another independent orientation coordinate, but the exact
failure of Euler lifting to descend to the projective tail field.

## Euler lift

For `Re(s)>1`, define

```text
P_epsilon(s) = sum_p epsilon(p) p^(-s),
Z_epsilon(s) = prod_p (1-epsilon(p)p^(-s))^(-1).
```

The logarithm of `Z_epsilon` is the plethystic lift

```text
log Z_epsilon(s)
  = sum_(k>=1) (1/k) P_(epsilon^k)(k s).
```

Ordinary differential-field operations do not contain the dilation `s -> k s`; this is the first
place at which the projective sign can reappear.

## Cyclotomic norm law `[PROVED ON PAPER]`

For every orientation,

```text
Z_epsilon(s) Z_(-epsilon)(s) = zeta(2s),        Re(s)>1.       (1)
```

Indeed, prime by prime,

```text
(1-epsilon(p)x)(1+epsilon(p)x)=1-x^2.
```

The right side is independent of `epsilon`.  Thus every projective tail point has a canonical
unordered pair of Euler sheets with determinant `zeta(2s)`.

## Exact tail-field/Euler-gauge correspondence `[PROVED ON PAPER]`

Let `G_fin` be the multiplicative group of finite Euler gauges generated by the local factors
`1-p^(-s)` and `1+p^(-s)`.  Euler-factor uniqueness gives

```text
epsilon = delta eventually
  iff Z_delta/Z_epsilon belongs to G_fin,                    (2)

epsilon = -delta eventually
  iff Z_epsilon Z_delta/zeta(2s) belongs to G_fin.           (3)
```

Combining `(2)`--`(3)` with the exact signed prime-zeta tail-field theorem gives

```text
L_epsilon = L_delta
  iff
  Z_delta is a finite Euler gauge of Z_epsilon
  or
  Z_delta is a finite Euler gauge of zeta(2s)/Z_epsilon.      (4)
```

Thus raw differential-field equality has exactly two Euler modes: same-sheet gauge equivalence and
opposite-sheet norm complementarity.

## Divisor-polarity law `[PROVED ON PAPER]`

Suppose the relevant Euler sheets continue meromorphically near `s0`.  In the exact opposite-sheet
case, additivity of meromorphic order gives

```text
ord_(s0) Z_epsilon + ord_(s0) Z_(-epsilon)
  = ord_(s0) zeta(2s).                                      (5)
```

Consequently, away from the divisor of `zeta(2s)`,

```text
ord_(s0) Z_(-epsilon) = -ord_(s0) Z_epsilon.                 (6)
```

The projective tail field therefore forgets divisor polarity: a zero on one Euler sheet is a pole
of the same order on the other sheet.

For the canonical class,

```text
P_-(s) = -P_+(s),
Z_+(s) = zeta(s),
Z_-(s) = zeta(2s)/zeta(s).                                  (7)
```

Hence `P_+` and `P_-` generate the same raw differential field.  At every nontrivial zeta zero
`rho` with `Re(rho)>=1/2`, `zeta(2rho)` is finite and nonzero, so `rho` is a pole of `Z_-` with the
same multiplicity with which it is a zero of `Z_+`.

## Reflection-center descent `[PROVED ON PAPER]`

Let

```text
Lambda(s)=pi^(-s/2) Gamma(s/2) zeta(s).
```

The completed norm of the two Euler sheets is

```text
Lambda_2(s)
  = pi^(-s) Gamma(s) Z_epsilon(s)Z_(-epsilon)(s)
  = Lambda(2s).
```

Therefore

```text
Lambda_2(s)=Lambda_2(1/2-s),                                (8)
```

whose fixed vertical line is `Re(s)=1/4`.  Projectivizing the global sign and then taking the
Euler norm descends the scale-one reflection center `1/2` to the determinant center `1/4`.

2. **Known squarefree identity.**  The specialization `Z_- = zeta(2s)/zeta(s)` is classical and

3. **Field mismatch.**  The analytic field equivalence uses the signed prime-zeta tail theorem.

4. **RH-equivalence attack.**  No zero location is inferred.  The theorem instead proves that an
   unpointed field forgets even zero-versus-pole polarity, so it specifies missing structure rather
   than restating RH.

## Parent-theory verdict

```text
zeta_(-chi)(s)=zeta_(chi^2)(2s)/zeta_chi(s)
```

and immediately uses it to exchange the zeros and poles of opposite Euler sheets in
