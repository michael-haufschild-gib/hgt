## 1. Prime exponential fields

For `lambda in C`, define the prime exponential field

```text
chi_lambda(p)=p^lambda=exp(lambda log p).
```

The logarithmic Marcinkiewicz norm is

```text
||b||_M = sup_(X>=2) 1/log X *
            sum_(p<=X) (log p)/p |b_p|.
```

Write `M_0` for the closure of finite-support prime fields and

```text
Q_log=M_log/M_0.
```

The point-character trichotomy is

```text
Re lambda<0  -> chi_lambda in M_0,
Re lambda=0  -> chi_lambda in M_log\M_0,
Re lambda>0  -> chi_lambda notin M_log.                 (1)
```

This is the existing logarithmic-Marcinkiewicz boundary theorem, written in exponent notation.

## 2. One-parameter horizontal grading

Fix `a>0` and `t in R`.  Consider a finite prime exponential polynomial whose exponents lie in the
lattice generated by `a` and `it`:

```text
b_p = sum_((m,n) in F) c_(m,n) p^(m a+i n t),          (2)
```

where `F subset Z^2` is finite.  Combine equal exponent pairs first.  For every horizontal grade
`m`, put

```text
P_m(v)=sum_(n:(m,n) in F) c_(m,n) exp(i n t v).         (3)
```

Then

```text
b_p=sum_m p^(m a) P_m(log p).                           (4)
```

Let `m_*` be the largest grade for which `P_(m_*)` is not identically zero.

### Theorem A: leading-grade classification

The field (2) satisfies exactly one of the following:

```text
m_*<0  -> b in M_0;
m_*=0  -> b in M_log and [b]=[P_0(log p)] in Q_log;
m_*>0  -> b notin M_log.                                (5)
```

### Proof

If `m_*<0`, every term is bounded by a fixed finite sum of `p^(-epsilon)` terms for some
`epsilon>0`.  The weighted prime sum is absolutely convergent, so `b in M_0`.

Suppose `m_*=0`.  The lower grades again belong to `M_0`.  The grade-zero profile `P_0(log p)` is
bounded, hence belongs to `M_log`.  Therefore

```text
[b]=[P_0(log p)].                                       (6)
```

Now suppose `m_*>0`.  If `t=0`, the nonzero leading profile is constant and (4) has size comparable
to `p^(m_* a)` after the lower grades are absorbed, so its normalized weighted prime sum diverges.

If `t!=0`, the continuous function `|P_(m_*)(v)|` is periodic and has strictly positive mean over
one period.  On each final full period before `V=log X`, the leading part of (4) contributes a
positive constant times

```text
exp(m_* a V)
```

to the logarithmic integral

```text
integral^X |x^(m_* a)P_(m_*)(log x)| dx/x.
```

The lower grades are exponentially smaller on that period.  Prime-number-theorem partial
summation transfers the same lower bound to

```text
sum_(p<=X) (log p)/p |b_p|.
```

After division by `log X`, the result still tends to infinity.  Thus `b notin M_log`.  QED.

## 3. The FE--Schwarz quartet

Let

```text
rho=1/2+a+i t,       a>0.
```

Its FE--Schwarz quartet is

```text
rho, 1-rho, conj(rho), 1-conj(rho).
```

Attach the normalized prime character

```text
tau_z(p)=p^(1/2-z).
```

The quartet characters have exponents

```text
tau_rho              : -a-it,
tau_(conj rho)       : -a+it,
tau_(1-rho)          :  a+it,
tau_(1-conj rho)     :  a-it.                           (7)
```

Every monomial in these four fields therefore has exponent `m a+i n t` for integers `m,n`.

### Theorem B: finite algebraic corona blindness

Let `P` be any complex polynomial in the four quartet characters.  If the resulting prime field
belongs to `M_log`, then its class in `Q_log` is the class of the sum of the horizontal-grade-zero
monomials of `P`.  In particular, that corona class is independent of `a`.

### Proof

Expand `P` into monomials and group them by their exponent pair `(m,n)`.  Theorem A says that
membership in `M_log` excludes every nonzero positive leading grade.  All negative grades vanish
in `Q_log`, and only grade zero survives.  A grade-zero monomial has exponent `i n t`, which contains
no `a`.  QED.

The same conclusion holds for a Laurent polynomial whenever its evaluated prime field is defined
and belongs to `M_log`: after equal exponents are combined, Theorem A applies unchanged.

## 4. Exact quartet collapses

The natural FE-paired products are

```text

tau_(conj rho) * tau_(1-rho) = chi_(2it),                (8)
```


The complete quartet product is


Thus every off-line quartet and every on-line double orbit produce the same complete symmetric

## 5. Exact norm separation of boundary phases

For real `t`, put

```text
e_t(p)=p^(-it).
```

Every `[e_t]` has quotient norm one.  For `t!=u`,

```text
||[e_t]-[e_u]||_(Q_log)=4/pi.                           (11)
```

### Proof

With `delta=t-u!=0`, multiplication by the isometric phase `e_u` reduces the norm to the class of

```text
e_delta-1.
```

Prime-number-theorem partial summation gives

```text
1/log X * sum_(p<=X) (log p)/p |p^(-i delta)-1|
  -> lim_(V->infinity) 1/V * integral_0^V 2|sin(delta v/2)| dv
  = 4/pi.                                               (12)
```

Every element of `M_0` has normalized full-prefix absolute mass tending to zero: delete a fixed
head, use the defining vanishing-tail property on the tail, and then let the head contribution be
divided by `log X`.  Therefore the triangle inequality makes `4/pi` a lower bound for the distance
from `e_delta-1` to `M_0`.

Conversely, delete a sufficiently large finite head of `e_delta-1`.  Equation (12) makes the
Marcinkiewicz norm of the remaining tail arbitrarily close to `4/pi`.  Finite heads lie in `M_0`,
so `4/pi` is also the quotient upper bound.  This proves (11).  QED.

Consequently

```text

```

is a norm-discrete orbit: every two distinct points have the same positive distance.  It admits no
nonconstant norm-continuous, let alone holomorphic, parameterization by an interval or complex
neighbourhood.
