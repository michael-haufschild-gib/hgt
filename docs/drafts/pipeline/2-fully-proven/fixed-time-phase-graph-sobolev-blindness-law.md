## Public sentence

Low sensitivity to every spectral phase does not make an observable typical along one static
Hamiltonian ensemble.  At one laboratory time that ensemble lies on a graph inside the completed
phase space, and the graph can carry probability one of smooth observables whose phase-Haar mean,
variance, and every subcritical derivative norm tend to zero.  In a many-body Hilbert space the
generic derivative order needed to see the graph is exponential in qubit number.

## 1. Why this theorem is needed

The parent low-replica law compresses the complete short-circuit union into one bounded observable
`F` and proves a target-cardinality-free phase Dirichlet bound.  Its proposed next step was to build
a mixing seminorm dual to that bound and compare the fixed-time static-local phase graph with
conditional phase Haar.

That proposal is false for a structural reason independent of spectral gaps, constants, replica
order, and circuit counting.  A graph measure is singular in every phase direction.  Dirichlet
energy only measures average phase variation and cannot control restriction to a set of phase
codimension `d`.  The failure persists for every integer Sobolev order below `d/2`.

The exact positive survivor is therefore incidence-specific: one must use that the target family is
chosen independently of the sampled Hamiltonian and is generated by shallow local circuits.  No
generic low-influence mixing theorem can supply that missing fact.

## 2. Abstract graph experiment

Let `(Omega,A,mu)` be any probability space and let `T^d=(R/2pi Z)^d`, `d>=1`, carry normalized
Haar measure `m_d`.  Let

\[
 \Phi:\Omega\longrightarrow\mathbb T^d                                  \tag{1}
\]

be any measurable map.  Its graph law and the conditionally completed law are

\[
 \nu_\Phi(d\omega,d\phi)
   =\mu(d\omega)\,\delta_{\Phi(\omega)}(d\phi),
 \qquad
 \lambda(d\omega,d\phi)=\mu(d\omega)m_d(d\phi).                         \tag{2}
\]

For a bounded measurable `F` define

\[
 \mathbb E_{\rm graph}F=\int F\,d\nu_\Phi,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_{\rm comp}F=\int F\,d\lambda.                               \tag{3}
\]

No regularity of `Phi` is needed below.  Thus the theorem applies before any eigenvalue chart,
nondegeneracy, or transversality estimate is introduced.

## 3. Smooth graph detectors with vanishing phase influence

### Theorem A -- phase-Dirichlet blindness

Assume `d>=3`.  There is a family of functions

\[
 F_\varepsilon:\Omega\times\mathbb T^d\longrightarrow[0,1]
 \qquad(0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_0)                                    \tag{4}
\]

which is smooth in the phase variable and satisfies

\[
 \boxed{\mathbb E_{\rm graph}F_\varepsilon=1,}                           \tag{5}
\]

while

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathbb E_{\rm comp}F_\varepsilon=O_d(\varepsilon^d),\qquad
 \operatorname{Var}_{\lambda}(F_\varepsilon)=O_d(\varepsilon^d),
 \qquad
 \int\|\nabla_\phi F_\varepsilon\|_2^2\,d\lambda
     =O_d(\varepsilon^{d-2}).}                                           \tag{6}
\]

Consequently the three completed quantities in `(6)` tend to zero, while

\[
 \boxed{
 \left|\mathbb E_{\rm graph}F_\varepsilon
       -\mathbb E_{\rm comp}F_\varepsilon\right|\longrightarrow1.}       \tag{7}
\]

In particular, no estimate whose right side tends to zero with completed mean, variance and phase
Dirichlet energy can compare every graph law `(2)` with conditional phase Haar.

### Proof

Choose `eta in C_c^infinity(R^d)` with `0<=eta<=1`, `eta(0)=1`, and support in the Euclidean unit
ball.  For `epsilon` below the injectivity radius of the flat torus, define

\[
 g_\varepsilon(\phi)=
 \eta\!\left({\operatorname{chart}_0(\phi)\over\varepsilon}\right)       \tag{8}
\]

inside the injectivity ball about zero and set it to zero outside.  Put

\[
 F_\varepsilon(\omega,\phi)
 =g_\varepsilon(\phi-\Phi(\omega)).                                     \tag{9}
\]

Translation invariance gives `F_epsilon(omega,Phi(omega))=g_epsilon(0)=1`, proving `(5)`.  For every
fixed `omega`, changing variables by the torus translation `phi mapsto phi-Phi(omega)` gives

\[
 \int_{\mathbb T^d}F_\varepsilon\,dm_d
   ={\varepsilon^d\over(2\pi)^d}\int_{\mathbb R^d}\eta,
 \qquad
 \int_{\mathbb T^d}F_\varepsilon^2\,dm_d
   ={\varepsilon^d\over(2\pi)^d}\int_{\mathbb R^d}\eta^2,              \tag{10}
\]

and

\[
 \int_{\mathbb T^d}\|\nabla_\phi F_\varepsilon\|_2^2\,dm_d
   ={\varepsilon^{d-2}\over(2\pi)^d}
      \int_{\mathbb R^d}\|\nabla\eta\|_2^2.                            \tag{11}
\]

Integrate `(10)--(11)` over `mu`.  The variance is bounded by the second moment in `(10)`.  This
proves `(6)--(7)`. `square`

### Corollary A.1 -- the proposed Dirichlet-dual Brown route is impossible

There is no finite function `C(d)` such that every measurable `Phi` and every phase-smooth
`0<=F<=1` obey

\[
 \left|\mathbb E_{\rm graph}F-\mathbb E_{\rm comp}F\right|
 \le C(d)\left(
   \operatorname{Var}_{\lambda}(F)
   +\int\|\nabla_\phi F\|_2^2d\lambda
 \right)^{1/2}.                                                           \tag{12}
\]

This remains false if the right side also contains any term that vanishes with the completed mean.

### Proof

Substitute `F_epsilon` from Theorem A into `(12)` and let `epsilon` tend to zero.  The left side
tends to one and the right side tends to zero. `square`

The corollary does not say that the actual circuit-incidence observable fails to mix.  It says its
mixing cannot follow from the parent's `O(r^2/D)` Dirichlet budget alone.

## 4. Every subcritical finite derivative hierarchy is blind

For an integer `k>=0`, define the conditional phase Sobolev quantity

\[
 \|F\|_{H^k_\phi(\lambda)}^2
 =\sum_{|\alpha|\le k}
   \int_{\Omega\times\mathbb T^d}
      |\partial_\phi^\alpha F|^2\,d\lambda.                              \tag{13}
\]

### Theorem B -- graph restriction requires half the phase codimension

If `2k<d`, the functions `(9)` satisfy

\[
 \boxed{
 \|F_\varepsilon\|_{H^k_\phi(\lambda)}^2
 \le C_{d,k,\eta}\sum_{j=0}^k\varepsilon^{d-2j}
 =O_{d,k,\eta}(\varepsilon^{d-2k})\longrightarrow0,}                    \tag{14}
\]

but their graph expectation is one.  Therefore restriction to the graph is not a bounded linear
functional on conditional phase `H^k` for any integer `k<d/2`.

### Proof

For every multi-index `alpha`, scaling `(8)` gives

\[
 \int_{\mathbb T^d}
   |\partial^\alpha g_\varepsilon|^2dm_d
 ={\varepsilon^{d-2|\alpha|}\over(2\pi)^d}
   \int_{\mathbb R^d}|\partial^\alpha\eta|^2.                            \tag{15}
\]

Translation by `Phi(omega)` does not change `(15)`.  Sum over `|alpha|<=k` and integrate over `mu`.
When `2k<d`, every exponent is positive and the largest derivative order dominates.  Equation `(5)`
then proves unboundedness of graph restriction. `square`

### Corollary B.1 -- many-body derivative-order barrier

For a `D`-dimensional state space, the projective spectral phase torus has `d=D-1` coordinates at
maximal projective rank.  A generic Sobolev-duality argument against the fixed-time graph therefore
cannot work with any integer derivative order

\[
 \boxed{k<{D-1\over2}.}                                                   \tag{16}
\]

For `n` qubits, `D=2^n`; every hierarchy of only `poly(n)` phase derivatives is subcritical.  In
particular, replacing the first-gradient estimate by finitely many higher gradients of order
`O(log n)` or `O(n)` does not repair the route.

This is a lower bound on a **generic restriction method**, not on the replica order required by the
physical shortcut event.  The parent theorem's `r=O(log n)` reporter remains exact; what fails is
the attempt to transfer it to a singular fixed-time graph using only generic phase smoothness.

## 5. Static-local specialization

Let `theta` denote the sampled coefficients of an ordinary time-independent local Hamiltonian and
let

\[
 \Phi_t(\theta)
 =t\big(E_2(\theta)-E_1(\theta),\ldots,
        E_D(\theta)-E_1(\theta)\big)\pmod {2\pi}.                         \tag{17}
\]

At fixed laboratory time `t`, the physical joint law of coefficients and phases is the graph law

\[
 \theta\longmapsto(\theta,\Phi_t(\theta)),                               \tag{18}
\]

whereas completed phase averaging replaces only the second coordinate by independent Haar phase.
Taking `Omega` to be the coefficient shell in Theorems A--B gives, without any spectral
regularity assumption:

1. graph observables can have physical expectation one and completed expectation tending to zero;
2. completed phase variance `O(r^2/D)` does not control their physical expectation;
3. no generic `poly(n)`-order phase Sobolev norm controls the restriction when `D=2^n`.

The obstruction is stronger than a dimension mismatch between coefficient space and phase space.
Conditioning on `theta` leaves a point mass in all `D-1` phase directions, so the relevant graph
codimension is the full projective phase dimension.

## 6. The opposite entropy trap: the physical image has a small arbitrary codebook

The failure of generic graph mixing may suggest charging the graph detector for the information
needed to select its phase centre.  That route also cannot reach Brown scale.

Let `Theta` be contained in the Euclidean ball of radius `R` in `R^m`, let `h_1,...,h_m` be
self-adjoint operators, and put

\[
 H(\theta)=\sum_{j=1}^m\theta_jh_j,
 \qquad
 B=\left(\sum_{j=1}^m\|h_j\|_{\rm op}^2\right)^{1/2}.                    \tag{19}
\]

For one fixed unit seed `psi_0`, define the time-`t` physical image

\[
 \mathcal O_t
 =\{e^{-itH(\theta)}\psi_0:\theta\in\Theta\}.                            \tag{20}
\]

### Theorem C -- coefficient entropy, not Hilbert dimension, covers the fixed-time image

For every `epsilon>0`, `O_t` has a vector-norm `epsilon`-net of cardinality at most

\[
 \boxed{
 N_\varepsilon(\mathcal O_t)
 \le\left(1+{2R|t|B\over\varepsilon}\right)^m.}                          \tag{21}
\]

The same bound holds for projective distance because projective distance is no larger than vector
distance after the displayed choice of representatives.

### Proof

Cauchy--Schwarz in the coefficient index gives

\[
 \|H(\theta)-H(\theta')\|_{\rm op}
 \le B\|\theta-\theta'\|_2.                                              \tag{22}
\]

Duhamel's identity gives

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 e^{-itH(\theta)}-e^{-itH(\theta')}
  =-i\int_0^t
     e^{-i(t-u)H(\theta)}
     \bigl(H(\theta)-H(\theta')\bigr)
     e^{-iuH(\theta')}\,du,
 \end{aligned}                                                           \tag{23}
\]

with the integral orientation reversed when `t<0`.  Hence

\[
 \|e^{-itH(\theta)}\psi_0-e^{-itH(\theta')}\psi_0\|
 \le |t|B\|\theta-\theta'\|_2.                                          \tag{24}
\]

A Euclidean radius-`R` ball has a radius-`delta` net of size at most
`(1+2R/delta)^m`.  Push forward such a net through `(20)` with
`delta=epsilon/(|t|B)`; the cases `t=0` or `B=0` need one point.  Equations `(21)` and the
projective claim follow. `square`

### Corollary C.1 -- classical selector length cannot witness Brown complexity

If an ordinary local ensemble has `m=O(n)` coefficients, `R B=poly(n)`, fixed positive accuracy,
and `|t|<=2^n`, then

\[
 \boxed{\log N_\varepsilon(\mathcal O_t)=O(n^2).}                         \tag{25}
\]

Thus the entire fixed-time ensemble image can be indexed by polynomially many classical bits even
when its individual states require exponentially many local quantum gates.  Any argument that
replaces shallow-circuit endpoints by an arbitrary codebook, or charges only selector information,
cannot prove a Brown-scale `Omega(2^n)` circuit lower bound.  The centres in the net `(21)` need not
be easy to prepare; their **gate geometry**, not their number or description index, is the missing
resource.

## 7. Conjecturing / failure / survivor loop

### C0 -- low phase influence implies fixed-time static mixing

**Verdict:** `[FALSIFIED]` by Theorem A.

The graph detector `(9)` has vanishing mean, variance and Dirichlet energy under completed phase
Haar but equals one on every physical sample.

### C1 -- add a polynomial number of phase derivatives

**Verdict:** `[FALSIFIED AS A GENERIC ROUTE]` by Theorem B.

Every derivative hierarchy of order `poly(n)` is below half the `2^n-1` phase codimension for large
`n`.  Its norm can vanish while graph restriction remains one.

### C2 -- smooth the phase graph transversely

**Verdict:** `[MATHEMATICALLY VALID, PHYSICALLY CHANGES THE MODEL]`.

Independent phase noise makes the physical law absolutely continuous and permits Sobolev or
transport comparison.  Brown--Susskind growth for one ordinary time-independent Hamiltonian may
not assume that fresh noise, because it is precisely the autonomous quenched claim being tested.

### C3 -- charge the winning selector by its classical information

**Verdict:** `[FALSIFIED AS A BROWN-SCALE ROUTE]` by Theorem C.

At all times through `2^n`, only `O(n^2)` bits select an arbitrary approximating centre for the
whole fixed-time image of an `O(n)`-parameter ensemble.  Exponential circuit complexity cannot be
a rate--distortion statement about that coefficient source alone.

### C4 -- use nonadaptive gate geometry rather than generic smoothness or selector entropy

**Verdict:** `[SURVIVES; EXACT OPEN CORE SHARPENED]`.

The counterexample `(9)` adapts its phase center to `Phi(theta)`.  A physical shortcut may choose
among endpoints of one Hamiltonian-independent shallow-circuit dictionary, but it may not insert
the sampled Hamiltonian's exact phase graph as a target.  The remaining theorem must quantify that
adaptivity gap using the actual gate-generated incidence geometry.

> **Nonadaptive graph-avoidance conjecture.**  For the ordinary fixed-shell local-Hamiltonian
> ensemble, after one target-independent return/carrier excision, the light-filtered complete
> incidence reporter of every depth-`s` local circuit image has small graph expectation at the
> geometric Brown time `t` whenever `s<c t/poly(n)` before capacity.  The proof must use the
> Hamiltonian-independence and local gate-generation of the endpoint image; it cannot depend only
> on target cardinality, replica order, phase derivatives, minimum spectral action, or completed
> phase variance.

This conjecture is not counted as a result.  Theorems A--C prove that its incidence-specific clause
is logically load-bearing, not stylistic.

The child `circuit-complexity-selection-synthesis-divergence-law.md` now identifies the exact scalar
functional carried by that gate geometry.  For the geometrically killed local-word prior, circuit
cost splits into selector information plus marginal synthesis divergence, and the hard gate Green
surprisal is exactly the preparation-relative distortion rate.  The small arbitrary codebook in
Theorem C controls only selector information; on a full static plateau the child proves that the
missing synthesis divergence is `Omega(D)`.  The live Brown estimate is therefore a lower-tail law
for gate Green surprisal, not another graph-Sobolev comparison.

## 8. What this changes at the Brown frontier

The exact next move in the low-replica parent asked for “a quantitative fixed-shell mixing seminorm
dual to the phase Dirichlet budget.”  Theorems A--B retire that target.  The replacement frontier is
narrower and harder:

1. keep the complete target union as one reporter;
2. retain the target-independent light/return excision;
3. expose the selector induced by the winning shallow circuit;
4. prove that a gate-generated selector cannot track the quenched spectral graph over more than a
   small coefficient-set mass;
5. use deterministic circuit composition only after that quenched selector bound is obtained.

The missing invariant is therefore not another scalar measure of phase mixing or source entropy.
It is the **tracking capacity of a local circuit dictionary against a Hamiltonian-indexed spectral
graph**.

## 9. Hostile audit

1. **Underlying engines are classical.**  Scaled smooth bumps, Sobolev trace thresholds, Duhamel's
   identity, and Euclidean covering bounds are standard.  No priority is claimed for Theorems A--C
   as abstract analysis.
2. **Application is a frontier correction, not Brown growth.**  The card proves that a proposed
   proof architecture cannot work from its stated inputs.  It does not bound the actual circuit
   incidence reporter.
3. **Adversarial detector is not a circuit reporter.**  That distinction is the positive survivor:
   the next proof must exploit exactly why shallow circuit endpoints cannot realize `(9)`.
4. **No minimum-gap loophole.**  The construction works for every measurable phase map, including
   maximally nondegenerate and transverse spectra.
5. **No replica loophole.**  Low replica remains sufficient to report capture.  Replica order and
   generic Sobolev trace order are different quantities; `(16)` forbids conflating them.
6. **Small source entropy is not an upper bound on circuit complexity.**  The net centres in `(21)`
   may themselves have exponential preparation complexity.  Theorem C rules out a proof method; it
   does not give shallow preparations.
7. **No claim of external novelty yet.**  Specialist review must decide whether the static-phase
   graph application and tracking-capacity formulation are new.  The mathematical correction is
   unconditional regardless of priority.
8. **Lean status.**  No final manuscript may use the result until the finite-torus bump/scaling
