## Purpose

The path proof preserves an observable transition graph only at the newly
attached endpoint. That is insufficient for a branching tree: a later leaf
may have to be attached at an old internal vertex.

The missing invariant is:

> a simple Hamiltonian has a connected, non-bipartite local-observable
> transition graph at **every** vertex.

The new insight is that leaf attachment preserves this invariant. A
transition at an arbitrary old vertex is generated by a two-step spectral
channel through the attachment vertex. Maximal projective rank separates the
channel's pole from every possible cancellation.

## Setup

Let \(T\) be a finite tree with at least two vertices and heterogeneous local
dimensions \(q_w\ge2\). Let
\[
  \mathcal H_T=\bigotimes_{w\in T}\mathbb C^{q_w}.
\]
For a simple tree-local Hermitian Hamiltonian \(H\), write
\(\mathcal G_w(H)\) for the graph on its energy eigenstates in which
\(\alpha\ne\beta\) are adjacent when
\[
  \langle\psi_\alpha,B_w\psi_\beta\rangle\ne0
\]
for some Hermitian one-site operator \(B_w\).

Call \(H\) **all-vertex good** when every
\(\mathcal G_w(H)\) is connected and non-bipartite.

Assume:

1. \(H_T\) is simple and maximally projective;
2. \(H_T\) is all-vertex good.

Attach a new leaf \(v\) of arbitrary dimension \(q_v\ge2\) at \(u\in T\).
Put
\[
  H_0=H_T\otimes I+
      I\otimes\sum_{a=0}^{q_v-1}s_a|a\rangle\langle a|,
  \tag{1}
\]
with shifts \(s_a\) chosen so every \(E_\alpha+s_a\) is distinct.

## New lemma — an arbitrary old vertex remains observable

Fix an old vertex \(r\in T\) and two new-site layers \(a\ne b\). Since
\(\mathcal G_u(H_T)\) and \(\mathcal G_r(H_T)\) are connected and have no
isolated vertex, choose old spectral labels
\[
  \alpha-\gamma\in\mathcal G_u(H_T),
  \qquad
  \gamma-\beta\in\mathcal G_r(H_T).
  \tag{2}
\]
For \(r=u\), choose a length-two walk
\(\alpha-\gamma-\beta\) in the non-bipartite connected graph
\(\mathcal G_u(H_T)\).

Choose Hermitian local operators \(A_u,B_r\) whose corresponding matrix
elements in `(2)` are nonzero. Perturb the new edge in direction
\[
  W_{ab}
  =
  A_u\otimes
  (|a\rangle\langle b|+|b\rangle\langle a|).
  \tag{3}
\]
Let
\[
  F_{r,ab}(x)
  =
  \left\langle
    \psi_{\alpha,a}(x),
    (B_r\otimes I)\psi_{\beta,b}(x)
  \right\rangle
  \tag{4}
\]
for the canonical simple eigenbranches through \(H_0+xW_{ab}\).

First-order eigenvector perturbation gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
F'_{r,ab}(0)
={}&
\sum_\delta
\frac{
  \langle\psi_\alpha,A_u\psi_\delta\rangle
  \langle\psi_\delta,B_r\psi_\beta\rangle
}{
  z+E_\alpha-E_\delta
}\\
&+
\sum_\delta
\frac{
  \langle\psi_\alpha,B_r\psi_\delta\rangle
  \langle\psi_\delta,A_u\psi_\beta\rangle
}{
  -z+E_\beta-E_\delta
},
\qquad z=s_a-s_b.
\tag{5}
\end{aligned}
\]

The selected \(\delta=\gamma\) term in the first sum has a nonzero residue at
\[
  z=E_\gamma-E_\alpha.
  \tag{6}
\]
Maximal projective rank says all nonzero ordered old gaps are distinct. It
also forbids a pole from the second sum at `(6)`, except for a trivial
two-term equality of spectral labels. That trivial equality would force
\(\gamma=\beta\) and the second intermediate label to equal \(\alpha\),
contradicting the strict edge \(\gamma-\beta\) in `(2)`.

Therefore the rational function `(5)` is not identically zero. Outside a
finite set of allowed shift differences \(z\), the derivative is nonzero.
Hence the cross-layer old-\(r\) transition in `(4)` is not identically zero
in physical Hermitian edge parameters.

## Simultaneous preservation

At zero edge coupling, every layer contains a copy of
\(\mathcal G_r(H_T)\). These within-layer graphs persist for sufficiently
small perturbation. For each old vertex \(r\) and each \(b\ne0\), equation
`(5)` supplies one cross-layer transition between layer \(0\) and layer
\(b\). There are finitely many required amplitudes. Each is a nonzero
real-analytic function of the physical Hermitian edge coefficients.

The complement of their proper zero sets is dense in every sufficiently
small coefficient ball. One coefficient choice therefore:

1. preserves simple spectrum;
2. preserves every selected within-layer spanning graph and odd cycle;
3. creates enough cross-layer edges to connect all layers at every old
   vertex.

Thus every old \(\mathcal G_r\) remains connected and non-bipartite.

The existing endpoint-extension argument applied at \(u\) makes the new
leaf graph \(\mathcal G_v\) connected and non-bipartite:

- for \(q_v\ge3\), each fixed-old-state layer already contains a triangle;
- for \(q_v=2\), the generated same-layer and cross-layer edges give the
  required triangle.

Consequently the extended Hamiltonian is all-vertex good.

## Coupled induction

The proof should run two statements together for every finite tree \(T\):

1. maximally projective tree-local Hamiltonians form a dense full-measure
   \(G_\delta\);
2. all-vertex-good simple Hamiltonians form a nonempty open set.

### Base

For two vertices the allowed edge term is an arbitrary Hermitian matrix on
\(\mathbb C^{q_u}\otimes\mathbb C^{q_v}\). A generic eigenbasis makes every
local transition graph complete at both vertices. The complete-graph local
maximality theorem supplies maximal projective rank.

The one-vertex case is the ordinary generic simple Hermitian spectrum and is
handled separately.

### Step

Remove any leaf \(v\) with parent \(u\). By induction:

1. the maximal-rank set on \(T-v\) is dense;
2. the all-vertex-good set is nonempty open.

Their intersection is nonempty. Choose \(H_{T-v}\) there.

- The all-vertex extension above produces one all-vertex-good witness on
  \(T\).
- The existing relation-destruction residue argument at \(u\) uses
  \(\mathcal G_u(H_{T-v})\) to prove that no nontrace analytic spectral
  relation is universal on the extended tree-local parameter space.
- The symmetric relation-polynomial argument then removes the discriminant
  and the countable family of rational relation zero sets, giving dense
  full-measure \(G_\delta\) maximality on \(T\).

This closes arbitrary branching without assuming a spanning path.

## From trees to arbitrary graphs

Every finite connected graph \(G\) contains a spanning tree \(T\). The
tree-local Hamiltonian space is a linear subspace of the \(G\)-local space.
For each rational spectral relation, the symmetric relation polynomial is
already nonzero on the tree subspace, hence is nonzero on the larger graph
space. The discriminant is likewise nonzero. Therefore the same generic
maximality holds on every connected graph.

Extra edges cannot create a universal relation because the parameter space
still contains the spanning-tree witness.

## Exact temporal-curvature corollary

Assume the arbitrary-graph theorem above. For any vertex subset \(S\), turn
off terms crossing its boundary and let \(G[S]\) be the induced interaction
graph. Simultaneous genericity over the finitely many subsets gives
\[
  \tau(S)
  =
  \sum_{C\in\pi_0(G[S])}
  \left(\prod_{w\in C}q_w-1\right).
  \tag{7}
\]

For two distinct vertices \(u,v\), define pair temporal curvature
\[
  \kappa(u,v)
  =
  \tau(\{u,v\})-\tau(\{u\})-\tau(\{v\}).
  \tag{8}
\]
Then
\[
  \boxed{
  \kappa(u,v)
  =
  \begin{cases}
  (q_u-1)(q_v-1),&\{u,v\}\in E(G),\\
  0,&\{u,v\}\notin E(G).
  \end{cases}}
  \tag{9}
\]

Thus the entire two-body interaction graph, and hence all graph distances and
connected components, is recovered from the nonadditivity of completed
temporal dimension.

Equation `(9)` is the exact mathematical candidate behind:

> **Space is the curvature of observer-completed time.**

## Physical Hermitian bridge: real-symmetric induction

The bilinear-versus-sesquilinear objection can be removed at the witness
level by strengthening the induction invariant:

1. the Hamiltonian is real symmetric in one fixed product basis;
2. every local transition graph is witnessed by real-symmetric one-site
   probes;
3. every leaf coupling uses a real-symmetric old-site probe tensored with a
   real-symmetric layer exchange.

At the two-site base, the full real-symmetric matrix space is allowed.
A generic real orthogonal eigenbasis makes the partial transition matrix of
every spectral pair have a nonzero real-symmetric local component at both
sites. The failure of one pair/site is a proper real-algebraic subset of the
orthogonal group. Finitely many such subsets do not cover either determinant
component: a column sign change transfers a witness between the two
components without changing nonvanishing. Assign a maximally projective real
eigenvalue list.

The leaf extension preserves real symmetry. Canonical eigenvectors and their
first derivatives may therefore be chosen real on the real coupling line.
For real vectors and a real-symmetric probe,
\[
  \psi_x^\dagger B\psi_y=\psi_x^{T}B\psi_y.
\]
Hence the bilinear first-derivative formula `(5)` is exactly the physical
Hermitian bra--ket derivative on this witness slice. Complex polarization is
not needed.

The real-symmetric slice is sufficient for genericity in the full Hermitian
parameter space: a rational-relation or transition polynomial nonzero on the
real slice is nonzero on the containing physical space.
