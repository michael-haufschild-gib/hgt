## The jump

Static quantum evolution has two inequivalent ways to produce an unexpectedly
easy state.

1. It can remain close to an observer-stationary coefficient fibre.  Resolving
   that fibre costs `v_(n,q) log t` nats at time `t`, where `v_(n,q)` is the
   exact projective/QFI rank of the local Hamiltonian action on the input.
2. It can explore its completed spectral orbit and land in the circuit-easy
   target.  That costs the exact completed-orbit target information.  A
   Fubini--Study rate--distortion cost is relevant only after a separate
   spectral-delocalization comparison has been proved.

The conjecture is that there is no third, unpriced mechanism.  Every mixed,
adaptive, resonant, fast-forwardable, or recurrence carrier must pay at least
one of these two costs.

## Exact finite model

Fix `q>=2` and the full weight-at-most-`q` Gaussian Pauli Hamiltonian
`H_n(J)` and product input `Phi_n` from
`observer-rank-autonomous-no-shortcut-law.md`.  Fix the same finite universal
two-qubit gate alphabet, clean-ancilla convention, and pure-state trace
distance.

Let `D_n=2^n`.  For an integer circuit budget `R` and error `epsilon`, let
`E_(n,R,epsilon)` be the set of projective pure states within trace distance
`epsilon` of a state prepared from `Phi_n` by at most `R` gates.  Let `mu_n`
be normalized Fubini--Study measure and define the exact rate--distortion
benchmark

\[
 I^{\rm FS}_{n,\epsilon}(R)
 :=-\log\mu_n(E_{n,R,\epsilon}).                    \tag{1}
\]

No circuit count, cap-volume approximation, or asymptotic replacement is
built into `(1)`.

For each coefficient vector `J`, let `G_J` be the compact projective spectral
phase closure generated by `H_n(J)` on `Phi_n`, let `nu_J` be normalized Haar
measure on `G_J`, and define

\[
 \sigma_{n,q,\epsilon}(R)
 :=\mathbb E_J\,\nu_J\{g:g\cdot\Phi_n\in E_{n,R,\epsilon}\},
 \qquad
 I^{\rm comp}_{n,q,\epsilon}(R):=-\log\sigma_{n,q,\epsilon}(R).
 \tag{2}
\]

This is exact completed spectral-orbit shortcut information for the same
Hamiltonian ensemble.  It includes eigenvector weights, gap relations,
superselection, and all non-Haar spectral structure rather than assuming them
away.

Put

\[
 v_{n,q}=2\sum_{k=1}^q{n\choose k},\qquad
 A_{n,q,\epsilon}(t,R)
 =\min\{v_{n,q}\log t,\ I^{\rm comp}_{n,q,\epsilon}(R)\}.
 \tag{3}
\]

## Conjecture A -- fixed-time shortcut action

For every fixed `q>=2`, sufficiently small fixed `epsilon>0`, and every
`eta>0`, there are `C_eta,c,a,b>0` such that for all sufficiently large `n`,

\[
 n^{C_\eta}\le t\le \exp(cD_n),\qquad
 0\le R\le {a t\over n^b},                         \tag{4}
\]

implies

\[
 \boxed{
 e^{-(1+\eta)A_{n,q,\epsilon}(t,R)}
 \le
 \Pr_J\{e^{-itH_n(J)}\Phi_n\in E_{n,R,\epsilon}\}
 \le
 e^{-(1-\eta)A_{n,q,\epsilon}(t,R)}.
 }
 \tag{5}
\]

The constants may depend on `q`, the gate alphabet, `epsilon`, and `eta`, but
not on `n`, `t`, or the circuit selected after seeing `J`.

Equation `(5)` is the conjecture.  Neither inequality is currently proved in
this generality.  The observer-stationary construction proves only the local
lower mechanism before the minimum changes branch.

## Conjecture B -- exceptional-carrier completeness

The upper inequality in `(5)` is equivalent in intended proof architecture to
the following structural statement.

> Any measurable shortcut family with probability larger than the right side
> of `(5)` contains either (i) a coefficient carrier whose transverse
> observer codimension is smaller than `v_(n,q)` at leading order, or (ii) a
> completed spectral target whose actual orbit information is smaller than
> `I^comp_(n,q,epsilon)(R)`.

Both alternatives contradict exact definitions.  A proof must make this
dichotomy quantitative and selector-uniform; it may not assume a carrier
classification.

This is the direct analogue of the successful exceptional-carrier pattern in
the RHTower corpus: cancellation cannot be discarded; it must reappear as a
transverse defect or as divergent/large exceptional mass.  Here the two debts
are observer codimension and Hilbert-space information.

## Consequences if proved

### Growth

Before the crossover

\[
 v_{n,q}\log t<I^{\rm comp}_{n,q,\epsilon}(R),     \tag{6}
\]

equation `(5)` reduces to the observer-rank no-shortcut law.  With the
autonomous simulation cone, this is the missing fixed-time linear-complexity
lower bound.

### Plateau

Once

\[
 v_{n,q}\log t\ge I^{\rm comp}_{n,q,\epsilon}(R),  \tag{7}
\]

shortcut probability stops improving with time and saturates at exact
completed-orbit mass `exp(-I^comp)`.  The plateau is therefore not a separate
phenomenon: it is saturation of locally accumulated observer information by
completed spectral capacity.  Identifying this with Fubini--Study
rate--distortion requires a further theorem proving
`I^comp=I^FS+o(I^FS)` in the target ensemble.

### Recurrence

On a completed uniquely ergodic spectral orbit, a target of stationary mass
`exp(-I^comp)` has natural return scale `exp(I^comp)`.  Thus the same completed
information controlling plateau rarity controls recurrence.  Turning this sentence into a theorem
requires the already separated completed-time phase law plus a target-mass
comparison; `(5)` alone does not authorize it.

### Fixed-locality versus full matrix dynamics

For fixed `q`, `v_(n,q)` is polynomial while projective capacity is
exponential, so the observer branch controls the entire pre-capacity window.
For unrestricted `q=n` dynamics, global spectral synchronization can be the
cheaper branch.  This explains why a finite-dimensional universal law cannot
always have exponent `v_(n,q)` and why fixed locality is load-bearing rather
than cosmetic.

## Quenched correction forced by the gapped-eigenline floor

The annealed quantity in `(2)` is dominated from below by rare Hamiltonians
whose entire orbit is easy. In the flagship Gaussian ensemble,

\[
 I^{\rm comp}_{n,q,\epsilon}(R)=O_q(n^q\log n)
\]

already for the zero-gate target. Hence the action `(3)` crosses from
`v_(n,q) log t` to its spectral branch by polynomial time, not near Hilbert
capacity. Equation `(5)` may still be a valid annealed law, but it cannot be
interpreted as typical Brown--Susskind growth.

The stronger physically relevant target must resolve Hamiltonian rarity and
conditional orbit information before averaging. A candidate formulation is
the quantile envelope

\[
 \inf_{B\ge0}\left\{
   \Pr_J\bigl(I_J^{\rm comp}(R)<B\bigr)
   +\exp[-\min\{v_{n,q}\log t,B\}]
 \right\},
\]

where `I_J^comp=-log nu_J(E_(n,R,epsilon))`. This is presently a
`[CONJECTURE]`, not a replacement theorem. Any successful adaptive law must
control the shortcut event on every resolved spectral-information stratum;
an annealed mean may be used only after that control is proved.

## Hostile falsification programme

The conjecture is false if any one of the following exists for fixed `q>=2`
and arbitrarily large `n`:

1. a mixed stationary--periodic carrier with total continuous-plus-periodic
   dimension exceeding `m_(n,q)-v_(n,q)+Omega(v_(n,q))`;
2. a nonlinear fast-forwardable family whose component entropy cancels an
   `Omega(v log t)` transverse cost;
3. a fixed-time local-Hamiltonian endpoint concentration on circuit-easy
   states larger than both the stationary-tube mass and the exact
   completed spectral-orbit mass by an exponential factor;
4. an adaptive circuit selector whose union has larger mass than its exact
   rate--distortion set;
5. a resonance singularity for which every finite observer jet loses rank
   without forcing a correspondingly small spectral target.

Known one-local fast forwarding falsifies the analogous `q=1` statement and
is therefore an essential control case.
