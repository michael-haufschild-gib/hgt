# The tame kernel of a number field is torsion

- **ID:** `garland-tame-kernel-torsion`
- **Stage:** 1-in-progress
- **Type:** THEOREM (paper, classical) — formalization programme
- **Blocks:** `deninger-algebraic-cyclotomic-milnor-amplitude` (its `(1)`)
- **Statement:** For a number field `K` with ring of integers `𝒪_K`, the tame kernel
  `K_2(𝒪_K) = ⋂_v ker ∂_v ⊆ K_2^M(K)` is torsion; equivalently `K_2^M(K) ⊗ ℚ = 0`.

## Why this card exists

`deninger-algebraic-cyclotomic-milnor-amplitude` imports this through Gallauer's Remark 6.8 →
Borel.  Everything on the near side of it is already unconditional in Lean
(`milnorKDegTwo_torsion_of_tameKernel_numberField`,
`deningerMilnorRealVanishing_of_tameKernelTorsion`), so this is the single remaining input.

## Established constraints (do not re-attempt)

- `ℚ̄` is no shortcut: `K_2^M(K)` torsion for all number fields `K` ⟺ `K_2^M(ℚ̄) = 0`, via
  `N ∘ res = [L:K]`.  Suslin's unique divisibility for algebraically closed fields does not give
  vanishing.
- The elementary mechanism "Steinberg relations among `S`-units span `Λ²(𝒪_S^×) ⊗ ℚ`" is **false**
  (unit rank ≥ 2 with no exceptional units).  Consequently "the tame kernel is generated by symbols
  of units" is also false.
- There is no transfer-based reduction of a general number field to `ℚ`.

## Decomposition

1. **Tate's theorem for `ℚ`** — `K_2^M(ℚ) ≅ {±1} ⊕ ⊕_p 𝔽_p^×` (Milnor, *Introduction to Algebraic
   K-Theory*, §11.6).  Elementary: a Euclidean-algorithm induction on the numerators.  Yields the
   case `K = ℚ` outright.  **First target.**
2. **Weight-two Gersten/localization for a Dedekind domain** — exactness of
   `K_2^M(K) →^∂ ⊕_v κ(v)^× → 0`, i.e. surjectivity of the total tame symbol.  Needs the
   approximation argument; partially served by the residue machinery already built
   (`MilnorKResidue*`).
3. **Finite generation** — Quillen's theorem that `K_n(𝒪_K)` is finitely generated, or Garland's
   direct finiteness argument for `K_2(𝒪_K)`.  This is the large half.
4. **Rank vanishing** — Borel's computation, `rank K_2(𝒪_K) = 0`.
5. Alternative route to keep in view: Moore's theorem plus class field theory (needs CFT, which
   mathlib also lacks).

### Ruled out this round

A literature sweep (arXiv and journal surveys, August 2026) found no proof of "the tame kernel of a
number field is torsion" that avoids Garland's finiteness theorem, Quillen finite generation with
Borel's rank computation, or Soulé's vanishing with Quillen–Lichtenbaum.  In particular:

- Bass–Milnor–Serre does **not** give it: the congruence kernel is a subgroup of `K_2(𝒪_S)` whose
  quotient injects into `∏_v K_2(𝒪_v)`, which carries no finiteness.
- Moore's theorem reduces the statement to "the wild kernel is torsion", which is the same problem.
- Transfer/Galois descent reduces the statement to a cofinal family of number fields, e.g. to
  `K_2^M(\bar{ℚ}) ⊗ ℚ = 0` alone, or to `K_3^M(ℚ(t)) ⊗ ℚ = 0` alone via Milnor's exact sequence for
  a rational function field — equivalent reformulations, not simplifications.
- "`K_2(𝒪_S)` is generated by symbols of `S`-units" is false in the strong form needed, and even if
  it held, the Steinberg relations among `S`-units do not span `Λ²(𝒪_S^×) ⊗ ℚ`.

## The next brick, decomposed: Milnor's exact sequence for `F(t)`

The residue maps needed for it already exist: `Polynomial F` is a principal ideal domain, hence a
Dedekind domain, `RatFunc F` is its fraction field, and `residueDataAtPlace (RatFunc F) v` supplies
the residue at every monic irreducible.  What is missing is exactness of

```
0 → K_n^M(F) → K_n^M(F(t)) --(⊕ ∂_π)--> ⊕_{π monic irreducible} K_{n-1}^M(F[t]/π) → 0.
```

Decomposition:

1. **The residue field at `π`.**  Identify `ResidueField ((v π).valuation (RatFunc F)).valuationSubring`
   with `F[t]/(π)`.  The chain is the one already used for number fields in
   `MilnorKTameResidueFinite.lean`: `valuationSubringAtPrime_eq_valuationSubring`, `IsLocalization`,
   `IsLocalization.algEquiv`, `RingEquiv.subringCongr`, `IsLocalRing.ResidueField.mapEquiv`.
2. **Split injectivity of `K_n^M(F) → K_n^M(F(t))`.**  The specialization at the place `(t)` is a
   retraction: `milnorSpecialization` at that place sends a symbol of constants to itself, because a
   constant is a unit of valuation zero and its residue is itself
   (`milnorSpecialization_of_units`).
3. **The degree filtration.**  `L_d ⊆ K_n^M(F(t))` generated by the classes of symbols whose entries
   are polynomials of degree `≤ d`.  Then `L_0 = image of K_n^M(F)`, `⋃_d L_d = K_n^M(F(t))`, and
   `∂_π(L_d) = 0` for `deg π > d`.
4. **The graded pieces.**  `⊕_{deg π = d} ∂_π : L_d/L_{d-1} ≅ ⊕_{deg π = d} K_{n-1}^M(F[t]/π)`.
   Surjectivity is division with remainder; injectivity is the construction of a section from the
   specialization maps.
5. **Consequence: the Bass–Tate–Kato norm.**  For a finite simple extension `E = F(a)` the norm
   `N_{E/F} : K_n^M(E) → K_n^M(F)` is `-∂_∞ ∘ (section at the minimal polynomial)`; independence of
   the choice of generator is Kato's argument.  The projection formula `N ∘ res = [E:F]` then gives
   the reduction of the card's statement to any cofinal family of number fields.

## Route audit — 2026-08-18

The `garland-formalization-programme` card's chosen route (Garland's `p`-adic curvature method) has
been audited against the literature and **does not close for this target**.  Garland's 1971
`K₂`-finiteness theorem is an archimedean argument (Riemannian geometry and harmonic forms, giving
`H²(SL_n(𝔬), ℝ) = 0` for `n > 6`); his 1973 building/spectral-gap theorem applies to **cocompact**
lattices in `p`-adic groups, and `SL_n(𝒪_S)` is non-uniform (an `S`-arithmetic lattice is uniform iff
the group is anisotropic, and `SL_n` is isotropic over every number field).  The cell stabilizers in
the Bruhat–Tits building are infinite arithmetic groups, so the equivariant descent is an isotropy
spectral sequence whose low rows are the very cohomology one wants to kill.

Consequence for the decomposition above: items 3 and 4 (finite generation, rank vanishing) stay as
they are, and there is no combinatorial shortcut past them.  Items 1 and 2 are unaffected — item 1
(Tate for `ℚ`) is **done**.  The route-independent work that remains useful is Matsumoto's theorem,
Milnor's exact sequence for `F(t)` with the Bass–Tate–Kato norm, and the localization identifying the
Milnor tame kernel of `𝒪_S` with `K₂(𝒪_S)`.

## The surviving elementary route, and its first brick — 2026-08-18

The route audit and the `k2-rank-divisibility-firewall` card together determine what is left:

1. the finite-coefficient theory provably cannot give the rank (firewall), and
2. for a **finitely generated** group the rank *is* visible modulo every `m`
   (`isTorsion_of_fg_of_no_int_quotient`).

So the whole card reduces to producing **finite generation of the tame kernel by elementary means**,
and then killing the resulting finite rank.  Tate's Euclidean descent does both at once when the
unit group is small, and this is what makes the route elementary:

> **Tate's descent, abstracted.**  For a Euclidean domain `R` with fraction field `F`, the descent
> that the corpus formalizes for `R = ℤ` shows that the degree-two tame kernel of `R` lies in the
> subgroup generated by the symbols `{u, v}` of **units of `R`**.  That subgroup is a quotient of
> `Λ²(R^×)`, hence finitely generated by Dirichlet — and **torsion as soon as `R^×` has rank at most
> one**, because `Λ²` of a rank-one group is torsion.

For a number field, `rank 𝒪_F^× = r₁ + r₂ − 1 ≤ 1` covers `ℚ`, the imaginary quadratic fields, the
real quadratic fields, the cubic fields with one real place, and the totally complex quartic fields.
Over any such field with `𝒪_F` Euclidean the argument is complete and elementary — no Garland, no
Borel, no Quillen, no Iwasawa.

### What remains on this route

**The Euclidean descent for a general Euclidean domain.**  The corpus has it for `ℤ`
(`MilnorKTateEuclid.lean`), where the filtration is by the largest prime and the key step needs
`k = (ab − c)/p < p`, which uses the archimedean triangle inequality for `|·|` on `ℤ`.  Abstracting
to a norm-Euclidean domain, the pieces behave as follows:

- *Representatives*: division with remainder gives, for every residue class mod `π`, a
  representative `c` with `N(c) < N(π)`; since `N` is multiplicative every prime factor of `c` then
  has norm `< N(π)`.  So `exists_tateRep` generalizes **for free**.
- *The Euclidean step*: the term `{k, W}` needs `k` to have all prime factors of norm `< N(π)`.
  Over `ℤ` this is `k < p`.  Over a general norm-Euclidean ring `N(ab − c)` is **not** bounded by
  `N(ab) + N(c)`, so the naive bound fails; a sharper choice of representative (Euclidean constant
  `γ < 1`) or an induction on `N(ab)` rather than on the prime is needed.  **This is the exact
  remaining gap on the elementary route**, and it is a bounded, concrete piece of mathematics, not a
  research programme.

### Beyond unit rank one

For `rank 𝒪_F^× ≥ 2` the descent still gives finite generation, and `Λ²(𝒪_F^×)` has positive rank,
so the rank must be killed by something else.  By the firewall that "something else" may be
finite-coefficient — the firewall only forbids finite-coefficient input *without* finite generation.
So the shape of a complete elementary proof is:

```text
Euclidean descent  ⟹  K₂(𝒪_S) finitely generated
      +
mod-m bound (Merkurjev–Suslin / Tate, plus finiteness of étale H² with finite coefficients)
      +
isTorsion_of_fg_of_no_int_quotient
      ⟹  K₂(𝒪_S) torsion.
```

with the unit-rank-≤ 1 case needing no mod-`m` input at all.

### The Euclidean route is capped at one archimedean place — proved, not guessed

Tate's step needs the "carry" `k = (ab − c)/π` to be smoother than `π`.  Over `ℤ` this is
`k ≤ ab/p < p`, and it uses the **ordering** of `ℤ` (`c ≥ 0`, so `ab − c ≤ ab`), not merely
multiplicativity of the norm.  Over a general number ring the only substitute is the archimedean
triangle inequality, and `N(x) = ∏_σ |σ(x)|` is sub-additive **only when there is a single
archimedean absolute value** — i.e. only for `ℚ` and the imaginary quadratic fields.  With two or
more archimedean places `∏_σ(|σ(x)| + |σ(y)|)` is not controlled by `N(x) + N(y)`, and the carry can
carry a prime of norm larger than `N(π)`.

The same failure appears in the "clean" version of the step: if `u ≡ 1 (mod π)` then Steinberg at
`x = −πt`, `t = (u−1)/π`, gives `{u, π} = {−1, u} + {t, u}`, so the obstruction is exactly the
support of the carry `t`; it is bounded only under an archimedean size control on `u`, which is what
choosing a small representative provides over `ℚ`.

This matches the literature (Tate's method is used for `ℚ` and for the Euclidean imaginary quadratic
fields, and nowhere else) and it means the elementary route settles precisely the fields of unit rank
`0` with one archimedean place — where `milnorSym2_isTorsion_of_rank_le_one` finishes it.  It is not
a route to the general card.


  monic irreducible is the image of a class of `K_{n+1}^M(F)`.  Descending the degree filtration one
  step at a time with `mem_degreeFiltration_pred_of_residues_zero`.  This is the **left exactness**
  of Milnor's sequence in final form.

### F22. The residue at infinity `[PROVED]`

- `MilnorResidueInfinity.lean` — `milnorKDegMap_comp` (functoriality composes), `ratFuncInv` (the
  inversion `t ↦ 1/t`, taken from `ratFuncFixedPointToInfinityEquiv 0`),
  `range_milnorKDegMap_const_le`, **`milnorResidueInfty`**, **`milnorResidueInfty_const`**.

  The residue at infinity is the residue at the place `X` read through the inversion.  What the norm
  needs from it is that it kills the constants, which is immediate: the inversion fixes them and a
  symbol of constants lies at the bottom of the degree filtration.

### F23. Right exactness at a single place `[PROVED]`

- `MilnorMonicPlaceFiniteness.lean` — `MonicIrred`, `finite_monicIrred_dvd`,
  **`finite_polyResidue_ne_zero`**: a class has nonzero residue at only finitely many monic
  irreducibles.
- `MilnorTotalResidueSurjective.lean` — `not_associated_of_monicIrred`, `exists_lift_at_place`
  (Milnor's section lift, with its degree bound and same-degree vanishing),
  **`exists_residue_single`**: every class of `K_n^M(F[X]/π)` is the residue at `π` of a class whose
  residue vanishes at **every** other finite place.  The section lift is corrected by subtracting
  lifts concentrated at the finitely many smaller-degree places that still see it — an induction on
  the degree.

### F24. The norm `[PROVED]`

- `MilnorNorm.lean` — **`milnorResidueInfty_eq_of_residues_eq`** (the residue at infinity depends
  only on the finite residues, because two classes with equal finite residues differ by a class of
  constants), **`milnorNormAt`**, **`milnorNormAt_spec`**.

  `N_π(x) = −∂_∞(y)` for any lift `y` of `x` concentrated at `π`.  This is Bass and Tate's norm,
  which every transfer argument in the arithmetic of Milnor `K`-theory — including Moore's theorem —
  needs.
