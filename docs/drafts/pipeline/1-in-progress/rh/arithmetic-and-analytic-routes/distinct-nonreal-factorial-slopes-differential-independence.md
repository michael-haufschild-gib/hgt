## Statement

For `1 <= ell <= d`, let

```text
alpha_ell = r_ell + i t_ell,       r_ell>0, t_ell!=0,
```

with pairwise distinct real parts `r_ell`.  Suppose the eventually nonzero
coefficient sequences satisfy

```text
a_(ell,n)
 = C_ell Lambda_ell^n Gamma(1+alpha_ell n)
   (1+O(exp(-c_ell n))),                                  (1)
```

where `C_ell,Lambda_ell` are nonzero.  Then the formal series

```text
f_ell(z)=sum_(n>=0) a_(ell,n) z^n
```

are jointly differentially algebraically independent over `C(z)`.

More precisely, after ordering

```text
0<r_1<...<r_d,
```

the last series `f_d` is hypertranscendental over the differential field

```text
C(z)<f_1,...,f_(d-1)>.
```

Thus the theorem is triangular and survives adjoining every derivative of every
lower-real-slope carrier.

## Why this is stronger than the parent theorem

The parent theorem excludes an algebraic differential equation for one series by
showing that the imaginary factorial slope creates a periodic logarithmic chirp
which cannot cancel in a finite endpoint expansion.  Separate
hypertranscendence does not imply joint differential independence: two HT
functions may differ by a rational function or satisfy a nonlinear differential
relation.

The new point is that a carrier of strictly smaller real factorial order remains
negligible even after:

1. adjoining all of its derivatives;
2. taking arbitrary finite polynomial products with the maximal carrier;
3. allowing it in every coefficient of a putative ADE for the maximal carrier.

The endpoint chirp of the unique maximal real slope therefore survives over the
entire differential field generated by the lower slopes.

## Mixed-slope endpoint lemma

Put

```text
G_(ell,n)=A_ell^n Gamma(1+r_ell n),
```

where complex Stirling asymptotics applied to `(1)` give two-sided bounds

```text
c_ell G_(ell,n) <= |a_(ell,n)| <= C_ell' G_(ell,n).       (2)
```

For every fixed derivative order `j`,

```text
u_(ell,j,n)=(n+j)_j a_(ell,n)
```

obeys

```text
|u_(ell,j,n)|
 <= C_(ell,j) A_ell^n Gamma(1+r_ell n)
    (n+1)^((r_ell+1)j).                                  (3)
```

Let `r_*=max_(ell<d) r_ell<r_d`.  For a fixed differential
polynomial in the `f_ell`, split its coefficient of degree `N` as follows:

1. **maximal-slope endpoints:** one `f_d`-jet factor has degree `N-q`
   with `q<R`;
2. **lower-slope false endpoints:** one lower-slope jet has degree
   `N-q` with `q<R`, but no `f_d`-jet does;
3. **interior terms:** no factor has degree greater than `N-R`.

For large `N`, an endpoint factor is unique.

### Lower-slope false endpoints

For every fixed `K`, Stirling gives

```text
A_ell^N Gamma(1+r_ell N)
 = o(A_d^N Gamma(1+r_d N) N^(-K))                         (4)
```

after harmlessly changing the exponential bases, because

```text
log Gamma(1+r_d N)-log Gamma(1+r_ell N)
 =(r_d-r_ell)N log N+O(N).
```

Fixed shifts, derivative weights, and the finitely many complementary
coefficients do not affect `(4)`.  Hence every lower-slope false endpoint is
smaller than the maximal envelope by an arbitrary power of `N`.

### Interior terms

Every lower envelope is bounded above by a maximal-slope envelope with the
**same exponential base as the maximal carrier**:

```text
A_ell^n Gamma(1+r_ell n)
 <= C A_d^n Gamma(1+r_d n).                               (5)
```

This is valid despite arbitrary `A_ell/A_d`: the saving
`exp(-(r_d-r_ell)n log n+O(n))` dominates every fixed exponential ratio.
Enlarging `C` absorbs the finite initial segment.  Keeping the exact base
`A_d` is essential; a generic larger base `B` would leave an uncontrolled
exponential factor after convolution.

For a monomial with a fixed number of factors, order a composition
`n_1+...+n_e=N-p` so that `n_1` is maximal and put
`s=N-n_1`.  Uniform Stirling bounds give

```text
product_h Gamma(1+r_d n_h) / Gamma(1+r_d N)
 <= C N^L exp(-r_d Delta(n_1,...,n_e)),                   (6)

Delta=N log N-sum_h n_h log(max(1,n_h)).
```

If `s<=N/2`, convexity gives

```text
Delta >= s log(N/s).
```

If `s>N/2`, maximality keeps every normalized part away from one, so
`Delta>=c_e N`.  Summing the polynomially many compositions with `s>=R`
therefore gives

```text
O_R(A_d^N Gamma(1+r_d N) N^(B-r_d R/2)).                  (7)
```

Derivative weights only change `B`.  Combining `(4)` and `(7)` proves:

> For every fixed differential polynomial, everything except its
> maximal-slope endpoints is
>
> ```text
> O_R(|a_(d,N)| N^(B-r_d R/2)).
> ```                                                               (8)

This is the mixed-slope extension of the factorial endpoint estimate in the
parent theorem.

## Differential-independence proof

Proceed by induction on `d`.  The case `d=1` is the parent nonreal-slope
hypertranscendence theorem.

Assume `f_1,...,f_(d-1)` are jointly differentially independent and put

```text
K=C(z)<f_1,...,f_(d-1)>.
```

Suppose that `f_d` is differentially algebraic over `K`.  Clearing the finitely
many differential-field denominators gives a nonzero polynomial differential
relation

```text
P(z, (f_ell^(j))_(ell<=d,j<=m))=0.                        (9)
```

Choose such a relation of least order in the `f_d` jets and, subject to that,
least degree in its highest `f_d` jet.  Independence of the lower family and
characteristic zero imply that at least one evaluated `f_d`-partial derivative
is nonzero.  Equivalently, the formal series

```text
P_(Y_(d,j))(z,(f_ell^(h)))
```

do not all vanish.

For a fixed endpoint cutoff `R`, exact coefficient bookkeeping gives

```text
0=[z^N]P
 =sum_(j=0)^m sum_(q<R) c_(j,q)
    (N-q+j)_j a_(d,N-q+j) + E_(N,R),                     (10)
```

where

```text
c_(j,q)
 =[z^q]P_(Y_(d,j))(z,(f_ell^(h)))                         (11)
```

and `(8)` bounds `E_(N,R)`.  The identity is combinatorial: selecting the
unique large maximal-slope factor differentiates the monomial with respect to
that factor, while its complement contributes `(11)`.

For every fixed integer `h`, `(1)` and complex Stirling give

```text
a_(d,N+h)/a_(d,N)
 =(Lambda_d alpha_d^alpha_d)^h
   N^(alpha_d h)(1+O_h(1/N)).                             (12)
```

Let

```text
M=max {j+r_d(j-q): c_(j,q)!=0}.                           (13)
```

Choose `R` past every pair attaining `M` and large enough that the exponent in
`(8)` is less than `M`.  Divide `(10)` by `a_(d,N)N^M`.  All smaller real
orders and the remainder decay by a fixed power of `N`, so

```text
T(log N)=O(N^(-eta)),                                     (14)
```

where

```text
T(x)=sum_((j,q):j+r_d(j-q)=M)
 c_(j,q)(Lambda_d alpha_d^alpha_d)^(j-q)
 exp(i t_d(j-q)x).                                       (15)
```

Distinct endpoint pairs in `(15)` have distinct frequencies.  Indeed equality
of frequencies gives equality of `j-q`; equality of real endpoint orders then
gives equality of `j`, hence of `q`.  Therefore `T` is a nonzero finite
trigonometric polynomial.

For any large real `x`, take the nearest integer `N_x` to `exp(x)`.
Then `log N_x=x+O(exp(-x))`; boundedness of `T'` and `(14)` give
`T(x)->0`.  Every frequency in `(15)` is an integer multiple of `t_d`, so `T`
is periodic.  A periodic continuous function tending to zero is identically
zero, contradiction.

Thus `f_d` is hypertranscendental over `K`, completing the induction.

## Zeta-moment reflection-pair theorem

For `Re(alpha)>0`, define

```text
R_alpha(z)=sum_(n>=0) (-1)^n zeta(-alpha n) z^n.           (16)
```

When `Im(alpha)!=0`, the zeta functional equation and dominance of one
exponential branch of the sine factor give

```text
(-1)^n zeta(-alpha n)
 =C_alpha Lambda_alpha^n Gamma(1+alpha n)
  (1+O(exp(-c_alpha n))).                                 (17)
```

Hence any finite family `(R_(alpha_ell))` with nonzero imaginary parts and
pairwise distinct real parts is jointly differentially algebraically independent.

For `s` in the open critical strip with `Im(s)!=0`, put

```text
s^dagger=1-conjugate(s).
```

The two parameters have the same nonzero imaginary part and real parts
`Re(s)` and `1-Re(s)`.  Therefore

```text
R_s and R_(s^dagger) are differentially dependent
iff s=s^dagger
iff Re(s)=1/2.                                            (18)
```

The forward implication uses the joint-independence theorem when the real parts
are distinct; the reverse implication is equality of the two series.

## Zero-anchored version and exact RH boundary

Define the zero-anchored orbit series

```text
F_s(z)=sum_(n>=0) (-1)^n zeta((1-n)s) z^n.                 (19)
```

Reindexing gives the exact identity

```text
F_s(z)=zeta(s)-z R_s(z).                                  (20)
```

At a nontrivial zeta zero `rho`,

```text
F_rho=-z R_rho.                                           (21)
```

The functional equation and Schwarz symmetry also make
`rho^dagger=1-conjugate(rho)` a zero, so the reflected identity holds as well.
Multiplication by the nonzero rational function `z` does not change the
generated differential field.  Consequently

```text
F_rho and F_(rho^dagger) are differentially dependent
iff Re(rho)=1/2.                                          (22)
```

This yields the exact reformulation

```text
RH
iff every nontrivial zero rho has a differentially dependent
    zero-anchored reflected factorial-moment pair.         (23)
```

### Anti-theatre verdict

Equation `(23)` is not an RH proof.  Dependence in `(22)` is a faithful line
reader.  The condition `zeta(rho)=0` only removes the constant term in `(20)`;
finite polynomial changes are differentially algebraic gauges and cannot turn
an independent off-line pair into a dependent one.

This proves a useful no-go:

> Any zero-to-HT mechanism that inserts zerohood only through finitely many
> Taylor coefficients or regularization counterterms cannot constrain the
> horizontal location of a zero.  A successful producer must change an
> infinite source tail, singular lattice, or completion class.

The RH-bearing open core is therefore not the classifier `(22)` but a
source theorem, constructed before reading the divisor, which forces the two
zero-anchored reflected carriers into one differential dependence class at every
actual Xi zero.  No such theorem is proved here.

## Hostile checks

1. **Separate HT is insufficient.**  The proof establishes triangular
   hypertranscendence over the full lower-slope differential field.
2. **Distinct real parts are load-bearing.**  Equal real slopes have comparable
   factorial envelopes; conjugate chirps may interfere and require a different
   multicarrier argument.
3. **Every maximal slope must be nonreal.**  The contradiction uses the periodic
   logarithmic chirp.  Real irrational slopes can instead use exact Gevrey-order
   rigidity, but that is not included in this theorem.
4. **Lower products do not raise factorial order.**  Their Cauchy products are
   endpoint dominated.  The split into false endpoints and true interior terms
   is required; applying only the common `r_d` envelope would miss false
   lower-slope endpoints.
5. **Minimal separant is required.**  An unreduced relation may have all first
   partial derivatives vanish after evaluation.
6. **The zeta zero is not smuggled into the proof.**  Joint independence holds
   for every parameter pair off the line, whether or not either parameter is a
   zeta zero.
7. **No convergence claim is made.**  Every series here is formal and
   factorially divergent.
