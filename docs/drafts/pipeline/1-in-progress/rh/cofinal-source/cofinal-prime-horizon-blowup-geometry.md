## Frozen finite model

`p in P_X`, put

\[
 y_{p,X}={\log p\over L},\qquad
 a_{p,X}(z)=p^{-1/2}\exp(z y_{p,X})=p^{-1/2+z/L}.
\tag{1}
\]

For `z` in a fixed compact set and `X` large, `|a_{p,X}(z)|<1`.  In
`ell^2(N_0)` define

\[
 \omega_{p,X}(z)
 =\sqrt{1-|a_{p,X}(z)|^2}\sum_{k\geq0}a_{p,X}(z)^k e_k,
 \qquad
 \Omega_X(z)=\bigotimes_{p\leq X}\omega_{p,X}(z).
\tag{2}
\]

At `z=0`, the squared coefficients are exactly the critical Wold laws

\[
 (1-p^{-1})p^{-k}.
\tag{3}
\]

Writing `z=u+i tau`, the real coordinate gives the radial twist `c=u/log X`, while the imaginary
coordinate gives the prime-clock phase `p^(i tau/log X)`.  Functional-equation reflection acts on
the centered parameter by

\[
 R(z)=-\bar z=(-u)+i\tau.
\tag{4}

Thus reflection exchanges radial mouths and preserves clock phase.

## Conjecture H1 -- prime logarithmic coherent limit

## Candidate proof of H1

The exact finite kernel is

\[
 K_X(z,w)=\prod_{p\leq X}
 {\sqrt{(1-p^{-1}e^{2u y_{p,X}})(1-p^{-1}e^{2v y_{p,X}})}
  \over 1-p^{-1}e^{(\bar z+w)y_{p,X}}},
\tag{8}
\]

where `u=Re z` and `v=Re w`.  Expanding the three logarithms gives

\[
 \log K_X(z,w)
 =\sum_{p\leq X}{F_{z,w}(y_{p,X})\over p}+R_X(z,w),
\tag{9}
\]

with

\[
 F_{z,w}(y)=e^{(\bar z+w)y}-{1\over2}e^{2uy}-{1\over2}e^{2vy}.
\tag{10}
\]

Here `F(0)=0`.  On any compact parameter set, the terms of logarithmic order at least two have a
factor `y` after cancellation at `y=0`.  Once `log X` exceeds a fixed multiple of the compact
radius, the geometric-series remainder is bounded by

\[
 |R_X(z,w)|
 \leq {C\over\log X}\sum_p{\log p\over p^{3/2}}
 =O((\log X)^{-1}),
\tag{11}
\]

uniformly on that compact set.

For every continuously differentiable `F` with `F(0)=0`, partial summation and the prime number
theorem give the logarithmic-prime weak limit

\[
 \sum_{p\leq X}{F(\log p/\log X)\over p}
 \longrightarrow
 \int_0^1{F(y)\over y}\,dy.
\tag{12}
\]

Indeed write `F(y)=y g(y)` with continuous `g`; then the left side is

\[
 {1\over\log X}\sum_{p\leq X}{\log p\over p}
 g(\log p/\log X),
\]

and the prime number theorem turns this into `integral_0^1 g(y)dy`.  Equations `(9)`--`(12)` prove
`(6)`, including local uniformity.  Since every `K_X` is a Gram kernel, `K` is positive definite.

## Conjecture H2 -- the common Fock interior

Let

\[
 \mathcal H=L^2((0,1),dy/y),\qquad \phi_z(y)=e^{zy}-1.
\tag{13}
\]

Then `phi_z in H`, and

\[
 |K(z,w)|
 =\exp\!\left(-{1\over2}\|\phi_z-\phi_w\|_{\mathcal H}^2\right).
\tag{14}
\]

More precisely, if

\[
 \vartheta(z)=\int_0^1{\operatorname{Im}(e^{zy})\over y}\,dy,
\tag{15}
\]

then `K` differs by the source phase gauge `exp(-i vartheta(z)+i vartheta(w))` from the normalized
bosonic coherent-state kernel associated with `phi_z` in the symmetric Fock space over `H`.
Thus the limiting object is not merely a distance formula: it is a concrete common representation
of the transition amplitudes between boundary sectors.

### Proof

The right side of `(14)` follows by taking the real part of `(5)`.  Expanding

\[
 \langle\phi_z,\phi_w\rangle

\]

shows that its integrand differs from the integrand in `(5)` by
`i Im(e^(zy))-i Im(e^(wy))`.  Integration gives exactly the gauge in `(15)`.

## Conjecture H3 -- one-mouth phase--redshift geometry

The projective coherent-state map induces, under the fidelity convention

\[
 ds^2=\lim_{dz\to0}-2\log|K(z,z+dz)|,

the metric

\[
 \boxed{\quad

 G(u)=\int_0^1 y e^{2uy}dy
 =\begin{cases}
 {e^{2u}(2u-1)+1\over4u^2},&u\ne0,\\
 1/2,&u=0.
 \end{cases}
 \quad}
\tag{16}
\]

The source-gauge Berry curvature has magnitude

\[
 2G(u)\,du\wedge d\tau.
\tag{17}
\]

The Gaussian curvature is

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \mathcal K(u)=-{1\over2G(u)}{d^2\over du^2}\log G(u)<0.
 \quad}
\tag{18}
\]

### Proof

Differentiate the feature curve: `partial_z phi_z(y)=y e^(zy)`.  Its squared Hilbert norm is
`G(u)`, proving `(16)` and the quantum-geometric tensor whose imaginary part gives `(17)`.

Let `nu_u` be the probability measure with density `y e^(2uy)/G(u)` on `(0,1)`.  Then

\[
 (\log G)''=4\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_u}(y)>0.
\tag{19}
\]

The standard curvature formula for a conformal metric depending only on `u` gives `(18)`.  Strictness
follows because `nu_u` is not a point mass.

## Conjecture H4 -- reflection-paired throat

Pair every state with its reflected mouth:

\[
 z\longmapsto \Omega_X(z)\otimes\Omega_X(-\bar z).
\tag{20}
\]

The limiting projective metric is

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 g_{\rm pair}=H(u)(du^2+d\tau^2),\qquad
 H(u)=G(u)+G(-u)=2\int_0^1 y\cosh(2uy)dy.
 \quad}
\tag{21}
\]

It has all of the following exact properties:

1. `R(u,tau)=(-u,tau)` is an isometry.
2. Its fixed seam `u=0` is totally geodesic and has `H(0)=1`.
3. The metric is complete and has two radial ends.
4. Its Gaussian curvature is strictly negative everywhere.
5. At the fixed seam,

   \[
   \boxed{\mathcal K_{\rm pair}(0)=-1.}
   \tag{22}
   \]

6. The paired Berry curvature is

   \[
   2(G(u)-G(-u))du\wedge d\tau,
   \tag{23}
   \]

   so it reverses across the mouths and vanishes exactly at the seam.

### Proof

Tensor-product quantum metrics add.  Pullback by `z -> -bar z` changes `u` to `-u` and preserves
`tau`, proving `(21)` and reflection symmetry.  The fixed set of an isometry is totally geodesic.

The function `H` is the moment-generating integral of the nondegenerate symmetric variable taking
values `+y` and `-y` with base weight `y dy`.  Hence `(log H)''>0`, which proves strict negative
curvature as in `(18)`.  At zero,

\[
 H(0)=1,\qquad H'(0)=0,\qquad H''(0)=2.
\]

Therefore

\[
 \mathcal K_{\rm pair}(0)
 =-{1\over2H(0)}(\log H)''(0)=-1.
\]

As `u -> -infinity`, `G(u)~1/(4u^2)`; as `u -> +infinity`,
`G(u)~e^(2u)/(2u)`.  Both radial lengths diverge, and a path escaping in `tau` with bounded `u`
also has infinite length.  This proves completeness.  Berry curvatures add, but the reflected
pullback reverses `du`, giving `(23)`.
