- **Statement:** For an `n`-point complex spectrum, a real center `c`, and a primitive
  `d`th root `ζ`, simultaneous invariance under conjugation and the affine rotation
  `z ↦ c + ζ(z-c)` is equivalent to two sharp finite moment conditions through order `n`:
  every centered power sum is real, and every centered power sum in a nontrivial cyclic
  character sector vanishes.

## Proof

**[PROVED HERE]** Translate the spectrum by the real center `c`. Because conjugation fixes `c`,
translation intertwines conjugation on the original spectrum with conjugation on the centered
spectrum. The sharp finite Satake–Torelli theorem therefore identifies conjugation closure with
reality of every centered power sum of order at most `n`. Independently, the affine cyclic
moment-symmetry theorem identifies invariance under `z ↦ c + ζ(z-c)` with vanishing of every
centered power sum of order at most `n` whose degree is not divisible by `d`. Taking these two
equivalences together gives the claimed simultaneous criterion. Both converses use finite
power-sum Torelli, so no labelled orbit decomposition or moment beyond `n` is required. No
computation is used. ∎

## Strict gain over corpus endpoints

The cyclic theorem sees only complex-linear rotation sectors. The Satake theorem sees only the
anti-linear conjugation involution and uses uncentered moments. Their closure classifies the full
affine dihedral generator pair at an arbitrary real center by one sharp centered finite-moment
packet. Neither endpoint supplies the joint anti-linear/cyclic criterion.
