## Frontier review (step 1 of the method)

The registry's hypertranscendence programme has two relevant poles.

1. **Archimedean carrier laws.** `finite-real-shift-gamma-projective-boundary-law`: a finite
   differential field generated by real Gamma translates that are *pairwise incongruent mod `ℤ`* has
   a constant one-point upper vertical projective boundary; hence every holomorphic vertically
   uniformly almost-periodic value DA over it has finite rational Bohr-frequency rank.
2. **The arithmetic marking of zeta.** `archimedean-relative-prime-cocycle-curvature`: for two
   distinct primes `p,q`, `zeta` is differentially transcendental over
   `K_(Gamma,Lambda) = C(s)<Gamma(s+lambda) : lambda in Lambda>_D` with `Lambda = Z log p + Z log q`.

Read together: the programme classifies the abstract archimedean relation geometry, and then *marks*
zeta inside it by a retained gauge class. The classical backdrop is **Postnikov**: the Dirichlet
`L`-functions are algebraically differentially independent — but over `C(s)`.

## Rejected first idea, recorded so it is not re-attempted

*All-primes strengthening of the two-prime card.* The card's proof is a **finiteness reduction**: any
candidate relation uses finitely many translates, and prime-log independence is used only to make
them pairwise incongruent mod `ℤ`. For `Lambda = ⊕_p Z log p`, a nonzero element is `log r` with
`r ∈ Q_{>0}\{1}`, and `log r ∈ Z` would force `r = e^m`, impossible since `e` is transcendental.
Hence **the all-primes case follows from the identical proof**. This is hypothesis-weakening, not
discovery, and is rejected under the ledger rule.

The sharp form of the parent theorem is therefore: *zeta is differentially transcendental over the
Gamma field saturated along **any** subgroup `Lambda ⊂ R` containing no nonzero integer.* Recorded as
a sharpening of an existing card, not as new knowledge.

## The conjecture

> **Relative Postnikov.** Let `Lambda ⊂ R` be a subgroup containing no nonzero integer, and let
> `K = C(s)<Gamma(s+lambda) : lambda in Lambda>_D`. Then the Dirichlet `L`-functions `{L(s,chi)}`,
> over all primitive characters `chi`, are differentially algebraically **independent over `K`**.

## Why this is not a weakening of either parent

- Against **Postnikov**: the base field is enlarged from `C(s)` to the translation-saturated
  archimedean field `K`. Independence over a *larger* base is a strictly stronger statement.
- Against the **two-prime card**: that treats a *single* function (`zeta`) over `K`. The conjecture
  is a *joint* statement over the whole `L`-family. The two-prime proof does not obviously extend:
  its final step is that zeta's translation cocycle retains curvature, and a joint version needs the
  characters' arithmetic independence, which is an input beyond the single-function case.

So the conjecture sits strictly outside the convex hull of its two parents.

## FULL-TEXT LITERATURE FINDING — the parent card implies an open conjecture

Extracted from Wang, Liu, Li, *On algebraic differential equations concerning the Riemann-zeta
function and the Euler-gamma function*, arXiv:2005.02707 (2020), introduction:

- **Markus's conjecture (OPEN).** Markus proved that `ζ(sin 2πz)` satisfies no nontrivial ADE whose
  coefficients are polynomials in `Γ` and its derivatives, and **conjectured that `ζ` itself satisfies
  no such equation.** In our language: **`ζ` is differentially transcendental over `C(z)<Γ>_D`.**
  This is stated as an open conjecture.
- **Wang–Liu–Li Theorem 1.1 (partial).** No nontrivial `P` with polynomial coefficients in `z`
  satisfies `P(ζ, ζ', …, ζ^{(m)}, Γ, Γ^{(n)}, Γ^{(l)}) ≡ 0`. That is Markus's conjecture restricted to
  coefficients built from **three** `Γ`-jets, not the full differential field generated by `Γ`.
- Background cited there: Hilbert's Problem 18 for `ζ` (solved by Ostrowski); Bank–Kaufman and
  Liao–Yang for meromorphic-coefficient classes; Li–Ye.
