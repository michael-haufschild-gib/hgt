# Nomological Homotopy Geometry — route-independent predictions

- **Status:** `[FIELD PREDICTIONS] [FORMULATED BEFORE CORPUS RECALIBRATION] [NOT RH]`
- **Purpose:** state what NHG predicts in any mathematical domain before proof routes are used as
  examples.

## P1. Curvature-tower equivalence principle

Two presentations are local gauges only if they induce equivalent pointed anchored Q-germs and
equivalent partial-flattening towers `(P_n,Ob_(n+1))`. Equality of solutions, endpoints, spectra,
determinants, or the first curvature class is insufficient.

**Exact support.** Structured Morita invariance of the minimal operation and leading curvature;
observer functoriality of every obstruction spectrum.

**Falsifier.** Two claimed gauges have different obstruction spectra or different effective
flattening branches.

## P2. False-flatness dichotomy

For a represented strict observer, an obstructed source can appear flatter only because:

1. a nonzero obstruction class enters the observer's cohomology kernel; or
2. the observer admits a zero-obstruction flattening branch that has no effective source lift.

Serial observers cannot repair either loss. Parallel observers repair class erasure exactly by
joint kernel separation; branch lifting remains an independent descent problem.

**Falsifier.** A strict observer displays false flatness while reflecting zero on every source
obstruction and effectively lifting every zero target branch.

## P3. Pro-Spencer location of unbounded curvature

On a fixed `d`-dimensional smooth base, local form-degree curvature obstructions terminate at degree
`d`. Therefore an infinite obstruction tower must occur in a pro-Spencer/jet base, an unbounded
coefficient filtration, or a completion boundary.

Differential hypertranscendence can drive noncompactness through unbounded jet depth. It need not
produce noncompact ordinary holonomy or a nonprecompact observer orbit.

**Falsifier.** A genuine infinite sequence of nonzero ordinary form-degree obstructions on one fixed
finite-dimensional smooth manifold with finite-amplitude coefficients.

## P4. Curvature-rank horizon

Let `W_n` be the linear span of the order-`n` obstruction spectrum. Any observer family whose total
degree-`n` cohomology rank is less than `dim W_n` erases a nonzero curvature direction. If
`dim W_n` is unbounded, no fixed finite-rank observer family is curvature-conservative at every
order.

**Falsifier.** A finite-rank linear observer injects an obstruction span of larger dimension.

## P5. Positive phase quotient and cross-polar repair

For finite Hilbert law tensors, an individual Gram shadow `T^*T` forgets exactly the left polar
partial isometry. For a family with common output, the full cross-Gram packet `T_i^*T_j`
reconstructs every tensor up to one common output partial isometry.

Thus positive scalarization loses phase, while positive cross-polarization can retain all relative
phase. Positivity itself is not universally lossy.

If the prior cyclic pairing is indefinite, positive observers form the polarization fibre
`O(p,q)/(O(p) times O(q))`; the full indefinite gauge group selects no invariant point. A chosen
polarization reduces gauge to a compact subgroup and gives a proper finite-dimensional radius, but
does not make the master action coercive. Ordinary sections exist over paracompact
constant-signature bases; a parallel positive section exists exactly when connection holonomy fixes
a polarization and therefore reduces to a compact stabilizer.

**Falsifier.** Two finite tensor families have identical complete cross-Gram packets but are not
related by one common output partial isometry.

## P6. Closed observation, open history

For a law-history fibration with specified transport, a recurrent observer loop can lift to a
nonclosed source history. Component nonclosure is measured by the homotopy-fibre boundary;
connection holonomy is separate. If the vertical holonomy has infinite order and the derived cost
is proper on its cyclic subgroup, repeated observer recurrence has divergent lifted cost.

**Falsifier.** Under those hypotheses, repeated holonomy powers have bounded cost.

## P7. Restricted/global holonomy separation

Analytic curvature and its Spencer jets can generate only restricted identity-component holonomy.
Flat global monodromy remains an independent extension by the fundamental infinity-groupoid.

**Falsifier.** Two analytic connections with identical local curvature towers and different flat
monodromy are declared locally gauge-equivalent by NHG.

## P8. No universal scalar field equation

Curvature obstruction, component boundary, holonomy, polar phase, prolongation growth, completion,
and proper action are different typed outputs. No one scalar invariant determines all of them.

NHG instead predicts compatibility diagrams:

\[
 \text{source-anchored Q-law}
 \longrightarrow
 \begin{cases}
   \text{curvature Postnikov tower},\\
   \text{observer descent tower},\\
   \text{polar/cross-polar packet},\\
   \text{integrated history and holonomy},\\
   \text{Spencer prolongation and completion},\\
   \text{derived action after reduction}.
 \end{cases}
\tag{P.1}
\]

The geometry is unified by one structured source object and functorial compatibility, not by
forcing heterogeneous invariants to be numerically equal.

## P9. Gauge-rigid event index

For a polarized finite-dimensional sourced Hessian path with regular crossings, spectral flow is
the sum of signatures of the Hessian derivative restricted to zero modes. That crossing tensor is
unchanged by unitary gauge; a pure gauge-conjugacy path has zero spectral flow.

Nonzero event index therefore requires transverse law change, polarization failure,
domain/admission change, or nonunitary observation.

**Falsifier.** A unitary-conjugacy path of self-adjoint finite matrices has nonzero spectral flow.

These predictions are field claims only where their typed hypotheses are constructed. A missing
comparison is reported as missing; a nonclosed operator is an admission defect; a dense radical is
a topology defect. Neither is renamed zero curvature. Corpus routes may test these predictions only
after the field objects have been fixed independently.
