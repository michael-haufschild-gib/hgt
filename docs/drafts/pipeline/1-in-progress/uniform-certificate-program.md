# Uniform certificate program: bounded local dependency and duality

- **ID:** UCP-001
- **Stage:** 1-in-progress
- **Type:** CANDIDATE
- **Source:** universal-law-field/STATUS.md — CANNOT-425, MUST-433, MUST-436, CANNOT-437
- **Statement:** Drive to a verdict the two remaining mechanism families that are simultaneously
  extreme-controlling and height-uniform: bounded local dependency, and duality certificates.

## Goal

The field's open core is an upper bound on `Σ_t #(free orbits at ordinate t)`. Two no-gos constrain
any method:

- **CANNOT-425 / MUST-433** — averages cannot control extremes (degree mismatch: the target is
  homogeneity degree 0, every pointwise average is degree 1).
- **CANNOT-437** — the required bound is an absolute constant, uniform in the height; no lossy
  version exists.

A candidate must pass **both**. A cross-domain scan for mechanisms that are both extreme-controlling
and height-uniform returned six: expander spectral gaps, property (T) via finite SOS, the Lovász
local lemma, quantum error-correction thresholds, renormalization fixed points, LP/minimax duality.
Their common mechanism is the point:

> **A size-independent constant arises from a single certificate reproduced at every scale — a fixed
> point, a finite identity, a local degree condition, a dual feasible point. Never from estimating
> at each size.**

Every method in this field estimates at each height. That is the diagnosis this program acts on.

Corpus audit of the four candidate mechanisms (2026-09-25):

| mechanism | corpus status |
|-|-|
| finite SOS certificate | **DEAD** — `ConePositivstellensatzFailure`: Λ-positivity is not semidefinite representable |
| bounded local dependency | **ABSENT** (the "Lovász" modules are lattice reduction, the other LLL) |
| duality certificates | **ABSENT** (existing "duality certificates" are curvature-sign, a different notion) |
| RG fixed point | ambiguous — corpus "universality/fixed point" naming is dense but denotes other objects |

The SOS refutation is informative rather than merely negative: the cone **is** sums of Hermitian
squares when all frequencies are real (Pólya–Szegő) and when all are imaginary (Fejér–Riesz); the
**mixed** case fails. Mixed frequencies are exactly the on-line/off-line mixture. Any surviving
mechanism must not inherit that failure.

## Approach

### Lane A — bounded local dependency

**A1 (done, recorded below).** The Lovász local lemma proper does **not** transfer: LLL proves the
*existence* of a configuration avoiding all bad events. Our divisor is given, not chosen. The
transferable members of this family are the **forcing** ones, where a size-independent local
condition forces a global conclusion:

- **Dobrushin uniqueness** — influence matrix norm `< 1` forces uniqueness of the Gibbs state;
  the constant is absolute and size-independent. Closest match.
- **Strong spatial mixing / correlation decay** — bounded influence forces exponential decay
  uniformly in system size.
- **Transfer-operator spectral gap** — Perron–Frobenius with a gap independent of the window.

**A2.** Is there a Gibbs / local-influence structure on the divisor? The corpus carries Gibbs
currents and theta-Gibbs moment machinery; determine whether the ordinate fibration with its
fibrewise boundary map admits an influence matrix.

**A3.** Bound the influence norm. Verdict condition: a uniform bound below 1 (**progress**), or a
proof that the norm reaches 1 at large height (**failure with a reason**).

### Lane B — duality certificates

**B1 (first task, cheapest potential kill).** Does `ConePositivstellensatzFailure` extend to general
duality, or is it specific to *semidefinite representability*? Every convex cone has a dual; the
failure shows no fixed-degree SDP relaxation decides membership, which does not by itself exclude a
dual certificate drawn from a non-semidefinite cone. Settle this before any construction.

**B1 — VERDICT: LANE B CLOSED (2026-09-25).** The cone is
`C = { h(u) = Σ_j t_j cosh(w_j u) even real : h ≥ 0 on ℝ }`. For a positivity problem `min_u h ≥ 0`
the LP/SDP dual **is** the moment side, and a dual feasible point certifying the lower bound **is**
an SOS certificate. So `ConePositivstellensatzFailure` is a **duality-gap theorem** for this cone,
not merely a statement about one proof style. The gap does not close with degree: the cylinder-lift
argument takes `Γ` to be generated by the frequencies of *any* putative finite representation, so it
is degree-free.

**B2 does not arise.**

*Confidence:* the corpus theorem is Lean-proved. The identification *dual certificate = SOS
certificate for this cone* is standard convex duality at paper level, not formalised; it is the
load-bearing step if this lane is ever reopened.

## No-go closure (mandatory when falsified)

### Lane B (closed 2026-09-25)

- **Exact killing theorem/mechanism:** `ConePositivstellensatzFailure` — Λ-positivity of the
  reflection cone is not semidefinite representable, with the explicit witness
  `h(u) = (1 − cos u) + (cosh u − 2)(cosh u − 3)`, nonnegative on `ℝ`, whose cylinder lift is `−1/4`
  off the diagonal. Since the SDP dual of the positivity problem is the moment side, this is a
  duality-gap theorem, and the cylinder-lift argument is degree-free, so no relaxation at any order
  reaches the cone.
- **Strongest positive survivor:** the cone **is** sums of Hermitian squares when all frequencies are
  real (Pólya–Szegő) and when all are imaginary (Fejér–Riesz). **The duality gap is created exactly
  by the mixture of real and imaginary frequencies — the same on-line/off-line mixture the hypothesis
  is about.** Both pure cases are tight; only the mixed case is not. The gap is therefore a measure
  of the object under study rather than an artefact of the method.
- **New insight:** positivity certificates for this field fail for the *same reason* the field is
  hard, not for a separable technical reason. A method that is tight at both pure ends and gaps in
  the middle cannot be repaired by strengthening the relaxation.
- **Status:** killing theorem Lean-proved in corpus; the dual = SOS identification is paper-level.

### Lane A (closed 2026-09-25)

- **Exact killing theorem/mechanism:** two, on the two sides. *Prime side:* the parity moments
  factor as `∏(1 − p^m)`, so local influences amplify by `p^m`; fixed-aperture contraction cannot
  supply the source. *Divisor side:* `tsum_zeroInfluence_eq_one` — the normalised influence matrix
  has row sums exactly `1`, so Dobrushin's strict contraction fails with **zero margin**.
- **Strongest positive survivor:** on the prime side the moments factor **exactly** into local prime
  factors, so **the locality is perfect and only the contractivity fails** — which isolates which
  half of the Dobrushin hypothesis is at fault. On the divisor side the failure is not by a large or
  height-growing factor but by exactly nothing, and the reason is structural: the influence matrix is
  stochastic *because* the marginality identity is an averaging identity.
- **New insight:** the surviving question is not contraction but **recurrence** — whether a bounded
  harmonic function for this explicit chain is constant. Contraction and recurrence are different
  mechanisms; this lane converts the first into the second, and the second is the question this field
  already reached from the transverse-equilibrium direction and found marginal.
- **Status:** divisor-side killing theorem Lean-proved (`ZeroInfluenceStochastic`); prime-side
  mechanism read off a Lean-proved corpus identity.
