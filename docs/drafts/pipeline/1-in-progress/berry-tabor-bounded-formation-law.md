## Frozen conjecture

Let \(\lambda_1\leq\lambda_2\leq\cdots\) be the Dirichlet eigenvalues of

\[
  B=[0,1]\times[0,2^{1/3}]\times[0,2^{-1/3}],
\]

counted with multiplicity, and unfold them by Weyl's law:

\[
  \xi_j=\frac{\omega_3}{(2\pi)^3}\lambda_j^{3/2}.
\]

The volume of \(B\) is one and \(\omega_3=4\pi/3\). Equivalently, before ordering,

\[
 \xi(m)=\frac{\pi}{6}
 \left(m_1^2+2^{-2/3}m_2^2+2^{2/3}m_3^2\right)^{3/2},
 \qquad m\in\mathbb Z_{>0}^3.
\]

For \(X_T\) uniform on \([0,T]\), define the randomly recentered point measure

\[
  \Xi_T=\sum_{j\geq1}\delta_{\xi_j-X_T}.
\]

`[CONJECTURE BT-BF]` For every nonnegative \(f\in C_c(\mathbb R)\),

\[
 \boxed{
 \lim_{T\to\infty}\frac1T\int_0^T
   \exp\!\left(-\sum_j f(\xi_j-x)\right)\,dx
 =\exp\!\left(-\int_{\mathbb R}(1-e^{-f(u)})\,du\right).
 }
\]

Equivalently, \(\Xi_T\) converges vaguely in distribution to the unit-rate Poisson point process.
The statement is frozen independently of a proof route.

**Public sentence.** One explicit integrable quantum box has locally random energy levels even
though every level is generated by a deterministic lattice formula.

**Risky consequence.** Randomly sampled local occupancy vectors converge jointly to independent
Poisson laws; in particular the Palm nearest-neighbour spacing law is exponential. Pair correlation
alone does not imply either conclusion.

  near-clock packets therefore have full phase-Haar laws without acquiring Poisson local gaps.
- **Representation lesson:** completed-time phase sampling and high-energy local spectral sampling
  are independent axes.
- **Positive survivor:** `WeakInteractionRecurrenceSingularityLaw.lean` already Lean-proves the
  sharp density/instability theorem exposing this separation.
- **Status:** `[LEAN-PROVED] [CORPUS]`; no duplicate formalization.

### C2 — prove every factorial correlation and deduce the point-process limit

- **Verdict:** `[BRIDGE] [PAPER-PROVED]`; viable sufficient route, not yet the arithmetic proof.
- **Correction to preliminary attack:** direct interchange of an infinite moment series would need
  uniform integrability, but that interchange is unnecessary. Independently thin each local point
  with retention probability \(1-e^{-f}\). The Laplace functional is exactly the thinned void

- **Representation lesson:** bounded transforms remain the endpoint, while finite-order correlation
  theorems can reach it through pointwise inclusion--exclusion rather than analytic moment-series
  exchange.
- **Positive survivor:** full Poisson convergence follows if every fixed-order correlation measure
  of the explicit spectrum is proved Poissonian. The first unknown arithmetic gate is order three.
- **Status:** pair correlation externally proved for the explicit system; deterministic triple and
  higher correlations remain open.

## Paper reduction R1 — subsequential stationary pair-Poisson laws

`[BRIDGE] [PAPER-PROVED]` Let \((\xi_j)\) be any locally finite sequence in
\(\mathbb R_{\geq 0}\), counted with multiplicity, whose counting function
\(N(t)=\#\{j:\xi_j\leq t\}\), extended by zero for \(t<0\), satisfies
\(N(t)/t\to1\). For \(X_T\sim\operatorname{Unif}[0,T]\), put
\(\Xi_T=\sum_j\delta_{\xi_j-X_T}\). Then:

1. the laws of \(\Xi_T\) are tight in the vague topology;
2. every subsequential limit is stationary;
3. for every bounded interval \(I\),
   \[
     \mathbb E\,\Xi_T(I)\longrightarrow |I|;
   \]
4. if the pair-correlation measures of \((\xi_j)\) converge to Lebesgue measure, every
   subsequential limit has intensity one and Poisson second factorial moment
   \[
     \mathbb E\,[\Xi(I)(\Xi(I)-1)]=|I|^2.
   \]

### Proof

For a bounded interval \(I=(a,b]\), Fubini and translation give

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E\,\Xi_T(I)
 &=\frac1T\int_0^T\bigl(N(x+b)-N(x+a)\bigr)\,dx\\
 &=\frac1T\left(\int_{T+a}^{T+b}N(u)\,du-\int_a^bN(u)\,du\right).
 \end{aligned}
\]

The first term tends to \(b-a\), uniformly over the fixed-width terminal interval, because
\(N(u)/u\to1\); the second is \(o(1)\). Hence local mean counts converge to Lebesgue length and are
uniformly bounded along every tail. Markov's inequality on the nested intervals \([-n,n]\), with a
summable error allocation, produces bounds \(M_n\) such that
\(\Xi_T([-n,n])\leq M_n\) simultaneously with arbitrarily high probability. Point measures with
these local mass bounds form a relatively compact set in the vague topology. This proves
tightness.

Let \(\tau_s\) translate a point measure by \(s\). For every bounded measurable functional \(F\),

\[
 \left|\mathbb E F(\tau_s\Xi_T)-\mathbb E F(\Xi_T)\right|
 \leq \frac{2|s|}{T}\,\lVert F\rVert_\infty,
\]

because the two center averages are over \([s,T+s]\) and \([0,T]\). Every subsequential limit is
therefore stationary.

For the second factorial moment, first take an interval of length \(L\). Integrating
\(\Xi_T(I)(\Xi_T(I)-1)\) over the center turns every ordered pair difference \(u=\xi_j-\xi_i\)
into the compact triangular weight \((L-|u|)_+\). Anchors within a fixed distance of the two center
boundaries contribute \(o(T)\): this follows by subtracting the pair-correlation asymptotics at
cutoffs \(T+O(1)\) and \(T-O(1)\). Pair-correlation convergence therefore yields

\[
 \mathbb E[\Xi_T(I)(\Xi_T(I)-1)]
 \longrightarrow \int_{\mathbb R}(L-|u|)_+\,du=L^2.
\]

Together with the first-moment limit this gives a uniform \(L^2\) bound for \(\Xi_T(I)\), hence
uniform integrability. A stationary locally finite point process has no atom at any fixed
location almost surely, so interval counts are continuous at the limit with probability one.
First and second factorial moments thus pass to every subsequential limit, proving (4).

For `[CONJECTURE BT-BF]`, Weyl's law gives \(N(t)/t\to1\), while Kim--Marklof--Welsh Theorem 1.1
provides the pair-correlation premise. Consequently every sequence \(T_n\to\infty\) has a
subsequence whose local law converges to a stationary unit-intensity point process with Poisson
two-point density. Full Berry--Tabor is reduced to uniqueness: rule out every non-Poisson process
inside this subsequential class.

## Paper reduction R2 — Bonferroni-thinning closure

`[BRIDGE] [PAPER-PROVED]` Let \(\Pi_T\) be locally finite point processes on \(\mathbb R\). Assume
that for every \(k\geq1\) and every nonnegative \(p\in C_c(\mathbb R)\) with \(p\leq1\),

\[
 \mathbb E\!\sum_{x_1,\ldots,x_k\in\Pi_T}^{\ne}
       \prod_{r=1}^k p(x_r)
 \longrightarrow \left(\int_{\mathbb R}p(u)\,du\right)^k.
 \tag{R2.1}
\]

Then \(\Pi_T\) converges in distribution to the unit-rate Poisson point process.

### Proof

Fix \(f\in C_c(\mathbb R)\), \(f\geq0\), and set \(p=1-e^{-f}\). Conditional on \(\Pi_T\), retain
independently each point \(x\) with probability \(p(x)\), and let \(Z_T\) be the number retained.
Only finitely many points meet \(\operatorname{supp}p\), and

\[
 \mathbb P(Z_T=0\mid\Pi_T)
 =\prod_{x\in\Pi_T}(1-p(x))
 =\exp\!\left(-\int f\,d\Pi_T\right).
 \tag{R2.2}
\]

Independent marking and (R2.1) give, for every fixed \(k\),

\[
 \mathbb E[(Z_T)_k]
 \longrightarrow \lambda^k,
 \qquad \lambda=\int_{\mathbb R}(1-e^{-f(u)})\,du.
 \tag{R2.3}
\]

For every integer-valued \(Z\geq0\), finite inclusion--exclusion gives the pointwise Bonferroni
bounds

\[
 \sum_{k=0}^{2m+1}\frac{(-1)^k}{k!}(Z)_k
 \leq \mathbf 1_{\{Z=0\}}
 \leq \sum_{k=0}^{2m}\frac{(-1)^k}{k!}(Z)_k.
 \tag{R2.4}
\]

Take expectations, then \(T\to\infty\) using only the finitely many limits through order
\(2m+1\). This sandwiches every subsequential limit of \(\mathbb P(Z_T=0)\) between the odd and even
partial sums of \(e^{-\lambda}\). Letting \(m\to\infty\) proves

\[
 \mathbb E\exp\!\left(-\int f\,d\Pi_T\right)
 \longrightarrow e^{-\lambda}.
 \tag{R2.5}
\]

These are exactly the Laplace functionals of the unit-rate Poisson point process, so they determine
vague convergence in distribution. No infinite moment-series interchange and no uniform
factorial-geometric moment bound occurs.

For `[CONJECTURE BT-BF]`, R2 changes the open arithmetic target from one moving grand-canonical
observable to a finite-dimensional hierarchy: for each fixed \(k\), prove (R2.1) for the randomly
recentered explicit ternary-form spectrum. Kim--Marklof--Welsh close \(k=2\); \(k=3\) is the first
unknown gate.

## Paper reduction R3 — star-theta factorization and triple nonescape

Let

\[
 Q(m)=m_1^2+2^{-2/3}m_2^2+2^{2/3}m_3^2,
 \qquad
 \Theta_{M,w}(s)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z^3}w(m/M)e(sQ(m)),
\]

where \(w\) is a compactly supported smooth weight and
\(e(x)=e^{2\pi i x}\). Use the Fourier convention
\(\psi(x)=\int_{\mathbb R}\widehat\psi(t)e(tx)\,dt\).

### R3.1 Exact star factorization

`[BRIDGE] [PAPER-PROVED]` The smoothed three-star count

\[
 \mathcal C_{M,L}=
 \sum_{m_0,m_1,m_2\in\mathbb Z^3}
 \prod_{r=0}^2w(m_r/M)
 \psi_1(L(Q(m_1)-Q(m_0)))
 \psi_2(L(Q(m_2)-Q(m_0)))
\]

has the exact representation

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal C_{M,L}=
 \int_{\mathbb R^2}\widehat\psi_1(t_1)\widehat\psi_2(t_2)
 \Theta_{M,w}(Lt_1)\Theta_{M,w}(Lt_2)
 \Theta_{M,w}(-L(t_1+t_2))\,dt_1dt_2.}
 \tag{R3.1}
\]

**Proof.** Apply Fourier inversion to the two difference cutoffs. The phase is

\[
 Lt_1Q(m_1)+Lt_2Q(m_2)-L(t_1+t_2)Q(m_0),
\]

so the three lattice variables separate. Absolute finiteness from the compact weight justifies
reordering.

The pairwise-distinct count is recovered pointwise by the partition-lattice identity

\[
 \mathbf1_{m_0,m_1,m_2\ \mathrm{distinct}}
 =1-\mathbf1_{m_0=m_1}-\mathbf1_{m_0=m_2}-\mathbf1_{m_1=m_2}
   +2\mathbf1_{m_0=m_1=m_2}.
 \tag{R3.2}
\]

Every proper diagonal in (R3.2) reduces to a one-difference count or a Weyl count. Hence existing
pair correlation plus Weyl asymptotics determine all diagonal main terms; only the fully
off-diagonal part of (R3.1) is new.

### R3.2 Triple-product nonescape from a subunit cusp moment

`[BRIDGE] [PAPER-PROVED]` For every measurable \(h:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R_{\geq0}\),

\[
 \int_{\mathbb R^2}h(s)h(t)h(s+t)\,dsdt
 \leq \left(\int_{\mathbb R}h(u)^{3/2}\,du\right)^2.
 \tag{R3.3}
\]

**Proof.** The left side is \(\int h(u)(h*h)(u)\,du\). Hölder with exponents
\(3/2,3\), followed by Young's \(L^{3/2}*L^{3/2}\to L^3\) inequality, gives
\(\lVert h\rVert_{3/2}^3=(\int h^{3/2})^2\).

Consequently, a uniform bound

\[
 \frac1L\int_{-3L}^{3L}h_M(u)^{3/2}\,du\ll1
\]

implies a uniform \(L^{-2}\) bound for the triangular three-time product on \([-L,L]^2\).
For a one-point theta magnitude satisfying the standard estimate
\(|\Theta|^2\ll\alpha_3\), this asks only for an averaged \(\alpha_3^{3/4}\) bound. The
Kim--Marklof--Welsh escape theorem gives an averaged \(\alpha_3^\beta\) bound for some
\(\beta>1\) on the pair-theorem minor arcs, strictly more than the exponent required by (R3.3).

`[SURVIVOR]` Triple order does **not** require a stronger cusp exponent than the 2026 pair theorem.
Once the exact single-theta carrier and cutoffs are matched, R3.3 closes minor-arc product
uniform integrability. Corpus theorem `HTGeometry.integral_sq_lineConvolution_le` confirms the
same Young-convolution architecture at the \(L^1*L^2\to L^2\) endpoint; the
\(L^{3/2}\) endpoint itself is absent from the corpus.

\[
 \phi:\mathfrak s\oplus\mathfrak s\longrightarrow

\[
 [\mathfrak q,\mathfrak q]
 =[\phi(\mathfrak s,0),\phi(0,\mathfrak s)]=0.

## Paper reduction R12 — prime-field orthogonality of good adjoint characters

Let \(k=\mathbb F_p\) with \(p\) odd, let \(\chi:k\to\{-1,0,1\}\) be the quadratic character, and
let

\[
 q(z)=\rho_1z_1^2+\rho_2z_2^2+\rho_3z_3^2
 \qquad(\rho_1\rho_2\rho_3\neq0).
\]

For three independent vectors \(z_0,z_1,z_2\in k^3\), put

\[
 A=q(z_0),\qquad B=q(z_1),\qquad C=q(z_2)
\]

and, for \(r\in k\setminus\{0,-1\}\),

\[
 L_r(A,B,C)=rA-(r+1)B-r(r+1)C.
 \tag{R12.1}
\]

This is the reduction modulo a degree-one prime of the non-square adjoint factor
\(G_{\lambda,\mu}\), on the chart \(r=\lambda/\mu\).

### R12.1 Exact diagonal and power-saving off diagonal

[BRIDGE] [PAPER-PROVED] For \(r,s\in k\setminus\{0,-1\}\),

\[
\sum_{z_0,z_1,z_2\in k^3}
\chi\!\left(L_r(A,B,C)L_s(A,B,C)\right)
=
\begin{cases}
p^9-p^8,&r=s,\\
O(p^6),&r\neq s,
\end{cases}
\tag{R12.2}
\]

with an absolute implied constant.

**Proof.** A nondegenerate ternary quadratic form has value distribution

\[
 \#\{z\in k^3:q(z)=t\}=p^2+\varepsilon_qp\chi(t)
 \tag{R12.3}
\]

for a sign \(\varepsilon_q\) depending only on \(q\). Push the sum in (R12.2) through
\((z_0,z_1,z_2)\mapsto(A,B,C)\) and expand the three factors in (R12.3). For
\(J\subseteq\{A,B,C\}\), the resulting character sum is

\[
 T_J(r,s)=\sum_{A,B,C\in k}
 \chi\!\left(\prod_{X\in J}X\;L_r(A,B,C)L_s(A,B,C)\right),
 \tag{R12.4}
\]

with coefficient \(p^{6-|J|}\varepsilon_q^{|J|}\).

If \(r\neq s\), then \(L_r,L_s\) are independent, so \(T_\varnothing=0\). For each coordinate
\(X\), the three linear forms \(X,L_r,L_s\) are independent. Their determinants, up to nonzero
signs, are

\[
 (r+1)(s+1)(s-r),\qquad rs(r-s),\qquad s-r,
\]

for \(X=A,B,C\), respectively. Hence \(T_{\{X\}}=0\) after an invertible linear change of
variables. Also \(T_{\{A,B,C\}}=0\): its polynomial is homogeneous of degree five, and summing on
each nonzero projective ray gives \(\sum_{t\in k^\times}\chi(t^5)=\sum_t\chi(t)=0\).

It remains to bound the three two-coordinate terms. For example, fix \(A,B\) and sum
\(T_{\{A,B\}}\) over \(C\). Both \(L_r,L_s\) have nonzero \(C\)-coefficients. The elementary
quadratic-character identity gives a constant \(-\chi(\alpha_r\alpha_s)\), except on the line in
\((A,B)\) where their roots coincide, where the correction is
\(p\chi(\alpha_r\alpha_s)\). The constant term vanishes after summing \(\chi(AB)\); the line
contains \(p\) points, so

\[
 |T_{\{A,B\}}(r,s)|\leq p^2.
\]

The same argument applies to the other two pairs. Their coefficients in (R12.4) are \(p^4\),
giving the \(O(p^6)\) off-diagonal bound.

For \(r=s\), \(L_r(A,B,C)\), viewed in the nine original coordinates, is a nondegenerate
odd-dimensional quadratic form. It has exactly \(p^8\) zeros. Since
\(\chi(L_r^2)\) is one off its zero set and zero on it, the diagonal is \(p^9-p^8\).

## Paper reduction R13 — square-free CRT large sieve for adjoint directions

Let \(Q\) be an odd square-free integer. At every prime \(p\mid Q\), choose a nondegenerate

\]

### R13.1 Composite correlation kernel

[BRIDGE] [PAPER-PROVED] Uniformly for good directions \(r,s\),

\[

 \ll_\varepsilon Q^{6+\varepsilon}\gcd(Q,r-s)^3.
 \tag{R13.2}
\]

If \(r=s\), the sum is exactly

\[
 Q^8\varphi(Q).
 \tag{R13.3}
\]

**Proof.** The Chinese remainder theorem factors the left side prime by prime. At a prime
\(p\mid Q\) where \(r\equiv s\pmod p\), R12.2 gives the exact factor
\(p^9-p^8\), bounded by \(p^9\). At every other prime it gives \(O(p^6)\). Since \(Q\) is
square-free, the product of the collision primes is exactly \(\gcd(Q,r-s)\). Consequently the
product is

\[
 O\!\left(C^{\omega(Q)}Q^6\gcd(Q,r-s)^3\right).
\]

For every \(\varepsilon>0\), \(C^{\omega(Q)}\ll_\varepsilon Q^\varepsilon\): primes below
\(C^{1/\varepsilon}\) contribute an \(\varepsilon\)-dependent constant and every remaining

### R13.2 Optimal complete direction-family second moment

[SURVIVOR] [PAPER-PROVED] For arbitrary complex coefficients \(a_r\) supported on good
directions,

\[

 \ll_\varepsilon Q^{9+\varepsilon}\sum_r|a_r|^2.
 \tag{R13.4}
\]

\[
 \sum_{r,s}|a_r||a_s|\gcd(Q,r-s)^3.
\]

Use the positive divisor majorant

\[
 \gcd(Q,r-s)^3\leq
 \sum_{\substack{d\mid Q\\d\mid r-s}}d^3.
\]

For a fixed \(d\mid Q\), partition directions into residue classes modulo \(d\). Each class in
\(\mathbb Z/Q\mathbb Z\) has \(Q/d\) elements, so Cauchy--Schwarz gives

\[
 \sum_{r\equiv s\ (d)}|a_r||a_s|
 =\sum_{u\bmod d}\left(\sum_{r\equiv u\ (d)}|a_r|\right)^2
 \leq \frac Qd\sum_r|a_r|^2.
\]

Therefore the gcd-kernel is at most

\[
 Q\sum_{d\mid Q}d^2\sum_r|a_r|^2
 \leq \zeta(2)Q^3\sum_r|a_r|^2,
\]

because square-freeness gives
\(\sum_{d\mid Q}d^2=Q^2\prod_{p\mid Q}(1+p^{-2})\leq\zeta(2)Q^2\).
Multiplication by \(Q^{6+\varepsilon}\) proves (R13.4). The internal Lean corpus already

## Paper theorem R47 — averaged cubic-minor content is subcritical

For a tuple \(x=(x_1,\ldots,x_k)\) of distinct nonzero integers, \(k\in\{3,4\}\), put

### R47.1 Two-root classification of every content prime power

`[SURVIVOR] [PAPER-PROVED]` Suppose \(\gcd(x_1,\ldots,x_k)=1\) and \(p^e\mid G(x)\).  Then there
is a unit \(t\bmod p^e\) such that

\[
 x_i\equiv0\ \hbox{or}\ t\pmod {p^e}
 \qquad(1\leq i\leq k).
 \tag{R47.2}
\]

**Proof.**  Some \(x_j\) is a \(p\)-adic unit.  Set \(t=x_j\).  Since
