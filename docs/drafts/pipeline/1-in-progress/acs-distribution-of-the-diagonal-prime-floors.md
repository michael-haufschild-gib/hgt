# Distribution of the diagonal ordinary prime floors

Conjecture: the ordinary floor loss on \(nD_n\), divided by \(n^2\), tends to \((4e^8-e^{-8})/2\) through all conductors. This would determine the limiting discrepancy of the actual ordinary logarithm from the signed Weil degree on the diagonal. The known all-conductor result is a strictly positive lower bound. [Diagonal floor loss](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/CountingHolography/Sections/TheDiagonalOrdinaryFloorLoss.lean)

On each compact subinterval of the moving long-lag range \(e^{-8}<y=p/n^2<4e^8\), the raw row has phase \(nf(y)+O(1)\), with \(f(y)=2[2H(\log y)-H(\log y-\log4)]/\sqrt y\). The upper-lag displacement has a smooth limiting correction on each spline piece. The original mesh costs \(O(1/n)\). Uniform distribution of this phase modulo one against prime logarithmic mass would give the factor \(1/2\). The two support edges and knots occupy arbitrarily small fixed relative intervals. Outside both long-lag supports one needs exact vanishing of the raw mesh.

For \(X=n^2\), a multiplicative average of width \(X^{-2/3}\) changes a Lipschitz periodic function of the phase by \(O(X^{-1/6})\). An unconditional \(L^1\) prime number theorem for these smoothed measures on fixed dilates would transfer the question to Lebesgue measure. The [short-interval zero sum](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/RHTower/PrimeAlmostAllShortIntervals.lean) bounds the measure of a fixed deficit. The proposed stronger estimate requires an arbitrarily small relative deficit in the [multiplicative window](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/RHTower/ChebyshevMultiplicativeWindowZeroSum.lean) and the correct total mass by the ordinary prime number theorem. Lebesgue equidistribution still requires ruling out constant phase on each active spline piece.
