## Founding distinction

A generated challenge can be new in two different senses.

1. **Representational birth:** no current state, row, term, or carrier element represents it.
2. **Operational birth:** adjoining it changes which transformations remain invisible and legal.

Neither implies the other. A theory of law formation that checks only the first can grow syntax
forever without changing its semantics. A theory checking only the second can discover a new legal
distinction already represented in the old carrier.

## Mechanism laws

### Law I -- fibre splitting, not image escape

Operational novelty occurs exactly when a generated reporter splits a fibre of the complete current
reporter. Nonmembership in a chosen representer image is neither necessary nor sufficient.

### Law II -- every effective birth spends semantic rank

On a finite carrier, each operationally effective cumulative reporter strictly increases the number
of observable fibres. Hence open effective genesis must spend carrier size, arity, admission,
lineage, or formation class.

### Law III -- representer growth needs a transport certificate

Every source-normal representer birth must name an old-invisible intervention excluded by the new
reporter, or certify that the birth is currently inert but prospectively active after a specified
future law. Without this certificate it is syntax growth, not law dynamics.

### Law IV -- immediate and prospective effectiveness differ

A currently inert representer may become effective after later carrier or operation growth. Such a
claim requires an explicit future history and survival theorem. “It may matter later” is not a
certificate.

### Law V -- feedback requires a closed causal cycle

A reflexive formation step must prove all arrows

```text
failure -> generated challenge -> representer -> fibre contraction
        -> changed legal execution -> next generated failure.
```

ULF-REE-1 closes the middle classification only. The final arrow back to a source-generated next
failure remains open.
