## Thesis

The universal law object may be noncommutative or otherwise nonclassical. Individual proof systems
are commutative contexts in which classical reasoning is valid. A “common source relation” may fail
not because it has not been found, but because no global deterministic valuation compatible with
all contexts exists.

The primitive candidate is:

\[
 (\mathcal A_{\mathrm{law}},
 \mathsf C(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{law}}),
 \Sigma_{\mathrm{law}},
 \omega_{\mathrm{src}},
 \Phi_t),
\]

where:

- \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{law}}\) is a noncommutative algebra, operator system, or higher algebra of
  law observables;
- \(\mathsf C(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{law}})\) is its observer-context category;
- \(\Sigma_{\mathrm{law}}\) is the spectral/event presheaf;
- \(\omega_{\mathrm{src}}\) is a source-selected positive state;
- \(\Phi_t\) is an admitted automorphism or modular history.

## First exact survivor

The connected-parity theorem constructs infinitely many finite context systems with:

1. classical local contexts;
2. deterministic completions on every proper face;
3. no deterministic completion on the full cover;
4. a compatible positive empirical state.

This proves that “all proper proof faces agree” need not imply one global classical law.

## What this changes

In NHG, observer loss is carried by a homotopy fibre. In nomological contextuality, the primary
failure may instead be the nonexistence of a global classical point. There need be no hidden point
whose coordinates observers forgot.

The two pictures are not equivalent:

- fibre geometry begins with a global law object and studies its projections;
- contextual geometry may have only a global state/presheaf with no global valuation.

This is the first genuine ontology fork.

## Fatal falsifier

contextual ontology is unnecessary. If the context cover is chosen after seeing the desired
obstruction, it is theatre.
