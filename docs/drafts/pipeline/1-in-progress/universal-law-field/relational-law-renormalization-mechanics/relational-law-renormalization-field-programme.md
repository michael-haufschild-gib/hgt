## 1. Founding pivot

Strict descent asks whether an observer quotient still carries a deterministic law.  When the
answer is no, existing universal-law mechanics either enlarge the observer kernel, sacrifice the
law, retain an obstruction, or change admission.  There is a prior option that requires no repair
selector:

> retain every observed source transition and allow the effective law to be multivalued.

Let `q:X->B` be an observer and `f:X->X` a source law.  The effective relation is

\[
 b\;\mathcal R_q(f)\;c
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists x\in X,\ q(x)=b\text{ and }q(fx)=c.
 \tag{RLR.1}
\]

This is the direct image of the graph of `f`.  It is canonical before any determinization, cost,
positive averaging, or route endpoint is chosen.

## 2. Proposed mechanism laws

### Law I — relational survival

Every total deterministic source law has a total effective relation on every surjective observer.
Strict descent is not an existence question; it is the special chamber in which the effective
relation is single-valued.

### Law II — determinism equals executable descent

The relation `R_q(f)` is right-unique exactly when `f` preserves the kernel equivalence of `q`.
Equivalently, exactly then there is a unique effective function `f_bar:B->B` with

\[
 qf=\bar f q.
 \tag{RLR.2}
\]

Thus the existing executable-descent theorem is the deterministic locus of a larger relational
mechanism.

### Law III — lax execution and splice production

For source laws `f,g`,

\[
 \mathcal R_q(gf)
 \subseteq
 \mathcal R_q(f);\mathcal R_q(g).
 \tag{RLR.3}
\]

The right side permits two different source witnesses whose intermediate states have the same
observation.  Its excess over the left side is the **splice defect**.  It consists of effective
two-step histories that no single source trajectory realizes.

If the later law `g` descends through `q`, equality holds for every `f`.  Conversely, on a nonempty
source, equality after every constant prelaw forces `g` to descend.  Exact effective composition
is therefore equivalent to zero splice response under a separating family of primitive probes.

### Law IV — strict observer scaling

For another observer `p:B->C`, direct observation and relational pushforward agree exactly:

\[
 \mathcal R_{pq}(f)=p_*\mathcal R_q(f).
 \tag{RLR.4}
\]

Observer scale is strict.  Execution is lax.  Their asymmetry is constitutive rather than a metric
choice.

### Law V — no deterministic selector repairs branching

If one observed input has two distinct effective outputs, every proposed deterministic effective
law disagrees with at least one source representative.  A choice function may select a branch, but
it cannot make `(RLR.2)` commute.  Determinization therefore requires source structure, averaging,
admission, or deliberate distinction sacrifice; choice alone is fitted repair.

### Law VI — branching is doctrine-relative

Further observation can either create branching by merging source inputs with different future
outputs, or erase branching by merging the outputs.  No scalar branch count is universally
monotone under coarse-graining.  The invariant datum is the relation and its lax-composition cells.

### Law VII — execution--observation interchange has two independent defects

For serial observers `X -q-> B -p-> C` and source laws `f,g`, there is a canonical filtration

\[
 \mathcal R_{pq}(gf)
 \subseteq p_*(\mathcal R_q(f);\mathcal R_q(g))
 \subseteq (p_*\mathcal R_q(f));(p_*\mathcal R_q(g)).
 \tag{RLR.6}
\]

The first gap is execution splice surviving further observation.  The second is a new midpoint
splice created by `p`.  They are independent.  The first vanishes when `g` preserves the
`q`-kernel; the second vanishes for all relations exactly when `p` is injective.  Consequently no
single scalar “curvature” can replace the typed defect filtration.

## 3. Why this is a higher mechanism

The state variable is not an RH carrier.  It is the effective law type produced by observation:

\[
 \text{function}
 \longrightarrow
 \text{relation}
 \longrightarrow
 \begin{cases}
   \text{strict descended law},\\
   \text{selected branch},\\
   \text{convex/probabilistic law},\\
   \text{topological closed relation},\\
   \text{admission-sensitive partial law}.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{RLR.5}

Those later arrows are promotion modes to classify, not independent coordinates.  Relational law
renormalization supplies the common pre-promotion mechanism and the exact false-history residue.

## 6. Hostile conjecture ledger

### Loop A — quotienting automatically produces a deterministic effective law

**False.** Kernel preservation is necessary and sufficient.

**Survivor:** every quotient produces a canonical total relation.

### Loop B — relational composition equals observed source composition

**False.** The four-state witness has strict inclusion in `(RLR.3)`.

**Survivor:** equality holds when the later law descends, and equality against all constant prelaws
characterizes descent.

### Loop C — more coarse-graining only increases nondeterminism

**False.** A constant postobserver erases every branch, while a non-invariant quotient can turn a
fine deterministic graph into a multivalued relation.

**Survivor:** relation pushforward is exact; branch cardinality is not a law invariant.

### Loop D — choose one branch to recover effective dynamics

**False.** Every deterministic choice misses a source transition at a branched input.

**Survivor:** a selector is an additional promotion datum whose source provenance must be tested.

### Loop E — relational law renormalization is already the universal field

**Open and currently unsupported.** It does not by itself produce positivity, topology,
hypertranscendence, recurrence, physical time, or source-normal selection.

**Survivor:** it is a route-independent transition law within Reflexive Law Mechanics and a sharp
anti-theatre gate for quotient-based effective-law claims.

## 8. Acceptance and death gates

Promote RLR beyond a Stage-1 mechanism programme only if:

1. the topological or convex extension has a nontrivial converse;
2. splice transport obeys a coherent higher composition law;
3. one source-generated relation predicts a later promotion response;
4. the prediction survives accepted presentation equivalence;
5. the significant theorem is Lean-certified under the universal-law umbrella.

Retire it if every later theorem is ordinary transition-system abstraction with no coupling to
promotion, admission, or source conservation.
