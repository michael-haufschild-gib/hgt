## 2. Six laws

### Law I — intervention genesis

A source intervention produces a tangent or finite difference before any target theorem is read.
Passive endpoint data are not a substitute.

### Law II — least test closure

Start with source-native reporters and close only under source-native operations: composition,
polarization, tensoring, Fourier character action, prolongation, or completion.  A route is one
subcalculus.  An arbitrary product of route outputs is forbidden.

### Law III — reconstruction

A test calculus is value-complete on a generated sector precisely when its joint evaluation map is
injective there.  This is tomography, Fourier inversion, Prony/Hankel reconstruction, and
coefficient recovery in one law.

### Law IV — admission is order reconstruction

Value reconstruction is insufficient.  A test calculus is admission-complete only when evaluation
reflects the relevant cone or order.  In commutative atomic sectors, scalar character or polynomial
tests can already be completely order-reflecting.  In coupled noncommutative sectors, matrix
amplification is an independent test-closure direction.

### Law V — gauge conservation under complete changes of test basis

If two source-generated test calculi are invertible changes of basis on the same complete response
space, every sharp compensation gauge and every inertia count agrees.  Their formulas may look
unrelated, but no obstruction can disappear.

### Law VI — cofinal selection is uniform complete admission

Finite reconstruction at every aperture does not imply one global law.  The exact final demand is
uniform order reflection on the product-directed set

\[
  (\text{source aperture})\times(\text{test depth})\times
  (\text{amplification depth})\times(\text{prolongation depth}).
\tag{CTC.2}
\]

Failure can occur by migrating witnesses, nonclosed admission, nonproper history, or an infinitary
formation not generated at any finite stage.
