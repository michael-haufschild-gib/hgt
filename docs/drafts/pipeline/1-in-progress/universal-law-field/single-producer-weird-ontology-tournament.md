## 1. Killing result that forces this tournament

`independent-slot-transitivity-and-cross-organ-nonprediction-law.md` proves that a product of
independently relabelable organ spaces has no nonconstant intrinsic invariant and no proper nonempty
intrinsic phase. Therefore any field primitive of the form

\[
(\text{relations},\text{positivity},\text{topology},\text{history},\text{cost},\ldots)
\]

is theatre unless one operation generates and couples those shadows. This applies to CEM as strongly
as to every geometric predecessor. The tuple may remain bookkeeping, but it cannot be the founding
mathematics.

## 2. Tournament rule

Each candidate below must produce, from one primitive:

1. an object or theorem endpoint;
2. a notion of admissible proof/history;
3. a positive or probabilistic shadow;
4. a lost phase/orientation/memory;
5. recurrent observation with potentially open lifted history;
6. an infinite-rank or noncompact regime capable of housing hypertranscendence;
7. a route-independent identity coupling at least three of these.

No candidate wins by analogy. First required deliverable is a finite native model where one formula,
not a tuple of declarations, forces at least four items.

## 3. Candidate L — complex action principle for mathematics

### Primitive

A complex action functional \(\mathcal S\) on a derived configuration object, together with its
allowed integration cycles. Laws are critical loci; proofs are thimbles or trajectories; theorem
values are amplitudes.

### Forced shadows

- relations: Euler--Lagrange/derived critical equations;
- positivity and inertia: real part and Hessian of \(\mathcal S\);
- phase/orientation: imaginary part and determinant line;
- proof cost: action of a trajectory or filling;
- no-go: absent thimble, singular critical locus, or Stokes jump;
- recurrence/open history: periodic amplitude with nontrivial determinant/metaplectic lift;
- hypertranscendence: non-Noetherian critical jet or infinitely many essential couplings.

### First exact test

Use the one-dimensional Gaussian family

\[
\mathcal S_\theta(x)=\tfrac12e^{i\theta}x^2.
\]

The critical locus is fixed, Hessian modulus is fixed, steepest-descent polarization rotates, and a
\(2\pi\)-loop returns the action while the square-root determinant acquires metaplectic sign. If one
formula cannot be made to couple exactness, positivity, Stokes chambers, and lifted history without
adding them separately, this candidate dies.

### Fatal collision

BV/AKSZ, Picard--Lefschetz theory, and derived critical geometry already provide the container. The
field survives only if a route-independent theorem about mathematical proof production follows from
the common action and is not ordinary stationary-phase theory with renamed nouns.

## 4. Candidate M — proof partition functions

### Primitive

For a theorem boundary \(b\), form a generating object over admissible proofs \(\pi\):

\[
Z_b(\beta,\vartheta)=\sum_{\pi:\,\partial\pi=b}
e^{-\beta A(\pi)}e^{i\vartheta M(\pi)}.
\tag{2}
\]

Here \(A\) is source-defined proof action and \(M\) is stable memory, not fitted complexity.

### Forced shadows

- theorem existence: support/nonvanishing of \(Z_b\);
- proof multiplicity and complexity: free energy and low-temperature asymptotics;
- positivity: real Gibbs specialization;
- phase loss: modulus or probabilistic normalization;
- recurrence/open history: periodic \(\vartheta\)-response with unbounded memory lift;
- no-go: destructive interference or divergent normalization;
- hypertranscendence: non-D-finite/non-differentially-algebraic partition function generated by proof species.

### Fatal test

If \(A\) and \(M\) are presentation-dependent, (2) is not mathematics of laws but statistics of a
chosen proof language. A winner needs invariance under an explicitly chosen stable change of
presentation while retaining nontrivial cost.

## 5. Candidate N — endogenous logic and self-modifying mathematics

### Primitive

A doctrine transformer \(\Phi\) whose state determines objects, admissible morphisms, equivalences,
and observers of the next state. A mathematical world is a fixed point, cycle, or nonterminating orbit
of \(\Phi\), not a category chosen in advance.

### Forced shadows

- routes: stable local phases or cycles of the doctrine transformer;
- no-gos: obstruction to extending a fixed point after a law mutation;
- positivity/completion: idempotent reflectors produced by the current doctrine;
- recurrence: periodic doctrine with nonperiodic witness/proof history;
- hypertranscendence: failure of every finite-order doctrine state to close the prolongation orbit;
- source selection: selection of a fixed point rather than a scalar inside one world.

### First exact test

Construct a finite transformer on equivalence relations whose observer state has period two while its
marked refinement strictly increases. Then seek a nonfinite version where every finite observer
cycles but no finite doctrine determines the source world.

### Fatal collision

Modal type theory, institutions, realizability, and dynamic epistemic logic already change ambient
logic. This candidate dies unless the endogenous fixed-point equation forces a new cross-organ law.

## 6. Candidate O — experimental Galois theory

### Primitive

Given a family of experiments \(E\), replace the automorphism group of an object by the groupoid of
transformations invisible to \(E\). Varying experiments produce a filtration of hidden-symmetry
groupoids and a Galois correspondence between experiments and indistinguishability doctrines.

### Forced shadows

- positivity: quotient by phase/isometry subgroup;
- intervention: adjoining an experiment shrinks the hidden group;
- no-go: nontrivial residual stabilizer;
- recurrence: closed orbit in a quotient with deck motion upstairs;
- hypertranscendence: no finite experiment family has trivial residual groupoid;
- route comparison: two routes are gauges only relative to the same experiment field.

### Fatal test

Classical Galois categories, Tannaka reconstruction, model-theoretic automorphism groups, and
identifiability theory occupy most of this architecture. It survives only if doctrine-changing
experiments obey a new Galois law that also controls cost or completion.

## 7. Candidate P — theorem amplitudes and positive canonical forms

### Primitive

A positive or oriented combinatorial object whose canonical differential form is the theorem.
Proof routes are triangulations or cluster charts; spurious singularities are chart artifacts that
cancel in the global form.

### Forced shadows

- many proofs: distinct triangulations of one canonical form;
- no-gos: forbidden facets or uncancelled spurious poles;
- positivity: primitive orientation/positive region;
- phase: lift from positive canonical form to oriented/complex cluster data;
- recurrence: cluster modular periodicity with open tropical \(g\)-vector lift;
- hypertranscendence: infinite-rank cluster/positive geometry or nonterminating mutation fan.

### Fatal test

Amplituhedra, cluster varieties, and canonical forms already realize these mechanisms. Candidate dies
unless theorem composition canonically produces gluing/residue identities across unrelated proof
families, rather than retrofitting each family with a polytope.

## 8. Candidate Q — non-objectal parallax mathematics

### Primitive

There is no universal hidden object. Primitive data are transition laws among mutually incompatible
shadows. A “thing” is the coherent parallax pattern obtained by moving among observation doctrines;
existence of a common lift is a special phase, not an axiom.

### Forced shadows

- routes: shadows, not charts of a presumed object;
- no-gos: impossibility of a common realization;
- positivity: one irreversible shadow transition;
- recurrence: a closed cycle of shadows with nontrivial transition memory;
- hypertranscendence: transition system has no finite effective completion;
- physical reality: reality may be consistency of transition laws rather than an underlying state.

### First exact test

Construct three pairwise realizable shadow transitions with no global object, while their cyclic
transition composite carries a stable memory invariant. Ordinary descent gives the first half; the
required new content is a memory invariant defined without postulating a total object.

### Fatal collision

Contextuality, gerbes, descent obstruction, and relational quantum mechanics are close parents. The
candidate survives only if transition memory predicts more than the ordinary obstruction class.

## 9. Candidate R — proof ecology and evolutionary law selection

### Primitive

Proof systems are interacting species in a resource category. Composition, competition, and
cooperation alter which laws remain viable. A theorem is an evolutionarily stable invariant of the
proof ecology, not merely an endpoint.

### Forced shadows

- positivity: viable resource cone;
- no-go: extinction or invasion barrier;
- recurrence: ecological cycle;
- open history: cumulative turnover despite recurrent population state;
- hypertranscendence: unbounded niche/prolongation creation;
- source selection: stability under all admissible invaders.

### Fatal test

Without a canonical interaction law and source-derived resource, this is evolutionary metaphor.
It must yield a minimax or fixed-point theorem coupling proof complexity, observer loss, and theorem
stability in a finite native model.

## 10. Candidate S — complex-weighted history groupoid

### Primitive

A history groupoid with one additive complex weight. Its chain complex, convolution algebra,
characters, time evolution, positive representations, and zeta function would be different shadows
of one composition law.

### Exact killing theorem

`additive-positive-groupoid-cost-triviality-and-directed-history-necessity-law.md` proves that every
nonnegative additive cost on a groupoid vanishes: \(A(g)+A(g^{-1})=A(1)=0\). Therefore one additive
complex cocycle cannot carry both reversible phase and nonnegative proof cost.

### Stronger survivor

Replace the groupoid by a polarized directed-history object: reversible core for phase/holonomy,
noninvertible or lax composition for cost/dissipation. Unlike a free tuple, this split is forced by
the incompatibility theorem. The open requirement is one exchange law coupling the two regimes.

### Fatal collision

Groupoid convolution/KMS systems, causal categories, Lawvere metrics, double categories, and
noncommutative geometry already supply containers. This candidate survives only if the forced
reversible/irreversible exchange yields a new invariant rather than repackaging thermodynamics.
