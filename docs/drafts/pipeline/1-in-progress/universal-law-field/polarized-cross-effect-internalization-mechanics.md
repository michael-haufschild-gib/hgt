## 2. Raw Boolean cross-effects

Let `I` be finite, let `A` be an abelian group, and let

\[
 f:\mathcal P(I)\longrightarrow A.
\]

Define its cross-effect packet by Boolean Möbius inversion:

\[
 (\mathsf Mf)(S)=
 \sum_{T\subseteq S}(-1)^{|S\setminus T|}f(T).
 \tag{PCI.1}
\]

For a packet `c:P(I)->A`, define recovery by

\[
 (\mathsf Zc)(S)=\sum_{T\subseteq S}c(T).
 \tag{PCI.2}
\]

### Theorem 2.1 -- complete intervention reconstruction

\[
 \boxed{\mathsf M\mathsf Z=1,\qquad \mathsf Z\mathsf M=1.}
 \tag{PCI.3}
\]

#### Proof

The coefficient of `c(U)` in `(MZc)(S)` is zero unless `U=S`. Indeed it is

\[
 \sum_{U\subseteq T\subseteq S}(-1)^{|S\setminus T|}
 =(1-1)^{|S\setminus U|}.
\]

The same Boolean interval calculation proves `ZM=1`. ∎

Thus interaction order is generated by the source intervention cube. It is not an observer label.

## 3. Complement duality is triangular before polarization

Let raw complement act by

\[

 \tag{PCI.4}

\[
 (\mathsf Kc)(S)=(-1)^{|S|}\sum_{U\supseteq S}c(U).

### Theorem 3.1 -- complement/cross-effect conjugacy

\[
 \boxed{\mathsf M\mathsf C\mathsf Z=\mathsf K,\qquad \mathsf K^2=1.}
 \tag{PCI.6}

#### Proof

Expanding `f=Zc`, the coefficient of `c(U)` in `(MCZc)(S)` is

\[
 \sum_{T\subseteq S,\,U\subseteq I\setminus T}

It vanishes unless `S subseteq U`; in that case it equals `(-1)^|S|`, proving `(PCI.5)`.
The involution law follows from `(PCI.3)`, `C^2=1`, and conjugacy. ∎

### Corollary 3.2 -- bounded interaction degree is not raw-duality complete

Assume `A` is nonzero. Let `P_{\le d}` restrict a packet to subsets of cardinality at most `d`.
For every `d<|I|`, there is no map `K_d` on degree-at-most-`d` packets such that

\[
 P_{\le d}\mathsf K=K_dP_{\le d}
 \tag{PCI.6a}
\]

for every packet.

#### Proof

Choose `U subseteq I` with `|U|=d+1` and let `c` be supported at `U` with nonzero value. Then
`P_{\le d}c=0`. But `(Kc)(S)=(-1)^|S|c(U)` for every `S subseteq U`; in particular
`(Kc)(emptyset)=c(U) ne 0`. Hence `P_{\le d}Kc ne 0`, contradicting `(PCI.6a)`. ∎

This is a route-independent no-go. Complete duality can inject an omitted high-arity interaction
into every lower raw degree. A finite raw reporter cannot be functional-equation closed merely by

## 4. Source polarization diagonalizes duality

\[

 \tag{PCI.7}
\]

\[
 \widehat f_q(S)=\mathbb E_q[f\psi_S^q].
 \tag{PCI.8}

Let `bar q_i=1-q_i`, let `R omega=1-omega`, and put `f^R=f o R`.

### Theorem 4.1 -- polarized complement diagonalization

1. `(psi_S^q)_S` is an orthonormal basis of `L^2(P_q)`.
2. `R_*P_q=P_barq`.
3. For every `S subseteq I`,

   \[
   \boxed{\widehat{f^R}_{\bar q}(S)=(-1)^{|S|}\widehat f_q(S).}
   \tag{PCI.9}
   \]

4. Consequently complement is a degree-preserving unitary

   \[
   U_R:L^2(P_q)\longrightarrow L^2(P_{\bar q}),
   \qquad U_Rf=f\circ R,

   \[
   \boxed{
   \sum_{|S|\in D}|\widehat{f^R}_{\bar q}(S)|^2

   \tag{PCI.11}
   \]

#### Proof

Independence gives orthonormality; cardinality `2^|I|` gives completeness. Complement sends each
Bernoulli coordinate of parameter `q_i` to one of parameter `1-q_i`, proving item 2. Moreover

\[

Multiplication over `S` and change of variables prove `(PCI.9)`. Parseval proves unitarity and

## 6. Internalization defect and global properness

### Theorem 6.1 -- fixed-fibre classification

For nonempty `I`, the following are equivalent:

1. `R_*P_q=P_q`;
2. complement is a unitary endomorphism of the single polarized law `L^2(P_q)` by composition;
3. `q_i=1/2` for every `i`.

#### Proof

Items 1 and 2 are the measure-preservation criterion for a composition unitary. Equality of product
laws implies equality of every one-coordinate marginal, so `q_i=1-q_i`. The converse is immediate.
∎

This motivates the distinction:

```text

internalized duality:   P_q  --R--> P_q           only on fixed polarization.
```

 \mathcal W_I(a)=D(P_{q(a)}\Vert P_{q(-a)}).

### Theorem 6.2 -- exact proper internalization work

\[
 \boxed{

 2a\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_i\tanh(\lambda_i a).}
 \tag{PCI.18}

#### Proof

Relative entropy of finite product laws is the sum of coordinate relative entropies. For one
coordinate,

\[
 D(\operatorname{Ber}(q)\Vert\operatorname{Ber}(1-q))
 =(2q-1)\log{q\over1-q}.

Under `(PCI.16)`, `2q_i(a)-1=-tanh(lambda_i a)` and

\[

so the finite sum is proper. ∎
