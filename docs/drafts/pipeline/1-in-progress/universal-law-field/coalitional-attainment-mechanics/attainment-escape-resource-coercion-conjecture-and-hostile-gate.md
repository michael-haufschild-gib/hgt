# Coalitional attainment mechanics: law birth, escape, and resource coercion

- **ID:** ULF-CAM-1
- **Stage:** 1-in-progress
- **Type:** CONJECTURE / FIELD-MECHANISM PROGRAMME
- **Frozen conjecture:** A positive law is born not when finite coalitions make a residual small,
  but when a source-produced proper resource prevents the witnessing coalition from escaping.
- **Status:** [CONJECTURE FROZEN] [MECHANISM NOT OBJECT] [ROUTE-INDEPENDENT]
  [HOSTILE AUDIT OPEN] [NOT RH]

## 1. Ontological reversal

Current universal-law cards often begin with a supplied kernel, cokernel, cone, topology, or
observer and classify what that receiver can see.  This programme reverses the question.

The primitive event is a **coalition of rules**.  A coalition may nearly cancel its collective
defect while continually replacing its members.  Small observed residual then does not mean that a
law exists.  It may mean that every witness has fled beyond every finite aperture.  A source
resource is constitutive only when its bounded sublevels prevent that flight.

The proposed field is therefore a mechanics of five operations:

1. **formation:** assemble finite positive coalitions;
2. **residual:** measure their collective failure;
3. **pricing:** assign source history/resource to coalition support;
4. **effectivization:** turn bounded-resource approximate coalitions into an actual carrier;
5. **escape:** record a closure-only law as infinite resource history rather than an existing law.

No manifold, connection, curvature tensor, or route label is primitive.

## 2. Frozen mathematical chart

Let \(V\) be a Banach space and let \(a_i\in V\), \(i\in\mathbb N\), be a bounded family of
rule defects.  A finite coalition is a finitely supported probability vector
\(\mu=(\mu_i)\).  Its residual is

\[
  R(\mu)=\left\|\sum_i\mu_i a_i\right\|.
\tag{CAM.1}
\]

An actual law carrier is a probability vector \(p\in\ell^1(\mathbb N)\) with

\[
  \sum_i p_i a_i=0.
\tag{CAM.2}
\]

An approximate law history is a sequence of finite coalitions \(\mu^{(n)}\) for which
\(R(\mu^{(n)})\to0\).  It is **tight** when, for every \(\varepsilon>0\), one finite aperture
contains all but \(\varepsilon\) of every coalition.

A source price \(W:\mathbb N\to[0,\infty)\) is **proper** when \(W_i\to\infty\).  Its coalition
cost is

\[
  \mathcal A_W(\mu)=\sum_i\mu_iW_i.
\tag{CAM.3}
\]

### Conjecture A -- coalitional attainment

The following are equivalent:

1. an actual law carrier exists;
2. a tight approximate law history exists;
3. an approximate law history exists whose cost is uniformly bounded for some proper source price.

### Conjecture B -- universal escape

If approximate law histories exist but no actual carrier exists, then for every proper price
\(W\) and every approximate history,

\[
  \mathcal A_W(\mu^{(n)})\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{CAM.4}
\]

Thus a closure-only law has open lifted history in every proper source gauge.  This is much stronger
than divergence in one fitted norm.

### Conjecture C -- reciprocal separator pricing

Suppose a continuous functional \(\varphi\in V^*\) satisfies
\(s_i:=\varphi(a_i)>0\), and a proper or nonproper source price satisfies
\(s_iW_i\geq\kappa>0\).  Then every finite coalition obeys

\[
  R(\mu)\,\mathcal A_W(\mu)
  \geq \frac{\kappa}{\|\varphi\|}.
\tag{CAM.5}
\]

This would turn qualitative escape into a sharp uncertainty law: residual suppression consumes
reciprocal history.

## 4. Compression target

If true, one mechanism explains at least these corpus phenomena.

| Corpus organ | CAM reading |
|---|---|
| singular source descent | closure-visible residual zero with non-tight witnesses |
| whole-space coercivity | a proper price whose bounded sublevels force effectivity |

## 5. Hostile conjecture tournament

### Loop 1 -- convex closure alone is the field

**Killed.**  It is already corpus machinery and gives no rate, cost, or source effectivity.

**Survivor:** closure becomes constitutive only after a source price decides whether approximate
witnesses can remain in bounded sublevels.

### Loop 2 -- use one infinite oriented matroid

**Killed as a universal mechanism.**  Bowler--Hochstaettler--Kaspar show that naive infinite
circuit axioms lose duality/strong elimination, while orthogonality and Farkas axiom systems give
different classes ([arXiv:2603.15843](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15843)).  Bowler--Joo obtain an
infinite Farkas theorem only in a finitary binary oriented-matroid regime, with binaryity essential
([arXiv:2406.12811](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12811)).

**Survivor:** admission/tightness cannot be erased by a purely combinatorial infinite-circuit
object.

### Loop 3 -- autocatalytic cores generate every positive law

**Killed as universal.**  Explicit-catalysis networks can be inconclusive at the stoichiometric
matrix level; current classifications need additional graph/algebra data.  Unit-stoichiometry cores
are special superpositions of at most two circuits, not a universal cofinal mechanism
([arXiv:2603.02770](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02770)).

**Survivor:** autocatalysis is a finite formation chart.  CAM asks whether its approximate cores
remain tight under source resources.

### Loop 4 -- small residual plus bounded rule defects already gives a law

**Countermodel target:** \(V=\mathbb R\), \(a_i=1/(i+1)\),
\(\mu^{(n)}=\delta_n\).  Residual tends to zero, but every coefficient is positive, so no probability
carrier has zero barycentre.  Any theorem omitting tightness/resource coercion is false.

### Loop 5 -- proper cost divergence depends on choosing the right cost

**Bold prediction:** false.  If Conjecture A holds, absence of a carrier forces divergence for
*every* proper price.  Hostile proof must rule out alternating low-cost subsequences and show that
any such subsequence would itself effectivize.

### Loop 6 -- unbounded rule families cause no trouble

**Expected failure.**  Tightness of coefficients need not control barycentres for unbounded
\(a_i\).  Either bounded rules or a uniform-integrability domination by the source price is
load-bearing.  The first theorem will keep boundedness explicit.

## 6. External collision and exact intended residue

Ingredients are classical: Prokhorov compactness, concentration compactness, Markov's inequality,
Farkas alternatives, and Cauchy--Schwarz.  Recent Farkas work treats nonclosed image cones and
distinguishes membership from closure membership
([arXiv:2603.11859](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11859)).  This programme makes no priority claim for
those ingredients.

Intended field residue is their coupled interpretation as a law-production mechanism:

\[
  \boxed{
  \text{formation} + \text{residual decay} + \text{source properness}
  \Longrightarrow \text{actual law};
  \qquad
  \text{no actual law} \Longrightarrow \text{universal resource escape}.}
\tag{CAM.6}
\]

## 7. Risky consequences

1. A completion-born positive carrier with uniformly bounded proper source cost must already be an
   admitted carrier; otherwise one of the claimed hypotheses is false.
2. A genuine law mirage cannot have a bounded-cost recurrent lift in any proper source gauge.
3. Route-specific closure arguments that do not exhibit tightness cannot establish positive law
   existence.
4. A source-derived reciprocal separator price supplies a quantitative lower bound on the action
   of every approximate proof history.
