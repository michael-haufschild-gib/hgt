## 1. Founding question

The corpus repeatedly reaches the same wall: a functional equation preserves a carrier, a common
recovery lift preserves information, a positive Gram records response, and a properness theorem
would select the neutral point -- but no source law produces the final zero.

The wrong assumption was that functional-equation-fixed laws should form a subgroup under the same
formation product as unconstrained laws. In a noncommutative generated response group, reversal is
anti-multiplicative. Its fixed set is not a group. It is a reflection space.

The programme asks for a mechanism, not a new object:

\[
 \boxed{
 \text{generate complete history}
 \to \text{reverse formation order}
 \to \text{identify native fixed fusion}
 \to \text{measure ordinary-fusion escape}
 \to \text{combine independent reversals}
 \to \text{prove proper selection}.}
 \tag{CRSM.1}
\]

## 2. Primitive laws

### Law I -- source response is ordered and complete

A legal intervention alphabet generates a complete word or iterated-integral signature. Scalar
moments, determinants, endpoint values, and positive forms are consumers of that signature. They
are not permitted to define it retrospectively.

### Law II -- a genuine functional equation reverses formation

On a complete Hopf response, reversal is the antipode. A sheet, prime, gamma, Fourier, or polarity
involution may additionally relabel primitive directions. The total action is therefore a twisted
antipode

\[
 \rho=\theta\circ S,
 \tag{CRSM.2}
\]

not merely a letter permutation and not merely an isometry of a final observer.

### Law III -- fixed histories have reflection fusion

The fixed locus

\[
 Q_\theta=\{g:\theta(g)=g^{-1}\}
\]

is closed under

\[
 x\triangleright y=xy^{-1}x.
 \tag{CRSM.3}
\]

It is generally not closed under ordinary formation \(xy\). Treating it as a subgroup destroys the
first noncommutative law.

### Law IV -- ordinary fusion creates an even exchange current

For fixed histories \(x,y\), functional reversal sends \(xy\) to \(yx\). The exchange defect is
the commutator packet

\[
 (xy)^{-1}yx.
 \tag{CRSM.4}
\]

In logarithmic response coordinates the first escaped term is \([X,Y]/2\), living in the
reflection-even primitive sector. This is the first candidate current generated by the field
rather than supplied by a route.

### Law V -- functional equation reconstructs even grades

If \(g=1+\sum g_n\) is fixed, then

\[
 g_n^+=-\frac12\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}g_i\theta(g_{n-i}).
 \tag{CRSM.5}
\]

Every even signature grade is forced by lower history. Free data live in the anti-invariant
primitive logarithm, not in arbitrary fixed coefficients.

### Law VI -- one reversal grades; several reversals may select

One reversal leaves its full odd primitive sector. A finite source-marked family
\((\Theta_j)\) generates

\[
 \mathcal A X=((I+\Theta_j)X)_j,
 \qquad
 \mathcal Q=\mathcal A^*\mathcal A.
 \tag{CRSM.6}
\]

The exact neutral-selection kernel is the common all-odd sector

\[
 \ker\mathcal Q=\bigcap_j\ker(I+\Theta_j).
 \tag{CRSM.7}
\]

Stable selection requires \(\mathcal Q\) to be bounded below. In infinite dimension injectivity is
not enough.

### Law VII -- common recovery conserves the obstruction

Split packet formation through a common sufficient kernel preserves KL and Jeffreys divergence
exactly. It carries reflection action and generated exchange into a hidden fibre without shrinking
them. Recovery is a transport law, not a selector.

### Law VIII -- transvections convert nonneutrality into open history

Composing the reflection at \(x\) with reflection at the identity generates

\[
 e\mapsto x^2\mapsto x^4\mapsto\cdots.
 \tag{CRSM.8}
\]

Any source-derived proper logarithmic length diverges unless \(x=e\). Thus bounded full
transvection history is a valid selection premise. Producing that bound remains arithmetic work.
