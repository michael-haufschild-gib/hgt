## 1. Why this tournament exists

The phrase “GR-level field” specifies explanatory reach, not curvature vocabulary. A successful
field must explain why many mathematically different proof systems produce recurrent families of
constraints, observers, positive shadows, completions, histories, and endpoint equivalences. It
need not be Riemannian, local, commutative, or even geometric in the classical sense.

Nomological Homotopy Geometry remains a serious candidate local chart. It is no longer presumed to
be the universal ontology.

## 2. Anti-theatre gate

Every fork must provide:

1. a primitive definable without inspecting any target proof;
2. at least two native, nonisomorphic model families;
3. one coordinate-free equivalence notion;
4. one theorem coupling at least three requested phenomena;
5. one countermodel that would kill the fork rather than merely narrow it;
6. one prediction not already built into the definitions;
7. a reason the recurring source-selection obstruction changes form rather than receiving a new
   name.

A framework that merely stores exactness, positivity, topology, and history in separate slots
fails.

## 3. Fork A — nomological contextuality

### Primitive

A noncommutative algebra or higher operator system \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{law}}\) of law observables,
its poset of commutative contexts \(\mathsf C(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{law}})\), the spectral/context
presheaf, and a source-selected positive state.

Proof systems are classical contexts, not coordinate charts on one classical space. Their local
truth assignments need not glue to a global valuation.

### Reinterpretation

- many proof routes: incompatible maximal contexts;
- no-go theorems: contextuality or failure of global section;
- positivity: state positivity before context restriction;
- phase loss: passage from amplitudes to contextual probabilities;
- recurrence: automorphism flow seen in recurrent commutative quotients;
- open lifted history: nontrivial unitary or modular history in the full algebra;
- source obstruction: not a missing scalar relation but impossibility of one global classical
  calibration compatible with every context.

### Native theorem target

Construct a law-context presheaf whose local sections all exist, while a finite contextuality
cocycle forbids a global section. Prove that adding any one context cannot repair the obstruction,
but a noncommutative state exists globally.

### Fatal test

If all intended proof observers embed into one commutative algebra without losing their decisive
data, contextuality is unnecessary and this fork collapses.

## 4. Fork B — operadic law genesis and Koszul duality

### Primitive

A colored dg operad \(\mathcal O\) of admissible law-forming operations, its Koszul dual cooperad,
and the bar--cobar law-generation process. A sourced law is a Maurer--Cartan twisting morphism.

Proof systems are representations of different colors or localizations of the same operad.
Relations and freedoms are Koszul-dual faces, not separate resources.

### Reinterpretation

- relation birth: differential in the bar construction;
- observer quotient: operadic restriction or change of colors;
- higher no-go: nontrivial syzygy/Koszul homology;
- hypertranscendence: non-Noetherian or nonconvergent bar tower;
- positivity: cyclic, modular, or dagger real form of the operad;
- recurrence: group-like automorphism of the completed bar construction;
- source obstruction: failure of a twisting morphism to extend across one missing color.

### Native theorem target

Prove a three-color example where every two-color restriction is formal and split, while the total
twisting morphism has a nonzero ternary obstruction. This would make “all proper proof faces look
complete while the universal law remains nontrivial” an exact operadic theorem.

### Fatal test

If the same phenomenon is presentation-dependent under operadic Quillen equivalence, the proposed
field invariant fails.

## 5. Fork C — renormalization geometry of law

### Primitive

A scale-enriched category of law objects with coarse-graining semigroup
\(\mathcal R_t\), fixed points, relevant/irrelevant tangent directions, and anomaly cocycles for
noncommuting reduction.

Proof systems are effective theories at different scales or along different renormalization
schemes.

### Reinterpretation

- endpoint equivalence: common universality class;
- no-go: relevant direction invisible to chosen coarse-graining;
- completion horizon: infrared/ultraviolet boundary;
- hypertranscendence: infinitely many relevant jet directions;
- positivity: Euclidean/real phase of the effective theory;
- recurrence: renormalization limit cycle;
- source obstruction: missing normalization condition selecting a point on an RG orbit.

### Native theorem target

Construct two inequivalent coarse-graining semigroups with identical fixed-point spectra but
different anomaly holonomy, proving endpoint data cannot classify law flow.

### Fatal test

If observers do not admit a coherent scale composition or universality relation, RG vocabulary is
decorative.

## 6. Fork D — anomaly inflow and theorem holography

### Primitive

A relative or extended field theory assigning bulk law objects to bordisms and proof systems to
boundary conditions. A no-go is a boundary anomaly; a universal source relation is a bulk
trivialization or inflow class.

### Reinterpretation

- many endpoints: boundary conditions of one bulk;
- proof equivalence: dual boundary presentations;
- no-go: nontrivial anomaly line or higher invertible theory;
- positivity: reflection-positive boundary condition;
- recurrence: mapping-class or modular holonomy;
- open lifted history: bulk path invisible on the boundary;
- source obstruction: absence of a canonical bulk orientation/trivialization.

### Native theorem target

Produce one finite algebraic extended-TQFT model in which three inequivalent boundaries have the
same scalar partition data but are separated by a bulk defect-fusion invariant.

### Fatal test

If no source-native locality, gluing, or bordism composition exists, “bulk” is fitted language and
the fork dies.

## 7. Fork E — law cobordism and singularity theory

### Primitive

Objects are law species; morphisms are cobordisms or singular transitions in which operations,
relations, or observer types can be born and die. Stable invariants live in a cobordism spectrum of
law events.

### Reinterpretation

- law change: surgery rather than deformation inside one moduli component;
- proof route: boundary chart of a law cobordism;
- no-go: non-null-cobordant event;
- hypertranscendence: noncompact end or infinite handle tower;
- positivity: orientation/Real structure;
- recurrence: closed projected cobordism with nonclosed lift in a covering spectrum;
- source obstruction: missing fundamental class of the law event.

### Native theorem target

Construct a finite law-surgery category where locally Morita-equivalent fibres are globally
distinguished by an event cobordism class.

### Fatal test

If cobordism class changes under addition of contractible presentation data, it is not a law
invariant.

## 10. Fork G — law filling and determinant torsion

### Primitive

A higher category in which theorem statements are boundaries, proofs are fillings, proof
transformations are higher fillings, and exact histories carry determinant or \(K\)-theory torsion.

### Reinterpretation

- endpoint theorem: fillable boundary;
- proof route: filling rather than coordinate path;
- no-go: non-fillability or lower bound on filling volume;
- positivity: unsigned volume of an oriented filling;
- recurrence: repeated boundary return;
- open lifted history: divergent filling area or determinant winding;
- source obstruction: missing choice of admissible cells and stable presentation equivalence.

### Native survivor

The family \(a^nb^na^{-n}b^{-n}\) is constantly the identity in
\(\langle a,b\mid[a,b]\rangle\) but has exact minimum filling area \(n^2\). Separately, acyclic
based two-term complexes have zero homology while retaining arbitrary multiplicative determinant
history and logarithmic winding.

### Fatal test

If every candidate filling invariant changes arbitrarily under harmless presentation moves, or if
the source admits canonical normal forms with uniformly trivial higher filling categories, this
fork dies.

## 11. Fork H — dynamic equivalence field

### Primitive

the doctrines deciding when objects count as identical evolve together.

### Reinterpretation

- no-gos: nondistributive or incompatible equivalence doctrines;
- positivity: phase-erasing equivalence;
- recurrence: periodicity after coarse equivalence;
- open history: nonperiodic source lift;
- hypertranscendence: failure of finite doctrine families to separate all prolongations.

### Native survivor

the open integer lift.

### Fatal test

If proof observers form one finitely generated distributive source-faithful doctrine lattice, this
fork contributes nothing.

## 14. Fork J — law-phase thermodynamics

### Primitive

A source-indexed fibration of exact/stable law categories over constitutive doctrine space, together
with a multiplicative complex metric-line amplitude on exact histories.

### Reinterpretation

- exactness, stable factorization, metric cost, determinant phase, and cofinal strictness are separate
  state variables;
- positive observation is a modulus quotient and can erase the side of a stability wall;
- an observer loop may return all endpoints while pumping determinant winding or categorical
  monodromy;
- hypertranscendence should mean unbounded stable/prolongation spectrum, not automatically geometric
  noncompactness.

### Native survivors

The metric-phase exactness theorem couples derived-zero laws to variable inverse cost, determinant
winding, positive phase loss, and cofinal nonclosed range. The (A_2) stability theorem fixes one exact
extension while opposite central-charge phases make it stable versus unstable with identical positive
moduli.

### Fatal test

If source cannot canonically produce a law category, charge/amplitude, and coherent doctrine changes,
then this is a sophisticated coordinate bag. Acceptance requires one nontrivial wall-crossing or
monodromy computation yielding at least three nonisomorphic corpus organs.

## 15. Fork K — counterfactual law science

### Primitive

A directed source law machine with legal interventions, composed before observing theorem endpoints.

### Reinterpretation

Proof routes need not all be gauges. Some may be passive observers, while others replace source
relations and are therefore counterfactual experiments. Complete endpoint agreement can coexist with
different intervention response.

### Native survivor

Two opposite deterministic bit-law systems have identical full passive joint distributions but are
separated exactly by `do(X=0)`. No passive statistic, including every positive expectation, recovers
the directed law.

### Fatal test

If three corpus routes cannot be typed as composable interventions on one pre-target source, causal
language is decorative. If they can, route comparison becomes a system-identification problem rather
than geometry.
