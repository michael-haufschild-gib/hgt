## 1. Three certified boundaries

### Boundary I -- closure

ULF-RLM-12 proves that exact reciprocal revision under fixed compatibility is idempotent in serial
schedule and post-first period two in synchronous schedule.

### Boundary II -- vacuity

ULF-ECM-1 proves that arbitrary state-indexed compatibility realizes every transition relation
using one test.

### Boundary III -- finite monotone termination

ULF-GTM-1 proves that no infinite history of strict compatibility additions exists on a finite
vocabulary.

Together:

\[
 \text{fixed exact}\Rightarrow\text{collapse},\qquad
 \text{arbitrary endogenous}\Rightarrow\text{no prediction},\qquad
 \text{finite monotone}\Rightarrow\text{termination}.
 \tag{GT.1}
\]

This is the law-genesis trilemma.

## 2. Remaining mechanisms

A sustained predictive history must use at least one mechanism not erased by (GT.1).

1. **Vocabulary growth.** Failure creates a genuinely new test/rule constructor, so the space of
   possible compatibility facts grows.
2. **Controlled withdrawal.** Old compatibility may be removed, but only with a certificate naming
   which proof transport broke. Otherwise arbitrary rewriting returns.
3. **Admission change.** Algebraic repair exists but enters or leaves the admitted domain; the
   revision records this singular boundary.
4. **Retained lineage.** Endpoint rulebooks may recur while extension, winding, or branch history
   remains open.
5. **Unbounded interaction arity.** Every finite proper face stabilizes, while a future-sensitive
   interaction remains outside all fixed horizons.

## 3. Candidate genesis accounting law

For every strict revision step assign a typed expenditure

\[
 g_n=(\Delta\text{symbols},\Delta\text{admission},
      \text{withdrawn certificates},\Delta\text{lineage},\Delta\text{arity}).
 \tag{GT.2}
\]

### Conjecture GT-A -- zero-expenditure rigidity

If `g_n=0`, every admitted source reporter and compatibility fact transports unchanged, and all
repair-order comparisons vanish, then the revision is presentation-equivalent to identity.

**Falsifier:** a strict, reporter-detectable revision with all five expenditures zero.

### Conjecture GT-B -- bounded-expenditure compactness

Under a source-derived well-founded resource order, bounded total expenditure forces termination or
observed recurrence with explicitly retained open lineage.

**Falsifier:** an infinite aperiodic lifted history with bounded resource and zero lineage escape.

### Conjecture GT-C -- interaction birth

Two witness-local minimal repairs exchange unless their dependency cones overlap. When they overlap,
the least retained exchange residue generates the next interaction law.

**Falsifier:** disjoint dependency cones with nonzero order residue, or overlapping cones whose
order dependence is discarded without changing future predictions.

## 4. Frontier-map interpretation

The current RH frontier supplies five late laboratories, not definitions:

- singular descent tests admission expenditure;
- oscillatory all-prime transport tests phase/certificate conservation;
- adaptive spectral orientation tests witness-dependent rule birth;
- whole-space coercivity tests vocabulary growth from unseen escape profiles;
- positive carrier existence tests whether target uniqueness can replace source production
  (the trilemma predicts it cannot).

No one route is privileged. The mechanism must be fixed before these realizations and make a
withheld prediction in at least three classes.

## 5. Acceptance and death gates

Accept an open-genesis law only if it:

1. excludes the one-test universality construction;
2. admits a formally proved nonterminating history not hidden in an arbitrary update oracle;
3. computes repair exchange from source dependency data;
4. conserves transported certificates;
5. realizes three frontier classes without changing its defining law.

Retire the program if every purported expenditure must be fitted after seeing route endpoints, or
if every open history is merely a renamed counter, universal cover, or growing free signature with
no source rule for growth.
