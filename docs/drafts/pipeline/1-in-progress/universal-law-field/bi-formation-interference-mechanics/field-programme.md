## 1. Pivot

The corpus has enough carriers, observers, sectors, completions, and endpoint equivalences.  Its
repeated failure is more specific: a source carries several native ways of composing laws, but a
proof route usually retains only one composition at a time.

Bi-formation Interference Mechanics begins with **two source-generated composition laws** and asks
how badly they fail to interchange.  Its first chart uses:

1. independent formation, whose logarithmic response is additive; and
2. reciprocal Hadamard mixing, which turns two source copies into even and odd channels.

The field variable is not another law object.  It is the complete response of one composition law
to being transported through the other.

## 2. Seven mechanism laws

### Law I — plural composition is constitutive

A source may have sequential multiplication, independent product, convolution, deletion,
polarization, completion, and observer composition.  Choosing one as universal erases the very
interactions that repeatedly survive route-local no-go theorems.

### Law II — interchange defect is generated, not fitted

Given two declared source compositions, compare their two composites.  Their difference, relative
entropy, mixed cumulant, commutator, or comparison cone is forced by those operations.  It may not
be chosen after inspecting an RH endpoint.

### Law III — complete response reconstructs nonlinear law

In the cumulant chart, independent formation adds cumulant generators and reciprocal Hadamard
mixing produces a two-variable interference germ.  Its mixed derivatives recover every cumulant of
order at least three.  Mean and covariance are the flat ambiguity.  Thus the complete interference
tower reconstructs the law modulo its quadratic/Gaussian sector.

### Law IV — positivity is a quotient of oriented interference

An odd response changes sign under reciprocal reflection.  Squaring it gives a positive reporter
and loses coorientation.  Positive Gram, Fisher, Hellinger, and energy shadows may therefore be
honest quotients of an earlier oriented current without being complete source laws.

### Law V — recurrence and open history are compatible

Reflection can make every even response two-periodic while independent formation depth adds the
same nonzero response at every stage.  Closed observer recurrence and open lifted history are then
two consequences of one interchange law.

### Law VI — formation carries a renormalization-covariant charge

At fixed scale, depth `m` adds `m` identical currents, so uniform boundedness forces the current to
vanish.  But scale is constitutive: under mean normalization `a -> a/m`, the order-`r` charge

\[
 m^{r-1}R_r(m,a/m)=R_r(1,a)
\]

is exactly conserved.  Under variance normalization, using `m^2` blocks at scale `a/m`,

\[
 m^{r-2}R_r(m^2,a/m)=R_r(1,a).
\]

Thus raw boundedness is only a fixed-gauge properness theorem.  The formation invariant is the
renormalized charge; selection must annihilate that charge by a source law rather than hide it by
normalization.

### Law VII — flatness is classified, not presumed

For analytic cumulant generators, complete Hadamard-interference flatness is exactly quadraticity,
hence Gaussianity in the probabilistic chart.  Arithmetic positive-jump formations are provably
nonflat because their third cumulant is positive.  Any future selection argument must transport or
bound this generated current; it cannot declare the source flat.

### Law VIII — split recovery is information-neutral

The interference law is module-valued.  Every linear observer commutes with canonical packet
formation, and every split encode/decode pair returns the complete response exactly.  Therefore a
linear common carrier can transport the charge but cannot select its vanishing.  Genuine selection
must enter through a nonsplit kernel, a nonlinear formation law, or source exactness before the
split observer stage.

## 4. Why this is not the old sector selector

Kakutani, Fock, essential-sector, and common-recovery theorems compare already-formed
representations.  They do not produce the reciprocal pair.  BFIM instead asks whether two native
source compositions interchange and retains their failure as a new current.  Sector singularity
may later consume that current, but it is not the field primitive.

The three-slot exchangeability theorem is another local interchange chart: formation and deletion
fail by a rank-one tensor square.  BFIM does not identify that square with a mixed cumulant.  It
states a higher research rule: both must arise as responses of declared composition laws and should
be compared only after a source functor transports them.

## 6. RH-facing programme

The first viable architecture now has five sharply typed steps.

1. Construct one pre-divisor arithmetic cumulant or log-partition germ `K` from the Euler/theta
   source, with some source-proved nonzero higher cumulant.  Positive-jump Euler formations already
   supply a strict third cumulant in an adjacent laboratory.
2. Prove the radial zero displacement `a = Re rho - 1/2` acts as the dilation `K(z) -> K(az)` on a
   coefficient-faithful formation response before Xi specialization.
3. Derive reciprocal Hadamard mixing from one literal functional-equation/source operation, not
   from a post-zero pair of observers.
4. Prove the renormalization-covariant mixed charge is zero, exact with zero boundary readout, or
   otherwise annihilated for structural reasons independent of RH.  Raw boundedness is insufficient.
5. All-order reconstruction then forces `a=0` whenever one higher cumulant is nonzero.

Steps 2–4 are open.  This is still a real advance over “find a source-native object”: the missing
producer is an interchange law between two specified source compositions, and the complete
response and selection theorem are explicit.
