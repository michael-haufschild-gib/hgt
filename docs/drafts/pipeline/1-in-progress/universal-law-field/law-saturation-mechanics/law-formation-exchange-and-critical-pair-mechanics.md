## 1. Why the first mechanism is too small

Operation-derived congruence closure explains forced observer loss and finite/cofinal saturation.
It does not by itself generate:

- positive/GNS completion;
- analytic or topological completion;
- differential prolongation;

- source-normal charge transport.

Adding these as independent fields would violate the Lean-certified independent-slot anti-theatre
theorem. The next question is therefore not “which extra objects should be stored?” It is:

> Which laws govern formation and interaction of doctrines themselves?

## 2. Proposed mechanism

Let a **law-formation process** \(T_D\) freely enforce doctrine \(D\). Examples to be realized,
not assumed as universal facts, include exact completion, congruence closure, positive/GNS
completion, differential prolongation, localization, reversible completion, and Hausdorff
completion.

The proposed calculus has six laws.

### Formation law

Applying \(T_D\) is universal among maps into worlds satisfying doctrine \(D\). Idempotent
formation processes are reflectors/closure operators; non-idempotent processes retain syntax or
history.

### Exchange law

Two doctrines combine without order memory only when there is a coherent exchange rule

\[
  \lambda_{DE}:T_DT_E\Longrightarrow T_ET_D.
  \tag{1}
\]

For monadic formations this is a distributive law; for rewrite systems it is a critical-pair
filler; for source/observer squares it is a represented comparison cell. Pairwise exchanges must
satisfy higher braid/Yang--Baxter coherence before three doctrines form one law.

### Critical-pair law

When (1) is absent, noninvertible, or noncoherent, order becomes mathematical history. The defect is
not automatically curvature or cohomology. Its type is determined by the formations involved:
extra quotient, missing domain, residual idempotent, relative class, or nonterminating rewrite.

### Saturation law

Repeatedly enforce all declared doctrines until a common fixed point or coherent algebra is reached.
Closure ordinal, minimal formation word, and surviving residual sectors are invariants of this
process. The Stage-3 theorem ULF-LSM-1 is the idempotent two-doctrine calibration.

### Charge-transport law

A source reporter or coframe that is natural for every formation cannot lose a charged class during
saturation. Killing such a class forces one of:

1. reporter/admission failure;
2. transfer to a compensating sector;
3. abandonment of the formation law.

This is the mechanism-level synthesis of the corpus's residue-preserving anomaly trilemma and
coframe-extension nonerasure theorem. It blocks “completion kills the obstruction” unless the exact
compatibility that fails is named.

The companion Lean-certified formation-reporter theorem now closes the finite-word core: primitive
reporter invariance implies conservation along every finite schedule; reporter change at joint
repair proves a non-finite horizon; every nonidentity formation responds in a separating coframe.
Continuity across an infinite completion remains extra structure.

### History-cost law

Costs live on directed formation words, not reversible endpoint equivalences. Minimal word length is
the first law metric; weighted, convex, or action costs are admissible only when derived from source
generators and compatible with composition. The Lean-certified groupoid-cost no-go forces the
directed/noninvertible part.

## 6. Three discriminating conjectures

### Conjecture A — source-generated exchange

One pre-target source signature generates at least three nonisomorphic formation processes and one
nonidentity exchange comparison among them. The comparison must be coefficient-faithful before
endpoint specialization.

**Falsifier:** every formation or comparison requires doctrine-specific auxiliary data not produced
by the source.

### Conjecture B — charged critical-pair conservation

For a source class separated by a full reporter coframe, every saturation schedule that exactifies
the class has a computable reporter-extension anomaly whose rank is at least the charge rank.

**Known support:** the corpus's coframe nonerasure and defect-rank theorems.

**Open strengthening:** prove schedule invariance or an exact exchange formula for the anomaly.

### Conjecture C — prolongation as formation horizon

Differential hypertranscendence is equivalent, in a source-generated differential chart, to failure
of finite generation of the prolongation formation under all bounded-order exchanges.

**Falsifier:** two laws with identical formation semigroup and reporter data but different
hypertranscendence phase.
