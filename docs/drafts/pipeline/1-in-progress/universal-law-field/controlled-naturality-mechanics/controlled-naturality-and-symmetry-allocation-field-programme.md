## Founding correction

Repeated requests for a “source-native selector” conceal two incompatible meanings of native.

- **Invariant output:** the new value is fixed by every old symmetry. Such an output cannot split an
  old symmetry orbit.
- **Equivariant output:** the new value transforms under an old symmetry. It can split an orbit by
  exporting that symmetry into a response action.

Law genesis therefore does not require symmetry breaking in every chart. It requires an exact
accounting of where old symmetry goes.

## Mechanism laws

### Law I -- Curie inheritance

A deterministic equivariant map cannot reduce point stabilizers. Every symmetry of an input remains
a symmetry of its output.

### Law II -- allocation trilemma

An operationally effective source-generated distinction must use at least one channel:

1. **export:** retain full equivariance but give the response a nontrivial action;
2. **mark:** adjoin source history whose stabilizer is smaller, making the map natural only under the
   retained subgroup;
3. **random lift:** preserve symmetry at distribution level while individual histories choose
   symmetry-breaking samples.

Invariant deterministic scalarization is the zero-allocation chamber and is inert on complete
orbits.

### Law III -- maximal retained symmetry

For a deterministic marked reporter, the legal symmetry after birth is the largest subgroup under
which the reporter is equivariant. Any declaration of a smaller group spends unexplained symmetry;
any larger group makes the reporter illegal.

### Law IV -- cumulative allocation

For fixed reporters, retained symmetry is intersection of their equivariance stabilizers. Hence
static cumulative allocation is order-independent. Genuine path dependence requires the next
reporter to be generated from the already-reduced law, not selected from a fixed list.

### Law V -- lineage conservation

The discarded coset or random canonicalization cannot be erased when later execution depends on
it. It is branch history. Averaging it produces a positive/invariant observer but may destroy the
law distinction.

### Law VI -- escape

On a finite symmetry group, strict stabilizer descent terminates. Open genesis requires changing
carrier, arity, topology/admission, group, or retained infinite lineage.

## Exact first theorem

ULF-CNM-1 proves the deterministic core. When old semantic fibres are exactly group orbits, an
equivariant challenge is operationally effective exactly when some symmetry moves an attained
response. Trivial target action forces inertness. Curie stabilizer inheritance holds pointwise. A
fixed auxiliary mark makes a jointly equivariant law equivariant under the mark stabilizer.

This is an allocation theorem, not a universal dynamics theorem.

## Hostile boundaries

1. Orbit-completeness is load-bearing. A reporter may split a fibre by separating two distinct
   orbits even if its target action is trivial.
2. A fixed mark's stabilizer is only a guaranteed retained subgroup; maximality needs a faithfulness
   hypothesis on the marked law.
3. Equivariance and stabilizers alone are static. Their intersection law cannot create history.
4. Probabilistic symmetry breaking needs measurable actions and conditional distributions; it is
   not part of ULF-CNM-1.
5. Hypertranscendence, positivity, recurrence, and completion are not consequences of the group
   theorem. They become separate allocation channels only after literal constitutive maps exist.

survives only if endogenous allocation predicts the next legal challenge. Otherwise CNM is a
