## Public sentence

An autonomous quantum system can remain simple only by paying in one of two
currencies: rarity of its local Hamiltonian or lack of complexity capacity in
that Hamiltonian's own spectral orbit.

## Exact finite model

Fix `q >= 2`. For `n >= q`, let

\[
 H_n(J)=m_{n,q}^{-1/2}\sum_{P:\,1\leq\operatorname{wt}P\leq q}J_PP,
 \qquad
 m_{n,q}=\sum_{k=1}^q3^k{n\choose k},
\]

where the coefficients are independent standard Gaussians. Let
`Phi_n=|0>^{\otimes n}`. Fix a finite universal two-qubit gate alphabet,
clean ancillas returned to zero, pure-state trace distance, an error
`0 < epsilon < 1/4`, and an integer gate budget `R`. Let
`E_(n,R,epsilon)` be the finite union of closed epsilon-balls around all
states prepared by at most `R` gates.

For every nonzero `J`, let `G_J` be the compact projective spectral-phase
closure generated by `H_n(J)` on the cyclic support of `Phi_n`, and let
`nu_J` be normalized Haar measure on `G_J`. Define the resolved completed
shortcut mass and information

\[
 s_J(R,\epsilon)=
 \nu_J\{g:g\Phi_n\in E_{n,R,\epsilon}\},
 \qquad
 I_J^{\rm comp}(R,\epsilon)=-\log s_J(R,\epsilon),                 \tag{1}
\]

with `-log 0=+infinity`. Put

\[
 v_{n,q}=2\sum_{k=1}^q{n\choose k}.                              \tag{2}
\]

The corpus proves that `(2)` is exactly the real projective-action rank and
the multiparameter pure-state QFI rank at `Phi_n`.

## Frozen conjecture

For every fixed `q >= 2`, sufficiently small fixed `epsilon`, and every
`eta > 0`, there are constants `a,b,c,C_eta > 0` such that, for all
sufficiently large `n`, every integer `R` and real `t,B` satisfying

\[
 n^{C_\eta}\leq t\leq \exp(c2^n),
 \qquad
 0\leq R\leq {at\over n^b},
 \qquad B\geq0,                                                   \tag{3}
\]

obey

\[
 \boxed{
 \Pr_J\!\left\{
   e^{-itH_n(J)}\Phi_n\in E_{n,R,\epsilon},\quad
   I_J^{\rm comp}(R,\epsilon)\geq B
 \right\}
 \leq
 \exp\!\left[-(1-\eta)
   \min\{v_{n,q}\log t,B\}\right]. }
                                                                    \tag{4}
\]

Equivalently, without hiding exceptional Hamiltonians inside an annealed
orbit average, `(4)` gives for every threshold `B`

\[
 \Pr_J\{e^{-itH_n(J)}\Phi_n\in E_{n,R,\epsilon}\}
 \leq
 \Pr_J\{I_J^{\rm comp}<B\}
 +e^{-(1-\eta)\min\{v_{n,q}\log t,B\}}.                            \tag{5}
\]

Taking the infimum over `B` is the quenched rarity--capacity envelope. The
conjecture is `(4)`, not a claim that the two terms in `(5)` have already
been estimated.

## Ontological reversal and shadows

The primitive is not ensemble-averaged complexity. It is the pair

\[
 (\text{Hamiltonian rarity},\ \text{its own completed-orbit information}).
\]

Time accumulates observer information only until it reaches the capacity of
the resolved Hamiltonian. This changes the meaning of the three traditional
regimes:

| Existing result or obstruction | Shadow of `(4)` |
|---|---|
| observer/QFI rank `v_(n,q)` | local information-acquisition rate |
| stationary-kernel tube | sharp lower obstruction at cost `v log t` |
| completed spectral plateau | resolved capacity `I_J^comp` |
| spectral recurrence | return governed by the same resolved target mass |
| gapped easy-eigenline box | rare stratum with `I_J^comp` near zero |
| commuting/anticommuting carriers | candidate low-cost strata already proved to pay at least observer codimension |
| adaptive circuit dictionary | the target in `(1)`, with no selector outside the event |

The risky consequence not supplied by any shadow is selector-uniform
conditional growth: after removing Hamiltonians whose own orbit has small
complexity capacity, no third mechanism beats observer-rank escape through
the exponential pre-capacity window.

## Preliminary hostile attack

### Gapped easy eigenlines

They killed the annealed predecessor but not `(4)`. On the proved box the
entire orbit is epsilon-easy, so `I_J^comp=0` at the relevant budget. For
every `B>0` the box is absent from the event in `(4)`; for `B=0` the bound is
trivial. The killing mechanism is now charged rather than averaged away.

### Small Hamiltonians

The ball `||J||=O(1/t)` remains near the initial ray at time `t`, while its
completed information is radial-scale invariant away from zero. Its Gaussian
mass is `t^{-m_(n,q)}` up to dimension factors. Since
`m_(n,q)>v_(n,q)` for `q>=2`, it does not violate `(4)`.

### Stationary-kernel tubes

A `1/t` transverse tube around the exact observer-stationary kernel has mass
of order `t^{-v_(n,q)}` and can have large completed information immediately
off the kernel. It therefore saturates the time branch of `(4)`. This is why
the exponent cannot be strengthened beyond observer rank.

### Linear commuting and anticommuting carriers

The corpus proves that each has transverse codimension at least
`v_(n,q)`. Neither produces a cheaper conditioned tube.

### Integrable and resonant nonlinear carriers

This is the live falsifier. A codimension `c<v_(n,q)` carrier whose exact
orbit is circuit-easy, but whose arbitrarily small transverse perturbations
have `I_J^comp>=B`, would create a transient `t^{-c}` shortcut tube inside
the conditioned event and kill `(4)`. No theorem excludes such moving,
nonlinear carriers. The conjecture survives only preliminarily.

### Fixed-size saturation

The upper time bound in `(3)` is load-bearing. No limit `t->infinity` at fixed
`n` is asserted.
