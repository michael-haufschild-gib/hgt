# Four square graphs cancelling both poles

For \(n\ge1\) put \(a=2n,\ b=4n+3,\ s=2(n+1)\). Take coefficients \(1,-2,-2,1\) at the squares of \(a,b,sa,sb\). Both sampled pole degrees vanish. The first is \(a-2b-2sa+sb=0\). Multiplication by \(a b s\) turns the reciprocal degree into the same equation in reverse order.

Write \(F\) for the real spline trace, \(t=2\log s,\ h=2\log(b/a)\) and \(H=\log4\). The degree is
\[
 10F(0)-8F(h)+2\big(F(t+h)-4F(t)+4F(t-h)\big).
\]
Here \(0<h-H=2\log(1+3/(4n))\le3/(2n)\). The trace derivative has size \(O(e^{w x})\) for \(x\ge0\) at the actual zero width \(w<1/2\). Replacing \(h\) by \(H\) in the long lags costs \(O(n^{2w-1})\). The short lag is bounded. With \(Tf(x)=f(x+H)\), the resulting long term is \((2-T)^2F(t-H)\), where \(t-H=2\log(n+1)\).

The exact degree identity, both zero pole degrees and the variation bound six are formalized by `integerProbe_fourSquareDivisor`, `fourSquareDivisor_degrees` and `fourSquareDivisor_mass`. **Target.** A one-sided degree bound of order \((n+1)^{2\sigma}\), \(0\le\sigma<1/2\), detects width at most \(\sigma\). Apply the positive inverse of \(2-T\) twice on the integer dilations \(n+1\mapsto2(n+1)\). The boundary terms vanish by the actual sublinear trace bound. The ordinary-prime power comparison would then test RH on a single four-graph family of total variation six with both poles zero.

**Why four graphs.** For any nonzero three-graph relation with both poles zero, write \(r<t\) for the two extreme square-root conductors, \(A,C>0\) for their multiplicities and \(-B\) for the middle one (up to overall sign). Multiplying the two moment equations gives \(B^2=A^2+C^2+AC(t/r+r/t)\). At fixed coefficient mass the log diameter is bounded. All self-pairings of such individual triples lie in a compact lag interval. Four graphs are the first possible support size for an unbounded degree at fixed mass.

**One multiplicity.** For a fixed mass bound \(M\), write \(L=3\,\mathrm{windowEnd}(N)\). The general harmonic mesh estimate gives \(O_M(m\sqrt L)\) sampling error and the Chebyshev bound gives \(7L\) floor error. For \(m^2\ge L\) their sum is \(O_M(m^2)\). Along each nonzero balanced ray this compares the continuous ordinary-prime contact with minus the Weil degree to \(O_M(1)\). Thus \(m=1+\lceil\sqrt L\rceil\) suffices for a uniform rational test even when the conductor labels have a common factor.

**Possible sharp mass threshold.** With total variation five, the only remaining split is two unit graphs against three. Write their square-root conductors as \(u\le v\) and \(x\le y\le z\). The two moment equations give \(u+v=x+y+z\) and \(uv=(x+y+z)xyz/(xy+xz+yz)\). Evaluating \((T-u)(T-v)\) at each of \(x,y,z\) puts all three strictly between \(u\) and \(v\). In particular \(v-z=x+y-u\le2y\) and \(1/u-1/x=1/y+1/z-1/v\le2/y\). Thus \(x/u,v/z\le3\) and the two endpoint dipoles have log widths \(O(u/y)\) and \(O(y/v)\). Their interactions with the middle graph are bounded by the first trace derivative. Their mutual interaction uses the mixed second difference and costs \(O((u/v)(v/u)^{2w})=O(1)\). This would bound every two-pole-free degree through mass five. The four-graph family would give the exact first mass, six, at which the ordinary section comparison can detect RH.

More generally, if the negative part has only one conductor \(s^2\) of multiplicity \(B\), both moments put every positive square-root conductor in \([s/B,Bs]\). Their multiplicities \(c_i\) satisfy \(B-\sum c_i=\sum c_i(r_i-s)^2/(2r_i s)\). Thus a single negative graph of bounded multiplicity cannot carry an unbounded degree, regardless of the size of its positive support.

The mass-five bound also has a termwise proof on the full closed spectral strip. Normalize at \(y\). For \(|\Re \lambda|\le1/2\), the lower dipole has transform bounded by \(2|\lambda|\log(x/u)\,y/u\le12|\lambda|\); the upper dipole has the same bound with \(2\log(v/z)\,v/y\). The remaining middle atom contributes one. Both opposite multipliers are therefore bounded by \(1+24|\lambda|\). The second absolute spectral moment is summable by the fourth-power decay of the lag coefficients and temperedness. For interlacing, setting \(D=xy+xz+yz\) gives the exact identity \(-D(x-u)(x-v)=x^2(y^2+yz+z^2)>0\), and likewise after permuting \(x,y,z\).
