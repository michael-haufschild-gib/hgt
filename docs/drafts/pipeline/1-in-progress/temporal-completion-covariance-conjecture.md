# Temporal completion covariance conjecture

Status: `[CONJECTURE FROZEN BEFORE PROOF SEARCH] [PROOF ROUTE UNKNOWN] [PRIORITY UNCHECKED]`

## Public statement

> **There is no observer-independent infinite future.**

More exact statement:

> **A universe supplies finite histories and the maps relating them. Turning those histories into
> an infinite past or future is an additional completion, not an absolute part of reality.
> Physical law is invariant under every completion that preserves all finite operational
> comparisons.**

This is intended as a temporal analogue of general covariance. Coordinate systems are not
spacetime. Likewise, a chosen mathematical infinity is not time.

## Ontological inversion

Standard formulations commonly start with a completed state space and a global time evolution,
then restrict them to finite experiments. Reverse the construction:

1. primitive reality is the directed system of every finite-resource history available to an
   observer;
2. an “infinite future” is a representing completion of that system;
3. two completions agreeing on every finite operational comparison are temporal gauges of the
   same finite reality;
4. only completion-covariant quantities are intrinsic physical observables;
5. failure of two completions to commute is curvature of the temporal-completion atlas, not a
   technical nuisance.

The conjecture does not say that infinity is unreal. It says infinity is relational: its physical
content lies in comparison maps and finite scaling laws, not in one preferred completed object.

## Proposed universal principle

Let \(\mathcal H\) denote the pro-object of finite operational histories and let
\(\mathbf{Comp}(\mathcal H)\) be its groupoid of faithful operational completions. A physical law
is intrinsic exactly when it descends to an invariant of this groupoid.

For two directed idealizations \(A\) and \(B\), define their temporal curvature as the comparison
defect between
\[
  \operatorname{Comp}_A\operatorname{Comp}_B(\mathcal H)
  \quad\text{and}\quad
  \operatorname{Comp}_B\operatorname{Comp}_A(\mathcal H).
\]
Vanishing curvature means the two infinities describe one temporal gauge. Nonvanishing curvature
means their order creates genuinely different asymptotic sectors. A finite experiment can detect
only quantitative precursors of that defect.

## Reinterpretation of existing problems

### Quantum recurrence

Exact recurrence is not automatically intrinsic. It is physical only when its asymptotic law is
forced uniformly by finite-history data. Discontinuous exact recurrence under arbitrarily small
finite-horizon perturbations is evidence of completion dependence, not a new microscopic force.

### Arrow of time

The arrow is not merely entropy increase and not merely an ordered pair of limits. It is the
orientation of a nonflat temporal-completion atlas: the direction in which finite histories admit
stable extension across physically allowed completions.

### Black-hole information

“Information is present at infinite time” and “information is absent at infinite time” can refer
to inequivalent temporal completions. The invariant question is whether finite recovery laws,
their scaling, and all comparison defects agree. A paradox stated only after selecting one
completion is gauge-dependent.

### Big Bang and “before time”

A Big-Bang boundary is not a first point on an already completed line. It is a boundary where the
finite-history pro-object changes its admissible completion groupoid. “Before” has physical
content only through a comparison invariant surviving into later finite histories.

### Time dimension

Temporal dimension is the number or growth law of independent extension directions required by
an observer's finite-history system. Different observers or scales can have different temporal
dimension without positing several external time axes.

## Risky consequences

### C1 — exact-infinity gauge test

Any two models agreeing on all finite operational histories but disagreeing only in a chosen
completion must give identical intrinsic physics. A claimed observable separating them refutes
the principle or reveals omitted finite data.

### C2 — uniform finite witness theorem

Every completion-invariant distinction must possess a uniform finite-resource witness or a
quantitative finite scaling law. A purely infinite distinction with no finite precursor is gauge.

### C3 — recurrence collision curvature

For spectral dynamics, noncommutation of long-time and system-size completion must be exhausted by
the condensation or dissolution of replica energy collisions near zero. No unrelated defect may
appear.

### C4 — observer-change naturality

Temporal curvature must transform functorially under observer restriction. A coarse observer may
lose components of the defect, but cannot manufacture a completion-invariant component absent
for every refinement.

### C5 — closed/open unification

Group recurrence, semigroup decay, continuous spectrum, and thermodynamic limits must arise as
strata of one completion-covariant temporal object. Changing strata may change temporal
dimension, but must preserve a common finite-history comparison law.

### C6 — black-hole discriminator

A finite-entropy system and its strict continuum or large-\(N\) completion can disagree about
late-time return only through an explicit nonuniformity visible in a finite-size/finite-time
scaling surface. If no such surface exists, the alleged information distinction is not intrinsic.

## Fatal tests

Reject or radically revise the conjecture if:

1. faithful completions preserving all finite comparisons nevertheless yield a finite operational
   separation;
2. the completion groupoid is too large to preserve ordinary experimentally established laws;
3. “intrinsic iff completion-invariant” is only a definition and yields no representation,
   rigidity, or classification theorem;
4. temporal curvature cannot be made canonical and composes without a cocycle law;
5. recurrence, open dynamics, and horizon limits require incompatible notions of finite history;
6. the proposal is already equivalent to a standard pro-observable, algebraic-QFT, or
   asymptotic-observable construction;
7. no new finite scaling identity follows;
8. a black-hole or cosmological interpretation requires importing the desired conclusion as a
   physical assumption.
