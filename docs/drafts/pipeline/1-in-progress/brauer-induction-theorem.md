# Brauer's induction theorem

- **ID:** A150-brauer
- **Stage:** 1-in-progress
- **Type:** THEOREM (classical; formalization)
- **Source:** `docs/reports/review-1211/obstacles-summary.md` §2.1 (A150), §2.14 question 4
- **Statement:** for a finite group `G`, every virtual character is an integer combination of
  characters induced from linear characters of Brauer-elementary subgroups:
  `AddSubgroup.closure (Set.range (brauerElementaryMonomialVirtualCharacter G)) = ⊤`
  (objects of `formal/GaloisForLFunctions/RHTower/Artin/ArtinBrauerElementary.lean`).

## Goal

The integral monomial span of the virtual characters. For the index law of an Artin character
the rational statement suffices and is proved (`exists_nsmul_mem_closure_monomial`,
`exists_le_negIndex_power_iff`; card `3-formalized/agz-virtual-character-index-law.md`, V9). The
integral statement is needed for the function `L(s, χ)` itself. Both uses also need the
reciprocity law (the `L`-function of an induced linear character is a Hecke `L`-function), which
is class field theory and is not in the corpus.

## Gates

- [x] B0 induced character formula: the character of `Representation.ind H.subtype ρ` at `g` is
  `Σ_{q ∈ H\G, r_q g r_q⁻¹ ∈ H} χ_ρ(r_q g r_q⁻¹)`, and `(1/|H|) Σ_{x, x g x⁻¹ ∈ H} χ_ρ(x g x⁻¹)`
- [x] B1 induction of functions by the formula: class-function property, the projection formula

- [x] B2 products of characters are characters; for every family of subgroups the span of the
  induced characters is an ideal of the ring of virtual characters, equal to it as soon as it
  contains the constant `1`
- [x] B3 descent: a virtual character in the span over the algebraic integers of the characters
  induced from a family lies in their integer span (no basis of irreducible characters is used:
  the multiplicities against the monomial characters separate the virtual characters)
- [x] B4 cyclic groups: `θ_C` (value `|C|` on generators, `0` elsewhere) is a virtual character

  characters induced from cyclic subgroups
- [x] B5 congruence: an integer-valued `𝓞`-combination of characters has `f(x) ≡ f(x_r) mod p`,
  `x_r` the `p`-regular part
- [x] B6 for a `p`-regular `x`: the subgroup `⟨x⟩ × P`, `P` a Sylow `p`-subgroup of the
  centralizer, is Brauer-elementary; the induced function of `|⟨x⟩|·1_{x}` ⊗ `1` is integer
  valued, `≢ 0 mod p` at `x`, and vanishes at the `p`-regular elements not conjugate to `x`
- [x] B7 with `|G| = p^n l`: `l` lies in the `𝓞`-span of the characters induced from
  `p`-elementary subgroups
- [x] B8 the virtual characters are spanned over `ℤ` by characters induced from elementary
  subgroups
- [ ] B9 every irreducible character of a Brauer-elementary group is induced from a linear
  character of a subgroup
- [ ] B10 the theorem

## Approach

Serre, *Linear representations of finite groups*, §10.1–10.5 and Theorem 16 [C]. `𝓞` is the
ring of algebraic integers in `ℂ` (in place of `ℤ[ζ_{|G|}]`); `𝓞 ∩ ℚ = ℤ`.

**B0.** Coinduced model: functions `f : G → V` with `f(h x) = ρ(h) f(x)`, `G` acting by right
translation; evaluation at representatives `r_q` of the right cosets identifies it with
`(H\G → V)` (corpus, `coinducedRightCosetEquiv`). There `g` acts by
`(T_g a)(q) = ρ(r_q g r_{q·g}⁻¹) a(q·g)`. The trace of an operator on `(Q → V)` is the sum of
the traces of its diagonal blocks, so `tr T_g = Σ_{q·g = q} χ_ρ(r_q g r_q⁻¹)`, and `q·g = q` iff
`r_q g r_q⁻¹ ∈ H`. Induction and coinduction are isomorphic for a subgroup of finite index
(Mathlib), and isomorphic representations have one character. Averaging over the `|H|`
representatives of each coset (`χ_ρ` is a class function on `H`) gives the second form.

**B1–B2.** By the formula. `Ind(f · Res ψ)(g) = (1/|H|) Σ f°(xgx⁻¹) ψ(xgx⁻¹) = Ind(f)(g) ψ(g)`.
Products: tensor products of representations (Mathlib). If `ψ` is a character of `G` and `χ` one
of `H`, then `χ · Res ψ` is a character of `H`, so `Ind(χ)·ψ` is in the span.

**B3.** *Integers.* Let `M ⊆ ℂ` be the `ℤ`-module generated by `1` and algebraic integers
`a_k`: finitely generated, torsion free, hence free; its elements are algebraic integers. If `g`
divides all coordinates of `1` in a basis, `1 = g w` with `w ∈ M`, so `1/g` is an algebraic
integer and rational, `g = ±1`. So the coordinates generate the unit ideal and there is a
`ℤ`-linear `π : M → ℤ` with `π(1) = 1`. If integer vectors satisfy `X = Σ_k a_k N_k`, then
`X = Σ_k π(a_k) N_k`. *Characters.* Write `x = Σ_k a_k v_k`, `v_k` in the integer span. The
pairing with a monomial character `μ` is linear: `⟨x, μ⟩ = Σ_k a_k ⟨v_k, μ⟩`, integers on both
sides. So `⟨x, μ⟩ = Σ_k m_k ⟨v_k, μ⟩` with integers `m_k` independent of `μ`, and
`x − Σ m_k v_k` is a virtual character without monomial multiplicities: it is zero (corpus).

**B4–B5.** Serre §10.2–10.3, Lemmas 5–7.

**B6.** Let `x` have order prime to `p` and `P` be a `p`-subgroup of the centralizer `Z(x)`. The
map `⟨x⟩ × P → G`, `(c, q) ↦ cq`, is a homomorphism (the factors commute) and is injective: if
`cq = 1` then `c = q⁻¹` is killed by `|x|` and by a power of `p`, so `c = 1`. Its range `H` is
elementary. A `p`-regular `z = xq` with `q ∈ P` has `q = 1`: with `m = |z|`, `q^m = x^{-m}` is
killed by `|x|` and by a power of `p`, so `q^m = 1`, and `q` is killed by `m` and by a power of
`p`. Let `ψ` on `H` be `|x|` on `x·P` and `0` elsewhere: it is the pullback along `H → ⟨x⟩` of
`|x|·1_{x}`, whose Fourier coefficients on the cyclic group are algebraic integers, so `ψ` is an
`𝓞`-combination of linear characters of `H`. For `ψ' = Ind ψ`:
`ψ'(s) = Σ_{q ∈ H\G} ψ°(r_q s r_q⁻¹)` is a natural number; for `p`-regular `s` the conjugate
`y s y⁻¹` is `p`-regular, so `ψ°(y s y⁻¹) = |x|·[y s y⁻¹ = x]`, and `ψ'(s) = 0` unless `s` is
conjugate to `x`; `ψ'(x) = (|x|/|H|)·|Z(x)| = |Z(x)|/|P|`, prime to `p` for `P` a Sylow
subgroup of `Z(x)`. A cyclic group is elementary for a prime not dividing its order (`P = 1`).

**B7.** `Ψ = Σ ψ'_x` over representatives `x` of the classes of `p`-regular elements. At a
`p`-regular `s` only the representative of the class of `s` contributes and
`Ψ(s) = [Z(x) : P_x]`, prime to `p` (no multipliers `n_x` are needed: `≢ 0` suffices). Every `y`
has a `p`-regular part `z = y^{qu}` (`|y| = qm`, `q` the power of `p`, `qu + mv = 1`), a power of
`y` with `y^q = z^q`; by B5, `Ψ(y) ≡ Ψ(z) mod p`. So no value of `Ψ` is divisible by `p`. With
`|G| = p^n l` and `N = φ(p^n)`, Euler gives `Ψ^N ≡ 1 mod p^n` pointwise; `l(Ψ^N − 1)` has values
in `|G|ℤ` and is in the span by B4; `lΨ^N` is in the span because the span is an ideal. So the
constant `l` is in the span.

**B8.** If a constant `m > 1` is in the span and `p | m`, then `gcd(m, l_p)` is in the span by
Bézout and is `< m` because `p ∤ l_p`. Starting from `|G|` (B4) this reaches `1`. Then B3
descends to the integer span and B2 gives the whole ring.

**B9.** Serre Theorem 16 for groups with a normal abelian subgroup not in the center, by
induction on the order: restriction to that subgroup is not isotypic, so the representation is
induced from the stabilizer of an isotypic component, a proper subgroup that is again
elementary (corpus: subgroups of a product of a `p'`-group and a `p`-group split).
