# The signed conductor prefix above the seventeen-ninths cutoff

Put \(U_n=\lfloor n^{17/9}\rfloor\), \(B(N)=\sum_{1\le n\le N}n^2T_n(U_n,n)\). Keep \(p>4\) prime with weight \(\log p\), \(v\le n\), \(p\nmid v\), and the original moving product endpoint. The comparison with \(\log P(N)\) costs \(O(N^3(1+\log(2N))^6)\) at every terminal. The intermediate target is the one-sided \(B(N)\ll N^{11/3}\), giving \(\Theta\le5/6\). The target for RH is \(B(N)\ll_\eta N^{3+\eta}\) for every \(\eta>0\). Both remain open. [Prefix and width theorems](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExceptionalLayerTheory/SquareConductors/TheSeventeenNinthsConductorPrefix.lean)

**Fixed product.** Write \(q=pv\), \(g(v)=\mathrm{innerJump}(1,v)\). Every eligible first prime has the same inner weight \(g(q)+\mathbf1_{q\text{ prime}}\). Its total Mangoldt mass is \(\log q\) less the disjoint masses of nonprime divisors, small primes, repeated primes above four, and coprime primes excluded by the cofactor cap. Factor these weights out of the common outer quotient cell before estimating its two ends. [Fixed-product proof](../2-fully-proven/acs-pvc-divisor-inversion-at-the-moving-product-boundary.md)

**Two possible cap entries.** At the last admissible conductor \(b\) for a given product in a partial octave, each eligible prime exceeds \(\lfloor\sqrt b\rfloor\). Three such primes would force two of them into a cofactor at most \(b\). Thus the ordinary-prime mass enters at at most two values \(q/p\). If distinct primes \(p,r\) both enter, then \(q=prt\) and \(qt\le b^2\). At the seventeen-ninths cutoff this forces \(t<b^{1/9}\).

**Shell test.** A paper proof gives \(O(N^3(1+\log(2N)))\) on the \(19/10\) shell and on the full range from \(17/9\) through \(19/10\), at every partial octave. After pricing the cap and prime-power terms, the conductor substitution \(x=t\sqrt q\) leaves a single product interval at each \(t\). The project's \(8/9\) prime-error bound then gives \(N^{269/90}\) for the signed integral. The rounding cost and the two original cutoff errors are included. The shell and the cutoff advance await Lean verification. [Paper proof](../2-fully-proven/acs-pvc-nineteen-tenths-shell-by-conductor-rescaling.md)

**The first quotient endpoint.** Write \(W(j)=\mathrm{centeredPrimeWeight}(1,j)\), \(w_n(k)=\mathrm{sampledSpline}(n,k)\), and \(L=\mathrm{splineHorizon}(n)\). Then \(W(0)=1\) and \(w_n(0)=w_n(L+1)=0\). Extending the horizon down from \(q\) to zero adds exactly \(-w_n(q)\) whenever the upper cut lies beyond \(L\). The original reading thus contains \(+w_n(q)\). On the complete product range the periodic extension has Mangoldt reading \(O((1+\log(2n))/n)\) per conductor before multiplication by \(n^2\). Indeed its horizon row, after subtracting the constant weight one, is \(\sum_{q\le k}\Lambda(q)(W(\lfloor k/q\rfloor)-1)=-k+O(1+\log k)\). The linear density has spline reading \(O(1/n)\) and the logarithmic error costs \(O((1+\log(2n))/n)\). The returned sample \(\sum_q\Lambda(q)w_n(q)\) includes the actual ordinary-prime contact. This isolates a term lost by completing the horizon across quotient zero. Restoring the sharp lower product cutoff requires its own estimate. **Residue test.** A right zero \(\rho\) gives residue \(-2m_\rho\,\mathcal M w(\rho)\ne0\) at \(2+2\rho\) for the complete conductor price. The continuation is regular at four. A term with cubic-logarithmic prefixes has a holomorphic Dirichlet series on \(\Re s>3\) by partial summation, so subtracting such terms preserves each right-zero residue. A proposed secondary term must be tested after summing the same cells through every terminal.
