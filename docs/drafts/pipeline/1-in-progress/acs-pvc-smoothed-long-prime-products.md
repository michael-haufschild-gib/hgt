# The signed conductor prefix above the seventeen-ninths cutoff

Put \(U_n=\lfloor n^{17/9}\rfloor\) and \(B(N)=\sum_{1\le n\le N} n^2T_n(U_n,n)\). Keep the ordinary factors \(p>4\) with weight \(\log p\), coprime cofactors \(v\le n\), and the original moving product endpoint. At every terminal, \(|\log P(N)-B(N)|\ll N^3(1+\log(2N))^6\). The open intermediate target \(B(N)\ll N^{11/3}\) gives \(\Theta\le5/6\). The one-sided \(B(N)\ll_\eta N^{3+\eta}\) for every \(\eta>0\) gives RH. [Seventeen-ninths prefix](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExceptionalLayerTheory/SquareConductors/TheSeventeenNinthsConductorPrefix.lean) · [One-sided price criterion](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/CountingHolography/Sections/OneSidedBoundsForTheSquareGeneratorPrefix.lean#L265)

**Verified product shell (§2.18).** Put \(V_n=\lfloor n^{19/10}\rfloor\). The true shell \(V_n<pv\le2V_n\) costs \(O(N^3(1+\log(2N)))\) on every \(N\le M\le2N\). Moving the full prefix cutoff from \(U_n\) to \(V_n\) costs \(O(M^3(1+\log(2M)))\) at every terminal. Thus the next whole-prefix target is the signed contribution with \(pv>V_n\) at exponent \(11/3\). For every fixed \(17/9\le\alpha<2\), the cost between \(U_n\) and \(\lfloor n^\alpha\rfloor\) is \(O_\alpha(M^3(1+\log(2M))+M^{2+2\Theta-(2-\alpha)/4})\). When \(\Theta>1/2\), the critical exponent can only come from products above every fixed subquadratic cutoff. At \(pv\asymp n^2\), the rescaled conductor \(t=n/\sqrt{pv}\) stays bounded and the small-mesh power saving no longer applies. **Fixed products (§2.3).** Every admissible first prime of \(q=pv\) has inner weight \(\mathrm{innerJump}(1,q)+\mathbf1_{q\text{ prime}}\). Its total log mass is \(\log q\) less four disjoint omissions. At the last conductor \(b\) there are at most two cofactor entries. Two imply \(q=prt,\ qt\le b^2,\ t<b^{1/9}\). Lean verified both terminal quotient samples and the replacement by \(\Lambda(q)-1\) at cost \(O(M^3(1+\log(2M)))\) on every \(J<n\le M\), uniformly in the moving cutoff. On paper the raw cofactor-cap differences have unbounded rank. At products \(pt\) with \(4B<p\le8B\) prime and odd \(B<t\le2B\), their matrix on \(B<b\le2B\) is \(-\log p\,\mathbf1_{b=t}\). The complete readings of these primes fall within the linear first-prime estimate below. **Returned endpoint (§2.18).** Starting at the first positive quotient returns \(+w_n(q)\) with the same far endpoint. Every fixed subquadratic power cutoff eventually retains the full ordinary-prime price, up to \(O(M^3)\) on every fragment. A superquadratic cutoff eventually removes it. The completed Mangoldt mesh costs \(O((1+\log(2n))/n)\) per conductor. At every quadratic divider \(2\le K\le L\), the two prime prices have opposite terms \(\pm n^3 A(K/n^2)\) with uniform \(O(n^{25/9})\) error, where \(A(v)=\int_0^v\mathrm{dyadicSpline}\). These bounds passed Lean. On paper \(A(v)>0\) exactly on \((e^{-8},4e^8)\). The poles at four cancel at \(K=n^2\), and the returned endpoint at \(17/9\) retains each right-zero residue. **Which cofactors remain at \(11/3\)?** Thinning the cap to \(\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor\) costs \(O(N^{7/2}\log(2N))\) on every terminal fragment. The remaining \(pv>n^{17/9}\) force \(p>n^{25/18}\) and have one prime divisor above the cap. Lean verified that the two signed prefixes have equivalent \(N^{11/3}\) upper bounds. [Cap loss and unique prime](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExceptionalLayerTheory/SquareConductors/TheUniqueLargePrimeAboveTheProductCut.lean)

**Removing the moving cofactor cap (§2.18).** Put \(F_n=\max(n,\lfloor L_n/(n+1)\rfloor,2\lfloor\sqrt{L_n}\rfloor)\), where \(L_n\) is the last spline horizon. Lean verified that the first primes \(p\le F_n\) cost \(O(M^3(1+\log(2M)))\) on every fragment. For \(p>F_n\), the exact cofactor interval is \(\lfloor U_n/p\rfloor<v\le\lfloor k/p\rfloor\le n<p\). Thus the remaining signed sum has the same one-sided bound criterion at each exponent above three. On paper one prime cutoff can serve the whole fragment, and the omitted lower products can be restored at cubic-logarithmic cost. **Density and zero tests.** For \(G_V(s)=\sum_{v\le V}g(v)v^{-s}\), the logarithmic stencil moment gives \(G_V(1)=O(1/V)\). The density error is positive at \(V=4K\) and negative at \(4K+3\) for \(K\ge1\). At each right zero \(\rho\), the same sums tend to one. For a common lower prime cut \(P\), continuous prime mass through product \(b\ge P\) has leading term \((c-1)P/2\), with \((1+c/2)\log2=1\). This term cancels on every sharp interval \(P\le a<pv\le b\), leaving \(O(P^2/a)\) uniformly in \(b\). At \(P=\max(4,F_n)\) the total continuous density over every remaining cell costs \(O(M^{20/9})\) on each fragment. The unpaid arithmetic term uses \(\theta(y)-\theta(x)-(y-x)\) on the actual prime intervals, with the original signs. Lean verified the zero response, the four-cap estimate, selection at the moving cut and the logarithmic normalization. The finite density estimates await audit. **Cancellation across adjacent cofactors.** Split at \(v\asymp h^{-1/2}\) with \(h=p/n^2\). The small cofactors see the seventh-power quotient bound. Lean verified that the seventh difference costs \(O(h^{-1}v^{-8})\), with \(O(h^{-1}D^{-7})\) total above \(D\). Lean verified that cancellation across the clipped cofactor interval pays all first primes through \(n^{19/10}\) across the **entire product range** at \(O(M^{73/20}+M^3\log(2M))\), below \(11/3\). At the RH threshold the same argument pays first primes through \(n^{12/7}\) at cubic-logarithmic cost. The remaining \(11/3\) sum has \(p>n^{19/10}>U_n\) and \(v\ll n^{1/10}\). [Signed first-prime fragment estimate](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExceptionalLayerTheory/SquareConductors/TheFirstPrimesThroughTheNineteenTenthsScale.lean#L213)

**Fixed-product inversion on odd products (§2.3).** Three product dilations and eight positive-quotient samples recover the ordinary prime singleton through both sharp cuts for odd \(u\) with \(U\le P\) and \(4u\le nP\). Outside this range the same reading has a surplus at or below \(U\), and a deficit above \(U\) from two explicit cofactor bands. Lean verifies that the explicit odd-factorial baseline minus the positive composite mass equals \(\psi(H)-H+O(\sqrt H)\). Each right zero forces a deficit and a surplus on every sufficiently late square-root window. A one-sided composite lower bound of order \(h^{5/6}\) throughout arbitrarily late such windows suffices for the whole-prefix \(11/3\) estimate. Square-root-plus lower bounds characterize RH, and the single-wave formula for this count characterizes a lone quartet. The composite lower bound remains open. Lean also verifies that the total negative reciprocal-octave mass at arbitrarily large terminals suffices. For the integer gcd product on \((m^r,2m^r]\) at horizon \(m^{r+1}\), Lean verified that a cofinal subpower bound on its total normalized deficit gives the whole-prefix estimate at \(r=6\) and is equivalent to RH at \(r=2\). For the integrated positive band, moving the first-prime cut to \(X^{19/20}\) leaves an explicit density and an error of order \(X^{143/180}\), below \(X^{5/6}\). **A positive quadratic band.** Put \(R=4e^8\). For \(3Rn^2/8<p\le2Rn^2/5\), only cofactor two and outer quotient one survive. Lean verified \(W(1)<0\), \(W(2)=1\) and strict negativity of the sampled spline. Immediately below \(pv/n^2=R/2\), the two adjacent quotients have opposite signs and their sum stays uniformly positive. Lean verified the per-conductor price \(Cn^3+O(n^{25/9})\) with explicit \(C>0\), and a negative contribution of at least quartic magnitude from the complementary primes on every complete late octave. The complete rescaled prime profile has three vanishing logarithmic moments on paper, forcing at least four alternating prime bands. On this same explicit band, subtracting its cubic density cancels the pole at four and retains every right-zero residue. A one-sided \(N^{11/3}\) bound on this single centered prime band would give \(\Theta\le5/6\) and hence the whole-prefix bound. Summing conductors first makes the arithmetic target \(\sum_p(\log p)K(p/X)-X\int K\le A X^{5/6}\) for one explicit nonnegative kernel, linear up to \(3R/8\) and decreasing to zero at \(2R/5\). Lean verified that the actual sharp-band Mellin integral in logarithmic coordinates is nonzero for every nonreal \(z\) with \(\Re z\le1\). The moment identities and continuation of the centered price series remain paper results. [Quadratic first-prime band](acs-pvc-a-positive-quadratic-band-of-first-primes.md)

**Cutting after conductor summation.** At \(K=\lfloor N^{17/9}\rfloor\), the integrated prime parts have leading terms \(\pm cN^{34/9}/2\), with \(c=\mathcal M(\mathrm{squarePrimeWeight})(-1)>0\). Adding \(cK(K+1)/2\) to the high-prime part gives \(H(N)\). On paper its error from \(\log P(N)\) is both \(O(N^{289/81})\) and \(O(N^{3+2\max(0,w-1/40)})\), giving the equivalent open target \(\Re H(N)\ll N^3\). The Mellin and one-sided deductions await kernel audit. [Fixed-cut criterion](../2-fully-proven/acs-pvc-the-quadratic-product-divider.md)

**Quartet and index alternative (§2.3).** Lean verified that the ordinary-prime radial exclusion law is equivalent to the absence of a single off-line quartet of any multiplicity, including when the zero divisor is infinite. A quartet passes the trial at its own frequency and multiplicity. On paper a lone quartet at \(\rho\) of multiplicity \(m\) is equivalent to the signed band price satisfying \(S_n-Cn^3=-2m\Re(M(\rho)n^{1+2\rho})+O(n^2\log^2(2n))\) at every late conductor. The converse follows from the residues and fixes both frequency and multiplicity. Excluding this approximation from ordinary-prime arithmetic remains open. Under failure of RH, the universal confined index bound of two is itself equivalent to a lone quartet. An independent arithmetic proof could establish nonnegativity on the common kernel of two functionals for every finite confined family and every positive horizon.
