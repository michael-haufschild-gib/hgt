# The signed conductor prefix above the seventeen-ninths cutoff

Put \(U_n=\lfloor n^{17/9}\rfloor\) and \(B(N)=\sum_{1\le n\le N} n^2T_n(U_n,n)\). Keep the ordinary factors \(p>4\) with weight \(\log p\), coprime cofactors \(v\le n\), and the original moving product endpoint. At every terminal, \(|\log P(N)-B(N)|\ll N^3(1+\log(2N))^6\). The open intermediate target \(B(N)\ll N^{11/3}\) gives \(\Theta\le5/6\). The one-sided \(B(N)\ll_\eta N^{3+\eta}\) for every \(\eta>0\) gives RH. **Fixed-product inversion (§2.3).** At \(q=pv\), all admissible first primes have inner weight \(g(q)+\mathbf1_{q\ {\rm prime}}\), where \(g=\mathrm{innerJump}(1,\cdot)\). Their log mass equals \(\log q\) less four disjoint divisor masses. At the last admissible conductor \(b\) in a partial octave, at most two entries \(q/p\) occur. Two distinct entries give \(q=prt,\ qt\le b^2,\ t<b^{1/9}\). Lean verified the quotient partition including both terminal samples. Replacing the capped row by the single signed measure \(\Lambda(q)-1\) costs \(O(M^3(1+\log(2M)))\) on every \(J<n\le M\), uniformly over cutoff sequences \(U_n\). [Exact omissions, cap entries and uniform transfer](../3-formalized/acs-pvc-divisor-inversion-at-the-moving-product-boundary.md)

**The returned prime endpoint (§2.18).** The first positive quotient cell contributes \(+w_n(q)\) after periodic completion with the same far cutoff. Every fixed \(\lfloor n^\alpha\rfloor\), \(0<\alpha<2\), eventually keeps the entire ordinary-prime endpoint. For \(\alpha>2\) it eventually vanishes. The retained price at \(17/9\) differs from the rational price by \(O(M^3)\) on every fragment. The completed Mangoldt mesh costs \(O((1+\log(2n))/n)\) per conductor. On paper the returned endpoint retains every right-zero residue at \(2+2\rho\). **Quadratic divider.** Write \(A(v)=\int_0^v\mathrm{dyadicSpline}(x)\,dx\). Lean verified opposite main terms \(\pm n^3A(K/n^2)\) with uniform \(O(n^{25/9})\) errors at every \(2\le K\le L\). On paper \(A(v)>0\) exactly on \(e^{-8}<v<4e^8\). At \(K=n^2\) the corresponding real poles at four cancel. [Densities and pole calculation](../2-fully-proven/acs-pvc-the-quadratic-product-divider.md)

**Fixed terminal cutoff.** After summing conductors, the integrated prime weight is \(cv\) below its first knot, with \(c=\mathcal M(\mathrm{squarePrimeWeight})(-1)>0\). At \(K=\lfloor N^{17/9}\rfloor\) the two parts contribute \(\pm cN^{34/9}/2\) to leading order. Add \(cK(K+1)/2\) to the high-prime sum to form \(H(N)\). On paper \(|H(N)-\log P(N)|\ll N^{289/81}\), below the intermediate target. At the actual width \(w=\Theta-1/2\) the error is also \(O(N^{3+2\max(0,w-1/40)})\). This gives the equivalent open RH target \(\Re H(N)\ll N^3\) at every terminal. [Centering coefficient and width argument](../2-fully-proven/acs-pvc-the-quadratic-product-divider.md)

**The \(19/10\) shell.** The conductor substitution \(x=t\sqrt q\) gives one product interval at each \(t\), verified in Lean. The paper bound for its signed integral is \(O(N^{269/90})\). With rounding and both moving endpoints, the \(19/10\) shell and the full \(17/9\)-to-\(19/10\) band cost \(O(N^3(1+\log(2N)))\) on every partial octave. Kernel verification of the shell bound is pending. [Partial-octave proof](../2-fully-proven/acs-pvc-nineteen-tenths-shell-by-conductor-rescaling.md)

**Which product scales can carry the zero width?** For a fixed \(17/9<\alpha<2\), test the entire band up to \(n^\alpha\) against \(\psi(q)-q\). The proposed bound on a fragment ending at \(M\) is \(O_{\alpha,\beta}(M^3\log(2M)+M^{2+\alpha\beta})\) for every \(\beta>\Theta\). The quotient mesh changes by \(O(1/q)\) between adjacent products, so Abel summation can retain both endpoints. Choosing \(\Theta<\beta<2\Theta/\alpha\) would put the band strictly below the critical exponent whenever \(w>0\). The bound for products beyond every such cut remains the target. **Unique first prime.** At the \(11/3\) target, thinning the cofactor cap from \(n\) to \(\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor\) costs \(O(N^{7/2}\log(2N))\) on every terminal fragment. Now \(pv>n^{17/9}\) forces \(p>n^{25/18}\), and \(p\) is the unique prime divisor larger than the cap. For the RH target, pair the cap \(\lfloor n^{1-\varepsilon}\rfloor\) with product cut \(\lfloor n^{2-\varepsilon}\rfloor\). Every surviving first prime exceeds the conductor itself. The remaining question is an upper bound on this signed largest-prime-factor sum. [Cofactor error and uniqueness](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExceptionalLayerTheory/SquareConductors/TheCompleteUpperProductTail.lean#L157)

**Quartet and index alternative (§2.3).** A bounded radial trial requires an attained extremal line with all ordinates in \(|\gamma|\mathbb Q\). Nonnegativity of every confined Gram form on the common kernel of two fixed arithmetic functionals is open. The cofactor cap itself has arbitrarily many independent entries across products. Choose a prime \(4B<p\le8B\). For \(B<t\le2B\), any first prime of \(pt\) other than \(p\) leaves cofactor at least \(p\). Increasing the cap from \(b-1\) to \(b\le2B\) therefore returns \(\log p\) exactly at \(b=t\). On odd \(t>1\) the actual inner jump is \(-1\), so the signed entries still distinguish every odd cofactor. A Gram cap requires an additional relation after combining the products.
