# The signed conductor prefix above the seventeen-ninths cutoff

Put \(U_n=\lfloor n^{17/9}\rfloor\), \(B(N)=\sum_{1\le n\le N}n^2T_n(U_n,n)\). Keep \(p>4\) prime with weight \(\log p\), \(v\le n\), \(p\nmid v\), and the original moving product endpoint. The comparison with \(\log P(N)\) costs \(O(N^3(1+\log(2N))^6)\) at every terminal. The intermediate target is the one-sided \(B(N)\ll N^{11/3}\), giving \(\Theta\le5/6\). The target for RH is \(B(N)\ll_\eta N^{3+\eta}\) for every \(\eta>0\). Both remain open. **Fixed product.** Write \(q=pv\), \(g(v)=\mathrm{innerJump}(1,v)\). Every eligible first prime has the same inner weight \(g(q)+\mathbf1_{q\text{ prime}}\). Its total Mangoldt mass is \(\log q\) less the disjoint masses of nonprime divisors, small primes, repeated primes above four, and coprime primes excluded by the cofactor cap. Lean verified the resulting sum over all quotient cells, including both samples of the last clipped cell. [Fixed-product proof](../3-formalized/acs-pvc-divisor-inversion-at-the-moving-product-boundary.md)

**Two possible cap entries.** At the last admissible conductor \(b\) for a given product in a partial octave, each eligible prime exceeds \(\lfloor\sqrt b\rfloor\). Three such primes would force two of them into a cofactor at most \(b\). Thus the ordinary-prime mass enters at at most two values \(q/p\). If distinct primes \(p,r\) both enter, then \(q=prt\) and \(qt\le b^2\). At the seventeen-ninths cutoff this forces \(t<b^{1/9}\).

**Shell test.** A paper proof gives \(O(N^3(1+\log(2N)))\) on the \(19/10\) shell and on the full range from \(17/9\) through \(19/10\), at every partial octave. After pricing the cap and prime-power terms, the conductor substitution \(x=t\sqrt q\) leaves a single product interval at each \(t\). The project's \(8/9\) prime-error bound then gives \(N^{269/90}\) for the signed integral. The rounding cost and the two original cutoff errors are included. The shell and the cutoff advance await Lean verification. [Paper proof](../2-fully-proven/acs-pvc-nineteen-tenths-shell-by-conductor-rescaling.md)

**Which endpoint carries the price?** In the compensated one-factor reading, let \(w_n(q)\) be the sampled spline. Subtracting the quotient sum starting at zero from the one starting at \(q\) returns \(+w_n(q)\). For a retained prime \(q>2\) the identity survives the original far cut \(D=\min(L+1,\lfloor(q+1)/2\rfloor^2)\), since \(q\le D\). Every \(q<e^{-8}n^2\) has zero sample. Thus every fixed cut \(\lfloor n^\alpha\rfloor\), \(0<\alpha<2\), eventually retains the **entire** ordinary-prime endpoint. For \(\alpha>2\) the retained endpoint eventually vanishes because the finite horizon is \(O(n^2)\). The unrestricted periodic Mangoldt reading costs \(O((1+\log(2n))/n)\) per conductor. In the raw two-factor reading a prime product has cofactor one and zero inner weight. **Whole-prefix consequence.** Write \(E(N)\) for the weighted returned ordinary-prime endpoint at the \(17/9\) cut. Its price differs from \(\log P(N)\) by \(O(N^3)\), so \(|B(N)-E(N)|\ll N^3(1+\log(2N))^6\). The Dirichlet series of the per-conductor quadratic errors is holomorphic on \(\Re s>3\). Hence the endpoint retains the full residue \(-2m_\rho\,\mathcal M w(\rho)\ne0\) at \(2+2\rho\) for every right zero and the removable singularity at four. Bounding \(E(N)\) from above remains open. **Splitting the quadratic range.** Put \(A(v)=\int_0^v\mathrm{dyadicSpline}(x)\,dx\). The lower and upper prime endpoints have density terms \(\pm n^3 A(K/n^2)\) with \(O(n^{25/9})\) errors uniform in the integer divider \(K\le L\). The coefficient is strictly positive throughout \(e^{-8}<v<4e^8\). At \(K=n^2\), the two Dirichlet series contain \(\pm A(1)\zeta(s-3)\). Their real poles at four cancel exactly. [Paper proof and Lean status](../2-fully-proven/acs-pvc-the-quadratic-product-divider.md)

**Cutting after the conductor sum.** Below its first knot the integrated prime weight is exactly \(cv\), with \(c=\mathcal M w(-1)>0\). Splitting at the terminal cutoff \(K=\lfloor N^{17/9}\rfloor\) produces opposite terms \(\pm cN^{34/9}/2\). On paper, adding \(cK(K+1)/2\) to the high-prime sum recovers \(\log P(N)\) within \(O(N^{289/81})\) at every terminal. Since \(289/81<11/3\), the intermediate target reduces to an upper bound on this explicitly centered high-prime sum. At cubic precision the exact first prime moment keeps the comparison error \(O(N^3)\). [Coefficient and proof status](../2-fully-proven/acs-pvc-the-quadratic-product-divider.md)
