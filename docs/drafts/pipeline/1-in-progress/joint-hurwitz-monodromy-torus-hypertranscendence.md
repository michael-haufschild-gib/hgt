## Constraint

Fix a finite family `(s_j)_{j∈J}` with `Re(s_j)>1`. At every branch point `a=-n`, one positive
loop acts on all singular germs simultaneously by the monodromy vector

\[
 q=(q_j)_{j∈J},\qquad q_j=e^{-2\pi i s_j}.
\]

The native invariant is the Zariski closure of the cyclic subgroup generated by `q` in the
algebraic torus `(ℂ^×)^J`. It is the full torus precisely when the monomials

\[
 q^\alpha=\prod_{j∈J}q_j^{\alpha_j},\qquad \alpha∈\mathbb N^J,
\]

are pairwise distinct; a sufficient intrinsic hypothesis is the `ℚ`-linear independence of
`1` together with the exponents `(s_j)`.

## Candidate theorem A — joint ladder independence

`[CONJECTURE]` Assume `1,(s_j)_{j∈J}` are linearly independent over `ℚ`. For every `N≥0`, the
finite family

\[
 \bigl\{a\mapsto\zeta(s_j+k,a):j∈J,\ 0≤k≤N\bigr\}
\]

is algebraically independent over `ℂ(a)` on the upper half-plane.

Equivalently, the union of all shifted ladders

\[
 \bigl\{\zeta(s_j+k,a):j∈J,\ k≥0\bigr\}
\]

is algebraically independent over `ℂ(a)` in the finite-subfamily sense.

## Candidate theorem B — joint differential hypertranscendence

`[CONJECTURE]` Under the same hypotheses, the differential fields generated by the individual
functions are jointly free over `ℂ(a)`:

\[
 \operatorname{trdeg}_{\mathbb C(a)}
 \mathbb C(a)\bigl(\partial_a^k\zeta(s_j,a):j∈J,k≥0\bigr)=\infty,
\]

and every finite jet family is algebraically independent. The bridge is the invertible value law

\[
 \partial_a^k\zeta(s_j,a)=(-1)^k(s_j)_k\zeta(s_j+k,a).
\]

This is stronger than proving each function hypertranscendental separately: it excludes every
mixed algebraic differential equation coupling distinct irrational exponents.

## Proposed proof mechanism

Let `M=N` and deform independently at the branch points `0,-1,…,-N`:

\[
 F_{j,k}^{c}(a)=\zeta(s_j+k,a)
 +\sum_{n=0}^{N}(c_{j,n}-1)(a+n)^{-s_j-k}.
\]

1. A loop around `-n` sends the whole column `(c_{j,n})_j` to `(q_jc_{j,n})_j` and fixes every
   other column. Therefore a relation for the genuine family holds at

   \[
   c_{j,n}=q_j^{m_n}\qquad(m_n∈\mathbb N).
   \]

2. Monomial injectivity of `α↦q^α` makes the cyclic orbit `(q_j^m)_j` Zariski dense in
   `ℂ^J`. Independent loop counts at the `N+1` branch points make the product orbit Zariski
   dense in `ℂ^{J×(N+1)}`.

3. At fixed `a`, the deformation-to-value map is block diagonal in `j`. Its `j`th block is

   \[
   W^{(j)}_{k,n}=(a+n)^{-s_j-k}
   =(a+n)^{-s_j}\bigl((a+n)^{-1}\bigr)^k,
   \]

   a nonsingular diagonal factor times the same Vandermonde matrix. Hence the joint affine
   deformation orbit maps onto the full value space `ℂ^{J×(N+1)}`.

4. The evaluated relation vanishes on the full value space at every upper-half-plane `a`; all
   polynomial coefficients vanish identically.

5. Apply the all-order derivative value relation blockwise. Each diagonal factor
   `(-1)^k(s_j)_k` is nonzero because `Re(s_j)>1`, so joint ladder independence is equivalent
   to joint finite-jet independence.

## Attack

- **Not a product of the one-exponent theorem.** Separate algebraic independence of each ladder
  does not exclude mixed relations. The cyclic vector orbit and its torus closure are new proof
  data.
- **No hidden independent loops per exponent.** One geometric loop supplies one common exponent
  `m_n`; the proof must establish Zariski density of `m↦(q_j^m)_j`. Treating the `j` coordinates
  as independently variable before that lemma would be invalid.
- **Exact degeneration.** If `s_i-s_j∈ℤ`, the two ladders overlap and joint independence is false.
  More generally, rational relations among `1,(s_j)` shrink the monodromy torus. The hypothesis
  sees the correct obstruction.
- **Rational single-exponent boundary.** For `s∈ℚ\ℤ`, the torus is finite and this proof cannot
  control degrees at or above the denominator. The positive conclusion is that the residual is
  exactly the finite-monodromy invariant ideal, which needs a second invariant.
- **Parent-theory risk.** Zariski density of cyclic torus subgroups and Vandermonde inversion are
  classical. Scientific novelty must rest on the exact fixed-exponent Hurwitz application and the
  mixed differential-independence theorem, not on either ingredient alone.
