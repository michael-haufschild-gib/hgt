## Public sentence

An isolated quantum system can reach its far future cheaply only by returning
to a past it could already reach cheaply.

## What was missing

Recurrence and circuit complexity have been treated as separate observables:
one asks whether a state comes back, the other whether a circuit can prepare
it.  The failed static Krylov-load route shows why that separation is wrong.
A time-independent carrier recognizes that a target belongs to the orbit's
polynomial history, but forgets *where in time* it belongs.  An easy past
state then looks like a permanent future shortcut even when its late overlap
is zero.

The missing primitive is therefore an **oriented circuit--orbit relation**.
It records both preparation cost and lag.  Complexity growth, plateau, and
recurrence are proposed to be three shadows of one statement: after quotienting
by genuine returns, autonomous time is an injective local computation.

## Frozen conjecture

Fix `q>=2`, a finite universal two-qubit gate alphabet, trace-distance
resolution `epsilon`, and the normalized full bounded-weight Gaussian Pauli
ensemble

\[
 H_{n,q}=\sum_{1\leq |P|\leq q}J_PP .                  \tag{1}
\]

Let `Phi_H(t)` be its normalized Choi orbit and let `Lambda(H)` be the explicit
interaction-rate normalization used by the matching product-formula upper
bound.  There are constants `a,A,c>0`, depending only on `q`, gate alphabet,
and fixed resolution, such that with probability at least
`1-exp(-c m)` over `H`, simultaneously for every pre-recurrence time `t` and
every circuit budget `s<=a Lambda(H)t`,

\[
 \boxed{
 C_\epsilon(\Phi_H(t))\leq s
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \exists u\in[0,A s/\Lambda(H)] :
 d_{\rm tr}(\Phi_H(t),\Phi_H(u))\leq 2\epsilon . }
                                                               \tag{ONS}
\]

Equivalently, every intersection between the late autonomous orbit and the
`s`-gate tube is generated by a spectral return to the initial orbit segment
which `s` gates can already reach by ordinary forward simulation.  A short
circuit is never a third mechanism.

The quantifiers in `(ONS)` are part of the conjecture.  `Pre-recurrence` means
the explicit finite-size interval ending at the first `2epsilon` return to the
simulable segment; it is not a hypothesis that assumes absence of shortcuts.

## Immediate consequences if true

1. **Ballistic growth.**  Before the first return to the simulable segment,
   `(ONS)` gives `C_epsilon(Phi_H(t))>a Lambda(H)t`; product-formula simulation
   supplies the matching upper rate up to stated polynomial factors.
2. **Plateau.**  After `a Lambda t` reaches state-space circuit capacity, the
   completed spectral-torus volume law makes returns to every submaximal
   simulable segment quantitatively rare, so complexity is maximal for density
   one.
3. **Recurrence.**  The first post-plateau low-complexity time is exactly the
   first spectral return to an earlier simulable segment.  Existing torus
   return-volume laws then set its doubly exponential scale.
4. **No-fast-forwarding classification.**  A genuine fast-forwarding family
   must create an off-diagonal component of the oriented relation.  Commuting,
   free, and finite-order models are exceptional because they do exactly that.
5. **Complexity without Haar randomness.**  The permanent local two-query
   witness remains valid on the same Hamiltonian at every time; `(ONS)` does
   not identify maximal complexity with Haar statistics.

## Conjecture-distance gate

`(ONS)` changes the meaning of complexity growth: time does not merely
correlate with circuit cost.  Circuit complexity is the injectivity radius of
the oriented autonomous orbit after genuine spectral returns are quotiented
out.  It compresses recurrence, no-fast-forwarding, and plateau into one
relation and makes the risky prediction that there is no third shortcut
mechanism in the irreducible local phase.

It is not “known recurrence plus a stronger anti-concentration assumption.”
The new content is the classification of every adaptive circuit intersection
as a translated return to the simulable past.

## Hostile falsifiers

1. A positive-measure fixed-`q` Gaussian set with late `s`-gate capture but no
   `2epsilon` return to `[0,A s/Lambda]` kills `(ONS)`.
2. A nonlinear noncommuting synthesis carrier of codimension below the proved
   observer-visible rank kills the proposed generic branch.
3. A hidden dependence of `A` on `n,t,s`, or the realized circuit kills the
   law.
4. A proof which first unions targetwise bounds and calls the union
   “recurrence” is circular and does not prove `(ONS)`.
5. A static carrier norm, design statement, Hall-rank theorem, or exact
   complexity result does not retain lag and cannot prove `(ONS)`.
6. Continuous gate alphabets with arbitrary angle as unit cost can violate the
   formulation; the finite alphabet and approximation convention are
   load-bearing and must remain explicit.

## First mathematical target

Define the lag-resolved replicated action of a target `U` by retaining the
complex phase pairing between its scalar-removed diagonal and the spectral
orbit at `(t-u)`, before taking absolute values or a circuit selector.  Prove
a selector-safe decomposition

\[
 \Pr(\text{shortcut at }t)
 \leq
 \Pr\!\left(\inf_{0\leq u\leq As/\Lambda}
   d_{\rm tr}(\Phi_H(t),\Phi_H(u))\leq2\epsilon\right)
 + \mathcal T_{n,q,r}(t,s),                              \tag{2}
\]

where `T` is a positive transverse replica functional which vanishes at the
full growing-replica rate for the ordinary local-Hamiltonian ensemble.
Unlike the retired Krylov load, `T` must be zero on an exact earlier orbit
target after that target has been assigned to the first term.
