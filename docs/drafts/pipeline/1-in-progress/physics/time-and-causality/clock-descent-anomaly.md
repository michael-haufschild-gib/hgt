## Finite theorem

Let `S : V -> V` be a linearized source constraint, let `q : V -> O` be a finite clock
observation, and let `T : O -> O` be a proposed finite shadow. Define the descent anomaly

```text
alpha = T q - q S.                                          (1)
```

Assume every finite observation can be realized by a genuine homogeneous source variation:

```text
for every y in O, there is w in ker(S) with q(w)=y.          (2)
```

Then, for every `y`, a witness `w` as in `(2)` satisfies

```text
alpha(w) = T(y).                                            (3)
```

Consequently:

1. if the comparison square commutes on `ker(S)`, then `T=0`;
2. every value in `range(T)` occurs as a descent anomaly on an actual source
   solution;
3. a nonzero finite formal shadow is not an approximation to an exact descended constraint: its
   entire range is generated by failure of descent.

This strictly strengthens the finite-conormal no-go, which is the scalar-valued special case.

## Forced topology theorem

Finite clock freedom also fixes the topology no-go exactly. Give the raw clock `K^N` its product
topology. If `W subset K^N` realizes arbitrary values on every finite coordinate set, then

```text
closure(W)=K^N.                                           (7)
```

Therefore every continuous linear map from `K^N` to a Hausdorff target which vanishes on `W`
vanishes everywhere. In particular, the raw product topology has no nonzero Hausdorff quotient
that can carry the constraint boundary.

This is a no-go with a strong positive conclusion:

> A nontrivial constrained HT boundary is necessarily a source-selected separation of a quotient

object. Continuation failure, positivity failure, and noncanonical boundary conditions can agree
