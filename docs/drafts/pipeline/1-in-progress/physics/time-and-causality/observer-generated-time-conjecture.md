## Universal representation principle

The conjecture asserts a construction with one universal property.

1. Every history accessible to \(\mathcal O\) extends uniquely to a
   continuous observable on \(\mathbb T_{\mathcal O}(P)\).
2. Those extended histories separate operational points.
3. If another temporal space makes exactly the same complete history family
   continuous, it factors uniquely through
   \(\mathbb T_{\mathcal O}(P)\) in the direction fixed by the universal
   property.
4. Operationally equivalent presentations of process and observer yield
   canonically equivalent temporal spaces.
5. Restriction of observer, probe family, horizon or resolution induces a
   canonical quotient or approximation map.
6. Increasing observational resources produces an inverse/direct system
   whose completion is the exact observer time.
7. Exact recurrence, return distributions and collision laws are invariants
   of the completed pointed dynamical object, including its named history
   functions, distinguished clock orbit and mean structure; they are not
   asserted to follow from the homeomorphism type alone.
8. Open evolution replaces group completion by a semigroup boundary;
   persistent and decaying histories are intrinsic strata of the same
   object.
9. Compatible finite-time process families have a canonical infinite-time
   completion, covering continuous spectra and thermodynamic limits without
   assigning a finite rank where none exists.
10. Observer change induces a comparison span between temporal spaces; the
    common quotient contains exactly shared operational histories.

The law fails if these structures require unrelated constructions or
noncanonical choices.

## Why this is a jump rather than terminology

Standard quantum mechanics places a state trajectory over an external
one-dimensional parameter. The conjecture reverses which object is
fundamental:

- the history algebra comes first;
- physical time is its representing space;
- scalar clock readings are evaluations along one distinguished orbit;
- temporal dimension belongs to the representing space, not to the clock
  parameter;
- observers can have inequivalent temporal spaces for one process; and
- exact eternity is completion of observer histories, not the limit of a
  longer ruler.

The claim is not that a state orbit can be drawn on a torus. The claim is
that a universal representation theorem makes this observer-generated
space the minimal complete carrier of every temporal prediction, and that
the same construction survives open and infinite dynamics.

## Immediate predicted laws

### 1. Observer-dependent temporal dimension

For finite closed dynamics with visible gap module
\(\Gamma_{\mathcal O}\),
\[
  \dim \mathbb T_{\mathcal O}(P)
  =
  \operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}\Gamma_{\mathcal O}.
\]
Different observers can therefore assign different dimensions while
agreeing on their shared history algebra.

### 2. One-dimensional clocks can fill multidimensional time

The image of \(\mathbb R\) under \(\iota_{\mathcal O}\) can be dense in
\(\mathbb T_{\mathcal O}(P)\). One experienced clock direction does not
imply one dimension of completed observer time.

### 3. Eternity is arithmetic and unstable

For finite Hermitian dynamics, every admissible completed temporal
dimension occurs densely under arbitrarily small spectral perturbation,
while every bounded history varies continuously. Thus the dimension of
completed time is nowhere locally inferable from finite-horizon closeness.

### 4. Interaction creates temporal dimension

For generic local many-body dynamics, interaction enlarges the character
algebra and hence the observer time. Arbitrarily weak generic interaction
can change completed temporal dimension from subsystem-linear to
Hilbert-space-exponential while bounded-time histories remain continuous.

### 5. Return is volume

Long-time return statistics are Haar or invariant-measure volumes of
neighborhoods in observer time when the completed object is an equicontinuous
group; collision corrections record failure of maximal character
independence.

### 6. Dissipation changes the kind of time

When histories decay, translation ceases to act invertibly on the completed
object. The peripheral sector is its recurrent group part; transient decay
belongs to a noninvertible semigroup boundary. Open and closed recurrence
are therefore two temporal geometries of one representation law.
