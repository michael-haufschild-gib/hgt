# Proof-blind temporal ontology jumps

Status: `[CONJECTURES FROZEN BEFORE PROOF SEARCH] [TRUTH HUNCHES] [PROOF ROUTES UNKNOWN]`

Purpose: prevent theorem-shaped extrapolation. These conjectures were written before a new
corpus search, literature search, or Lean experiment. Provability is deliberately not used as a
selection criterion. None is presently a manuscript claim.

## The missing primitive

Existing approaches usually begin with a state space, spacetime, causal order, Hamiltonian,
channel, or probability law and ask what an observer can recover. Reverse that order. Begin only
with physically executable comparisons among clocks: which histories can be distinguished, which
calibrations translate one clock into another, and which translations remain compatible around
closed comparison loops.

The wager is that time, space, gravity, horizons, recurrence, and irreversibility are not six
structures. They are six visible faces of one compatibility object.

## Jump A — Temporal equivalence principle

> Clocks do not inhabit spacetime. Spacetime is the unique compatibility object generated when
> every possible clock comparison is glued consistently.

Primitive data are resource-indexed clock histories and their empirically meaningful comparison
maps. A physical spacetime is not an input. It is the representing object, if one exists, of the
complete compatible clock atlas.

This is stronger than relational time. It claims a bidirectional equivalence:

1. a spacetime determines its complete clock atlas up to operational gauge;
2. a complete consistent clock atlas determines a unique spacetime up to geometric gauge.

Risky consequences:

- spacetime dimension is reconstructed, not stipulated;
- causal order is the compatibility domain of clock translations, not prior structure;
- metric scale is invisible to one clock species but recoverable from sufficiently distinct clock
  species;
- two physically distinct spacetimes cannot have the same complete clock atlas;
- failure of clock-atlas representability is a precise obstruction to any single-spacetime
  description.

Fatal tests:

- two inequivalent spacetimes with identical complete clock atlases;
- a consistent clock atlas with two inequivalent geometric representing objects;
- unavoidable nongeometric clock information that cannot be separated as matter fiber data.

## Jump B — Gravity is clock-comparison curvature

> Gravity is the holonomy left after locally consistent clock calibrations are transported around
> a closed comparison loop.

The metric is a local linearization of the clock atlas. Curvature is the obstruction to choosing
one calibration that linearizes all clock comparisons globally. Einsteinian curvature should be
recoverable from an infinitesimal clock-comparison cocycle, while stress-energy should appear as
the obstruction's source or integrability defect.

Risky consequences:

- flatness is equivalent to path-independent clock calibration;
- gravitational redshift and parallel-transport holonomy are two projections of one comparison
  curvature;
- metric reconstruction and curvature reconstruction require finite, explicitly bounded families
  of clock experiments in fixed dimension;
- the contracted Bianchi identity becomes a consistency identity of comparison loops;
- equivalence-principle violation appears as species dependence of the same cocycle.

Fatal tests:

- comparison holonomy fails to determine Riemann curvature even with a complete local clock family;
- vacuum curvature cannot be represented without adding prior geometry;
- cocycle consistency does not force the relevant conservation identity.

## Jump C — Matter is a temporal-gluing defect

> A localized physical excitation is a stable obstruction to extending one compatible clock
> identification through another.

Matter is not asserted to be “made of time.” The sharper claim is structural: after gauge and
geometry are quotiented out, matter degrees of freedom are precisely the residual fibers needed
to complete the clock atlas. Mass, charge, and spin should classify inequivalent types of stable
extension defect.

Risky consequences:

- matter-free geometry is the fiber-trivial stratum of a temporal atlas;
- conserved charges classify obstructions to globally trivializing temporal fibers;
- particle/antiparticle pairing is duality of extension defects, not an analogy with special
  functions;
- species-dependent propagation supplies exactly the information missing from a single clock
  species when reconstructing metric scale.

Fatal tests:

- residual fibers contain clock-reconstructible geometry rather than independent matter;
- known charge sectors do not correspond to stable gluing obstructions;
- the proposal only restates gauge-bundle language without a new invariant or theorem.

## Jump D — Horizons hide time directions

> A horizon does not destroy a history. It transfers distinguishable temporal directions from one
> observer's atlas into the kernel of that observer's comparison map.

For globally reversible dynamics, visible recurrent dimension, irreversible quotient dimension,
and hidden kernel dimension should satisfy an exact conservation law under change of observer.
A black-hole horizon is the gravitational realization of a permanent, geometrically constrained
nonfaithful clock comparison.

Risky consequences:

- information loss is rank transfer, not deletion;
- horizon formation has an exact observer-dimension balance law;
- horizon entropy measures growth of unresolved temporal directions or their boundary capacity;
- evaporation must either return the hidden directions, transfer them to another observer sector,
  or violate global reversibility;
- exact thermality and recoverable fine history occupy sharply different strata of the same atlas.

Fatal tests:

- no observer-independent conserved completion exists;
- kernel growth cannot be related to any accepted black-hole information measure;
- the law depends on finite-dimensional artifacts or on choosing Hilbert--Schmidt normalization.

## Jump E — The arrow of time is failure of cancellability

> Reversible recurrence and irreversible flow are the group and proper-semigroup strata of one
> temporal action; the arrow of time is the observer-relative failure to cancel that action.

Closed-system recurrence, open-system mixing, coarse-graining, and horizon-induced information
loss should admit a single canonical decomposition into recurrent group, decaying semigroup, and
invisible kernel components. Refining an observer changes the allocation but not the completed
temporal object.

Risky consequences:

- recurrence and irreversibility cannot be independent empirical properties;
- every physically admissible arrow has a minimal reversible temporal completion;
- exact recurrence survives precisely on the maximal cancellable component;
- permanent memory loss is exactly equality in the observer kernel, while approximate loss has a
  stability law governed by distance to that kernel;
- the closed/open boundary is a genuine wall crossing of one invariant, not a change of subject.

Fatal tests:

- minimal completion is nonunique in a physically consequential way;
- continuous spectrum or thermodynamic limits destroy the decomposition;
- standard dilation theory already gives the full claim with the same canonical invariants.

## Jump F — The Big Bang is a boundary of observability, not a first event

> “Before time” means change lying in the common kernel of every clock that can exist on the later
> side of a temporal-atlas boundary.

The Big Bang is conjectured to be a rank-changing boundary of internal observability rather than a
distinguished point on a pre-existing external time line. Inflation, if part of this description,
is rapid expansion of the separating power and compatibility domain of the clock atlas.

Risky consequences:

- the phrase “before the Big Bang” has a noncircular operational meaning without external time;
- temporal dimension can be born or change across a cosmological boundary;
- inflationary mode production should be expressible as creation of independently distinguishable
  clock directions;
- a pre-temporal sector can affect later observables only through boundary invariants that survive
  the common clock kernel;
- the framework supplies a sharp criterion distinguishing “no earlier state” from “earlier but
  forever unobservable.”

Fatal tests:

- the common-kernel definition secretly assumes an external evolution parameter with physical
  content;
- no cosmological observable distinguishes the two boundary cases;
- the proposal cannot recover ordinary cosmic time in a semiclassical regime.

## Joined conjecture — Temporal atlas law

The ambitious synthesis is:

> Every physical universe has one completed temporal atlas. Spacetime is its compatibility
> geometry; gravity is its comparison curvature; matter is its residual fiber; recurrence is its
> compact cancellable stratum; irreversibility is its proper-semigroup stratum; and horizons are
> observer-map kernels. Apparent laws governing these subjects separately are shadows of the
> functorial conservation of this atlas under change of observer and scale.

This is an ontological conjecture, not yet a theorem. It earns attention only if it yields all of:

1. a non-definitional reconstruction theorem;
2. a new invariant shared by at least three formerly separate domains;
3. a finite experimental null test with a quantitative stability bound;
4. a result surviving infinite dimension, continuous spectrum, and open dynamics;
5. a black-hole or cosmological consequence not inserted as an assumption;
6. a direct external-priority audit showing that the joined theorem is absent from literature.

If those six obligations cannot be met, the joined conjecture is not the master paper.

## Conjecture protocol

1. Freeze the statement before searching for a proof.
2. Derive consequences that could make the statement false.
3. Search corpus and literature only after the commitments are fixed.
4. Reject any survivor that is merely a change of vocabulary.
5. Formalize only theorem shadows that materially discriminate the ontology.
6. Keep manuscript claims strictly below the boundary of completed unconditional proofs.
