## Standing lessons (update in place)
- **L-BAL (balance law).** Every purely topological count of the zeros of an FE-symmetric entire
  function closes as an identity in which off-line pairs are balanced by something else, so the
  count contains no RH content. This covers degree, winding, Gauss–Bonnet, Poincaré–Hopf,
  Riemann–Hurwitz, Morse counts, X-ray nets and nodal conservation laws. The things that do the
  balancing:
  - extra critical points (Levinson–Montgomery);
  - U-turns of level curves;
  - folds of nodal curves on reflection axes;
  - migration reversals at orbifold corners.

  Reason: inside the class of ι-equivariant maps, two adjacent on-line zeros are equivariantly
  homotopic to an off-line pair through a collision. Topology therefore reads only `N_total` and
  the signed on-line count. This is the corpus orientation/two-torsion gate restated topologically.
- **L-SIG (signature law).** Every known mechanism that forces the zeros onto a line reduces to one
  of four things: a definiteness/signature (Hermite–Sylvester, Hermite–Biehler, Krein, Seifert
  pencils, Lee–Yang interlacing); a uniqueness/flip argument (CCM ground states); IVT plus an exact
  count plus a model with margin (Rankin–Swinnerton-Dyer, GORZ, Ki); or self-adjointness. A
  topological route must therefore compute a signature topologically, the way a Hirzebruch or
  Atiyah–Patodi–Singer index theorem does, or supply an RSD-type model with an exact count.
- **L-DEF (deformation dichotomy).** Families through `ξ` come in two kinds:
  - Isotopies of the zero configuration, for example the Teichmüller flow of `ξ² ds²` or
    qc-conjugations. These keep the RH status constant and carry no new arithmetic.
  - Configuration-changing families: heat flow, a-points, theta powers, Lerch/Hurwitz parameters,
    Epstein lattices, Chowla–Selberg truncations. In these, `ξ` sits on the boundary of the RH
    region (no slack).
- **L-EUL (Euler rigidity).** Holomorphic multi-parameter Euler products continue meromorphically to
  `ℂⁿ` only when they are products of `ζ`/`L` at linear forms (Kurokawa; Bhowmik–Essouabri–Lichtin
  natural-boundary theorem). Their real-part zero amoebas are therefore line arrangements, and
  convexity in several complex variables (Bochner's tube theorem: complement components of the
  real-part zero set are convex) gains nothing.
