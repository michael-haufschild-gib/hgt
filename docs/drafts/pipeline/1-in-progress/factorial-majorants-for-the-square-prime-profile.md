# Factorial majorants for the square prime profile

For the three square conductors the limiting unrounded test in the prime coordinate \(v=p/n^2\) is \(W(v)=v^{-1/2}[2H(\log v)-H(\log(v/4))]\), where \(H\) is the cubic spline autocorrelation. Its integral is zero. On the critical line its Mellin transform at \(\rho=1/2+i\gamma\) is \((2-4^{i\gamma})|\widehat B(\gamma)|^2\). Infinitely many actual critical zeros have \(\widehat B(\gamma)\ne0\).

Factorial comparisons use \(\log k!=\sum_d\Lambda(d)\lfloor k/d\rfloor\). A balanced finite combination of dilated floor kernels is a sum of fractional-part kernels. Its Mellin transform has the factor \(\zeta(s)\), so it vanishes at each such \(\rho\). Thus it cannot coincide with \(W\). A pointwise majorant with the same integral would coincide almost everywhere.

The Mellin separation, fixed square-root lower bound and off-line power obstruction are formalized. [Zeros detected by the square prime weight](../3-formalized/zeros-detected-by-the-square-prime-weight.md)

**Exact factorial interpolation.** At scale \(X\), put \(b(u)=\sum_{\ell\ge1}\mu(\ell)W(\ell u/X)\) for \(u>0\) and \(c_j=b(j)-b(j+1)\). All sums have finite support at each positive argument. Divisor inversion gives \(\sum_{k\ge1}b(ky)=W(y/X)\). The floor sum \(N_X(y)=\sum_j c_j\lfloor j/y\rfloor=\sum_k b(\lceil ky\rceil)\) therefore interpolates every positive integer degree exactly. Its balance is \(\sum j c_j=W(1/X)=0\) at large scales. If \(W'\) is bounded and supported in \([a,b]\subset(0,\infty)\), then \(|b'(u)|\ll X/u^2\) by summing \(\ell/X\) up to \(bX/u\). Consequently \(|N_X(y)-W(y/X)|\ll X/y^2\) for every \(y\ge1\), and its scaled integral over \(y\ge K\) is \(O(1/K)\). The remaining discrepancy lies at small degrees even though it vanishes at every prime. The unresolved bound is the cost of those coefficients against Stirling's remainder and main terms. Lean proves exact interpolation at every positive integer degree, balance at one, and uniqueness when the degree-zero coefficient is fixed. **Zero multiplicity changes the target.** At scale $X$ let $B$ bound both error height and $X$ times error mass. The sharp Mellin bound gives $|X^s(M_W-M_N)(s)|\le B/\Re s$ throughout the strip. At a seen zero $\rho$ of multiplicity $m$, apply Cauchy's derivative estimate of order $m-1$ on a fixed disk about $\rho$. The derivative equals $X^\rho(\partial_s+\log X)^{m-1}M_W(\rho)$, whose polynomial factor has leading coefficient $M_W(\rho)\ne0$. Hence $B\gg X^{\Re\rho}(\log X)^{m-1}$. A square-root budget would force simplicity of all seen critical zeros. Allowing subpower losses tests the zero abscissa without imposing simplicity.

For the canonical interpolant, a Mertens bound \(M(Y)\ll Y^\sigma\), \(0<\sigma<1\), gives \(|b'(u)|\ll X^\sigma/u^{1+\sigma}\) by partial summation against the compact smooth function \(vW'(v)\). Balance bounds the error by \(O(X^\sigma)\) for \(y<1\). Summation over \(k\) bounds it by \(O(X^\sigma/y^{1+\sigma})\) for \(y\ge1\). Hence its scaled \(L^1\) error is \(O(X^{\sigma-1})\). Conversely such paired bounds imply \(\beta\le\sigma\) at every seen zero by taking \(t=1/X\). This predicts an exact zero-abscissa law for interpolation on the integer degrees.

**Integer factorial representations.** The prefix matrix \((\lfloor j/k\rfloor)_{1\le j,k\le L}\) has determinant one over \(\mathbb Z\). Prescribe the floor response \(e(k)\) to equal the ordinary contact valuation at primes and zero elsewhere. Its unique integral coordinates \(c_j\) satisfy \(d_{\rm ord}=\prod_{j\le L}(j!)^{c_j}\) and \(\sum j c_j=e(1)=0\). Two response vectors give the same rational generator exactly when their sums on each chain \(p,p^2,\ldots\le L\) agree. The rows away from prime powers are free. The open arithmetic question is whether a choice of these free rows controls the signed Stirling sum on the diagonal. **Freedom in the Stirling main term.** Write a positive rational as a balanced product of factorials with integer exponents and let its entropy be $S=\sum_j c_jj\log j$. Two identities preserve the rational and its balance:
$$4!\,(1!)^3=3!\,(2!)^2,\qquad 6!\,(1!)^2=5!\,3!.$$
The entropy increments are $\log(16/27)$ and $\log(6^6/(5^5 3^3))$. Their ratio is irrational by the valuation at five and then the valuation at two. Hence at every horizon at least six, the entropies of the representations of any representable rational $q$ are dense on the real line. The infimum of $\max(|S|,|\log q-S|)$ over these representations is exactly $|\log q|/2$. Choosing free rows to control either term alone gives no information about their sum. Every positive rational has an exact zero-entropy realization. This includes the actual ordinary prime generator with all flooring errors. **Nearby critical zeros.** A common square-root budget at all large scales would also constrain clusters of distinct critical zeros. Fix a critical zero of ordinate $t_0$, a radius $R<1/2$ and any finite set of other critical ordinates $t_j$ within $R$. Normalize the residual by $X^{s-1/2}$ and rotate it by $X^{-it_0}$. On the disk about $1/2+it_0$ its modulus is bounded by $C/(1/2-R)$. At each step average residuals over the two log scales $u$ and $u+\pi/|t_j-t_0|$. After all steps the average has the same central value in modulus, $|M_W(1/2+it_0)|$, and vanishes exactly at each selected zero. The scale rotations form independent two-point averages, so no spacing or rational-independence hypothesis is needed. Dividing by the disk Blaschke factors yields
$$
(1/2-R)|M_W(1/2+it_0)|\prod_j \frac{R}{|t_j-t_0|}\le C.
$$
This product criterion constrains the local arrangement of distinct zeros even if every zero is simple. The individual fixed-window obstruction alone gives no contradiction for the actual cubic spline.

For the canonical interpolant the two Stirling contributions can be estimated separately above the zero abscissa. Put $b(u)=\sum_{k\ge1}\mu(k)W(ku)$, so its Mellin transform on $\Re s>1$ is $M_W(s)/\zeta(s)$. A Mertens bound $M(y)\ll y^\sigma$ gives $b(u)=O(u^{-\sigma})$ and $b'(u)=O(u^{-1-\sigma})$ near zero. For $\sigma<1$ the transform is regular near one. The pole of zeta and the vanished mean of $W$ give a double zero, hence $\int b=\int b\log u=0$. Summation by parts expresses the discrete entropy as $\sum_{j\ge1}b(j/X)[j\log j-(j-1)\log(j-1)]$. Comparison with $\int b(u/X)(1+\log u)\,du=0$ costs $O(X^\sigma)$ by the derivative bound, including the interval below one. The coefficient-weighted Stirling remainder is also $O(X^\sigma)$. Thus the same explicit interpolant should attain every power strictly above the zero abscissa, while the Mellin obstruction excludes every smaller power.
