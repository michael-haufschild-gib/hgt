The positive paper survivor retains those exits. Suppose the current head
is `-b<=0` and all nonempty oriented coordinates are nonnegative. For
selected prime q let a be its singleton, and, over **every** nonempty
q-free label, write x,y for parent and partner. Put

`T=sum(max(x-y,0)^2)`, `U=sum(max(y-x,0)^2)`, `C=sum(x*y)`.

Then the complete before/after good and defect energies are

`G=a^2+T+U+2C`, `D=b^2`, `G'=T`, `D'=(a+b)^2+U`.

With `v=sqrt(a^2+T+U+2C)-sqrt(T)>=0`, contraction of the
geometric radius is equivalent to the exact signed comparison

`b*(v-a) >= sqrt(T)*v-C`.

Proof: the radius inequality is
`sqrt((a+b)^2+U)<=b+v`. Both sides are nonnegative, so it is equivalent
to the squared inequality. Substituting
`v^2+2v sqrt(T)=a^2+U+2C` gives the displayed comparison.
This keeps the created nonempty defect U rather than requiring it to vanish.
For U>0 the radius change strictly decreases as b grows; sufficiently
large b allows contraction exactly when `U+2C>2a sqrt(T)`. Indeed the
left radius increment tends to a as b grows, is strictly greater than a
at every finite b, and the other increment is v. At equality no finite
head pays a positive U. These statements are paper algebra; their source
