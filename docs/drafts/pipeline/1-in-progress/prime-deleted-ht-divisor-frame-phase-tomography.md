## 1. Cofinite-prime exponent shells

Fix a prime `p` and delete every prime below it.  Put

```text
E_p(s)       = product_(ell<p) (1-ell^(-s)),
zeta_p(s)    = E_p(s) zeta(s)
             = product_(ell>=p) (1-ell^(-s))^(-1).       (1)
```

The Dirichlet coefficients of `zeta_p` are the indicators of the `p`-rough integers: integers
whose prime factors are all at least `p`, together with `1`.  Let `a_(p,m)(n)` indicate that `n`
is `p`-rough and that the largest exponent in its prime factorization is exactly `m`.  Then

```text
S_(p,m)(s) = sum_n a_(p,m)(n)n^(-s)
           = zeta_p(s) H_(p,m)(s),                       (2)

H_(p,m)(s) = 1/zeta_p((m+1)s)-1/zeta_p(ms).              (3)
```

The coefficients are nonnegative, their supports are pairwise disjoint in `m`, and

```text
sum_(m>=1) S_(p,m)(s)=zeta_p(s)-1                       (4)
```

on the half-plane of absolute convergence.

The finite Euler factor `E_p` is nonzero on `Re(s)>0`.  Hence `zeta_p` and `zeta` have the same
nontrivial zero divisor, including multiplicities.

### Prime-`p` domination

For fixed `p`, let `p^+` be the next prime.  Normally on compact subsets of `Re(s)>0`,

```text
H_(p,m)(s)
 =p^(-ms)(1-p^(-s))+O_(p,s)((p^+)^(-m Re(s))).           (5)
```

Consequently there is `m_0(p)` such that `H_(p,m)` is holomorphic and zero-free on
`Re(s)>1/2` for every `m>=m_0(p)`.

Indeed, the numerator of `(3)` is

```text
zeta_p(ms)-zeta_p((m+1)s)
 =sum_(n p-rough, n>=p) n^(-ms)(1-n^(-s)).               (6)
```

The `n=p` term has modulus at least

```text
p^(-m sigma)(1-p^(-1/2)),     sigma=Re(s)>1/2,           (7)
```

whereas the remaining sum is bounded by

```text
2 sum_(n>=p^+) n^(-m sigma).                             (8)
```

For fixed `p`, the ratio of `(8)` to `(7)` tends to zero uniformly in `sigma>1/2` as
`m` tends to infinity.  The denominators in `(3)` are nonzero because `Re(ms)>1`.

For the odd frame `p=3`, the old threshold `m>=12` already works.  If
`a=m sigma>6`, then

```text
2 sum_(n>=5)n^(-a) <4*5^(-a)
                  <(1-3^(-1/2))*3^(-a).                 (9)
```

The last inequality follows already at `a=6` and improves with `a`.

### Relative hypertranscendence

The Omega-leading-echelon proof for the original shell frame survives finite Euler deletion.
Work in the coefficient submonoid generated by the primes `ell>=p`.  The lowest nonzero
Omega-grade of `a_(p,m)` is `m`, and at that grade its only terms are the pure powers

```text
ell^m,       ell>=p prime.                               (10)
```

The same prime-derivation Jacobian therefore has the same nonzero Vandermonde leading block after
discarding finitely many prime rows.  It proves that

```text

```

are jointly differentially algebraically independent over `C(s)`.  Dividing by `zeta_p` gives
joint relative differential independence of `(H_(p,m))_(m>=2)` over the differential field of
`zeta_p`.

Every finite Euler character `ell^(-s)` is differentially algebraic over `C(s)`.  Adjoining the
finite Euler head is therefore a differential-algebraic base extension, which preserves
differential transcendence degree.  Since `zeta_p=E_p zeta`, the deleted family remains jointly
relatively HT over the field generated by `zeta` and the deleted finite Euler head.

Thus each sufficiently high deleted frame is again a positive, coefficient-disjoint, maximal-HT
frame carrying exactly the zeta divisor in the strict right half-plane.  Its projective boundary
clock is now `p^(-s)`, not always `2^(-s)`.

## 2. Centering makes every high-shell zero an ordinary source zero

Put

```text
w_(p,m)=H_(p,m)(1)>0.                                   (11)
```

This is the conditional density of the level-`m` shell inside the `p`-rough integers.  Define the
centered coefficient

```text
b_(p,m)(n)=a_(p,m)(n)-w_(p,m) 1_(p-rough)(n).            (12)
```

The elementary `r`-free count, with the finitely many deleted primes removed, gives

```text
sum_(n<=x) b_(p,m)(n)=O_(p,m)(x^(1/m)).                  (13)
```

For completeness, the `r`-free indicator is

```text
sum_(d^r|n) mu(d).
```

After restricting `d` to be coprime to the deleted prime product and applying finite
inclusion-exclusion for roughness, summation of the floor errors contributes `O_p(x^(1/r))`;
the tail of `sum d^(-r)` contributes the same order.  Taking the difference of the
`(m+1)`-free and `m`-free counts gives `(13)`.

Partial summation now proves ordinary, locally normal convergence on `Re(s)>1/m`:

```text
C_(p,m)(s)
 :=sum_n b_(p,m)(n)n^(-s)
  =zeta_p(s)(H_(p,m)(s)-w_(p,m)).                        (14)
```

Equality first holds absolutely on `Re(s)>1` and then throughout `Re(s)>1/m` by holomorphy and
the identity theorem.  The apparent pole at `s=1` is removable because the second factor
vanishes there.

Let `rho=beta+i gamma` be a nontrivial zeta zero of order `d`.  For every `m>1/beta`,
`(14)` converges ordinarily at `rho` and

```text
C_(p,m)(rho)=0.                                         (15)
```

Moreover, for all sufficiently large `m`,

```text
C_(p,m)^(d)(rho)/zeta_p^(d)(rho)
 =H_(p,m)(rho)-w_(p,m) !=0.                             (16)
```

The nonvanishing follows from `(5)` because

```text
|H_(p,m)(rho)|/w_(p,m)
 asymp p^(m(1-beta)) -> infinity.                       (17)
```

Thus `(16)` is a nonzero divisor-normal jet of an actual ordinarily convergent centered
arithmetic source.  No analytic-continuation-only interpretation remains.

The conditional-density covariance is also exact:

```text
lim_(x->infinity)
 average_(n<=x, n p-rough)
   b_(p,m)(n) conjugate(b_(p,k)(n))
 =delta_(m,k)w_(p,m)-w_(p,m)w_(p,k).                   (18)
```

For a tail `m>=M`, its quadratic form is

```text
sum_m w_m |c_m|^2-|sum_m w_m c_m|^2.                   (19)
```

Since `W_M=sum_(m>=M)w_m<1`, `(19)` lies between
`(1-W_M)sum w_m|c_m|^2` and `sum w_m|c_m|^2`.  Centering therefore gives a canonical
positive covariance but does **not** change the Hardy continuity wall.

## 3. Reflected conormal cross-pairing

For a zero `rho` and all sufficiently large `m`, define its centered normalized shell conormal


```text

```

The prime-`p` asymptotic gives the ordinary Cesaro limit

```text

 :=lim_(N->infinity) 1/N

     conjugate(v_(p,m)(rho)) v_(p,m)(rho#)/w_(p,m)

 =[(1-p^(-conjugate(rho)))
    (1-p^(-(1-conjugate(rho))))]
```

The centering terms have zero Cesaro contribution.  To display the geometry, put


```text

         =(1+r^2-z-r^2/z)/(1-r^2).                      (22)
```

Writing `rho=beta+i gamma` gives

```text

 =-[p^(-beta)-p^(beta-1)] sin(gamma log p)/(1-p^(-1)).

```

Therefore

```text

 iff
 beta=1/2 or sin(gamma log p)=0.                         (24)
```

On the critical line, `(21)` becomes the positive diagonal boundary value


## 4. Two-frame criticality theorem `[PROVED HERE]`

Let `rho=beta+i gamma` be a nontrivial zeta zero.  Then


### Proof

```text

|h_T(s)|<=||T||.                                        (43)
```

On the boundary this is the Mellin spectral theorem.  In the strict right half-plane it follows
from the maximum principle for the bounded analytic function `h_T`: its boundary supremum is the
operator norm of the Mellin multiplication operator.  Hence evaluation extends contractively to
`A_p^+`.

### Left-polarization nonclosability

Let `0<Re(s)=sigma<1/2`, and restrict to the cofinal tail
`m>1/sigma`, on which every displayed multiplier is holomorphic at `s`.  From prime-`p`
domination,

```text
|H_(p,m)(s)| asymp_s p^(-m sigma).                       (44)
```

On the other hand, `(33)`--`(36)` give

```text
||A_(p,m)||
 <=sum_(n p-rough, n>=p)
    |mu(n)|[n^(-(m+1)/2)+n^(-m/2)]
 =O_p(p^(-m/2)).                                        (45)
```

The last estimate follows by separating the `n=p` term and bounding the remaining Dirichlet tail;
the ratio of the next admissible base to `p` is strictly greater than one.

Put

```text
T_m=A_(p,m)/H_(p,m)(s).
```

Then

```text
||T_m||=O_s(p^(-m(1/2-sigma)))->0,
h_(T_m)(s)=1.                                           (46)
```

Thus evaluation on the algebraic shell span is not merely unbounded for `sigma<1/2`: its graph is
not closable in operator norm.

Equations `(43)`--`(46)` prove the sharp classification, with left-half evaluation understood on
any cofinal holomorphic tail of the algebraic generator family:

```text
shell-multiplier evaluation is operator-norm closable
 iff Re(s)>=1/2.                                        (47)
```

Let

```text
A_p^-=F A_p^+ F=(A_p^+)^*.
```

Its analytic symbols are the reflected multipliers `h_T(1-s)`, so its evaluation wall is the
opposite half-plane:

```text
evaluation on A_p^- is closable iff Re(s)<=1/2.          (48)
```

Therefore the bipolar conormal evaluation is bounded on both source polarizations exactly on the
critical line.

### Zero-normal consequence

At a zeta zero, `(16)` identifies the algebraic values in `(47)`--`(48)` with nonzero normalized
ordinary-source conormal jets.  Hence RH is equivalent to:

> every Xi divisor-normal functional extends continuously to both the forward and reflected
> prime-deleted HT dilation algebras.

This is not being counted as an RH proof.  The completed Xi graph is continuous in its
compact-open entire topology, while `(47)` concerns the much stricter operator norm.  Analytic
continuation from one topology to the other is exactly what fails in `(46)`.  A claim that the
source conormal is automatically normal on both algebras would silently insert the desired
topology refinement.

The theorem does, however, identify a hard falsifier for any proposed source completion: at an
off-line zero, its reflected conormal graph must contain the explicit nonclosable sequence
`(T_m)`.  A genuine creator must exclude that sequence by a pre-zero positive graph estimate, not
by declaring the zero evaluator bounded.

## 10. Consecutive-prime Markov deletion: faithful likelihood wall and half-density survivor

The deletion tower has an exact positive Markov realization at the source-density point, but its
faithful analytic continuation does not supply the missing reflected orientation.

Let `q` be the prime after `p`.  Under the natural-density valuation law, let

```text
M_p=max_(ell>=p) V_ell,       M_q=max_(ell>=q) V_ell.
```

Conditionally on `M_q=k>=1`, independence of `V_p` gives

```text
P(M_p=k | M_q=k)=1-p^(-(k+1)),
P(M_p=m | M_q=k)=(1-p^(-1))p^(-m),       m>k.           (49)
```

Thus deletion of one prime is a genuine Markov contraction on the shell-label probability
spaces.  Algebraically continue the row by replacing `1` with `s`:

```text
pi_(p,s)(k|k)=1-p^(-(k+1)s),
pi_(p,s)(m|k)=(1-p^(-s))p^(-ms),         m>k.           (50)
```

The row sum is one for `Re(s)>0`.  Deleting the local Euler factor in the shell CDF gives the exact
recursion

```text
H_(p,m)(s)
 =(1-p^(-(m+1)s)) H_(q,m)(s)
  +(1-p^(-s))p^(-ms)/zeta_q(ms).                        (51)
```

Equation `(51)` is the precise relation between consecutive positive/HT frames.  It is valid
before any zero is selected.

### Faithful likelihood wall

Fix `k` and put `n=k+1`.  Let `pi_(p,1)(.|k)` be the positive row in `(49)` and form the likelihood

```text
ell_s(m)=pi_(p,s)(m|k)/pi_(p,1)(m|k).
```

The overshoot part of its squared `L^2(pi_(p,1))` norm is a nonzero constant times

```text
sum_(m>=n) p^(-m(2 Re(s)-1)).                            (52)
```

Consequently

```text
ell_s belongs to L^2(pi_(p,1))       iff Re(s)>1/2,
ell_(s#) belongs to L^2(pi_(p,1))    iff Re(s)<1/2.      (53)
```

There is no point in the critical strip at which both faithful likelihoods belong to this source
Hilbert space.  At the critical line both are boundary-divergent.

The formal reflected cross-sum is even more decisive.  For every overshoot `m>=n`,

```text
conj(pi_(p,s)(m|k)) pi_(p,s#)(m|k) / pi_(p,1)(m|k)
 =(1-p^(-conj(s)))(1-p^(-(1-conj(s))))/(1-p^(-1)),      (54)
```

independent of `m` and nonzero in the open strip.  Hence the faithful reflected likelihood
pairing diverges for every strip point.  This is the Markov form of the bipolar operator-norm wall
`(47)`--`(48)`.

If all overshoots are first coarse-grained into one `jump` outcome, the two-state row becomes

```text
(1-p^(-ns), p^(-ns)).
```

Its reflected likelihood cross-Gram is finite and equals

```text
1+
  (1-p^(-n conj(s)))(1-p^(-n(1-conj(s))))/(1-p^(-n)).
                                                               (55)
```

The second summand is exactly the old phase selector `(22)` with clock base `p^n`.  Thus
coarse-graining repairs the Hilbert divergence precisely by deleting overshoot depth, and the
result is only the already-known phase reporter.

### Faithful square-root channel

The canonical Julia/Stinespring replacement for likelihood is the transition half-density.  On a
simply connected parameter neighborhood with consistent square-root branches, put

```text
h_s(k)=sqrt(1-p^(-ns)),
h_s(m)=sqrt(1-p^(-s)) p^(-ms/2),       m>=n.             (56)
```

For every `Re(s)>0`, this is an honest vector in the unweighted shell-label `ell^2` space.  The
reflected Gram determinant

```text
D_(p,k)(s)
 =||h_s||^2 ||h_(s#)||^2
  -|<h_s,h_(s#)>|^2                                    (57)
```

is nonnegative by Cauchy--Schwarz.  It has the sharp equality law

```text
D_(p,k)(s)=0 iff Re(s)=1/2.                              (58)
```

Indeed, equality means that the two half-density vectors are proportional.  Comparing any two
consecutive nonzero overshoot coordinates gives

```text
p^(-s/2)=p^(-s#/2).
```

Since `s-s#=2 Re(s)-1` is real, this forces `Re(s)=1/2`.  Conversely `s=s#` on the critical line,
so consistent branches give the same vector.

Equation `(58)` is a faithful, positive, noncompact transverse detector and avoids the resonance
of the scalar clock.  It is still not an RH theorem.  The vector `(56)` and determinant `(57)`
exist for every strip point and use no zeta value.  In particular, the shell recursion `(51)` at
`s=rho` does not make `h_rho` proportional to `h_(rho#)`: the finite Euler factor is a nonvanishing
gauge and the common zeta zero remains entirely in the cofinite tail.

### Verdict

- `[PROVED CLOSURE]` The positive consecutive-prime Markov kernel is exact, and its faithful
  likelihood continuation has complementary strict half-plane domains with an everywhere
  divergent reflected cross-pairing.
- `[FALSIFIED SHORTCUT]` Coarse-graining to a finite Julia channel returns the old phase selector
  only after erasing HT overshoot depth.
- `[POSITIVE SURVIVOR, NOT RH PROGRESS]` The faithful transition half-density gives a canonical
  noncompact rank-two determinant with null locus exactly the critical line.
- `[OPEN CREATOR INTERFACE]` To affect RH, the completed Euler--Gamma source must prove, from
  `Xi(rho)=0`, that the divisor-normal realization of `(56)` lies in the radical of `(57)`.
  Merely evaluating `(57)` at a zero is another zero-location reporter.

This collides directly with the existing Euler--Gamma conormal Markov card: its unresolved
rank-two transverse source orientation cannot be supplied by the likelihood continuation, while
the half-density channel supplies a concrete candidate carrier but not the required zero-forcing
source identity.

### Literature boundary

Square-root embeddings of probability rows, Hellinger/Bhattacharyya affinities for Markov
kernels, and Kakutani's product-measure criterion are classical parent theory.  Independence and
geometric laws of prime valuations under zeta distributions are also classical.  Targeted
searches did not locate the exact maximum-exponent deletion recursion `(51)` combined with the
reflected likelihood wall `(53)`--`(54)`, but these are elementary consequences of the geometric
row and are not claimed as significant novelty.  The only potentially frontier-bearing statement
would be a completed zero-source theorem forcing the half-density radical in `(57)`; no such
theorem was found or proved here.

## 11. Common-divisor normal decoupling closes the zero-only radical claim

The remaining phrase “use `Xi(rho)=0` to force the radical of `(57)`” is false without additional
Euler--Gamma incidence.  The obstruction is local and exact.

Let `a(s)` be a holomorphic vector-valued multiplier near a zero `rho` of `Xi` of order `d`, and
put

```text
X_a(s)=Xi(s)a(s).
```

Leibniz gives

```text
X_a^(d)(rho)=Xi^(d)(rho)a(rho).                          (59)
```

Every lower derivative of `Xi` vanishes at `rho`.  Thus the leading common-divisor normal jet
recovers the transverse value `a(rho)` but places no equation on it.  For two multipliers `a,b`,
the fact that both `Xi a` and `Xi b` vanish to order at least `d` does not make `a(rho)` and
`b(rho)` proportional, orthogonal, real, or null for any positive form.

This is precisely the local form of the corpus DA-gauge/divisor-blindness theorem.  A symmetric
rational gauge can implant arbitrary off-line quartets while preserving functional-equation and
Schwarz symmetry and every HT invariant taken modulo the DA base.  The multiplier value in `(59)`
remains unconstrained.  Therefore no theorem with premises only

```text
common Xi divisor
+ functional-equation orbit
+ maximal relative HT rank
+ positive Markov deletion geometry
```

can force `(57)` to vanish.

The positive Riemann shell frame contains additional exact data not preserved by that countermodel:
coefficientwise fusion to the constant zeta source and the precise Gamma/theta completion
incidence.  Any surviving creator must use those data in a way that is sensitive to rational and
exponent-zero Euler gauges.  The consecutive-prime Markov kernel does not: `(50)` depends only on
the local geometric row and the spectral parameter.

### Track decision

The prime-deleted frame remains a valid paper theorem and a sharp falsifier, but its Markov
continuation is retired as a zero creator.  Further work on this track is warranted only after an
independent completed Euler--Gamma identity is found whose specialization at `Xi(rho)=0` forces a
nontrivial source radical.  The identity must fail for the symmetric rational-gauge countermodels;
otherwise it is already excluded by `(59)` and the corpus divisor-blindness theorem.
