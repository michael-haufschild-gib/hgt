# Opposite degrees in a section algebra

**Candidate obstruction.** Ordinary dimensions of rational section spaces with signed additive degree cannot give the spline Tate form. If a degree and its negative both carry nonzero sections, their product is constant, so each space is a line. The reversible degrees should form a subgroup. A fixed twist gives a more general test: products of opposite sections land in one finite-dimensional space. Multiplication by a nonzero opposite section then bounds the smaller source dimension.

**Survivor to verify.** The minimum of the two quadratic ray limits is zero whenever the minimum of the two dimensions is bounded. This permits one-sided big divisors. The even spline form would have to vanish identically, contradicting its nonintegrality.
