# The positive inverse of the two-pole filter

For the integer atom $g=(2-5T+2T^2)B$ at conductors 1, 4 and 16, the negative index on integer translates counts all distinct right zeros. [Two-pole-free spline](../3-formalized/two-pole-free-spline-with-full-negative-index.md)

The finite arithmetic problem is the sign of the regularized archimedean pairing minus the prime contacts on integer translates of \(g\). The fixed atom has total variation nine. Uniform comparison for pairs of its translates is the narrower target.

Multiplication by \(1-T\) also annihilates the mean. The resulting four coefficients are \(2,-7,7,-2\). Its extra multiplier zeros are real spectral parameters, so all off-line zeros remain visible. For its autocorrelation \(H\), the prime-square contribution in a remote window has expected limit \(\tfrac12\int H=0\); higher powers decay. This would recover the signed trace from an ordinary-prime sum with error tending to zero.

**A positive inverse.** On the log-four lattice let $L=5-2(T+T^{-1})=(2-T)(2-T^{-1})$. The atom is $-TLB$, so its translated self-pairing is $L^2$ applied to the original trace. If $(2-T)f\ge0$, then $f(j)\ge2^{-k}f(j+k)$ for every $k$. The right side tends to zero when $|f(j)|=O(r^{|j|})$ with $r<2$. The backwards factor has the same property, and each factor preserves this growth. Applying the four inverses preserves any constant lower bound for $L^2f$. The actual width gives $r=4^w<2$. Thus a common lower bound for the two-pole-free atom's pairings at rational conductor ratios would pass to the original trace with the same constant. All ratios involved are realized by pairs of integer conductor translates.
