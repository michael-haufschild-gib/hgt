# The tame kernel of a number field is torsion

- **ID:** `garland-tame-kernel-torsion`
- **Stage:** 1-in-progress
- **Type:** THEOREM (paper, classical) — formalization programme
- **Blocks:** `deninger-algebraic-cyclotomic-milnor-amplitude` (its `(1)`)
- **Statement:** For a number field `K` with ring of integers `𝒪_K`, the tame kernel
  `K_2(𝒪_K) = ⋂_v ker ∂_v ⊆ K_2^M(K)` is torsion; equivalently `K_2^M(K) ⊗ ℚ = 0`.

## Why this card exists

`deninger-algebraic-cyclotomic-milnor-amplitude` imports this through Gallauer's Remark 6.8 →
Borel.  Everything on the near side of it is already unconditional in Lean
(`milnorKDegTwo_torsion_of_tameKernel_numberField`,
`deningerMilnorRealVanishing_of_tameKernelTorsion`), so this is the single remaining input.

## Established constraints (do not re-attempt)

- `ℚ̄` is no shortcut: `K_2^M(K)` torsion for all number fields `K` ⟺ `K_2^M(ℚ̄) = 0`, via
  `N ∘ res = [L:K]`.  Suslin's unique divisibility for algebraically closed fields does not give
  vanishing.
- The elementary mechanism "Steinberg relations among `S`-units span `Λ²(𝒪_S^×) ⊗ ℚ`" is **false**
  (unit rank ≥ 2 with no exceptional units).  Consequently "the tame kernel is generated by symbols
  of units" is also false.
- There is no transfer-based reduction of a general number field to `ℚ`.

## Decomposition

1. **Tate's theorem for `ℚ`** — `K_2^M(ℚ) ≅ {±1} ⊕ ⊕_p 𝔽_p^×` (Milnor, *Introduction to Algebraic
   K-Theory*, §11.6).  Elementary: a Euclidean-algorithm induction on the numerators.  Yields the
   case `K = ℚ` outright.  **First target.**
2. **Weight-two Gersten/localization for a Dedekind domain** — exactness of
   `K_2^M(K) →^∂ ⊕_v κ(v)^× → 0`, i.e. surjectivity of the total tame symbol.  Needs the
   approximation argument; partially served by the residue machinery already built
   (`MilnorKResidue*`).
3. **Finite generation** — Quillen's theorem that `K_n(𝒪_K)` is finitely generated, or Garland's
   direct finiteness argument for `K_2(𝒪_K)`.  This is the large half.
4. **Rank vanishing** — Borel's computation, `rank K_2(𝒪_K) = 0`.
5. Alternative route to keep in view: Moore's theorem plus class field theory (needs CFT, which
   mathlib also lacks).

### Ruled out this round

A literature sweep (arXiv and journal surveys, August 2026) found no proof of "the tame kernel of a
number field is torsion" that avoids Garland's finiteness theorem, Quillen finite generation with
Borel's rank computation, or Soulé's vanishing with Quillen–Lichtenbaum.  In particular:

- Bass–Milnor–Serre does **not** give it: the congruence kernel is a subgroup of `K_2(𝒪_S)` whose
  quotient injects into `∏_v K_2(𝒪_v)`, which carries no finiteness.
- Moore's theorem reduces the statement to "the wild kernel is torsion", which is the same problem.
- Transfer/Galois descent reduces the statement to a cofinal family of number fields, e.g. to
  `K_2^M(\bar{ℚ}) ⊗ ℚ = 0` alone, or to `K_3^M(ℚ(t)) ⊗ ℚ = 0` alone via Milnor's exact sequence for
  a rational function field — equivalent reformulations, not simplifications.
- "`K_2(𝒪_S)` is generated by symbols of `S`-units" is false in the strong form needed, and even if
  it held, the Steinberg relations among `S`-units do not span `Λ²(𝒪_S^×) ⊗ ℚ`.

## The next brick, decomposed: Milnor's exact sequence for `F(t)`

The residue maps needed for it already exist: `Polynomial F` is a principal ideal domain, hence a
Dedekind domain, `RatFunc F` is its fraction field, and `residueDataAtPlace (RatFunc F) v` supplies
the residue at every monic irreducible.  What is missing is exactness of

```
0 → K_n^M(F) → K_n^M(F(t)) --(⊕ ∂_π)--> ⊕_{π monic irreducible} K_{n-1}^M(F[t]/π) → 0.
```

Decomposition:

1. **The residue field at `π`.**  Identify `ResidueField ((v π).valuation (RatFunc F)).valuationSubring`
   with `F[t]/(π)`.  The chain is the one already used for number fields in
   `MilnorKTameResidueFinite.lean`: `valuationSubringAtPrime_eq_valuationSubring`, `IsLocalization`,
   `IsLocalization.algEquiv`, `RingEquiv.subringCongr`, `IsLocalRing.ResidueField.mapEquiv`.
2. **Split injectivity of `K_n^M(F) → K_n^M(F(t))`.**  The specialization at the place `(t)` is a
   retraction: `milnorSpecialization` at that place sends a symbol of constants to itself, because a
   constant is a unit of valuation zero and its residue is itself
   (`milnorSpecialization_of_units`).
3. **The degree filtration.**  `L_d ⊆ K_n^M(F(t))` generated by the classes of symbols whose entries
   are polynomials of degree `≤ d`.  Then `L_0 = image of K_n^M(F)`, `⋃_d L_d = K_n^M(F(t))`, and
   `∂_π(L_d) = 0` for `deg π > d`.
4. **The graded pieces.**  `⊕_{deg π = d} ∂_π : L_d/L_{d-1} ≅ ⊕_{deg π = d} K_{n-1}^M(F[t]/π)`.
   Surjectivity is division with remainder; injectivity is the construction of a section from the
   specialization maps.
5. **Consequence: the Bass–Tate–Kato norm.**  For a finite simple extension `E = F(a)` the norm
   `N_{E/F} : K_n^M(E) → K_n^M(F)` is `-∂_∞ ∘ (section at the minimal polynomial)`; independence of
   the choice of generator is Kato's argument.  The projection formula `N ∘ res = [E:F]` then gives
   the reduction of the card's statement to any cofinal family of number fields.

## Route audit — 2026-08-18

The `garland-formalization-programme` card's chosen route (Garland's `p`-adic curvature method) has
been audited against the literature and **does not close for this target**.  Garland's 1971
`K₂`-finiteness theorem is an archimedean argument (Riemannian geometry and harmonic forms, giving
`H²(SL_n(𝔬), ℝ) = 0` for `n > 6`); his 1973 building/spectral-gap theorem applies to **cocompact**
lattices in `p`-adic groups, and `SL_n(𝒪_S)` is non-uniform (an `S`-arithmetic lattice is uniform iff
the group is anisotropic, and `SL_n` is isotropic over every number field).  The cell stabilizers in
the Bruhat–Tits building are infinite arithmetic groups, so the equivariant descent is an isotropy
spectral sequence whose low rows are the very cohomology one wants to kill.

Consequence for the decomposition above: items 3 and 4 (finite generation, rank vanishing) stay as
they are, and there is no combinatorial shortcut past them.  Items 1 and 2 are unaffected — item 1
(Tate for `ℚ`) is **done**.  The route-independent work that remains useful is Matsumoto's theorem,
Milnor's exact sequence for `F(t)` with the Bass–Tate–Kato norm, and the localization identifying the
Milnor tame kernel of `𝒪_S` with `K₂(𝒪_S)`.

## Landed 2026-08-18 — the residue field at a finite place, named and compatible

`formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/DedekindResidueFieldAtPlace.lean` (wired into the
`HTGeometry` umbrella; `lake env lean` and module build clean; axiom audit for all declarations is
`propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound`).

The tree had this chain inlined once, inside the proof that the residue fields of a number ring are
finite.  It is now a named isomorphism **with its compatibility**, which is what any computation with
the residue or the specialization of Milnor `K`-theory at a finite place needs — in particular the
localization step that the decomposition above still owes.

```text

placeResidueChain                   κ(v.asIdeal) ≅ ResidueField (valuation subring at v)
residueFieldAtPlaceEquiv            ** κ(v) ≅ R ⧸ v.asIdeal **
algebraMap_mem_valuationSubringAtPlace, placeIntegerElt
residueFieldAtPlaceEquiv_residue    ** it sends the residue of r ∈ R to r mod v **
residue_placeIntegerElt_surjective  ** every residue class at a finite place comes from R **
```

The compatibility is the point: without it the isomorphism is inert.  It is proved by pushing
`algebraMap R κ(v.asIdeal) r` through the chain — `IsScalarTower` into the localization,
`ResidueField.map_residue` twice, `AlgEquiv.commutes` for the localization comparison, and
`Subtype.ext` for the subring congruence, which is the identity on underlying elements.
