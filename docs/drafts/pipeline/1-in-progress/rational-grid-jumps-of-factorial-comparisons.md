# Which quotient classes can factorials represent?

Let a right-continuous step function have period one, vanish at zero and jump only at positive multiples of $1/q$. Write $J(n)$ for its jump at $n/q$. Is it a finite real linear combination of $\lfloor\alpha t\rfloor$ with positive real slopes precisely when $J(n)$ depends only on $\gcd(n,q)$?

At a rational point, irrational slopes have no jumps. A rational slope contributes $\mathbf1_{d\mid n}$, where $d$ is the denominator of $\alpha/q$ in lowest terms. In a finite expansion $J(n)=\sum_d a_d\mathbf1_{d\mid n}$, a maximal support index $d$ has a nonzero Fourier coefficient at the primitive frequency $1/d$. Its value over a common period $P$ is $P a_d/d$. Periodicity forces $d\mid q$. Removing this term preserves periodicity, so every support index divides $q$. The corpus proves this period classification for divisor sieves and explicitly computes their projection onto a period. [Exact translation periods](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/BezoutValuationIncidence/GcdOrbitProjectionAndExactTranslationPeriods.lean) Conversely, divisor Möbius inversion recovers the coefficients from $J(g)$ for $g\mid q$. Summing the jumps recovers the floors $\lfloor qt/d\rfloor$. Zero total jump imposes the single relation $\sum_{d\mid q}a_d/d=0$.

The last quotient class has unequal jumps at $n\equiv-1,+1\pmod q$ for $q\ge3$. Any fixed comparison consequently differs on an open interval of $t$. At every sufficiently large base $N$, primes with $N/p$ in a closed subinterval have positive linear logarithmic mass by the prime number theorem. For integer $p\ge1$, $\lfloor\lfloor\alpha N\rfloor/p\rfloor=\lfloor\alpha N/p\rfloor$ exactly. For a comparison majorizing each selected Mangoldt level the excess has one sign, so the ordinary-prime interval alone gives a linear excess at every large base. For representable jumps the symbol factors as $\zeta(s)\sum_{d\mid q}a_d d^{-s}$, explaining the common zero at every right zero of $\zeta$.
