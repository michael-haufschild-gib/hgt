## Parent structure: ternary rings of operators

`[LITERATURE / REDISCOVERED PARENT]`

Let \(X\subset B(H_{\rm in},H_{\rm out})\) be a norm-closed operator space satisfying

\[
XX^\dagger X\subseteq X.                                      \tag{TR1}
\]

Such an \(X\) is a ternary ring of operators (TRO). It canonically generates

\[
\mathcal A_X=\overline{\operatorname{span}}(X^\dagger X),
\qquad
\mathcal B_X=\overline{\operatorname{span}}(XX^\dagger),       \tag{TR2}
\]

and the linking algebra

\[
\mathcal L_X=
\begin{pmatrix}
\mathcal B_X & X\\
X^\dagger & \mathcal A_X
\end{pmatrix}.                                                 \tag{TR3}
\]

Under fullness hypotheses, \(X\) is an imprimitivity bimodule implementing strong Morita
equivalence between \(\mathcal A_X\) and \(\mathcal B_X\).

This is established operator algebra:

- G. K. Eleftherakis, *TRO equivalent algebras*,
  [arXiv:math/0607488](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0607488);
- G. K. Eleftherakis and E. T. A. Kakariadis, *Strong Morita equivalence of operator spaces*,
  [arXiv:1603.09609](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.09609);
- F. Abadie and D. Ferraro, *Applications of ternary rings to \(C^*\)-algebras*,
  [arXiv:1612.08476](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.08476);
- M. Neal and B. Russo, *Operator space characterizations of \(C^*\)-algebras and ternary
  rings*, [arXiv:math/0201189](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0201189).

Therefore `(TR1)`--`(TR3)` are not discoveries.
