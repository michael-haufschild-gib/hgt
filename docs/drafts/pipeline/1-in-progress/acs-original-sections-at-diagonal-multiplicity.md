# Original sections at diagonal multiplicity

For $D_n=e_1+2e_{n^2}-e_{(2n+1)^2}$, does the full section lattice at $m=n$ contain $\exp(cn^2)$ sections on arbitrarily late conductors? On a cofinal set the signed Weil degree $Q(D_n)$ is nonnegative. At every sufficiently large conductor the actual ordinary floors save at least $\delta n^2$. [Cofinal degrees](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/CountingHolography/Sections/DoublingOnThreeFixedMultiplicities.lean) · [Diagonal floors](../3-formalized/acs-prime-floor-loss-on-the-diagonal.md)

It suffices to show that the radius minus the full log generator, after removal of the ordinary floor loss and division by $n^2$, differs from $Q(D_n)$ by $o(1)$. For every multiplicity $m$, Lean bounds the discrepancy between the full log generator plus ordinary floor loss and the full integrated prime contact by $O(n)$. On adding the sampled poles, the full section surplus differs from $V(mD_n)+J(mD_n)-m^2P_*(D_n)$ by $O(n+m)$, where $J$ is the original jet radius. Both estimates hold on every conductor. [Full ideal and original radius](../3-formalized/acs-the-full-prime-ideal-at-diagonal-scale.md)

For any conductor and resolution sequences tending to infinity, the unscaled three-square jet tends to one fixed constant. The two upper spline atoms have relative dilation tending to four. Their fixed-window limit and the decay of their interaction with the low atom give the common limit. The remaining step is to compare it with the fixed-conductor Tate limit.
