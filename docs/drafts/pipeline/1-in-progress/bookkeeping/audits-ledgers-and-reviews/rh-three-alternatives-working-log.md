## Follow-up: three common obstacles

User asks which three mathematical obstacles current routes and all possible routes share. Synthesis from the current primitive gates and cross-route lessons:

1. **Selective arithmetic input (`ORD + ORIENT`, with `DYN` in physical routes):** derive a law from the actual source that excludes off-line zeros. An exact RH-equivalent criterion is not a proof of its premise. Functional-equation reflection pairs displacements `d` and `-d`; it does not force `d=0`.
2. **Complete global coverage (`COF`):** exclude every possible exceptional zero, including sparse and escaping configurations. For example, an off-line count `o(T)` still permits finitely many off-line zeros. Fixed-degree or fixed-cutoff convergence does not justify a joint limit with an escaping degree/cutoff. Required uniformity depends on the argument; no claim that every parameter must share one constant.
3. **Faithful transfer (`LAND + COMP`):** retain enough actual zeta information through any representation, quotient, regularization or limit to make the final conclusion apply to the original function. Cancellation of two opposite reports is not annihilation of either report. Conversely, exact recovery can preserve an obstruction without proving that it vanishes.

These are a synthesis of recurring current-route failures, not a theorem that every possible RH proof must meet the same technical barriers or get stuck. Universally, a proof must establish its restrictive premises, cover every nontrivial zero, and justify its implications. It need not use a reflection-odd observable, a cofinal approximation, positivity, rigidity, spectra, or singular completion. STATUS explicitly rejects blanket necessity claims outside proved premises. No new mathematics or formalization claimed for this synthesis.
