# Sharp autonomous-ADE frequency rank

- **ID:** `sharp-autonomous-ade-frequency-rank`
- **Stage:** 1-in-progress
- **Type:** CONJECTURE / CROSS-OPERATOR TRANSDUCTION
- **Scientific status:** `[CONJECTURE FOR n>=2] [PROVED FOR n=1] [HOSTILE AUDIT IN PROGRESS]`
- **Statement:** If a nonconstant holomorphic uniformly almost-periodic function has minimal
  autonomous algebraic-differential order `n`, then its rational Bohr-frequency rank is at most
  `n`.

## Exact conjecture

Let `f` be holomorphic on a horizontal strip and uniformly almost periodic under real translations
on every closed substrip. Let `Gamma(f)` be the additive group generated by its Bohr spectrum.
Assume that `n>=1` is the least integer for which

```text
P(f,f',...,f^(n))=0
```

for some nonzero `P in C[Y_0,...,Y_n]`. Then

```text
rank_Q Gamma(f) <= n.                                      (1)
```

The equation may be implicit and singular, and no global initial-value uniqueness is assumed.

## Why this is the live frontier

The paper-proved finite regular-chart theorem in
`../2-fully-proven/new_scientific_knowledge/almost-periodic-autonomous-ade-finite-frequency-law.md`
gives

```text
rank_Q Gamma(f) <= 2n.                                     (2)
```

The factor `2` enters only by forgetting the holomorphic dynamics and bounding a compact hull
chart by the real dimension of a complex `n`-manifold. Claim `(1)` asserts that an almost-periodic
minimal set of the holomorphic flow is forced to be totally real in the dimension-theoretic sense.

The order-one case is now proved in the stronger periodic form in
`../2-fully-proven/not_new_scientific_knowledge/first-order-autonomous-algebraic-ap-periodicity.md`:
normalizing the curve `P(f,f')=0` embeds the whole Bohr hull into a Riemann surface; the
Cartwright--O'Brien phase-cube argument excludes two independent frequencies, and the theorem that
non-circular solenoids do not embed in surfaces excludes a noncyclic rank-one frequency group.
Thus the genuinely open range begins at `n=2`.

The conjecture is also proved for every finite-dimensional transversely holomorphic phase carrier
in `../2-fully-proven/new_scientific_knowledge/phase-holomorphic-autonomous-ade-frequency-rank.md`.
There the all-jet fibre is exactly the multiplicative symmetry group of the active Laurent lattice;
Noetherianity reduces it to finitely many jets and gives `rank_Q Gamma(f)<=n`. Consequently a
counterexample cannot be an ordinary analytic quasiperiodic torus. It must exploit a genuinely
rough transverse phase, a non-Lie protorus, or wild singular-chart gluing.

The adjacent Cartwright--O'Brien theorem gives fewer than `p` new frequencies for a regular
first-order system in `R^p`, hence at most `2n-1` after viewing a regular complex order-`n` scalar
equation as a real system. It neither proves `(1)` nor covers singular implicit equations.

## Equality family

Let `lambda_1,...,lambda_n` be rationally independent reals and let

```text
f(z)=sum_(j=1)^n a_j exp(i lambda_j z),    a_j!=0.          (3)
```

The frequency rank is `n`. The Vandermonde system recovers the `n` exponential summands from the
first `n` jets, while the constant-coefficient linear equation

```text
prod_(j=1)^n (D-i lambda_j) f=0                         (4)
```

has order `n`. Algebraic independence of exponentials with rationally independent exponents rules
out an autonomous equation of lower order. Thus `(3)` attains equality in `(1)`.

## Hostile attacks

### A. Dense elliptic flow

An order-one algebraic curve suggests a two-real-dimensional hull. A dense real line on an elliptic
curve would have rank two, but every nonconstant meromorphic coordinate has poles. Density forces
the orbit arbitrarily close to a pole, contradicting boundedness and uniform almost periodicity on
the real axis. A holomorphic coordinate on the compact elliptic curve is constant. This standard
candidate therefore does not falsify `(1)`.

### B. Full-dimensional invariant torus

A holomorphic vector field on a projective complex torus can have a dense real orbit of real
dimension `2n`, but an autonomous scalar ADE produces a regular jet locus in an affine/Stein
variety. The missing proof must show that a compact equicontinuous minimal set in that affine
holomorphic flow cannot carry complex tangent directions; otherwise a positive-dimensional compact
complex analytic piece should be forced, which an affine variety cannot contain.

### C. Transversely rough quasiperiodicity

Holomorphy in the time strip controls Fourier coefficients by `exp(-eta|lambda|)` but need not give
smoothness in coordinates of a rational basis when the basis is Liouville. A finite-rank hull can
therefore be a transversely rough protorus embedding. Any proof of `(1)` must be topological or
uniform-algebraic and cannot silently assume a smooth torus parametrization.

### D. Singular branch switching

The minimal-separant theorem prevents the entire hull from remaining singular, but different hull
points may use different regular times. The finite closed-chart argument proves `(2)`. To prove
`(1)`, total reality or an equivalent half-dimensional bound must be stable under this finite
closed gluing; a regular-flow argument at one globally chosen time is insufficient.

## Open core

`[OPEN CORE]` Prove or refute the following local dimension theorem.

> Let `X` be the compact connected abelian hull of a bounded holomorphic almost-periodic orbit. If
> a finite open cover of `X` admits injective jet charts into a complex `n`-manifold, with chart
> transitions induced by a holomorphic local flow and the real translation orbit dense, then
> `dim X<=n`.

This statement must use the bounded holomorphic realization. Without the affine/Stein or
observable condition it is false: a dense linear real flow on a compact complex torus has hull
dimension `2n`.

## Falsifier

One explicit bounded holomorphic uniformly almost-periodic solution with minimal autonomous order
`n` and `n+1` rationally independent Bohr frequencies kills `(1)`. A valid example must be exact,
not approximate, and must remain holomorphic and uniformly bounded on every smaller strip.
