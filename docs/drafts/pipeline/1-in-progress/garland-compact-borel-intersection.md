## Exact groups and finite-dimensional input

Let C₁,C₀ be the images of SO₂(R), represented by the actual stabilizer of
i in SL₂(R), under the finite and oriented affine embeddings. Let Z be
the order-two subgroup generated by z(-1), and put Kᵢ=Z Cᵢ and
K=⟨K₀,K₁⟩. The centrality of Z makes Kᵢ a subgroup.

The ordinary real stabilizer consists of matrices [[a,b],[-b,a]] with
a²+b²=1 (the sign of b is a convention). Its upper triangular intersection
is {±I}. Consequently, using the exact coroot identities,

    C₁∩B = {1,h₁(-1)},
    C₀∩B = {1,z(-1)h₁(-1)}.

Both enlarged groups Kᵢ therefore meet B in the same subgroup

    M = {z(c)h₁(a) : c,a∈{1,-1}}.

Prove the stabilizer matrix characterization, both intersection equalities,
and M⊂K₀∩K₁ directly. The faithful central/finite Cartan embedding makes
these four elements distinct over R.

## Lowest-coefficient obstruction

The projection of a finite rotation is a constant invertible matrix C.
For a nontriangular rotation its entry C₁₀ is nonzero. The projection of
an affine rotation has the shape

    A = [[a,b X⁻¹],[-b X,a]],

with b≠0 exactly when it is outside B. All its coefficients below degree
-1 vanish, and its coefficient matrix at degree -1 is b E₀₁.

For matrices M,N bounded below in degrees r,s, respectively, prove that
their product is bounded below by r+s and that its coefficient matrix
at r+s equals coeffᵣ(M) coeffₛ(N). This is the finite matrix sum combined
with the actual lowest-coefficient identity for Laurent multiplication.

Consider an alternating product of elements from K₀\M and K₁\M.
Central signs have no effect on its matrix projection. If there are
n>0 affine factors, the product is bounded below by -n. Its coefficient
at -n is obtained by replacing each affine factor by b E₀₁ and each
finite factor by its constant matrix. Between successive affine factors
there is precisely one nontriangular finite factor, and

    E₀₁ C E₀₁ = C₁₀ E₀₁.

Thus the middle product is a nonzero multiple of E₀₁. Any finite factors
at either endpoint are invertible, so the full coefficient matrix is
nonzero. A negative Laurent coefficient excludes membership in the
matrix Iwahori, hence excludes membership in B by the proved preimage
criterion. If there is no affine factor, alternation leaves a single
finite nontriangular factor, which is also outside B.

This proves that every nonempty alternating word in K₀\M and K₁\M lies
outside B. The argument requires no norm, positivity representation,
assumed amalgam theorem, or assumed central-kernel statement.

The coefficient argument in fact works over every coefficient field for
alternating finite and oriented affine SL₂ matrices whose source lower-left
entries are nonzero. Rotations are needed only to identify the excluded
letters with the common sign torus. The same proof gives the generic
`alternating_simpleMatrixWord_not_mem_iwahori` statement. For formal
induction, an affine-headed word has boundary `[[u,v],[0,0]]` in degree
`-m`, with `m>0` and `(u,v)≠(0,0)`. Prepending an affine–finite pair
multiplies both entries by the nonzero product of their source lower-left
entries and increases `m` by one. A finite prefix preserves nonvanishing
by invertibility. This is the usual reduced-word obstruction, not a new
mathematical claim.

## Reduction of arbitrary words

Form the actual amalgamated pushout of the subtype inclusions M→Kᵢ.
These inclusions are injective. Its universal homomorphism into the loop
group restricts to the inclusion on each Kᵢ, so its range contains K.
Every g∈K therefore has a preimage in that pushout. The audited
`deningerExists_letterProd` theorem writes this preimage either as an
element of M or as a nonempty alternating list of factors outside M.
Mapping the list into the loop group preserves its product and its
factor indices. The coefficient argument puts the second possibility
outside B. Thus g∈K∩B forces g∈M. Together with M⊂B∩K this proves
B∩K=M, without assuming that the pushout map is injective.

## Spherical-character consequence

The two multiplicative Borel torus coordinates take values ±1 on M, so
every complex character defined using their logarithmic absolute values
is trivial on B∩K. With the separate Iwasawa existence theorem, if
g=b₁k₁=b₂k₂, then b₂⁻¹b₁=k₂k₁⁻¹ belongs to B∩K. Thus the character
values of b₁,b₂ coincide. This yields a well-defined spherical function,
left B-equivariant and right K-invariant, on the actual central loop group.
