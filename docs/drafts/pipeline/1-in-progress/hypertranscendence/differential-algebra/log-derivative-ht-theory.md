## Differential-field facts

Let \(K=\mathbb C(z)\).  The elements differentially algebraic over \(K\) form a differential
field and remain differentially algebraic under antiderivatives and exponentials.

### Log-derivative equivalence

For every nonzero meromorphic function \(G\),
\[
 \boxed{
 G\text{ is HT over }K
 \iff
 \frac{G'}G\text{ is HT over }K.
 }
\]

**Proof.**  If \(G'/G\) is DA, then an antiderivative \(\log G\) is DA and its exponential
\(G\) is DA.  Conversely, if \(G\) is DA, then \(G'/G\) is DA by differential-field
closure. \(\square\)

Consequently,
\[
 \Gamma\text{ HT}\iff\psi\text{ HT}.
\]
Hölder's theorem therefore gives the hypertranscendence of \(\psi\).

### Derivative equivalence

For every meromorphic \(G\),
\[
 \boxed{G\text{ is HT}\iff G'\text{ is HT}.}
\]
This follows because DA functions are closed under differentiation and antiderivation.

## Self-composition exclusion law

Let \(f\) be a nonzero HT meromorphic function and put \(\phi_f=f'/f\).  Then
\[
 \boxed{
 \phi_f(f(z))\text{ and }f(f(z))\text{ are not simultaneously DA over }K.
 }
\]

**Proof.**  The chain rule gives
\[
 (f\circ f)'(z)
 =\phi_f(f(z))\,f(f(z))\,f'(z).
\]
If the first two factors were DA and \(f\circ f\) were DA, the left side would be DA.  But
\(f'\) is HT, and multiplication by a nonzero DA function preserves HT, so the right side would
be HT. \(\square\)

For \(f=\Gamma\), this becomes
\[
 \boxed{
 \psi(\Gamma z)\text{ and }\Gamma(\Gamma z)
 \text{ are not simultaneously DA}.
 }
\]
Equivalently,
\[
 \psi(\Gamma z)\text{ DA}\Longrightarrow\Gamma(\Gamma z)\text{ HT},
\]
\[
 \Gamma(\Gamma z)\text{ DA}\Longrightarrow\psi(\Gamma z)\text{ HT}.
\]

This is a structural differential-field consequence of Hölder's theorem.  It is classified as a
bridge rather than new scientific knowledge.

## Factorial pole-cluster values of \(\psi(\Gamma z)\)

Fix \(n\geq0\).  For all sufficiently large integers \(m\), the distinguished inverse-Gamma
value \(z_{n,m}\) near \(-n\) satisfies
\[
 \Gamma(z_{n,m})=-m,
\]
\[
 z_{n,m}
 =-n-\frac{(-1)^n}{n!m}
 +\frac{\psi(n+1)}{(n!)^2m^2}+O(m^{-3}).
\]
It is a simple pole of \(\psi(\Gamma z)\), with residue
\[
 \operatorname{Res}_{z=z_{n,m}}\psi(\Gamma z)
 =\frac{(-1)^n}{n!m^2}
 -\frac{2\psi(n+1)}{(n!)^2m^3}+O(m^{-4}).
\]

The exact analytic interpolation is
\[
 \widehat\rho_n(x)
 =-\frac1{\Gamma'(z_n(x))}
 =-x^2z_n'(x),
 \qquad
 \Gamma(z_n(x))=-\frac1x,
\]
and the actual residues are the samples
\[
 \operatorname{Res}_{z=z_{n,m}}\psi(\Gamma z)=\widehat\rho_n(1/m).
\]

## Algebraic value consequence

The pole clusters prove
\[
 \boxed{
 \psi(\Gamma z)
 \notin\overline{\mathbb C(z,\psi(z))}^{\mathrm{alg}}.
 }
\]

Near \(-n\), every element algebraic over the meromorphic germ field generated by \(z\) and
\(\psi(z)\) has a finite Puiseux ramification and no additional singularities in some punctured
neighborhood.  The infinitely many poles \(z_{n,m}\to-n\) give the stated transcendence.

Since \(\psi\) is transcendental over \(\mathbb C(z)\), the pair
\[
 \{\psi(z),\psi(\Gamma z)\}
\]
is algebraically independent over \(\mathbb C(z)\).

The infinite pole set \(\{z_{n,m}\}\) therefore gives
