# Moving divisors in the centered prime wave

The [large-divisor moment theorem](../3-formalized/ecs-large-divisor-moments-of-the-prime-wave.md) controls all small divisors in absolute value and the ordered zeroth moment above the cutoff.

**All divisor scales.** Put $a_d(t)=d^{-1/2}(\log d)^kD(t-\log d)$, $d\ge1$. For $0\le b<1/6$, translation and Tonelli give the exact finite cubic integral
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}\sum_d |e^{bt}a_d(t)|^3\,dt
 =\left(\sum_d (\log d)^{3k}d^{3b-3/2}\right)\int_{\mathbb R}e^{3bu}|D(u)|^3\,du.
\]
Indeed $|D(u)|\ll_\sigma e^{u/2}+e^{\sigma u}$ for $1/2<\sigma\le2$ by confinement, negative support and summability of $n^{-1/2-\sigma}$. Choose $3b+\sigma<1$ and apply the two subcritical squared norms with tilts $(3b+1/2)/2$ and $(3b+\sigma)/2$. The Dirichlet factor converges for $3b<1/2$ and diverges at equality. For any sets $S(t)$ of at most $e^{\kappa t}$ divisors, Hölder bounds $(\sum_{d\in S}|a_d|)^3\le |S|^2\sum_d |a_d|^3$. Thus a single integrable majorant controls the cubed absolute contribution with weight $e^{rt}$ whenever $r+2\kappa<1/2$, with no restriction on the sizes of the selected divisors. This applies to the true logarithmic Möbius row, including selections made after observing its coefficients. An exponentially large prime fluctuation cannot be supplied by those selected terms. The signed interaction among the remaining divisors is open.

**Upper truncation.** At a real cutoff $Y\ge e^t$ use the equivalent logarithmic weight $\log(Y/d)$, since the complete zeroth moment vanishes for $t\ge0$. Put $H(v)=\sum_{d\le v}\mu(d)/d$. The signed polar tail $\sum_{d>Y}\mu(d)\log(Y/d)/d$ equals $\int_Y^\infty H(v)\,dv/v$ by partial summation and $H(v)\log v\to0$. The [Mertens harmonic bound](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/RHTower/MertensPowerBoundsControlMobiusHarmonicSums.lean) and [seven-eighths estimate](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExponentDynamicsOfTheCriticalStrip/TheAbscissaAvoidsAnIntervalBelowSevenEighths.lean) give $H(v)=O(v^{-1/8})$. On $d>Y$ the uncentered integer row has absolute cost $O(e^{2t}d^{-5/2})$ by confinement. Consequently all divisors above $Y$ together cost $O(e^{t/2}Y^{-1/8}+e^{2t}Y^{-3/2})$ in the centered logarithmic representation. Taking $Y=e^{ct}$ with $c>4$ leaves exponential decay and reduces the infinite ordered first moment to a moving finite band. Sizes above the band have been controlled only in their forced natural order.

For a squarefree modulus $q$, the normalized coprime-reference discrepancy is $(q/\varphi(q))\sum_{d\mid q}\mu(d)a_d$ with $k=0$. Both $q/\varphi(q)$ and the number of summands are at most $\tau(q)$. For every fixed $K$, the [divisor bound](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/RHTower/Sieve/DivisorBound.lean) gives $\tau(q)=e^{o(t)}$ uniformly for $q\le e^{Kt}$. The cubic estimate then controls even a different modulus at every time with one integrable majorant for the cubed discrepancy at every exponential rate $r<1/2$. Removed prime atoms cost $O(\tau(q)(1+t)e^{-t/2})$, since only $p>e^t$ contributes. Thus relative late sign masses and square-root variances survive every adaptive polynomial-size modulus. The remaining arithmetic question concerns the signed large-gap sums within its reduced residue classes.

**Ordinary primes.** Their exact inverse coefficients are $w(d)=\sum_{p\mid d}\mu(d/p)\log p$. On squarefree $d$ this is $-\mu(d)\log d$. On $d=p^2m$ with $m$ squarefree and $(p,m)=1$ it is $-\mu(m)\log p$. It vanishes on every other nonsquarefree integer. Moreover $|w(d)|\le\log d$. For $Q(n)=\Lambda(n)-1_{\mathbb P}(n)\log n$ one has $w=-\mu\log-\mu*Q$ and $\sum Q(n)/n<\infty$. The harmonic prefix of $\mu*Q$ is $\sum_{j\le H}(Q(j)/j)H(\lfloor H/j\rfloor)$ and converges to zero by dominated convergence. Hence the ordered $w$-sum of centered multiples gives the ordinary-prime polar error itself. The entire signed row on nonsquarefree divisors equals minus the proper-power wave and converges at late times to $-\mathcal LF(0)/2$. Only the logarithmically weighted squarefree row can carry an exponentially large late fluctuation.

For independent signs on prime bases, weight every proper power $p^j$ by the same $c_p$, $|c_p|\le1$. The resulting proper-power sequence is still absolutely summable at the reciprocal weight. After Möbius inversion its only nonzero coefficients are at $p^2m$ with $m$ squarefree and $p\nmid m$: the consecutive powers cancel when $p^3\mid n$, and two repeated prime factors force a square in each complementary divisor. Thus the increasing-order nonsquarefree row selected by any $c_p$ has absolute value bounded by the full positive proper-power wave evaluated on $|\Phi|$. Prime squares give the latter the finite limit $\tfrac12\int |\Phi|$, and higher powers vanish by dominated convergence. This would bound all selections of square bases simultaneously, including a different selection at every time.

For the sharp bounds before inversion, see [the two sign masses of the proper prime powers](../3-formalized/ecs-two-sign-masses-of-proper-prime-powers.md).

**Integrable sign error.** On paper, the absolute error after replacing each proper-power sign mass by its limiting constant integrates to $O(e^{-T/40})$ over the entire late ray. The same bound transfers both sign parts from the twice-centered Mangoldt wave to the ordinary-prime wave.
