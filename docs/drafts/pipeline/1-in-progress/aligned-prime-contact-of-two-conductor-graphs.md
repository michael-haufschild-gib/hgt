# The aligned prime contact

Take the effective pair of conductor graphs $1,p$. At ratio $p$, the shifted copy of the second spline aligns exactly with the first. The row contains the square of one translate of $B$. Its normalized logarithmic debit is at least $(2\log p/\sqrt p)\int B^2>0$. When $\log p>8$, the support of $B$ kills the other three products. Ratios $p^r$ for $r\ge2$ then miss both supports. The predicted $p$-adic valuation per squared multiplicity of the full modulus is exactly $2\int B^2/\sqrt p$.

This distinguishes the interpolation height over $\mathbb Q(T)$ from the finite-place cost after passing to rational sections. For each fixed prime pair, a nonzero integer paying this one row already has positive quadratic log size. Its comparison with the pole and jet radius remains open.
