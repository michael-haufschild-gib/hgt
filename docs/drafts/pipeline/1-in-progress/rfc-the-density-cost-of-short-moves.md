# Positive density of long moves on every large octave

- **Stage** 1-in-progress
- **Question** Can a restricted matching move almost every squarefree integer in each large octave while all but a vanishing fraction of its edges have length at most a fixed power of the height below one?

Write \(V_t^\pm(x)\) for the integers in \((x/2,x]\) of the indicated Möbius sign with every prime factor at most \(x^t\). Both signs have positive density for \(t=1\). If this holds for \(t=2/(k+2)\), put \(a=2/(k+4)\), \(b=2/(k+3)\). For each prime \(x^a<p\le x^b\), append \(p\) to each opposite-sign integer in \(V_t^\mp(x/p)\). Its old prime factors are at most \((x/p)^t<x^{(1-a)t}=x^a<p\). The product recovers \(p\) as its largest prime, and \(x/p\ge x^{1-b}\) exceeds the density threshold uniformly. Summing the positive reciprocal mass of the prime window proves positive lower density at exponent \(b\). Induction reaches every positive smoothness exponent.

For \(0<\gamma<1\) fix \(0<b<(1-\gamma)/2\). Each genuine one-to-two exchange at a vertex smooth up to \((2X)^b\) in \((X,2X]\) retains a cofactor \(c\ge n/(2X)^{2b}>X^\gamma\) eventually. Its displacement, a nonzero multiple of \(c\), exceeds \(X^\gamma\). Consequently every restricted matching, even one chosen separately at each cutoff, must leave a positive fraction fixed or use a positive fraction of edges longer than \(X^\gamma\). The density bound holds for each Möbius sign separately.

For a single involution, integrating the actual unpaid count over every sharp octave gives \(\sum_{X\le x\le2X} U_F(x)\gg_\gamma X^{1+\gamma}\). Deleting endpoint collars of width \(2X^\gamma=o(X)\) preserves the positive density. A fixed interior vertex stays unpaid throughout at least \(\lfloor X^\gamma\rfloor\) subsequent cutoffs. The edge of a moving vertex crosses at least that many consecutive cutoffs inside the octave, on its left or right. Each charged edge has at most two originating vertices. Thus the unsigned power obstruction also holds on average on every octave.

**Signed obstruction.** At fixed nonintegral \(u>2\), the sharp signed prefix on all \(y\)-smooth integers, \(y=x^{1/u}\), has leading term \(x\omega'(u)/(\log y)^2\), where \(\omega\) is Buchstab's function. Subtracting the prefixes at \(x\) and \(x/2\) with the same \(y\) keeps half this leading term. There are arbitrarily large nonintegral \(u\) with \(\omega'(u)\ne0\). Otherwise \(\omega\) would be constant on a terminal half-line, and \((u\omega(u))'=\omega(u-1)\) would propagate constancy back to \(\omega(u)=1/u\) on \((1,2)\). Taking \(u>2/(1-\gamma)\) exhibits an entire family fixed by every power-short matching whose signed sum has power exponent one. Cancellation with its complement is necessary. The friable asymptotic is an external analytic input awaiting formalization. [De la Bretèche and Tenenbaum, Corollary 1.3](https://tenenb.perso.math.cnrs.fr/PPP/Friable-real.pdf)
