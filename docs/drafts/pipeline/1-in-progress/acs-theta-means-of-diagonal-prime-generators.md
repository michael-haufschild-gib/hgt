# Theta averages of the diagonal prime generators

For the square divisor (D_n=e_1+2e_{n^2}-e_{(2n+1)^2}), can one bound the mean of the actual signed log generator divided by (n^2) at every translated logarithmic scale? Use the positive Riemann theta source as a fixed averaging density, assigning each log coordinate (u) to the integer (n=\lceil e^{u/2}\rceil).

The two long lags of the signed degree are translates of the real spline trace. The theta transform vanishes on every active trace exponent. Sampling at integer squares changes each long lag by (O(n^{2w-1})), where (w<1/2) is the zero width. The short lag tends to the trace at (\log 4). This predicts a fixed limiting mean for the signed degree and a uniformly bounded mean for the normalized ordinary log generator. The pointwise upper bound at every conductor remains open.
