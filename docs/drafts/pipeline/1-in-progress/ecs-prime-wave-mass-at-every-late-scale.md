# Signed prime-wave mass at every late scale

Let $\rho$ be a right zero with $\delta=\Re\rho-1/2$ and $\mathcal LF(1/2-\rho)\ne0$. For each $0<\eta<\Re\rho$, do both signs of the polar-subtracted ordinary-prime state wave have integral at least $c e^{\delta T}$ over $[\eta T,T]$ for every sufficiently large $T$?

Write $c(n)=\Lambda(n)-1$, $a(n)=(-1)^{n+1}$ and $b=a*c$. At a zero of multiplicity $m$, the zero detector $Q_b(e^t)e^{-t/2}$ has a nonzero $e^{(\rho-1/2)t}$ term and bounded error. Its divisor kernel $Q_a(e^t)e^{-t/2}$ is bounded by $Ct e^{-t/2}$ on $t\ge0$ by its logarithmic Lipschitz bound and vanishing at zero. Convolution with the confined profile multiplies the main term by $\mathcal LF(1/2-\rho)$. This predicts an exponentially large absolute integral of the integer-centred Mangoldt wave at every late scale.

For sign separation, the positive kernel $H(v)=1_{v\ge0}e^{-v/2}(\lfloor e^v\rfloor\bmod 2)$ sends the centred Mangoldt measure to a bounded function by the square-root alternating divisor count. Convolve $H$ with $1_{v\ge0}e^{-v/2}$. The resulting weight $J(v)=e^{-v/2}\int_0^v(\lfloor e^s\rfloor\bmod 2)ds$ lies between $ve^{-v/2}/2$ and $ve^{-v/2}$ for $v\ge0$. On each adjacent odd-even pair of unit intervals, $dx/x$ assigns the longer log interval to the odd member, including every prefix of the pair. Thus $J$ dominates the zero detector in absolute value, while $J$ applied to the centred wave stays bounded. Earlier times $u<\eta T$ cost $O((1+T)^2e^{(\eta-1/2)T})$ by the elementary wave bound. Integer quadrature and the bounded proper-power contribution transfer the sign estimates to ordinary primes. The required estimate on distant centred prime pairs remains open.

**Consequence for even gaps.** The [growing-sieve bound](../3-formalized/ecs-growing-sieve-for-integer-centering.md) would transfer either sign's exponential mass to the same integer-centered row after every sieve $P\subseteq\{p\le T^2\}$, uniformly in $P$. Cauchy–Schwarz then gives integrated squared mass $\gg e^{2\delta T}/T$. For individual coefficients $b_n(t)$ of that row, confinement gives $\sum_n|b_n(t)|^2\ll(1+t+\delta_P^{-1})^2$ on the positive ray by summing inverse-power tails from $n\ge e^t$. Each fixed additive shift costs at most this diagonal norm. Since $\delta_P^{-1}=e^{o(T)}$, every gap below $e^{\alpha T}$ together costs $e^{\alpha T+o(T)}$ for $\alpha<2\delta$. If $2\in P$, the positive excess must therefore come from **signed even gaps above $e^{\alpha T}$ at every late scale**. This last conclusion still depends on the signed-mass theorem.

**Prescribed divisibility of the large gaps.** Fix $q\ge2$ and remove its prime factors from both rows. Write $S_a$ for the centered row in residue $a\bmod q$. Cauchy–Schwarz gives $\sum_a S_a^2\ge (\sum_a S_a)^2/\varphi(q)$. Only gaps divisible by $q$ occur on the left. Summed over all residue classes, the diagonal costs $e^{o(T)}$ after integration, and each fixed nonzero shift costs the same. Thus for $q\le e^{\gamma T}$, $\gamma<2\delta$, and $K=e^{\alpha T}$, $\alpha<2\delta$, the diagonal and the $\lfloor K/q\rfloor$ admissible short shifts cost $o(e^{2\delta T}/(qT))$. The remaining signed excess is carried by gaps larger than $K$ **divisible by the prescribed $q$**, with both endpoints coprime to $q$. Choose $\gamma<\eta<\Re\rho$, possible because $2\delta<\Re\rho$. Every prime dividing $q$ lies below $e^{\eta T}$, so deleting it from the actual wave costs zero on this interval. The proved $L^1$ reference bound costs at most $(1-1/\sqrt2)^{-\omega(q)}=e^{o(T)}$, since $\omega(q)!\le q$. No distribution theorem for primes in residue classes enters this localization.

**Shorter windows.** Can both signs be forced within $|t-T|\le(1/2+\varepsilon)\log T$? Set $y=e^{-2v}$, $g(r)=r^2e^{-r^2}$ and $J(v)=e^{-v/2}\sum_{n\ge1}(-1)^{n+1}g(ne^{-v})$. The sum is $y\vartheta_4'(y)/2$, where $\vartheta_4(y)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}(-1)^ne^{-yn^2}$. For $y\ge1$ pair the decreasing terms $n^2e^{-yn^2}$. For $y\le1$, differentiating the Poisson formula $\vartheta_4(y)=\sqrt{\pi/y}\sum_j e^{-\pi^2(j+1/2)^2/y}$ gives positive summands. Both tails of $J$ decay as a power times $e^{-c e^{2|v|}}$.

At a right zero of multiplicity $m$, the bilateral Laplace transform of $J$ has a zero of order $m$ at $z=\rho-1/2$, by the alternating symbol times $\Gamma((\rho+2)/2)/2$. The $m$-fold primitive with kernel $1_{v>0}e^{zv}v^{m-1}/(m-1)!$ can therefore be written as a tail integral at either end. The two Gaussian tail estimates should bound its modulus by a constant times $J$. For $b=a*(\Lambda-1)$ the smoothed row $e^{-v/2}\sum b(n)g(ne^{-v})$ is bounded by Abel summation and has transform vanishing to order exactly $m-1$ at $z$. The same primitive of this row has a nonzero $e^{zv}$ term and bounded remainder for $v\ge0$. The open analytic steps are the Mellin identities on the conditional half-plane, tail comparison for the divided kernel, and transfer of this identity to the signed ordinary-prime wave. **A positive fractional-part window.** Put $K(v)=e^{-v/2}\{e^v\}$ and $L(x)=\log(\lfloor x\rfloor!)$. For $Y\ge x>0$ the divisor identity gives
\[
 \sum_{n\le Y}\Lambda(n)\{x/n\}-\int_0^Y\{x/y\}\,dy
 =x(\log x-1)-L(x)+x\left(\sum_{n\le Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)}n-\log Y+\gamma\right).
\]
Indeed the sum of the floors is $L(x)$, and the integral is $x[\log(Y/x)+1-\gamma]$ by the substitution $r=x/y$. The [reciprocal Mangoldt remainder](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/RHTower/MertensVonMangoldtEulerConstant.lean) tends to zero. For the actual polar-subtracted Mangoldt wave $E_\Lambda$, truncate the convolution at $t\le M$ and put $x=e^{T-u}$, $Y=e^{M-u}$ in the translated state row. For $M\ge T$ the formula applies for every $u\le0$. The remainder is bounded by $e^{T/2}\int |\Phi(u)|e^{-u/2}du$ times the supremum of the reciprocal remainder on $[e^M,\infty)$. Hence
\[
 (K*E_\Lambda)(T)=\int_{-\infty}^0 \Phi(u)e^{(u-T)/2}\,[x(\log x-1)-L(x)]\,du
 =O_\Phi((1+T)e^{-T/2})\quad(T\ge0).
\]
Stirling's estimate gives the last bound. The [arithmetic cutoff identity and density normalization](../3-formalized/ecs-fractional-part-average-of-the-mangoldt-measure.md) are formalized. The ordinary-prime response consequently tends to $-\tfrac12\mathcal LF(0)\int K$. This window is nonnegative and its signed response is determined at every center. An upper bound for either sign mass inside the window remains open.

Stirling’s constant predicts the first nonzero signed term:
\[
 e^{T/2}(K*E_\Lambda)(T)\longrightarrow
 -\tfrac12\log(2\pi)\,\mathcal LF(1/2).
\]
Indeed $x(\log x-1)-L(x)=(\{x\}-1/2)\log x-\tfrac12\log(2\pi)+O(1/x)$. Box-average $\Phi$ over log shifts of length $h=e^{-2T/3}$. The [first absolute increment estimate](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExclusionOfTheCriticalState/Wall/TheFirstAbsoluteMomentOfStateIncrements.lean) bounds the $L^1$ approximation error by $O(\sqrt h)$; the derivative of the average has $L^1$ norm at most $2\|\Phi\|_1/h$. On $u\le1$, a primitive from $-\infty$ of $e^{u/2}(T-u)(\{e^{T-u}\}-1/2)$ is $O((1+T)e^{-T})$, by the bounded periodic primitive of $\{x\}-1/2$ and integration by parts. Thus the sawtooth costs $O((1+T)e^{-T/3})$. The precise asymptotic and its transfer to the physical wave await Lean verification.
