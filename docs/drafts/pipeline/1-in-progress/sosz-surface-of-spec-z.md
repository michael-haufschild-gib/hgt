# The surface of Spec ℤ

- **ID:** SOSZ
- **Stage:** 1-in-progress
- **Type:** CANDIDATE
- **Source:** [review 0811 W14, §2.8a, §2.11](../../../reports/review-0811/obstacles-summary.md)
- **Statement:** construct, or derive the shape of, a two-dimensional object whose Riemann–Roch gives an
  `ℕ`-valued (or log-cardinality-valued) quadratic count with Tate limit `Q` on integer probes.

## Goal

`riemannHypothesis_iff_tateLimit_of_count` makes RH equivalent to a count `K ≥ 0` with
`K(ma)/m² → Q(a)`. Over a curve `K` is `½δ` on `C × C`, a dimension by Riemann–Roch. The task is the
`ℤ`-analogue, defined without `Q`. Lean code: `formal/GaloisForLFunctions/SurfaceOfSpecZ/`.

## Specification forced by the corpus

| Requirement | Source |
|---|---|
| trace (Rosati) type, pairs `ρ` with `1 − ρ̄`; kernel counts are blind | `KernelCountsAreBlindToComplexQuartets` |
| full rank, quadratic; no linear count | `LinearCountsAreDegenerate` |
| asymptotically Hecke invariant | `tateLimit_dilate` |
| integrality of the coefficients, infinite rank; no integral lattice carries a zero at 3 primes | `FrobeniusLatticeRigidity` |
| reads the test beyond its bidegree | LST `bidegree_h1_eq` |
| an exact RR polynomial in `m` with integral intersection numbers has a half-integral Tate limit, so intersection numbers must be real (log-cardinalities plus archimedean terms) | elementary |

## Big picture

The two half-products exist and assemble the whole off-diagonal: `Γ_m·Γ_n` = one `𝔽₁`-cusp (toric,
integral) + the even jets of the dilation (archimedean, real, infinite rank) + torsion collisions over
the primes (`ℙ¹_ℤ`, discontinuous in `log n`). Each local index is nonnegative; Weil's defect is
`(m + n) − Γ_m·Γ_n` off the diagonal. The diagonal `Γ_n² = n(2 − 2g)` diverges only through the jet
tower at coincidence: the infinite genus is archimedean. This is Connes' local trace formula in
integer normalization, read from the `𝔽₁`-side; `Spec ℤ ×_{𝔽₁} Spec ℤ` behaves like the square of the
inverse limit of `𝔾_m` under the Frobenii (the adelic solenoid with radial scaling).

Signs: in Weil's kernel the primes enter as the Abel limit of the torsion log-energy of `ℙ¹_ℤ`, which
is positive before renormalization, and the archimedean channels (cusp and jets) enter negatively.
With atomic graphs the diagonal is `+∞` and carries everything. After smoothing, on probes orthogonal
to the pole plane the prime sum loses its main term together with the pole (the prime number theorem
pairs them), and what is left is the zero sum itself.

Smoothed by the cubic spline, the Frobenius graphs at integer degrees are a curve in every numerical
respect: genus `c_B(0)/2`, traces `c_B(log n)`, Hasse–Weil at every degree iff RH, the self-improvement
from a crude bound is free, and one side of the crude bound suffices (Landau in place of Pringsheim).
In Weil's terms, positivity on the prime two-bumps `B ± τ_{log p}B` is RH. The growth exponent of a
one-sided bound is the zero width, at the primes and at every degree set with power-saving gaps (C49).

Which degrees decide. Gaps: at width `a` the traces are Lipschitz with constant `≍ e^{au}`, so gaps
`x^θ` lower the width by `1 − θ` per step; every degree set with power-saving gaps decides the line from
below for every configuration down to its deepest real zero (C45), and from above for every
configuration (C46): the primes, the `pᵏ`, the `nᵏ` with `O(x^{1/k})` elements (C47). Sampling: a weight
whose series has a pole at `s = 1` and no other singularity on `Re s = 1` sees every attained top layer,
from `ζ(1 + it) ≠ 0` alone (C35, C38): the primes in a residue class, density-one sets of primes (C44),
the `pᵃqᵇ` of two primes with `O(log² x)` elements (C50). The exact criterion for attained tops is
Bohr density of the log-degrees, from both sides, real zeros masking only from below (C65, C67); every
sampling weight has Bohr-dense support (C68), and so has every set of long runs of integers
`[x, e^L x]`, at density zero and without power-saving gaps. One prime resonates at the heights
`2πm/log p` and aliases (C33, C52); two cannot resonate together. The attained top cannot be dropped
at the single degrees of any finitely generated monoid: quartets made resonant by simultaneous
Dirichlet approximation on successive windows keep the samples bounded below (C58), and so do
genuine tempered configurations at the degrees of any finite set of primes, their heights placed at
the zeros of `B̂` where the spline coefficients are in phase (C61), and at the very smooth
degrees, where every prime takes part (C64). Which side these towers blind is the sign `(−1)^{o+1}` of
`B̂²` at the zeros of the spline `B̂ = sinc^{o+1}`: the lower side for the cubic spline, the upper
side for the quartic (C62); the primes, infinitely generated with power-saving gaps,
need no attainment (C45). The heights of C61 are simultaneous returns of the prime logarithms
(homogeneous Dirichlet), and the tower needs them early: a return of `m` generators to precision
`1/U` below height `e^{o(U)}`. When every later level is in phase on each window the weights need no
ceiling, and Dirichlet's bound leaves room for the `(log d)^{1/4}`-smooth degrees (C64), for every
`(log d)^c`-smooth set with `c < 1` by finer windows (estimate): towers live on degree sets with
`x^{o(1)}` elements. Denser smooth degree sets would need earlier returns of all primes `≤ y`, which the quantitative
Kronecker conjecture of review 0811 §2.11 (B97: late returns) excludes, consistently with the integer
degrees deciding without attainment (C18). Two-sided, the planes `⟨Γ_m, Γ_n⟩` of
the monoid `⟨p, q⟩` decide RH with no attainment, their lags being dense (C51), and so do their
effective classes `Γ_m + Γ_n`, one-sided (C56), for every configuration and for `L(s, χ)` (C57);
positivity on the
whole group ring of the two Frobenii is the Bochner problem on `T²` of `ArithmeticPositiveDuality`. For `ζ` the von Mangoldt series
`Σ_z e_z ζ'/ζ(s − kz)` gives a second bootstrap, `b ↦ b − (½ − b)/k` (C32). The Rankin square of the
traces diverges exactly at twice the top weight (C36).

Over `𝔽_q` the crude bound is Stepanov's count, an upper bound on points, the side blind to real
eigenvalues. Over `ℤ` the same side is blind exactly to the zeros shallower than the deepest real zero;
`ζ` has none, so it sees everything, while for a real character a Siegel zero would mask. At every
sampling set this is exact for attained tops: a lower bound forces the top to contain a real zero
(C39), and a top consisting of one real zero keeps the traces bounded below at all degrees (C40).
Exactly: the lower side sees the sign of the top layer `F_top`, an almost periodic function, through
its Bohr pairings with nonnegative exponential polynomials (C43). The other side has no blind spot
(C37).

The object the corpus is missing is therefore a natural count: on the effective classes `Γ_1 + Γ_d`
of the planes of one degree set with power-saving gaps (C48), i.e. a reason for an upper bound on the
smoothed point counts there; or, two-sided, a binary count on each plane of the rank-two lattice
`p^ℤq^ℤ` of two Frobenii (C51), or one-sided on the effective classes `Γ_m + Γ_n`, `m, n ∈ ⟨p, q⟩`
(C56). Counts on the single-degree classes `Γ_1 + Γ_{pᵃqᵇ}` alone give only the abscissa law, and
for general configurations nothing more (C61); so do counts at the very smooth degrees (C64), and
counts on any Bohr-dense set of degrees, such as runs of integers, give the abscissa law (C65). The
support of the count decides which half of RH it carries: Bohr density gives AAL, power-saving gaps
give RH. Positivity of Weil's form on their group ring
`ℤ[x^{±1}, y^{±1}]`,
a positive measure on `T²` (`ArithmeticPositiveDuality`), is the corresponding Rosati positivity.

Reading. The traces at the prime powers are point counts: for the virtual motive
`M = Σ_z e_z ℤ(−z)` over `Spec ℤ`, `#M(𝔽_{pᵏ}) = Σ_z e_z p^{kz} = 2 Re S(k log p)`, with
`ζ_M(s) = Π_z ζ(s − z)^{e_z}` and `−ζ_M'/ζ_M(s) = Σ_n Λ(n) 2 Re S(log n) n^{−s}`. Its singularities are
`1 + z` (from `ℤ(−z)`) and `z + ρ'` (from `M ⊗ H¹(Spec ℤ)`, the sum set of the zeros). The prime-degree
theorems are Deligne's Landau–Rankin purity argument for `M`: positivity of the coefficients of
`−ζ_M'/ζ_M` (here the one-sided hypothesis) and Landau's theorem against the poles `1 + z`. Deligne
gets positivity by squaring, as the even powers `M^{⊗2k}` have nonnegative point counts
`(2 Re S(log p))^{2k}`, and controls their weights by cohomology. Over `ℤ` the second input, a bound on
the growth of the moments uniform in `k`, is RH (C34); equivalently, a half-plane of convergence
common to all `−L'/L(M^{⊗2k})` (C41).

The atomic surface of degree `N` is, unconditionally, a curve of genus `≍ N log N`. In the normalized
channels the jets have spectral radius `≍ N log N`, the pole (off the primitive part) `O(N log N)`,
and the primes, a Hecke operator, `≍ log N`. Resolution decides what is seen: at the atom spacing
`1/N` the archimedean self-energy dominates by a factor `N`; the arithmetic, and with it RH, appears
only after smoothing at a fixed scale, where all channels are `O(1)`.
