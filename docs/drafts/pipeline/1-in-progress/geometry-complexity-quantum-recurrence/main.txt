Four structural laws for loss, spectral constraints, observability, and finite-horizon inference

                                                      Abstract
        Quantum recurrence is often compressed into one question—when does a state return?—although loss,
   spectral constraints, unequal state weights, restricted measurements and finite records change different parts
   of the answer. We prove that these mechanisms form one persistent phase geometry with four successive
   laws. Loss selects the exact spectral carrier that can recur. Integer-character action determines the carrier’s
   projective phase hull: zero-action relations and independent temporal directions partition the full capacity,
   with ranks summing to D − 1. Weights pull a target metric onto this hull, while an observer’s coherence
   graph selects its visible quotient. Finite records determine finite-time behavior but need not determine the
   completed relation geometry.
       This finite-horizon boundary is sharp even for complete process information. For every finite Hermitian
   Hamiltonian of dimension at least three, every observation horizon and every positive operator resolution,
   its entire bounded-time propagator tube contains two commuting simple-spectrum Hamiltonians with one
   common eigenbasis. For every one fixed normalized state cyclic in that basis, one has physical survival-fidelity
   first-return exponent zero and the other has maximal exponent (D − 2)/2. Thus even a uniformly accurate
   movie of full evolution over an arbitrarily long but finite interval does not identify asymptotic recurrence
   scaling.
        For arbitrary spectral type, the completed survival-fidelity distribution obeys a sharper universal law. Its
   support is always one closed interval, with no forbidden fidelity bands, and its upper endpoint is exactly
   p2 , where p is total weight of the initial state in the pure-point—physically, bound-state—spectral sector.
   Consequently the full stationary return distribution recovers bound-state weight as square root of its support
   edge. This is distinct from the classical time-averaged survival probability, which measures sum of squared
   atomic weights rather than their total mass.
       This framework gives exact results at each level: late-return ceilings for finite lossy dynamics; character-
   complete finite and countable phase laws; sharp weighted deadlines; observer-compressed Born-record returns;
   arbitrary-spectrum collision identities; and complete finite-record ambiguity. It also identifies the maximal
   information retained by common experiments. All scalar survival-return data, even under every probability
   law of observation times, determine exactly the spectral difference measure and forget clock orientation.
   Resolved replica measurements instead recover the full projective relation lattice. Two explicit uniform nine-
   level spectra prove strict separation: their scalar survival fidelity agrees at every time, but a finite resolved
   detector distinguishes their hidden phase constraints.
       The same laws remain nontrivial under locality and increasing system size. For generic static onsite-plus-
   edge Hamiltonians on every finite interaction graph, temporal rank equals the sum of maximal component
   ranks simultaneously for all induced subsystems; pairwise rank curvature reconstructs supplied graph adja-

   conull set of Hamiltonians simultaneously attains all D − 1 relative phase directions, sharp stroboscopic and

Public summary. Quantum systems can revisit earlier states, but a return is not governed by one universal
clock. Some information may have been lost permanently; surviving energy components may obey hidden
relations; a chosen detector may see only some of their relative phases; and a finite record may be compatible
with radically different futures. This paper separates these effects and proves how they fit together.
    The strongest spectrum-wide conclusion is an exact diagnostic of what part of a state remains bound. Over
completed time, possible survival probabilities never form several separated bands: they fill one interval. The
top of that interval is square of total bound-state weight. Thus full distribution of returns reveals information
that average return loses. A state spread over many bound levels can have a small average return while still
having same upper return edge as any other fully bound state.
    The main conceptual result is a hierarchy of information. A standard survival measurement can be perfect
for its purpose yet blind to part of recurrence structure. We give two concrete nine-level systems with exactly
same return probability at every time, while a more resolved multi-copy measurement tells them apart. Thus
observing all values of one return signal does not amount to learning all temporal organization inside a quantum
system.
    There is an even stronger limit on prediction. Suppose an experimenter knows complete quantum evolution,
not merely one signal, throughout any chosen finite interval. Arbitrarily close to that entire observed evolution
are two systems sharing same energy basis and same prepared state, yet one returns exactly and repeatedly
while the other’s increasingly accurate first returns obey largest possible logarithmic scaling law. No finite
movie, however precise, can decide which asymptotic recurrence clock governs system without additional global
spectral information.
    Spatial simplicity does not remove this organization. Generic static nearest-neighbour chains use every
available relative phase direction: an n-site chain can have only linearly many local couplings yet an exponential
number of rationally independent spectral gaps. This makes a high-accuracy return a simultaneous alignment
problem in q n − 1 directions, so its share of long times shrinks doubly exponentially with n. Commuting
finite-range models obey an exact linear phase-capacity ceiling, identifying algebraic commutativity—not one-
dimensional geometry—as the compressing mechanism. For almost every Hamiltonian in full local path family,
this geometry also fixes actual first return of every state. A state occupying exactly s energy levels has logarithmic
survival-fidelity return-time exponent max{s − 2, 0}/2. Thus changing only prepared state’s exact spectral
support changes recurrence dimension inside one unchanged Hamiltonian; full-support states give (q n − 2)/2.
This is an asymptotic theorem, not claim that macroscopic recurrences are experimentally reachable. It also
exposes an experimentally important singularity. Adding an arbitrarily weak population tail across all levels
changes exponent from zero to maximal while altering survival signal by at most four times tail weight at
every time. Coarse apparatus can therefore see an eigenstate-like history forever even when exact recurrence
geometry has full dimension; deliberate tail injection supplies a finite-system crossover test. One fixed countable
nearest-neighbour field now joins several regimes that are usually studied separately: compatible finite chains
have an early complexity floor, repeatedly leave every circuit family below almost Hilbert- space size, return
to that family, and converge to one well-defined infinite- chain dynamics. On every literal n-qubit prefix,
fraction of completed time spent near complete continuous circuit family below b2n /[64(n + 1)]c two-qubit gates
vanishes as n grows. This is stronger than restricting to one fixed gate alphabet or polynomial depth. At

1     One recurrence problem, four structural laws
The quantum recurrence theorem guarantees arbitrarily accurate returns for finite closed systems [1]. Quanti-
tative work now bounds recurrence times for broad finite systems [8, 9], treats mixed states and many-body
concentration [10, 30], and gives exact arithmetic criteria for Floquet recurrence [11]. Many-body recurrences
have been observed in an isolated quantum gas [12], and coupled-qubit tests have been proposed [13].
    Yet “the recurrence time” is not a suﬀicient organizing object. It mixes four questions with different answers:

 persistent carrier −→ phase action and relation hull −→ target metric and observer quotient −→ finite record
                                                                                                                  (1)
Loss deletes transient coordinates. Exact spectral relations cut the ambient phase torus down to the orbit
actually visited. State weights define how distance from return is measured, while an observer graph identifies
phase differences that no selected probe can see. A finite record is finally a restriction of this already reduced
geometry, not a substitute for its completed arithmetic structure.
   This factorization is the paper’s universal principle. Its laws apply to lossy finite dynamics, arbitrary spectral
measures, finite local many-body Hamiltonians, countable pure-point spectra and specified bounded infinite-
chain models. They do not claim that all such systems share one numerical timescale. They identify which
exact object controls each recurrence question and how that object changes under loss, interaction, measurement
and finite observation.

1.1    First-principles scope
Every theorem either starts from an explicitly stated Hamiltonian or contraction, or proves existence inside
an explicitly stated coeﬀicient class. States, effects, metrics and target classes are specified. Deductions use
Born rule, spectral decomposition, unitary evolution, compact-group characters, exact arithmetic relations,
probability measures and operator-norm circuit approximation. Randomness, chaos, eigenstate thermalization
and random-matrix statistics are never hidden premises. Generic conclusions explicitly say generic; exact-model
conclusions display their couplings.
    This is first-principles mathematics conditional only on those model axioms. It is not a microscopic derivation
of a laboratory Hamiltonian from a more fundamental theory of matter. Experiment therefore tests whether a
device realizes stated carrier, spectrum, coupling graph and measurement map. It cannot prove a mathematical
implication, an infinite-time statement or a thermodynamic limit. It can corroborate or falsify claim that
physical device realizes stated model and its finite-system consequences. Section 6 gives concrete protocols and
separates mathematical derivation, model identification and empirical test.

5. Two finite labelled spectra have the same projective relation lattice if and only if every resolved replica detector

   Statements two and three are Law II’s existence and capacity principles. They do not assume a limiting

    Logical dependence matters. Laws I–IV are structural statements: they say which mathematical object
controls survival, phase motion, visible return and inference. Local many-body theorems then ask whether
sparse interactions force Law II’s phase hull to be small. Answer is no for generic nearest-neighbour paths and

complexity postulates. Law IV finally proves that even complete finite-time propagator knowledge cannot
certify physical recurrence exponent; weaker finite records consequently cannot certify completed arithmetic.

6    Experimental tests and falsification targets
Experiments do not prove exact arithmetic, infinite-time or thermodynamic statements. They test a different
proposition: whether a controlled device, within characterized preparation, Hamiltonian and readout error,
realizes a finite member of the stated mathematical family. This distinction is especially important for recurrence
because an undetected detuning can become decisive only beyond the observation window. Hamiltonian-learning
and randomized-measurement methods provide established routes for model calibration and nonlinear observable
estimation [44, 45]. Nine direct tests follow from our laws.




                                                        20
Full-process horizon-instability stress test. Use a programmable three- or four-level system to realize two
diagonal Hamiltonians in same calibrated basis: one with simple commensurate gaps and one candidate whose
gap ratios avoid every near-resonance resolved by chosen spectroscopic aperture. Prepare same state with inde-
pendently certified nonzero population on every level. Process reconstruction throughout [−T, T ], combined with
generator-error propagation, tests bound (126). Beyond T , measure first survival-fidelity returns for decreasing
thresholds and compare effective log–log slopes with 0 and (D − 2)/2. A within-horizon discrepancy exceeding
joint preparation, Hamiltonian and readout error falsifies calibrated pair; a return-time record inconsistent with
exact periodic branch falsifies that branch. Finite data cannot certify non-very-well-approximability or either
asymptotic exponent. That limitation is theorem’s experimental content: experiment can exhibit finite-horizon
indistinguishability followed by divergent return behavior, but cannot convert bounded evidence into proof of
completed recurrence law.

Bound-state mass from stationary return edge. Use a controllable trapped-plus-scattering system and
prepare state whose bound-sector population p is measured independently by spectroscopy or state-selective
release. Sample survival fidelity at randomized times in successively wider centered windows and reconstruct
empirical distribution, including calibrated preparation and readout uncertainty. Theorem 4 predicts upper

finite record cannot certify exact support edge: convergence claim requires model-specific bounds on unresolved

7    Position relative to current results
Kronecker–Weyl phase occupation, consequences of rational independence, and all-order spectral-form-factor
moments are established ingredients [19, 24]. Homometric structures with equal autocorrelation are classical
[40, 41]. The contribution is therefore not torus averaging or homometry in isolation. Mathematical center is
the joined persistent-phase factorization: carrier selection, an exact existence criterion for finite and countable
phase laws, action–constraint capacity conversion, weighted and observer-dependent target geometry, maximal
scalar system and clock quotients, complete resolved tomography, and a sharp finite-record boundary. Explicit
scalar–resolved separation shows why none of these layers can be collapsed into one return time.
    Classical Wiener–Eberlein theory identifies long-time mean squared Fourier–Stieltjes amplitude with sum
of squared atomic masses [2]. Theorem 4 concerns different invariant of full stationary law: its support is
necessarily connected and its maximum is square of total atomic mass. It therefore distinguishes ℓ2 collision
weight from ℓ1 bound-state weight and supplies exact inverse formula for latter. In pure-point case, connected-
support mechanism has classical ancestry: Jessen–Wintner support theorem identifies support of asymptotic
law of a uniformly almost-periodic vector function with closure of its range, which is connected for a continuous
real-time scalar orbit [3]. Our claim is narrower: arbitrary mixed spectral measure reduces exactly to scaled
                                                                    √
pure-point carrier, giving full interval [ρ, p2 ] and inversion p = max supp. Targeted search found no source
stating that mixed-spectrum support-edge package; this remains absence evidence, not proof of priority.
    First-return literature sets a sharper comparison. Campos Venuti computes average spacing of fidelity-
level crossings and explicitly distinguishes it from first recurrence; near exact return, calculation uses Gaussian
approximation [7]. Gupta–Short obtain deterministic Dirichlet upper bounds, including D − 2 phase exponent
for continuous uniform recurrence, without matching generic lower law in local coeﬀicient family [9]. Kotowski–
Oszmaniec prove packing upper bounds and random-diagonal lower bounds and explicitly leave tight recurrence

8    Interpretation and order of limits
Three distinctions prevent misreading the laws. First, completed occupation is a frequency statement, not a
first-return estimate: inverse return density need not equal first hitting time. Second, thermodynamic density
takes T → ∞ at each fixed finite volume before sending volume to infinity. The resulting extinction law says
that recurrence windows occupy a vanishing fraction of completed time; it does not say that an infinite-system
orbit returns in operator norm. The same-field desert theorem adds a deterministic consequence that density
extinction alone does not contain: one volume-uniform speed scale converts exact finite-volume occupation into
arbitrarily late return-free intervals whose certified length diverges; closedness and syndeticity then turn these
into lower bounds on actual consecutive-return gaps. This still is not a first-return distribution, a first-hit
estimate from time zero or an infinite-volume return claim.
    Third, physical realization depends on theorem. Generic graph laws allow arbitrary onsite and edge terms
on the supplied finite graph and do not assume translation invariance. The countable results concern pure-
point diagonal spectra. The l-bit construction supplies a bounded self-adjoint Hamiltonian, strongly continuous
unitary dynamics and uniform finite-volume completion for one absolutely summable diagonal field. Theorem
28 instead supplies standard quasi-local C ∗ dynamics for one noncommuting nearest-neighbour Pauli field and
compact-time convergence of literal finite prefixes. Its absolute summability and position-dependent decay are
essential; no translation-invariant or infinite-volume recurrence claim follows. The exact-return nested path is
static and geometrically nearest-neighbour with a different, commuting arithmetic structure. These hypotheses
are part of models, not hidden claims about all local or disordered systems.
    Circuit complexity is included only where recurrence geometry controls a precisely defined target set. Com-
pleted target occupation does not imply Haar randomization. Theorem 27 adds a pointwise statement: one
static nearest-neighbour orbit remains outside an entire near-Hilbert continuous-circuit target for exponentially
long intervals. A two-sided linear envelope still does not imply monotonicity; Theorem 30 proves both scale-
separated order and an explicit nearby backtrack. Exact return instead enforces the robust reflection law of
Theorem 31.


9    Synthesis: recurrence as persistent phase geometry
Four laws answer different versions of the same physical question. Law I says whether any recurrent signal
remains after loss and gives its exact ceiling. For arbitrary energy spectra it also proves stationary survival values
have one interval of support whose upper edge is squared total bound-state mass. Law II says what completed
motion is possible on the surviving carrier: relations are locked directions, temporal rank is complementary
free capacity, and local interaction can convert one into the other. Law III says which portion of that motion
matters for a target or measurement. Weights set accuracy cost; observer coherences set visible dimension;
scalar survival and resolved replicas retain provably different information. Law IV says where finite data stop:
agreement throughout any prescribed horizon need not fix completed geometry or one later probability.
    This organization keeps apparently different results in one theory rather than one catalogue. Generic local
rank, return-density extinction, countable completion, Born-record recurrence and circuit complexity all ask
how much of the same phase hull is occupied by a specified target. Their dimensions differ because carrier,
constraints, weights, observers and record classes differ. For generic static nearest-neighbour paths this synthesis
reaches every actual state orbit: exact support size s gives survival-fidelity first-return exponent max{s−2, 0}/2,
while same spectral relation freedom also fixes all replica floors. Exact zeros select dimension, positive weights
select metric constants, and observer coherences select visible quotient. First hitting and completed collision
statistics are therefore two projections of same phase geometry for one unchanged Hamiltonian. Vanishing-
tail corollary makes distinction operational: exact zeros choose dimension discontinuously, while small positive
weights can hide that dimension uniformly for all times above their own scale. Finite-resolution effective support
and exact-resolution recurrence exponent are therefore compatible descriptions of different orders of limits, not
competing laws. No extra complexity-dynamics postulate is needed to obtain return-forced reflection, while the
explicit local path shows why reflection does not imply pointwise growth before midpoint. A different static
path supplies the pointwise bridge from rare circuit occupation to exponentially wide hard plateaux through


                                                         25
quantitative local compilation and autonomous backward composition. The same-field theorem supplies missing
finite-to-infinite coherence: early ramp, late hard excursion and recurrence belong to literal restrictions of one
countable Hamiltonian; complete continuous-circuit target has vanishing completed occupation at near-Hilbert
gate budget on every literal prefix; and prefixes converge to one infinite-chain dynamics. Finite-aperture law
bridges completed occupation to laboratory time for every finite spectrum: finite spectroscopic packet splits
exactly into persistent zero-action sheets and nonzero sheets whose correction decays as inverse window length.
It also resolves apparent tension between finite recurrence and thermodynamic extinction: every finite prefix
returns with bounded gaps, but necessary gap scale cannot remain bounded as prefix grows: explicit consecutive
gaps diverge.
    For interested non-specialists, the central message is concrete. A system may look recurrent or nonrecurrent
depending on what survived and what is being measured. Full long-time return distribution reveals total bound-
state weight through its largest supported value, whereas average return sees only how that weight is divided
among individual levels. Even a perfect recording of one return probability can miss hidden phase organization.
Local interactions do not automatically simplify this organization, and increasing a chain can make accurate
returns occupy an extraordinarily small share of long times. For one fixed local field this rarity is operational:
larger prefixes contain arbitrarily long epochs in which no global phase makes evolution close to identity, even
though each prefix eventually returns again; those epochs sit between certified consecutive visits to return tube.
At same time, even complete family of arbitrary continuous two-qubit circuits below b2n /[64(n + 1)]c gates
captures a vanishing long-time share of same finite-prefix evolution. At finite size, same target exclusion can
persist continuously for 2⌊L/2⌋ units of time under one static nearest-neighbour Hamiltonian, not merely at
isolated instants. Nevertheless exact recurrence, when engineered, places a global symmetry on complexity
history. The paper turns these statements into exact laws and marks which parts a finite experiment can test.
Such tests can corroborate or falsify physical realization of model; they cannot convert agreement into proof of
universal mathematical implication.

C.1    Generic component rank
For a connected two-site graph, the allowed edge term is the full Hermitian matrix space on the tensor product.

H     Countable completion and reflection
For a countable maximally projective spectrum, every finite restriction has maximal projective rank. Its annihi-
lator is therefore trivial, so the orbit closure is the full product torus. Removing any bounded initial time does
not change this closure. Two independent coordinates rule out a nonzero exact global return. For a positive
summable phase-defect probe, uniform tail control reduces approximation to finitely many coordinates. Com-
pact minimality then gives a bounded hitting window. Connectedness of the image and continuity of the probe
turn approximate level visits into exact visits for every interior level.
    Absolutely summable diagonal perturbations can be chosen arbitrarily small and maximally projective on
every finite restriction. They are trace class, preserve the maximal diagonal domain, and preserve self-adjointness.
A separable perturbation ui vj has the same properties on a product carrier. Along its aﬀine coupling ray, each
nonzero balanced character can vanish at at most one coupling unless it annihilates both bare spectrum and
direction. The chosen direction has no such nonzero character, leaving only a countable exceptional set. This
proves Theorem 17.
    Finally, autonomous composition gives UR−t = UR Ut∗ . Inverting and reversing a word preserves its length
in an inverse-closed dictionary. Choi-metric unitary invariance and the triangle inequality add only the return
defect ∆R , proving both inequalities in (125). When ∆R = 0, the two bounds are inverse inequalities and give
exact palindromic symmetry.

O.1    Equal local information, opposite global recurrence
There exist two explicit full-rank qutrit states with a common spectrum and positive spectral weights such
that their weighted variance, nonzero Hilbert–Schmidt speed and nonzero symmetric-logarithmic-derivative
quantum Fisher information agree. For the same tick protocol, the first returns at every tick. The second uses
an irrational frequency, has an inverse-resolution lower bound before return and a matching constructive upper
bound. Therefore these standard local information quantities do not determine global recurrence arithmetic.

P.6 Sharp support and forecast error
On r common distinct nodes, equality of moments of orders 0, . . . , r − 1 gives a square Vandermonde system for
the weight difference; its determinant is nonzero. Hence distinct measures matching the first n moments require

max{|y|, |y − 1|} ≥ 1/2. Therefore every forecasting rule has error at least 1/2 on one compatible clock.

Q Global data boundary
Finite moments are not the only incomplete local data. There is no universal classifier of persistent mean recur-
rence from complete polynomial local data on positive-energy spectra with all moments. Likewise, no classifier
based on the complete perturbative jet of every bounded continuous spectral observable, or of every perturbative
spectral-form-factor order, works uniformly over compact positive-spectrum families. The separating families
agree to all specified local or perturbative orders while having opposite global recurrence phases.
   Compact support is the exact positive moment boundary used here. If two probability measures are carried
by one compact interval and have the same Hamburger moment sequence, polynomial density in the continuous
functions on that interval makes their integrals agree on every continuous function; Riesz uniqueness gives
equality of measures.
