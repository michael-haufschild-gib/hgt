                                                          Abstract
         Quantum recurrence is usually studied through separate questions about survival under loss, spectral
     arithmetic, recurrence time, many-body locality, spectral statistics, observability, circuit complexity and

     collision hierarchy. Second, interaction controls the dimension and factorization of time. For arbitrary finite

Public summary. A connected quantum system can be built from static local interactions, generate almost
all of its possible independent phase clocks, spend almost all completed time in states no short circuit can
reproduce, remain detectably nonrandom, and still return. This remains true for one consistent sequence of
growing finite volumes, and fixed-input hard examples can be chosen algorithmically. The same phase geometry
determines how rare the return is, which interaction links can be inferred, and what information a return must
restore.


1    One object behind recurrence
The quantum recurrence theorem guarantees approximate returns for finite closed systems [1]. Quantitative
work has since bounded recurrence times for prescribed states and for simultaneous return of all states [2, 3, 4],

                                                            1
isolated arithmetic mechanisms for exact Floquet periods [5], and observed or proposed recurrences in interacting
many-body devices [6, 7]. A separate line of research studies circuit complexity along quantum evolution. The
expected growth–plateau–recurrence pattern is central to the second law of quantum complexity [8]; saturation
and recurrence have been proved for random circuits and stochastic local Hamiltonian evolution [9], while time-
independent constant-local Hamiltonian ensembles remain eﬀiciently distinguishable from Haar randomness and
fail to form unitary 2-designs [10].
    These results leave a structural question. What is common to recurrence of a state, recurrence of a full chan-
nel, observer-limited return, completed spectral statistics and circuit hardness of one fixed static Hamiltonian?
Our answer is that each is a target problem on the same compact phase hull. The hull is fixed by persistent
spectrum and exact rational relations; interaction fixes its factorization; an observer or preparation model fixes
the target capacity; the group or semigroup law fixes reflection and recovery.

1.1   Contributions and boundaries
The paper proves four laws.
1. Persistence. Loss removes transient phase coordinates before recurrence begins. The surviving carrier and
   its stationary collision hierarchy are exact.
2. Interaction and factorization. Generic projective temporal rank is computed for every finite interaction
   graph. The rank atlas reconstructs the supplied graph, and its factorization law controls all completed
   spectral-form-factor moments.
3. Target capacity. The same Haar target volume governs strict recurrence, observer resolution and robust
   circuit complexity. This produces static-local completed-time complexity plateaux, fixed-input computable
   hard witnesses and complete hard recurrence polygons without Haar randomization.
4. Recovery and information. Returns reflect circuit shells and recover Markovian information, but finite earlier
   records do not identify the completed recurrence geometry.
    The first conceptual identity below is a bridge from compact-group equidistribution, not a standalone novelty
claim. The new mathematical content is in its exact local-network, collision, complexity and recovery instan-
tiations. We do not prove a first-return time, a monotone complexity ramp from time zero, a mixing time,
an infinite-volume recurrence, or a Haar-design statement. These exclusions are consequences of the theorem
domains, not generic limitations language.

8     Discussion
8.1     What is universal
The return-capacity principle is universal at the level at which its hypotheses are universal: persistent au-
tonomous dynamics supplies a compact monothetic phase group; completed occupation is Haar occupation on
that group; targets encode the operational question. Four exact transformations then organize the subject:

      loss selects carrier −→ interaction fixes hull −→ capacity fixes rarity −→ return fixes recovery.       (55)

This is a set of structural laws rather than one scalar law. No single number simultaneously records carrier,
factorization, target geometry and semigroup composition.

8.2     Complexity without randomness
The static-local conclusions separate three notions often conflated in informal discussions. Full projective tempo-
ral rank gives dense phase motion and exact completed occupation. Large target information makes short-circuit
tubes rare. Neither property makes the unitary Haar-random. Indeed constant-local static Hamiltonian ensem-
bles fail even the design criterion [10], while Theorem 15 preserves an explicit local separator at every time.
Computational hardness and Haar randomization are therefore logically and operationally distinct.

8.3     Thermodynamic meaning
For every connected finite volume, global return density depends only on Hilbert dimension, although subsystem
temporal curvature reconstructs the interaction topology. In thermodynamic sequences the return fraction
vanishes doubly exponentially in site number. Theorem 7 realizes the entire sequence on one generic coeﬀicient
assignment rather than retuning each volume. This is still a sequential finite-volume theorem:

                                   first T → ∞ at fixed n,       then n → ∞.                                  (56)

It does not establish recurrence of an infinite quasi-local algebra, nor does it say that experimentally accessi-
ble waiting times follow inverse completed density. Quantitative first-hitting bounds require Diophantine or
probabilistic input of the kind studied in [3, 4].

8.4     Exact open boundary
The paper closes the completed-time plateau, common-time fixed-input existence, arbitrarily late hard cycles,
return-side decline and Markovian recovery. It leaves one central complexity question open: prove a first, fixed-
error, laboratory-time complexity growth law for one ordinary time-independent local Hamiltonian. Known
recurrence dimension, Haar-volume, minimum-gap and static-ensemble coverage arguments cannot supply it.
Likewise, Theorem 15 does not yet instantiate all vertices against a Brown-scale growing circuit heap. These
are distinct finite-time and growing-replica problems.


9     Methods and proof organization
Carrier selection uses the semisimple peripheral decomposition of a finite contraction. Arbitrary spectral types
use Wiener averaging and convolution collision identities. Finite hulls use Pontryagin duality and Kronecker–
Weyl equidistribution. Generic local spectral rank is proved by constructing an all-vertex-good real-symmetric
tree witness, using second-order eigenvalue-response poles to eliminate balanced rational relations, then lifting
through spanning trees and polynomial zero-set genericity. Component collision laws follow by replica expansion
and histogram classification.
    Target capacity combines exact phase-ball bounds with finite dictionaries. The Choi cap is eigenbasis-free
because phase coeﬀicients are uniform in the energy basis. Circuit heaps use pair projections in a bounded-degree
dependence graph. Fixed-input hardness uses a local-Clifford collision average, heavy-coordinate excision, torus
moments, Fatou–Tonelli transfer and computable rational approximation. Prime polygons use finite cyclic phase
grids, replica moments and a stable open-target shadowing argument. Reflection and recovery are consequences
of the group and semigroup laws plus contractivity of Choi and diamond metrics. Finite-record ambiguity uses
binomial parity filters, unitary transport, Krylov tridiagonalization and second quantization.
    Detailed proofs of new laws appear first in the appendices. Full proofs of the earlier carrier, observer and
inference results then follow, so the present PDF is logically self-contained.

point spectra, observer families, local spin systems and finite-record inference. All arguments are deterministic.
No random-matrix premise, numerical experiment or unproved nonresonance assumption is used.

J.1    Replica height and phase-grid readout
Write k = k + − k − with disjoint nonnegative parts. Balancedness gives |k + |1 = |k − |1 =: h(k). Expanding the
rth fidelity replica shows that the character k has positive coeﬀicient exactly when h(k) ≤ r. Therefore its first
possible replica order is h(k), and a replica detects it exactly when k ∈ LE and h(k) ≤ r.
   Each order-r phase polynomial has coordinate Fourier bandwidth at most r. The discrete Fourier transform
on a (2r + 1)-point grid in each cycled coordinate therefore recovers every coeﬀicient exactly. A smaller one-
coordinate grid aliases two admissible frequencies, proving worst-case sharpness. This proves the replica-height
law, exact finite-grid spectroscopy, and its Nyquist boundary.

Theorem 27 (Finite observer capstone). For Hamiltonian phases zi (t) = e−itEi , total squared observer dis-
placement equals E(z(t)). This quantity is zero if and only if phases agree on every edge, equivalently on every
connected component. The range rank of the graph coboundary, and hence visible projective phase capacity, is
r − c, where c is the number of components.
    The identity follows by blockwise expansion. The zero law uses nonnegativity. The rank formula follows
because the kernel of the graph coboundary consists of functions constant on components.
    Synchronizing a spanning forest yields a recurrence by (Q + 1)r−c ticks with explicit squared-distance error.
Independent hard frequencies on a rank-r −c graph show that no smaller universal exponent is possible. Cauchy–
Schwarz controls every Hilbert–Schmidt normalized linear probe by total displacement. An operator contraction
has Hilbert–Schmidt energy at most matrix dimension, giving the square-root dimension conversion; the identity
contraction attains the general factor. A triangle inequality adds two calibration errors. If the resulting tick
horizon is below the certified coherent window, the recurrence is a device-feasible theorem.
    For countable pure-point blocks, the same phase-energy identity holds exactly when the nonnegative edge
series is summable. Truncating to a finite head, synchronizing it and bounding the tail proves quantitative
recurrence and the zero law. In the Hamiltonian specialization, zero means every positive nonzero observer
block is phase locked.

N.1    Equal local information, opposite global recurrence
There exist two explicit full-rank qutrit states with a common spectrum and positive spectral weights such
that their weighted variance, nonzero Hilbert–Schmidt speed and nonzero symmetric-logarithmic-derivative
quantum Fisher information agree. For the same tick protocol, the first returns at every tick. The second uses
an irrational frequency, has an inverse-resolution lower bound before return and a matching constructive upper
bound. Therefore these standard local information quantities do not determine global recurrence arithmetic.

For p < 1, all wp,j are positive. The Krylov vectors ψp , Kψp , . . . , K n ψp are linearly independent by the Vander-
monde determinant. Gram–Schmidt yields a basis starting at ψp , and self-adjointness makes K tridiagonal in

    At p = 1, only one parity of energy levels is occupied. Tridiagonalize the occupied Krylov block and place
the unused levels in a diagonal spectator block. This gives a tridiagonal representative with one zero interface
coupling. Conversely, the endpoint of a connected tridiagonal path is cyclic: the eigenvalue equation propagates
a zero endpoint coordinate through the path. An unused eigenvector orthogonal to the fixed endpoint therefore
forces at least one zero coupling. This proves the exact connectedness dichotomy and the closed-interval nearest-
neighbour realization.

O.6     Sharp support and forecast error
On r common distinct nodes, equality of moments of orders 0, . . . , r − 1 gives a square Vandermonde system for
the weight difference; its determinant is nonzero. Hence distinct measures matching the first n moments require
at least n + 1 nodes. The binomial/parity pair uses exactly n + 1, so the support threshold is sharp.
    Any forecaster receives one input compatible with terminal values zero and one. For its real output y,
max{|y|, |y − 1|} ≥ 1/2. Therefore every forecasting rule has error at least 1/2 on one compatible clock.


P     Global data boundary
Finite moments are not the only incomplete local data. There is no universal classifier of persistent mean recur-
rence from complete polynomial local data on positive-energy spectra with all moments. Likewise, no classifier
based on the complete perturbative jet of every bounded continuous spectral observable, or of every perturbative
spectral-form-factor order, works uniformly over compact positive-spectrum families. The separating families
agree to all specified local or perturbative orders while having opposite global recurrence phases.
   Compact support is the exact positive moment boundary used here. If two probability measures are carried
by one compact interval and have the same Hamburger moment sequence, polynomial density in the continuous
functions on that interval makes their integrals agree on every continuous function; Riesz uniqueness gives
equality of measures.
