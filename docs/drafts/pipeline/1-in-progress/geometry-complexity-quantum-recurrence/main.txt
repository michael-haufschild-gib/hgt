                                                        Abstract
         Recurrence is usually described by a return time. That description conflates four logically different
     operations: irreversible loss selects what remains; exact energy relations determine where the surviving
     phases can move; preparation and measurement determine what counts as a return; and a finite record
     restricts what can be inferred about completed dynamics.
         Our central result is a universal stationary survival law. Let µ be the energy distribution of any normalized
     state under any time-independent self-adjoint Hamiltonian, let p be its total pure-point weight—the bound-
     state population in standard Schrödinger settings—and let Rµ,T be the distribution of the Born survival
     probability when time is sampled uniformly from [−T, T ]. Then Rµ,T converges weakly for every probability
     spectrum. If p > 0, its limit is exactly the stationary return law of the normalized pure-point spectrum, with
     every fidelity multiplied by p2 ; if p = 0, it is a point mass at zero. Moreover
                                                                     √
                                        supp Rµ = [ℓµ , p2 ],    p = max supp Rµ .
     Thus continuum spectral weight disappears from the complete stationary return distribution in one prescribed
     way, and total pure-point population is recovered from its stationary support edge. No discreteness, spectral
     simplicity, nonresonance, randomness, chaos, mixing or locality assumption is used. The factor p is fixed
     before the return law is formed by the spectral projection of the prepared state; it is not introduced by fitting
     the law.
        This carrier principle is Law I. Laws II–IV show how exact energy relations determine the completed
     phase orbit, how preparation support and observer coherences determine which quotient is physically visible,
     and why finite records need not determine the completed return law. As a geometric corollary, the small-
     target occupation exponent of every finite projective spectrum equals its orbit dimension and complements
     exact relation rank. We discharge locality explicitly: generic static nearest-neighbour qudit paths attain
     maximal projective dimension, whereas fixed-range commuting models obey a linear ceiling. For every finite
     spectrum, every fixed survival moment has an explicit finite-window error at most min{1, (T δ)−1 } once δ
     resolves its nonzero replica detunings. An explicit reset-noise channel supplies a second finite-horizon error
     budget directly in Born probabilities. One deliberately inhomogeneous, absolutely summable chain shows
     that finite-prefix recurrence, circuit-hard excursions and standard infinite-volume dynamics can coexist; it is
     an existence witness, not a claim about generic or translation-invariant thermodynamic matter. No infinite-
     time or thermodynamic statement is presented as an empirical fact, and the closed-system stationary law is
     not asserted for uncalibrated open dynamics.
Public summary. A quantum state can contain discrete spectral components that remain available for later
return and continuum components that disperse. In standard quantum-mechanical scattering problems these are
the bound and scattering sectors. The paper proves exactly how that distinction appears in the complete long-
time distribution of survival probability. The continuum contributes no residual stationary spread. The discrete
component supplies the entire distribution, reduced by the square of its independently measured population.
Consequently the largest value supported by the stationary return distribution is exactly that square.
    For finite spectra, relative phases behave like clock hands. Exact energy relations constrain combinations
of their angles, while preparation and measurement decide which combinations matter. This gives a second
exact accounting result: independent phase motion and independent exact constraints partition the available
projective dimension. That geometric identity is a corollary of the recurrence framework, not the evidence for
its physical relevance.
    It also proves a sharp limit on prediction. Even complete knowledge of the evolution over any finite time
interval cannot determine the ultimate high-precision return law: two Hamiltonians can be arbitrarily close
throughout the observed interval yet have opposite asymptotic behavior, from exact periodic return to the
largest possible return exponent. Finally, one-dimensional nearest-neighbour interactions do not by themselves
reduce recurrence dimension, while commuting local structure does. The paper gives finite-window inequalities
that turn calibrated spectra and a stated noise budget into laboratory predictions. Its infinite-chain construction
is intentionally engineered: along that one summable field, finite systems remain recurrent but their return
occupation vanishes as size grows. This proves logical compatibility and failure of a uniform finite-prefix clock;
it does not describe a generic bulk phase or assert recurrence of an infinite system.

Law I proves carrier selection, including the stationary theorem above. Law II determines exact phase constraints.

what a finite record can identify. In physical terms, loss selects the spectrum that survives. Exact relations
determine its completed phase orbit. State support and measurement determine the return target actually seen.
Finite observation determines only a bounded-time record and need not identify the completed orbit. Laws I–IV
prove these four transformations. Locality, circuit targets and thermodynamic limits are then stress tests of the
same framework, not additional universal premises.

1.1     Logical and empirical scope
Theorems are derived from displayed Hamiltonians, contractions, states, measurements, metrics and target
families using standard spectral, compact- group, probability and operator-norm results. A definition is never
counted as evidence for a physical relation: in the central law, the input hψ, Ppp (H)ψi and output Rµ are
specified independently before their relation is proved. Generic statements are marked generic; constructed
examples display their assumptions; and no empirical model is promoted to a universal premise. Mathematical
implications are proved in the appendices. Experiments cannot prove an infinite-time limit or a theorem about
all Hamiltonians. They can test whether a device realizes the stated finite Hamiltonian and whether its measured
finite-resolution consequences agree with the prediction. A failed prediction is not reclassified as an unexplained
sector of the law: it rejects the calibrated model, the measurement model, or both. Section 6 gives protocols
and keeps model calibration, finite-data inference and mathematical deduction separate.

    Logical dependence matters. Laws I–IV are structural statements: they say which mathematical object
controls survival, phase motion, visible return and inference. Local many-body theorems then ask whether
sparse interactions force Law II’s phase hull to be small. Answer is no for generic nearest-neighbour paths and
yes, with exact ceiling, for fixed-range commuting l-bits. First-hit, collision and thermodynamic laws quantify
two sides of this boundary. Born and circuit results are Law III with concrete target families, not independent
complexity postulates. Law IV finally proves that even complete finite-time propagator knowledge cannot
certify physical recurrence exponent; weaker finite records consequently cannot certify completed arithmetic.
One countable static nearest-neighbour field closes finite-to-infinite loop with compatible ramps, circuit-hard
excursions, recurrence and UHF dynamics. One explicit commuting family separately supplies exact recurrence,
reflected descent and failure of pointwise monotonicity.

6    Experimental tests and falsification targets
Experiments do not prove exact arithmetic, infinite-time or thermodynamic statements. They test a different
proposition: whether a controlled device, within characterized preparation, Hamiltonian and readout error,
realizes a finite member of the stated mathematical family. This distinction is especially important for recurrence
because an undetected detuning can become decisive only beyond the observation window. Hamiltonian-learning
and randomized-measurement methods provide established routes for model calibration and nonlinear observable
estimation [50, 51]. Each test must therefore report three logically separate objects: a calibrated finite model,
a theorem-level finite-window bound, and a statistical measurement interval. When open evolution is relevant,
a fourth object—a device-to-ideal channel bound—is required. Agreement outside these stated budgets is not
claimed. Direct tests follow from the laws.

Finite-window survival-moment certificate. Use a calibrated finite D-level platform, prepare known spec-
tral weights wj , choose moment order m, and obtain a conservative positive lower bound δm on every nonzero
replica detuning in (19). Randomize evolution times uniformly in [−T, T ] and estimate the left side of (20).
The model prediction is the explicitly computable collision mass Cm with deterministic window error at most
min{1, (T δm )−1 }, enlarged by independently propagated preparation and readout uncertainty. A confidence
interval disjoint from that certified band falsifies the calibrated finite model. A detuning whose uncertainty
interval contains zero must be classified as unresolved; treating it as nonzero would invalidate the certificate.

Device-to-ideal open-system calibration. Implement a stroboscopic unitary channel U and a controlled
reset branch to realize (9). State and process measurements can test the aﬀine Born law (10) directly over
several effects, including the return effect. Independent calibration of r then predicts the one-step and N -step
operational envelopes (11)–(12). Any measured effect probability outside the corresponding ideal envelope falsi-
fies channel model or calibration. This is a controlled open-system robustness test, not a claim that uncontrolled
decoherence preserves recurrence.

Stationary carrier scaling and bound-state edge. In a controllable trapped-plus-scattering setting, mea-
sure bound-sector population p independently by spectroscopy or state-selective release and compare long-
window empirical survival laws of full state and normalized bound-sector state. Theorem 1 predicts the p2
rescaling in infinite-window limit. Unlike finite-spectrum moment protocol, this arbitrary-spectrum comparison
has no universal convergence rate. It is therefore a sequence of finite-window model checks, not a finite exper-
iment that can certify exact support edge, weak limit or absence of a narrow interior gap. Every report must
include observation window, continuum leakage, unresolved detunings and confidence bands.

Full-process horizon-instability stress test. Use a programmable three- or four-level system to realize two
diagonal Hamiltonians in same calibrated basis: one with simple commensurate gaps and one candidate whose
gap ratios avoid every near-resonance resolved by chosen spectroscopic aperture. Prepare same state with inde-
pendently certified nonzero population on every level. Process reconstruction throughout [−T, T ], combined with
generator-error propagation, tests bound (141). Beyond T , measure first survival-fidelity returns for decreasing
thresholds and compare effective log–log slopes with 0 and (D − 2)/2. A within-horizon discrepancy exceeding
joint preparation, Hamiltonian and readout error falsifies calibrated pair; a return-time record inconsistent with
exact periodic branch falsifies that branch. Finite data cannot certify non-very-well-approximability or either
asymptotic exponent. That limitation is theorem’s experimental content: experiment can exhibit finite-horizon
indistinguishability followed by divergent return behavior, but cannot convert bounded evidence into proof of
completed recurrence law.

Phase-volume corollary on one programmable spectrum. Use a programmable D-level system and
keep D fixed while imposing different numbers of exact balanced energy relations. Ramsey interferometry or
process reconstruction gives ΦE (t). Uniform-time samples and smooth inner/outer approximations to phase
balls bracket FE (ε). The completed-law prediction is

transfers one unit from α to ρ. This is a test of the geometric corollary, not an independent route to validating
the carrier law. Finite data cannot certify rational independence or an asymptotic slope; report window, phase
resolution, spectroscopic uncertainty and integer search aperture.

    The engineered same-field Theorem 31 has no proposed direct experiment. Its simultaneous coupling selection

7    Position relative to current results
Classical Wiener–Eberlein theory identifies long-time mean squared Fourier–Stieltjes amplitude with sum of
squared atomic masses, while the Eberlein decomposition matches the strongly almost-periodic component with
the pure-point part of the underlying measure [4, 5]. Thus pure-point selection and its extension to polynomial
statistics are classical ingredients, not novelty claims. Jessen–Wintner identify support of an asymptotic law of
a uniformly almost-periodic vector function with closure of its range [6]; this supplies the pure-point connected-
support ancestry. Theorem 1 makes a different arbitrary-spectrum statement. Centered-time survival laws
always converge; their entire limit measure equals the normalized pure-point law scaled by p2 ; its support is
[ℓµ , p2 ]; and the stationary-law upper edge recovers total pure-point mass. Only the joined no-gap support and
inverse-edge result is advanced as candidate novelty. Exact-object searches found no earlier statement of that
result, but absence from a search is not proof of priority; no absolute priority claim is made.
     Relations between return rates and dimension are not new in themselves. Gao related mean recurrence statis-
tics to information dimension, and Barreira–Saussol and Saussol proved return-rate/local-dimension identities
under hyperbolic or rapid-mixing assumptions [11, 12, 13]. Kronecker–Weyl phase occupation and consequences


                                                        23
of rational independence are also established ingredients [25, 30]. Accordingly, Theorem 6 is presented as a
premise-complete finite-spectrum corollary, not as discovery of a new recurrence–dimension principle. It identi-
fies the dimension exactly from spectral arithmetic at degeneracies and resonances without mixing or typicality.
    Homometric structures with equal autocorrelation are classical [46, 47]. Their role here is to prove a limit of
scalar stationary data: even the entire survival trace can discard labelled relation geometry. Resolved observa-
tions, local realizations and finite-record obstructions then determine how far the persistent-carrier law survives
changes of observation and scale.
    First-return literature sets a sharper comparison. Campos Venuti computes average spacing of fidelity-
level crossings and explicitly distinguishes it from first recurrence; near exact return, calculation uses Gaussian
approximation [10]. Gupta–Short obtain deterministic Dirichlet upper bounds, including D − 2 phase exponent
for continuous uniform recurrence, without a matching generic lower law in a local Hamiltonian family [15].
Kotowski–Oszmaniec prove packing upper bounds and random-diagonal lower bounds and explicitly leave tight
recurrence for physical dynamics generated by local noncommuting Hamiltonians open [14]. Theorem 10 closes
typical-case path-family version: on every simple chart, almost every full onsite-plus-edge Hamiltonian has
one simultaneous exact first-return law for every normalized state. Exact spectral support size s, not ambient
dimension alone, gives exponent max{s − 2, 0}/2; arbitrary positive weights on same support change only finite
comparison constants. This upgrades effective-support upper bounds to an exact almost-everywhere physical
law, including periodic one- and two-level boundary strata. It does not cover every local Hamiltonian or give
finite-resolution constants uniform over states or parameter space. Theorem 24 goes beyond state support in a
different direction. It gives exact exponent for arbitrary finite weighted matrix-observer families, with effective
dimension r = D − c(G) selected by observer coherence graph, and removes exceptional Hamiltonians once so
every observer may be chosen afterward. Its static-local clause proves existence of one bounded-degree one-
dimensional Hamiltonian carrying this entire observer hierarchy and certified circuit-complexity ramp. The
cited first-return results do not contain the conjunction of an all-observer quantifier, graph-rank exponent and
same-Hamiltonian local ramp. Our result remains almost-everywhere/existential, asymptotic in resolution and
does not provide uniform convergence rate or classify every local Hamiltonian. Kotowski–Oszmaniec also show
that deleting a spectral tail gives a uniformly close effective-support orbit and use that fact for deterministic
upper bounds. Corollary 11 identifies complementary sharp boundary: a positive tail of total mass η keeps
ordinary survival deficit within 4η of eigenstate value for every time, but changes exact first-return exponent
from zero to maximal on same generic local Hamiltonian. The contribution claimed here is the conjunction
of horizon-free scalar closeness, exact coarse no-exit law, maximal asymptotic exponent activation and static
bounded-degree locality. Effective-support continuity and metric Diophantine extremality are not claimed as
new separately.
    Theorem 35 addresses a different inverse question. The cited recurrence bounds fix Hamiltonian and infer
return behavior; truncated Diophantine filling-time theory fixes finite arithmetic information and bounds torus
coverage [8]. Monitored first-detection recurrence instead inserts a projective test after each unitary step and
can have quantized mean return time [9]; it is not unmeasured survival fidelity. The cited results do not show
that every finite-horizon full-propagator neighborhood of every Hermitian Hamiltonian contains, in one common
eigenbasis and for same cyclic state, both zero and maximal physical first-return exponents. Distinct content is
this exact conjunction, not density of rational spectra, metric Diophantine extremality or Duhamel continuity
separately.

dependent nearest-neighbour qubit Pauli field, compatible at every size, with a laboratory lower ramp, arbitrarily

physical operator-norm capture by the complete continuous two-qubit circuit class: one persistent zero-action

literal compatibility across volumes. Candidate novelty concerns this conjunction, not Fejér approximation, sinc

clock can be uniform in volume: a deterministic return-free scale proportional to inverse completed return occu-

of current open program of obtaining recurrence bounds for physical local noncommuting Hamiltonians [14];
Theorem 10 separately gives exact typical-case all-state support-stratified physical first-return exponent in full
finite path family, but neither theorem gives optimal bounds for every local system. The engineered witness is
not evidence for a translation-invariant or finite-density thermodynamic phase. Nor does it prove exponential-
duration complexity growth, near-maximal saturation, typicality or infinite-volume recurrence.
    Theorem 30 addresses a sharper finite-volume frontier. The cited results do not combine one deterministic
time-independent nearest-neighbour path, arbitrary continuous two-qubit gate parameters, fixed operator error,

    These results are also complementary to jump theorems for unitarily invariant random Hamiltonians [40].
They respect the obstruction that constant-local time-independent Hamiltonian ensembles need not become Haar
random [23]. A static geometrically local SFF ramp has recently been proved by a clock embedding [35]; our

ramp.
    Unlike random-Hamiltonian jump results, (138) concerns one explicit deterministic, static, commuting, geo-
metrically nearest-neighbour Hamiltonian. It certifies a strict decrease between two specified nearby times by

read as deterministic pointwise monotonicity, even within a window that has two-sided linear growth envelopes.

piecewise local dynamics require nearly linear space–time gate count [41]; generic commuting two-local Hamilto-

8    Interpretation and order of limits
Three distinctions prevent misreading the laws. First, completed occupation is a frequency statement, not a
first-return estimate: inverse return density need not equal first hitting time. Second, thermodynamic density
takes T → ∞ at each fixed finite volume before sending volume to infinity. The resulting extinction law says
that recurrence windows occupy a vanishing fraction of completed time; it does not say that an infinite-system
orbit returns in operator norm. The same-field desert theorem adds a deterministic consequence that density
extinction alone does not contain: one volume-uniform speed scale converts exact finite-volume occupation into
arbitrarily late return-free intervals whose certified length diverges; closedness and syndeticity then turn these
into lower bounds on actual consecutive-return gaps. This still is not a first-return distribution, a first-hit
estimate from time zero or an infinite-volume return claim.
    Third, physical realization depends on theorem. Generic graph laws allow arbitrary onsite and edge terms
on the supplied finite graph and do not assume translation invariance. The countable results concern pure-
point diagonal spectra. The l-bit construction supplies a bounded self-adjoint Hamiltonian, strongly continuous
unitary dynamics and uniform finite-volume completion for one absolutely summable diagonal field. Theorem
31 instead supplies standard quasi-local C ∗ dynamics for one noncommuting nearest-neighbour Pauli field and
compact-time convergence of literal finite prefixes. Its absolute summability and position-dependent decay are
essential; no translation-invariant or infinite-volume recurrence claim follows. The exact-return nested path is
static and geometrically nearest-neighbour with a different, commuting arithmetic structure. These hypotheses
are part of models, not hidden claims about all local or disordered systems.
    Circuit complexity is included only where recurrence geometry controls a precisely defined target set. Com-
pleted target occupation does not imply Haar randomization. Theorem 30 adds a pointwise statement: one
static nearest-neighbour orbit remains outside an entire near-Hilbert continuous-circuit target for exponentially
long intervals. A two-sided linear envelope still does not imply monotonicity; Theorem 33 proves both scale-
separated order and an explicit nearby backtrack. Exact return instead enforces the robust reflection law of
Theorem 34.


9    Synthesis: recurrence as persistent phase geometry
The common invariant is the stationary return measure itself. Theorem 1 derives it from the actual Born survival
trace and proves that only the persistent spectral carrier survives completed-time sampling. Law I identifies
that carrier under loss and arbitrary spectral type. Law II determines the carrier’s completed phase motion and
exact constraints; (29) is its dimension bookkeeping, not a replacement for the physical law. Law III pushes
the same phase motion through specified states, observers and circuit targets. Law IV proves where finite data
stop: agreement throughout any prescribed horizon need not fix the completed measure, its geometry or one
later probability.
    This organization keeps apparently different results in one theory rather than one catalogue. Generic local
rank, return-density extinction, countable completion, Born-record recurrence and circuit complexity all ask
how much of the same phase hull is occupied by a specified target. Their dimensions differ because carrier,
constraints, weights, observers and record classes differ. For generic static nearest-neighbour paths this synthesis
reaches every actual state orbit: exact support size s gives survival-fidelity first-return exponent max{s−2, 0}/2,
while same spectral relation freedom also fixes all replica floors. Exact zeros select dimension, positive weights
select metric constants, and observer coherences select visible quotient. First hitting and completed collision
statistics are therefore two projections of same phase geometry for one unchanged Hamiltonian. Vanishing-
tail corollary makes distinction operational: exact zeros choose dimension discontinuously, while small positive
weights can hide that dimension uniformly for all times above their own scale. Finite-resolution effective support
and exact-resolution recurrence exponent are therefore compatible descriptions of different orders of limits, not
competing laws. No extra complexity-dynamics postulate is needed to obtain return-forced reflection, while the
explicit local path shows why reflection does not imply pointwise growth before midpoint. A different static
path supplies the pointwise bridge from rare circuit occupation to exponentially wide hard plateaux through


                                                        26
quantitative local compilation and autonomous backward composition. The engineered same-field theorem
proves only a compatibility statement: early ramp, late hard excursion and recurrence can belong to literal
restrictions of one countable summable Hamiltonian while prefixes converge to one infinite-chain dynamics. It
does not promote that construction to a generic thermodynamic law. Finite-window laws bridge completed
occupation to laboratory time for every finite spectrum: resolved survival moments obey (20), and a finite
spectroscopic packet splits exactly into persistent zero-action sheets and nonzero sheets whose correction decays
as inverse window length. Proposition 2 separately carries ideal finite-system predictions to Born records
under one calibrated open-channel model. These bridges resolve apparent tension between finite recurrence
and thermodynamic extinction: every finite prefix returns with bounded gaps, but necessary gap scale cannot
remain bounded as prefix grows: explicit consecutive gaps diverge.
    For interested non-specialists, the central message is concrete. A system may look recurrent or nonrecurrent
depending on what survived and what is being measured. Full long-time return distribution reveals total bound-
state weight through its largest supported value, whereas average return sees only how that weight is divided
among individual levels. Even a perfect recording of one return probability can miss hidden phase organization.
Local interactions do not automatically simplify this organization, and increasing a chain can make accurate
returns occupy an extraordinarily small share of long times. One engineered summable field proves that larger
prefixes can contain arbitrarily long epochs in which no global phase makes evolution close to identity, even
though each prefix eventually returns again. This is a mathematical existence result, not a claim about ordinary
bulk matter. At finite size, same target exclusion can persist continuously for 2⌊L/2⌋ units of time under one
static nearest-neighbour Hamiltonian, not merely at isolated instants. Nevertheless exact recurrence, when
engineered, places a global symmetry on complexity history. The paper turns these statements into exact laws
and identifies which parts have finite-time tests. Resolved spectra give an explicit 1/(T δ) moment error, and
a calibrated reset channel gives an explicit device-to-ideal probability budget. Such tests can falsify physical
realization of a model; they cannot convert agreement into proof of its infinite-time consequences.

For a connected two-site graph, the allowed edge term is the full Hermitian matrix space on the tensor product.

small edge perturbation joins the layer transition graphs. For every old observer site, the first-order response
of a cross-layer matrix element is a rational function of a layer gap. One selected transition contributes a
nonzero simple-pole residue; maximal projective rank prevents cancellation by another oriented gap. Avoiding
the finitely many zeros of these response functions makes every old transition graph connected while preserving
an odd cycle. The local matrix algebra at the new leaf supplies its connectivity and odd cycle.

the old transition graph. Vanishing residues force relation weights to agree along the categorical product of

S Countable completion and reflection
For a countable maximally projective spectrum, every finite restriction has maximal projective rank. Its annihi-
lator is therefore trivial, so the orbit closure is the full product torus. Removing any bounded initial time does
not change this closure. Two independent coordinates rule out a nonzero exact global return. For a positive
summable phase-defect probe, uniform tail control reduces approximation to finitely many coordinates. Com-
pact minimality then gives a bounded hitting window. Connectedness of the image and continuity of the probe
turn approximate level visits into exact visits for every interior level.
    Absolutely summable diagonal perturbations can be chosen arbitrarily small and maximally projective on
every finite restriction. They are trace class, preserve the maximal diagonal domain, and preserve self-adjointness.
A separable perturbation ui vj has the same properties on a product carrier. Along its aﬀine coupling ray, each
nonzero balanced character can vanish at at most one coupling unless it annihilates both bare spectrum and
direction. The chosen direction has no such nonzero character, leaving only a countable exceptional set. This
proves Theorem 19.
    Finally, autonomous composition gives UR−t = UR Ut∗ . Inverting and reversing a word preserves its length
in an inverse-closed dictionary. Choi-metric unitary invariance and the triangle inequality add only the return
defect ∆R , proving both inequalities in (140). When ∆R = 0, the two bounds are inverse inequalities and give
exact palindromic symmetry.
