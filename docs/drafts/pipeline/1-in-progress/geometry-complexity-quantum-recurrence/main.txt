       Persistent phase geometry of quantum recurrence
 Four structural laws for loss, spectral constraints, observability, and finite-horizon inference

                                                      Abstract
         Quantum recurrence is controlled by exact spectral relations, whereas every finite-time experiment is
     continuous under small changes of the Hamiltonian. We show that this mismatch produces a sharp singularity:
     one arbitrarily weak diagonal operator-Schmidt-rank-one interaction can convert the completed recurrence
     geometry of two finite systems from at most additive temporal dimension to the full product-space dimension,
     create at least the product of their non-global spectral capacities in new independent phase directions, and
     simultaneously drive every full-support replica moment to its minimum collision value. Nevertheless every
     operator-normalized Heisenberg observable changes by at most the explicit dimension-free Duhamel bound
     2|t|δ on a perturbation of norm at most δ.
         This singular principle organizes four structural laws. Loss selects the persistent carrier and fixes the
     exact asymptotic return ceiling. Spectral constraints determine completed temporal dimension: for any
     finite interaction graph, generic onsite-plus-edge Hamiltonians have an exact component formula, subsys-
     tem temporal curvature reconstructs the supplied graph, and homogeneous nearest-neighbour qudit paths
     have strict return density doubly exponential in their length. Observability is governed by target capac-
     ity: coherence-graph rank gives the visible exponent, while static connected networks and bounded-weight
     Pauli Hamiltonians obey exact completed-time circuit-target bounds and recurrent hard-epoch laws without
     becoming Haar random. An explicit nested family of static diagonal nearest-neighbour qubit paths has a
     bounded infinite-chain completion, a two-sided linear operator-norm circuit-complexity envelope throughout
     a finite laboratory-time window, an exact factorial-four return, and exact complexity reflection about that
     return. Its actual complexity rises strictly above the zero-complexity endpoints at every ramp point and can-
     not backtrack across suﬀiciently scale-separated laboratory times; exact return forbids global monotonicity.
     Finite-horizon information remains incomplete: one common earlier record, common finite moments and one
     common spectrum are compatible with every later return probability.
         The finite theory extends to countable pure-point spectra. Arbitrarily small domain-preserving trace-
     class perturbations can produce unbounded finite-window temporal rank, dense future projective orbits,
     no nonzero exact global return, and recurrent traversal of the full range of every positive summable phase-
     defect probe. Completed-time thermodynamic densities take the long-time limit at fixed finite volume before
     system size; separately, the nested path has a uniformly convergent static-energy completion. We assert no
     first-return scale, all-pairs pointwise monotonicity of complexity, infinite-volume orbit recurrence, or Haar
     randomization.
Public summary. A tiny interaction may be almost invisible for any fixed duration yet completely reorganize
what a quantum system does over arbitrarily long time. The reason is arithmetic: long-time recurrence depends
on exact relations among energy levels, and exact relations can disappear under an arbitrarily small perturbation.
We turn that observation into quantitative laws for loss, local many-body systems, measurement, computational
complexity and the limits of prediction. For one concrete one-dimensional chain, the same analysis proves that
the number of two-qubit gates needed to reproduce its motion lies between explicit linear bounds for a controlled
time window and obeys the reverse history before an exact return. This is a rigorous envelope, not a claim that
complexity increases at every instant.


1    A singular law behind recurrence
The quantum recurrence theorem guarantees arbitrarily accurate returns for finite closed systems [1]. Quanti-
tative work includes recurrence-time estimates for generic nonintegrable models [2], bounds for general finite
Hamiltonians [3, 4], concentration bounds and many-body recurrence estimates [21], and exact arithmetic cri-
teria in Floquet models [5]. Many-body recurrences have been observed in an isolated quantum gas [6], while
coupled-qubit experiments have been proposed [7]. A separate literature studies proposed complexity growth
laws and static chaotic evolution [8, 11], as well as rigorous complexity saturation and recurrence in random
circuits, stochastic Hamiltonian dynamics and unitarily invariant random generators [9, 23].

3.5     Countable spectra
Theorem 12 (Complete saturated recurrence). Let a countably infinite spectrum have no nonzero finitely
supported balanced integer relation. Relative to every anchor, projective relation lattice is trivial; every future
tail of phase orbit is dense in full countable projective product torus; zero is only exact global return time; and
every strictly positive summable phase-defect probe has lower limit zero, upper limit its absolute maximum, and
crosses every interior level within a uniformly bounded waiting window from every starting time.
    Moreover, every countably infinite real pure-point spectrum admits an arbitrarily operator-norm-small,
domain-preserving, self-adjoint trace-class diagonal perturbation with all these properties and with unbounded
finite-window temporal rank. For a product of two countable spectra, one rank-one separable trace-class
interaction direction can be chosen so that all but a countable set of coupling strengths have complete saturated
law.
   This countable result concerns pure-point diagonal Hamiltonians. It does not assert recurrence for continuous

5     Law IV: finite-horizon records do not determine completed geom-
      etry
Energy-constrained time series can already exhibit severe extrapolation limits [18]. The next theorem gives
a distinct static nearest-neighbour ambiguity in which one common earlier record is compatible with every
terminal return probability.
Theorem 20 (Complete finite-record ambiguity). Fix moment order m, target time T > 0, strict earlier horizon
0 ≤ H < T , and tolerance δ > 0. There exist one normalized initial state, one return projector, one common
earlier record, and a positive nearest-neighbour one-particle Hamiltonian Kp for every p ∈ [0, 1] such that:
1. all Kp have one common spectrum and are unitarily equivalent;
2. their first m energy moments in fixed initial state agree;
3. their return probabilities are uniformly δ-close to common record throughout [−H, H];
4. fixed-protocol return probability at time T is exactly p;
5. construction has a particle-number-preserving nearest-neighbour free-fermion lift with same complete spectrum
   in every occupation sector.
Every forecaster receiving only common record has terminal absolute error at least 1/2 for one compatible system.
   Perfect-return endpoint forces one zero path coupling in fixed nearest-neighbour representation; strict subunit
probabilities admit a connected Jacobi representative. This endpoint rigidity is part of classification, not an
omitted genericity assumption.
   There is a complementary all-depth boundary.
Theorem 21 (Global completion and compact determinacy). No classifier based only on complete polynomial
cyclic-local data decides persistent mean recurrence for all positive-energy spectra with all moments. Even on a
fixed compact positive interval, neither complete perturbative jet of every bounded continuous spectral observable
nor complete all-times perturbative spectral-form-factor jet universally decides nonperturbative recurrence phase.
Conversely, if one representative of a complete moment sequence is supported on a nondegenerate compact
interval, then every probability measure with those moments is identical to it.
  Finite records, complete local moment data and complete perturbative data are distinct information regimes.
Compact exact moments cross determinacy boundary; finite-horizon data do not.


6     Position relative to current results
Abstract implications of rational independence—torus averaging, all-order SFF moments and exponential limit-
ing laws—are not claimed as new [13, 15]. Nor do completed-time density formulas replace current recurrence-
time bounds [3, 4]. Principal new results are joined statements:
1. exact product-sized discontinuity of completed temporal dimension under arbitrarily weak interaction, to-
   gether with a dimension-free finite-time continuity bound and all-replica collision extremality;
2. generic maximal projective rank for arbitrary static finite interaction graphs, simultaneous subsystem graph
   reconstruction, and its nearest-neighbour thermodynamic consequences;
3. complete countable-spectrum saturation under arbitrarily weak domain-preserving trace-class perturbations
   and along a separable interaction ray outside a countable exceptional set;

                                                         8
4. static bounded-weight Pauli recurrence–hardness alternation with a permanent operational separation from
   Haar randomness.
5. one explicit nested static nearest-neighbour qubit path with bounded infinite-chain energy, a robust finite-
   time linear circuit-complexity envelope, exact return and exact reflected descent.
    Complexity result is complementary to saturation theorems for random circuits and stochastic Hamiltonian
evolution [9] and to jump theorems for unitarily invariant random Hamiltonians [23]. It also respects obstruction
that constant-local time-independent Hamiltonian ensembles need not become Haar random [10]. A static
geometrically local SFF ramp has recently been proved by a clock embedding [20]; our completed-time rank and
collision laws address a different regime and do not assert a finite-time random-matrix ramp.
    Hamiltonian-simulation lower bounds provide important comparison points. Worst-case one-dimensional
piecewise local dynamics require nearly linear space–time gate count [24]; generic commuting two-local Hamilto-
nians can require quadratic gate count at short time [25]; and recent coeﬀicient-sensitive optimal lower bounds
use local bounded-degree classical hard instances, with time-independent unit-time constructions and piecewise-
constant extensions to later times [26]. Theorem 18 is complementary: it follows one explicit nested static
one-dimensional family through a linear laboratory window, a bounded thermodynamic completion, an exact
return and the reflected complexity history. It does not claim optimal constants or a general simulation lower
bound.
    Completed target occupation is also distinct from Hamiltonian fast-forwarding and its connection to ex-
ponentially precise measurement [12], and from explicit transcendence-based examples of exponential circuit
complexity [19].


7     Scope and open boundary
All local-network genericity statements allow arbitrary onsite and edge terms on a supplied finite graph. They
do not impose translation invariance. All thermodynamic density statements take T → ∞ at fixed volume
before size limit. All countable results concern pure-point diagonal spectra. Bounded-weight Pauli complexity
theorem is q-local but all-to-all in support. The nested path theorem is static, geometrically nearest-neighbour
and circuit-hard over explicit finite-time windows, but its diagonal couplings decay with position; it is neither
translation invariant nor a uniform-transport model. Its thermodynamic statement concerns bounded static
energy and uniform finite-volume truncation, not infinite-system circuit complexity.
    Principal open boundary is stronger dynamical shape: all-pairs pointwise monotonicity inside the laboratory
window, a parametrically longer growth regime, and a saturation plateau for one uniform-strength noncommut-
ing bounded-degree Hamiltonian are not proved. Completed occupation does not determine first onset, and
inverse completed density is not asserted to be a first-return time. Infinite-volume quasi-local orbit recurrence
remains outside present scope.


8     Proof organization
Appendices give self-contained proofs in order used above. They begin with finite contraction selection and
arbitrary spectral measures, develop projective detuning map and interaction conversion, prove static path and
arbitrary-graph genericity, and then derive completed return densities, observer deadlines, circuit-target bounds,

L.1    Equal local information, opposite global recurrence
There exist two explicit full-rank qutrit states with a common spectrum and positive spectral weights such
that their weighted variance, nonzero Hilbert–Schmidt speed and nonzero symmetric-logarithmic-derivative
quantum Fisher information agree. For the same tick protocol, the first returns at every tick. The second uses
an irrational frequency, has an inverse-resolution lower bound before return and a matching constructive upper
bound. Therefore these standard local information quantities do not determine global recurrence arithmetic.

M.4     Nearest-neighbour path and endpoint rigidity
For p < 1, all wp,j are positive. The Krylov vectors ψp , Kψp , . . . , K n ψp are linearly independent by the Vander-
monde determinant. Gram–Schmidt yields a basis starting at ψp , and self-adjointness makes K tridiagonal in
that basis. Strictly positive spectral weights make every forward Jacobi coupling nonzero.
    At p = 1, only one parity of energy levels is occupied. Tridiagonalize the occupied Krylov block and place
the unused levels in a diagonal spectator block. This gives a tridiagonal representative with one zero interface
coupling. Conversely, the endpoint of a connected tridiagonal path is cyclic: the eigenvalue equation propagates
a zero endpoint coordinate through the path. An unused eigenvector orthogonal to the fixed endpoint therefore
forces at least one zero coupling. This proves the exact connectedness dichotomy and the closed-interval nearest-
neighbour realization.

M.6     Sharp support and forecast error
On r common distinct nodes, equality of moments of orders 0, . . . , r − 1 gives a square Vandermonde system for
the weight difference; its determinant is nonzero. Hence distinct measures matching the first n moments require
at least n + 1 nodes. The binomial/parity pair uses exactly n + 1, so the support threshold is sharp.
    Any forecaster receives one input compatible with terminal values zero and one. For its real output y,
max{|y|, |y − 1|} ≥ 1/2. Therefore every forecasting rule has error at least 1/2 on one compatible clock.

N      Global data boundary
Finite moments are not the only incomplete local data. There is no universal classifier of persistent mean recur-
rence from complete polynomial local data on positive-energy spectra with all moments. Likewise, no classifier
based on the complete perturbative jet of every bounded continuous spectral observable, or of every perturbative
spectral-form-factor order, works uniformly over compact positive-spectrum families. The separating families
agree to all specified local or perturbative orders while having opposite global recurrence phases.
   Compact support is the exact positive moment boundary used here. If two probability measures are carried
by one compact interval and have the same Hamburger moment sequence, polynomial density in the continuous
functions on that interval makes their integrals agree on every continuous function; Riesz uniqueness gives
equality of measures.
