       Persistent phase geometry of quantum recurrence
 Four structural laws for loss, spectral constraints, observability, and finite-horizon inference

                                                        Abstract
         Quantum recurrence is often compressed into one question—when does a state return?—although loss,
     spectral constraints, unequal state weights, restricted measurements and finite records change different parts
     of the answer. We prove that these mechanisms form one persistent phase geometry with four successive
     laws. Loss selects the exact spectral carrier that can recur. Integer-character action determines the carrier’s
     projective phase hull: zero-action relations and independent temporal directions partition the full capacity,
     with ranks summing to D − 1. Weights pull a target metric onto this hull, while an observer’s coherence
     graph selects its visible quotient. Finite records determine finite-time behavior but need not determine the
     completed relation geometry.
         This framework gives exact results at each level: late-return ceilings for finite lossy dynamics; character-
     complete finite and countable phase laws; sharp weighted deadlines; observer-compressed Born-record returns;
     arbitrary-spectrum collision identities; and complete finite-record ambiguity. It also identifies the maximal
     information retained by common experiments. All scalar survival-return data, even under every probability
     law of observation times, determine exactly the spectral difference measure and forget clock orientation.
     Resolved replica measurements instead recover the full projective relation lattice. Two explicit uniform nine-
     level spectra prove strict separation: their scalar survival fidelity agrees at every time, but a finite resolved
     detector distinguishes their hidden phase constraints.
         The same laws remain nontrivial under locality and increasing system size. For generic static onsite-plus-
     edge Hamiltonians on every finite interaction graph, temporal rank equals the sum of maximal component
     ranks simultaneously for all induced subsystems; pairwise rank curvature reconstructs supplied graph adja-
     cency. On every open simple-spectrum chart of the full static nearest-neighbour n-qudit path family, one
     conull set of Hamiltonians simultaneously attains all D − 1 relative phase directions, sharp stroboscopic and
     first-gap-clock exponents D − 1 and D − 2, every positive-weight replica floor, and zero-only exact projective
     return. Its strict completed return density is exponentially small in D = q n and doubly exponentially small
     in path length. An exact commuting l-bit classification shows why this is a locality theorem rather than
     a parameter-counting accident: geometric locality alone does not compress phase capacity, whereas fixed-
     range commuting structure does. Separate countable and bounded infinite-chain theorems establish uniform
     thermodynamic completion and strongly continuous Hilbert-space dynamics. Finally, target geometry yields
     static circuit-occupation laws and a single-field finite-to-thermodynamic lifecycle. There exists one count-
     able, static nearest-neighbour qubit Pauli field, fixed before system size is chosen, whose every finite prefix is
     maximally projective and syndetically recurrent, whose paired prefixes obey a quantitative linear laboratory
     complexity floor, and whose suﬀiciently large prefixes undergo arbitrarily late near-Hilbert circuit-hard ex-
     cursions with consecutive re-entry. The same absolutely summable coeﬀicients define a bounded self-adjoint
     element of the qubit UHF algebra; finite-prefix Schrödinger and Heisenberg dynamics converge uniformly on
     compact time intervals to its unitary and point-norm continuous automorphism groups. A separate explicit
     commuting path supplies exact return, exact reflected descent, and a proved failure of pointwise monotonicity
     inside its excursion window.
Public summary. Quantum systems can revisit earlier states, but a return is not governed by one universal
clock. Some information may have been lost permanently; surviving energy components may obey hidden
relations; a chosen detector may see only some of their relative phases; and a finite record may be compatible
with radically different futures. This paper separates these effects and proves how they fit together.
    The main conceptual result is a hierarchy of information. A standard survival measurement can be perfect
for its purpose yet blind to part of recurrence structure. We give two concrete nine-level systems with exactly
same return probability at every time, while a more resolved multi-copy measurement tells them apart. Thus
observing all values of one return signal does not amount to learning all temporal organization inside a quantum
system.
    Spatial simplicity does not remove this organization. Generic static nearest-neighbour chains use every
available relative phase direction: an n-site chain can have only linearly many local couplings yet an exponential
number of rationally independent spectral gaps. This makes a high-accuracy return a simultaneous alignment
problem in q n − 1 directions, so its share of long times shrinks doubly exponentially with n. Commuting

1     One recurrence problem, four structural laws
The quantum recurrence theorem guarantees arbitrarily accurate returns for finite closed systems [1]. Quanti-
tative work now bounds recurrence times for broad finite systems [3, 4], treats mixed states and many-body
concentration [5, 25], and gives exact arithmetic criteria for Floquet recurrence [6]. Many-body recurrences have
been observed in an isolated quantum gas [7], and coupled-qubit tests have been proposed [8].
    Yet “the recurrence time” is not a suﬀicient organizing object. It mixes four questions with different answers:

 persistent carrier −→ phase action and relation hull −→ target metric and observer quotient −→ finite record
                                                                                                                  (1)
Loss deletes transient coordinates. Exact spectral relations cut the ambient phase torus down to the orbit
actually visited. State weights define how distance from return is measured, while an observer graph identifies
phase differences that no selected probe can see. A finite record is finally a restriction of this already reduced
geometry, not a substitute for its completed arithmetic structure.
   This factorization is the paper’s universal principle. Its laws apply to lossy finite dynamics, arbitrary spectral
measures, finite local many-body Hamiltonians, countable pure-point spectra and specified bounded infinite-
chain models. They do not claim that all such systems share one numerical timescale. They identify which
exact object controls each recurrence question and how that object changes under loss, interaction, measurement
and finite observation.

1.1    First-principles scope
Every theorem either starts from an explicitly stated Hamiltonian or contraction, or proves existence inside
an explicitly stated coeﬀicient class. States, effects, metrics and target classes are specified. Deductions use
Born rule, spectral decomposition, unitary evolution, compact-group characters, exact arithmetic relations,
probability measures and operator-norm circuit approximation. Randomness, chaos, eigenstate thermalization
and random-matrix statistics are never hidden premises. Generic conclusions explicitly say generic; exact-model
conclusions display their couplings.
    This is first-principles mathematics conditional only on those model axioms. It is not a microscopic derivation
of a laboratory Hamiltonian from a more fundamental theory of matter. Experiment therefore tests whether a
device realizes stated carrier, spectrum, coupling graph and measurement map. It cannot prove an infinite-time
or thermodynamic theorem, but it can falsify corresponding finite-system predictions. Section 6 gives concrete
protocols and separates these two kinds of claim.

    Logical dependence matters. Laws I–IV are structural statements: they say which mathematical object
controls survival, phase motion, visible return and inference. Local many-body theorems then ask whether sparse
interactions force Law II’s phase hull to be small. Answer is no for generic nearest-neighbour paths and yes, with
exact ceiling, for fixed-range commuting l-bits. First-hit, collision and thermodynamic laws quantify two sides
of this boundary. Born and circuit results are Law III with concrete target families, not independent complexity
postulates. Law IV finally explains why finite records cannot certify completed arithmetic. One countable static
nearest-neighbour field closes finite-to-infinite loop with compatible ramps, circuit-hard excursions, recurrence
and UHF dynamics. One explicit commuting family separately supplies exact recurrence, reflected descent and
failure of pointwise monotonicity.

3.8     Countable spectra
Theorem 15 (Complete saturated recurrence). Let a countably infinite spectrum have no nonzero finitely
supported balanced integer relation. Relative to every anchor, projective relation lattice is trivial; every future
tail of phase orbit is dense in full countable projective product torus; zero is only exact global return time; and
every strictly positive summable phase-defect probe has lower limit zero, upper limit its absolute maximum, and
crosses every interior level within a uniformly bounded waiting window from every starting time.
    Moreover, every countably infinite real pure-point spectrum admits an arbitrarily operator-norm-small,
domain-preserving, self-adjoint trace-class diagonal perturbation with all these properties and with unbounded
finite-window temporal rank. For a product of two countable spectra, one rank-one separable trace-class
interaction direction can be chosen so that all but a countable set of coupling strengths have complete saturated
law.
   This countable result concerns pure-point diagonal Hamiltonians. It does not assert recurrence for continuous
spectra or a global return of an infinite many-body system.

6    Experimental tests and falsification targets
Experiments do not prove exact arithmetic, infinite-time or thermodynamic statements. They test a different
proposition: whether a controlled device, within characterized preparation, Hamiltonian and readout error,
realizes a finite member of the stated mathematical family. This distinction is especially important for recurrence
because an undetected detuning can become decisive only beyond the observation window. Hamiltonian-learning
and randomized-measurement methods provide established routes for model calibration and nonlinear observable
estimation [36, 37]. Four direct tests follow from our laws.

Scalar blindness versus resolved recurrence. Implement diagonal nine-level Hamiltonians with energies
E + and E − in (8), prepare uniform superposition, and measure survival probability across same time grid. Law
III predicts identical scalar traces for every time and therefore for every randomized sampling clock. A second
run measures the resolved character in (10), using a multi-copy interferometric or randomized-measurement
realization appropriate to platform. That record separates two devices. Experimental target is not mere
recurrence: it is simultaneous scalar agreement and resolved disagreement. A statistically significant scalar
mismatch after calibrated errors would falsify claimed implementation; failure of resolved separation would
falsify either Hamiltonian calibration or measurement map.

Observer-graph quotient. For one calibrated finite Hamiltonian, choose two probe families whose coherence
graphs differ by a controlled bridge. Prepare the same states, record the same Born observables, and compare
return windows. Theorem 19 predicts which phase differences become visible, exact visible dimension D − c,
and graph–resolution deadline. Existing many-body recurrence experiments already illustrate why recurrence
is observable-relative [7]; the present law turns that qualitative choice into a rank and a finite certificate. Finite
data can test deadline and zero-set predictions but cannot certify absence of every unmeasured arithmetic
relation.

Local interaction reconstruction and return-density scaling. On a programmable qudit chain or small
interaction graph, perform calibrated spectroscopy on induced one- and two-site subsystems. Candidate tempo-
ral ranks give pair curvature (29); positive curvature should track programmed edges. Across increasing path

Complexity excursion, exact return and reflection. Implement commuting controlled-phase path (76)

explicitly calibrated coeﬀicient witness and finite-size error bounds for chosen observables. We therefore attach
no experimental confirmation claim to its infinite-volume clause.

7    Position relative to current results
Kronecker–Weyl phase occupation, consequences of rational independence, and all-order spectral-form-factor
moments are established ingredients [14, 19]. Homometric structures with equal autocorrelation are classical
[32, 33]. The contribution is therefore not torus averaging or homometry in isolation. Mathematical center is
the joined persistent-phase factorization: carrier selection, an exact existence criterion for finite and countable
phase laws, action–constraint capacity conversion, weighted and observer-dependent target geometry, maximal
scalar system and clock quotients, complete resolved tomography, and a sharp finite-record boundary. Explicit
scalar–resolved separation shows why none of these layers can be collapsed into one return time.
    That hierarchy has following physical realizations and consequences:
1. loss selects an exact persistent carrier, while arbitrary spectral types obey one collision hierarchy and exact
   approximate-versus-periodic recurrence classification;
2. completed temporal dimension has a product-sized discontinuity under arbitrarily weak interaction, together
   with a dimension-free finite-time continuity bound and all-replica collision extremality;
3. generic maximal projective rank for every static finite interaction graph, simultaneous subsystem graph
   reconstruction, sharp same-draw D − 1/D − 2 first-hit clocks on nearest-neighbour paths, exact commuting-
   local compression boundary, and thermodynamic consequences;
4. complete countable-spectrum saturation under arbitrarily weak domain-preserving trace-class perturbations
   and along a separable interaction ray outside a countable exceptional set, together with a bounded infinite-
   chain phase cascade;
5. same-record post-exit recurrence for arbitrary finite Hermitian Born histories, with least deadline inside
   exact graph–resolution certificate family, and a local finite-record construction compatible with every later
   return probability;
6. static circuit-target occupation and recurrent-hardness laws, plus one countable nearest-neighbour coeﬀicient
   field whose literal prefixes share a laboratory complexity floor, near-Hilbert circuit-hard excursion–re-entry,
   syndetic projective recurrence and compact-time convergence to one UHF dynamics; and one explicit com-
   muting path with a two-sided finite-time linear envelope, exact return, exact reflected descent, and a certified
   failure of pointwise monotonicity inside that envelope.
    Completed-time densities complement rather than replace current first-return bounds [3, 4]. Pure- and
mixed-state return theorems do not by themselves supply same-record Born escape and return with an observer-
compressed certificate [5, 3]. Likewise, higher-order autocorrelation results address reconstruction from corre-
lation data [34, 35]; Theorem 17 instead identifies exact maximal quantum survival quotients and proves that
resolved recurrence recovers precisely the projective spectral relation lattice.

                                                         15
    Full growth–saturation–recurrence theorems currently concern random circuits and stochastic Hamiltonian
evolution [11]. A static, time-independent, translation-invariant one-dimensional Hamiltonian can support ex-
ponentially long complexity growth through universal encoded simulation, but known proof uses computational-
complexity assumptions [10]. Theorem 24 addresses a different frontier: unconditional existence of one ordinary
nearest-neighbour qubit Pauli field, compatible at every size, with a laboratory lower ramp, arbitrarily late
near-Hilbert circuit-free intervals with consecutive re-entry, syndetic finite-volume recurrence and a standard
UHF thermodynamic dynamics. It does not prove exponential-duration growth, near-maximal saturation, a
recurrence timescale uniform in volume, typicality, translation invariance or infinite-volume recurrence.
    These results are also complementary to jump theorems for unitarily invariant random Hamiltonians [27].
They respect the obstruction that constant-local time-independent Hamiltonian ensembles need not become Haar
random [12]. A static geometrically local SFF ramp has recently been proved by a clock embedding [24]; our
completed-time rank and collision laws address a different regime and do not assert a finite-time random-matrix
ramp.
    Unlike random-Hamiltonian jump results, (86) concerns one explicit deterministic, static, commuting, geo-
metrically nearest-neighbour Hamiltonian. It certifies a strict decrease between two specified nearby times by
integer-separated upper and lower bounds. Thus proposed “second-law” behavior of complexity [9] cannot be
read as deterministic pointwise monotonicity, even within a window that has two-sided linear growth envelopes.
    Hamiltonian-simulation lower bounds provide important comparison points. Worst-case one-dimensional
piecewise local dynamics require nearly linear space–time gate count [28]; generic commuting two-local Hamilto-
nians can require quadratic gate count at short time [29]; and recent coeﬀicient-sensitive optimal lower bounds
use local bounded-degree classical hard instances, with time-independent unit-time constructions and piecewise-
constant extensions to later times [30]. Theorem 26 is complementary: it follows one explicit nested static
one-dimensional family through a linear laboratory window, a bounded thermodynamic completion, an exact
return and the reflected complexity history. It does not claim optimal constants or a general simulation lower
bound.
    Completed target occupation is also distinct from Hamiltonian fast-forwarding and its connection to ex-
ponentially precise measurement [13], and from explicit transcendence-based examples of exponential circuit
complexity [23].


8    Interpretation and order of limits
Three distinctions prevent misreading the laws. First, completed occupation is a frequency statement, not a
first-return estimate: inverse return density need not equal first hitting time. Second, thermodynamic density
takes T → ∞ at each fixed finite volume before sending volume to infinity. The resulting extinction law says
that recurrence windows occupy a vanishing fraction of completed time; it does not say that an infinite-system
orbit returns in operator norm.
    Third, physical realization depends on theorem. Generic graph laws allow arbitrary onsite and edge terms
on the supplied finite graph and do not assume translation invariance. The countable results concern pure-
point diagonal spectra. The l-bit construction supplies a bounded self-adjoint Hamiltonian, strongly continuous
unitary dynamics and uniform finite-volume completion for one absolutely summable diagonal field. Theorem
24 instead supplies standard quasi-local C ∗ dynamics for one noncommuting nearest-neighbour Pauli field and
compact-time convergence of literal finite prefixes. Its absolute summability and position-dependent decay are
essential; no translation-invariant or infinite-volume recurrence claim follows. The exact-return nested path is
static and geometrically nearest-neighbour with a different, commuting arithmetic structure. These hypotheses
are part of models, not hidden claims about all local or disordered systems.
    Circuit complexity is included only where recurrence geometry controls a precisely defined target set. Com-
pleted target occupation does not imply Haar randomization, and a two-sided linear envelope does not imply
monotonicity; Theorem 26 proves both scale-separated order and an explicit nearby backtrack. Exact return
instead enforces the robust reflection law of Theorem 27.


9    Synthesis: recurrence as persistent phase geometry
Four laws answer different versions of the same physical question. Law I says whether any recurrent signal
remains after loss and gives its exact ceiling. Law II says what completed motion is possible on the surviving
carrier: relations are locked directions, temporal rank is complementary free capacity, and local interaction can
convert one into the other. Law III says which portion of that motion matters for a target or measurement.
Weights set accuracy cost; observer coherences set visible dimension; scalar survival and resolved replicas retain
provably different information. Law IV says where finite data stop: agreement throughout any prescribed
horizon need not fix completed geometry or one later probability.



                                                       16
    This organization keeps apparently different results in one theory rather than one catalogue. Generic local
rank, return-density extinction, countable completion, Born-record recurrence and circuit complexity all ask
how much of the same phase hull is occupied by a specified target. Their dimensions differ because carrier,
constraints, weights, observers and record classes differ. No extra complexity-dynamics postulate is needed
to obtain return-forced reflection, while the explicit local path shows why reflection does not imply pointwise
growth before midpoint. The same-field theorem supplies missing finite-to-infinite coherence: early ramp, late
hard excursion and recurrence belong to literal restrictions of one countable Hamiltonian, and those restrictions
converge to one infinite-chain dynamics.
    For interested non-specialists, the central message is concrete. A system may look recurrent or nonrecurrent
depending on what survived and what is being measured; even a perfect recording of one return probability
can miss hidden phase organization. Local interactions do not automatically simplify this organization, and
increasing a chain can make accurate returns occupy an extraordinarily small share of long times. Nevertheless
exact recurrence, when engineered, places a global symmetry on complexity history. The paper turns these
statements into exact laws and marks which parts a finite experiment can test.

For a countable maximally projective spectrum, every finite restriction has maximal projective rank. Its annihi-
lator is therefore trivial, so the orbit closure is the full product torus. Removing any bounded initial time does
not change this closure. Two independent coordinates rule out a nonzero exact global return. For a positive
summable phase-defect probe, uniform tail control reduces approximation to finitely many coordinates. Com-
pact minimality then gives a bounded hitting window. Connectedness of the image and continuity of the probe
turn approximate level visits into exact visits for every interior level.

Independent hard frequencies on a rank-r −c graph show that no smaller universal exponent is possible. Cauchy–
Schwarz controls every Hilbert–Schmidt normalized linear probe by total displacement. An operator contraction
has Hilbert–Schmidt energy at most matrix dimension, giving the square-root dimension conversion; the identity
contraction attains the general factor. A triangle inequality adds two calibration errors. If the resulting tick
horizon is below the certified coherent window, the recurrence is a device-feasible theorem.
    For countable pure-point blocks, the same phase-energy identity holds exactly when the nonnegative edge
series is summable. Truncating to a finite head, synchronizing it and bounding the tail proves quantitative
recurrence and the zero law. In the Hamiltonian specialization, zero means every positive nonzero observer
block is phase locked.

N.1     Equal local information, opposite global recurrence
There exist two explicit full-rank qutrit states with a common spectrum and positive spectral weights such
that their weighted variance, nonzero Hilbert–Schmidt speed and nonzero symmetric-logarithmic-derivative
quantum Fisher information agree. For the same tick protocol, the first returns at every tick. The second uses
an irrational frequency, has an inverse-resolution lower bound before return and a matching constructive upper
bound. Therefore these standard local information quantities do not determine global recurrence arithmetic.

O.4    Nearest-neighbour path and endpoint rigidity
For p < 1, all wp,j are positive. The Krylov vectors ψp , Kψp , . . . , K n ψp are linearly independent by the Vander-
monde determinant. Gram–Schmidt yields a basis starting at ψp , and self-adjointness makes K tridiagonal in
that basis. Strictly positive spectral weights make every forward Jacobi coupling nonzero.
    At p = 1, only one parity of energy levels is occupied. Tridiagonalize the occupied Krylov block and place
the unused levels in a diagonal spectator block. This gives a tridiagonal representative with one zero interface
coupling. Conversely, the endpoint of a connected tridiagonal path is cyclic: the eigenvalue equation propagates
a zero endpoint coordinate through the path. An unused eigenvector orthogonal to the fixed endpoint therefore
forces at least one zero coupling. This proves the exact connectedness dichotomy and the closed-interval nearest-
neighbour realization.

O.6    Sharp support and forecast error
On r common distinct nodes, equality of moments of orders 0, . . . , r − 1 gives a square Vandermonde system for
the weight difference; its determinant is nonzero. Hence distinct measures matching the first n moments require
at least n + 1 nodes. The binomial/parity pair uses exactly n + 1, so the support threshold is sharp.
    Any forecaster receives one input compatible with terminal values zero and one. For its real output y,
max{|y|, |y − 1|} ≥ 1/2. Therefore every forecasting rule has error at least 1/2 on one compatible clock.

P     Global data boundary
Finite moments are not the only incomplete local data. There is no universal classifier of persistent mean recur-
rence from complete polynomial local data on positive-energy spectra with all moments. Likewise, no classifier
based on the complete perturbative jet of every bounded continuous spectral observable, or of every perturbative
spectral-form-factor order, works uniformly over compact positive-spectrum families. The separating families
agree to all specified local or perturbative orders while having opposite global recurrence phases.
   Compact support is the exact positive moment boundary used here. If two probability measures are carried
by one compact interval and have the same Hamburger moment sequence, polynomial density in the continuous
functions on that interval makes their integrals agree on every continuous function; Riesz uniqueness gives
equality of measures.
