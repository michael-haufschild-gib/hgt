# Factorial completion of the divisible cofactor rows

At the report's sharp cut \(Q=\lfloor2\sqrt n\rfloor\), every cofactor through \(V\) can be removed at cost \(O_\varepsilon(V\sqrt n\,n^\varepsilon)\), at the original integer \(n\). Set \(V=\lceil\sqrt{4n/Q}\rceil\). The cost is \(O_\varepsilon(n^{3/4+\varepsilon})\). The surviving primitive cofactors satisfy \(V<v\le V^2\). Each is either prime or has all its prime factors at most \(V\) and squarefree radical larger than \(V\).

Write \(A_v\) for the right-minus-left ordinary prime price over \(pv\in(3n,4n],(2n,3n]\), with \(p>Q\) and \(p\nmid n\). Write \(L_v=(4n/v-Q)_+-2(3n/v-Q)_++(2n/v-Q)_+\). For \(n>1,\ 1\le Q\le 2n,\ V\ge1\), put \(\kappa_V(v)=\sum_{e\mid v,\,e\le V}\mu(e)\). The paper deduction gives
\[
 D(n)=\sum_{\substack{V<v\le4n/Q\\(v,n)=1}}\kappa_V(v)(A_v-L_v)
 +O\!\left(2^{\omega(n)}[VQ+\sqrt{nV}+V\log(4n)]\right).
\]
The implicit constant is absolute. The complete rows include their unit cofactor. Its length vanishes and its prime price equals \(-D(n)\). All other cofactors through \(V\) have coefficient zero.

**One complete divisor row.** For \(r\ge1\) let
\[
 F_r=\log\frac{\lfloor4n/r\rfloor!\,\lfloor2n/r\rfloor!}{\lfloor3n/r\rfloor!^2}.
\]
Legendre's identity gives \(F_r=\sum_s\Lambda(s)[\lfloor4n/(rs)\rfloor-2\lfloor3n/(rs)\rfloor+\lfloor2n/(rs)\rfloor]\). Every bracket has absolute value at most one. Keeping ordinary primes \(p>Q\) with \(p\nmid n\) costs at most \(\psi(Q)+\psi(4n/r)-\theta(4n/r)+\log n\). This prime sum is exactly \(\sum_{r\mid v}A_v\), by writing \(v=rw\) and counting all positive \(w\).

For \(x=n/r\), the factorial ratio satisfies \(F_r=c_0x+O(1+\log(4n))\). Rounding \(x\) down changes each factorial argument by at most three, and the constant in the integer factorial ratio is bounded. For the length sum, the set of \(w\) with \(L_{rw}>y\) has endpoints \(2x/(Q-y)\) and \(4x/(Q+y)\) for \(0<y<Q/3\). Its count differs from its length by at most one, apart from endpoint heights of measure zero. Integrating gives \(\sum_{r\mid v}L_v=c_0x+O(Q/3)\) for every real \(x>0\). The complete centered row therefore costs \(O(Q+\sqrt{n/r}+\log(4n))\), uniformly in \(r\). The existing square-root slice proof uses the same constant for all eligible divisors. **Recombine at the same midpoint.** For \(e\le V\) coprime to \(n\), the primitive \(e\)-divisible row is the sum of the complete \(ed\)-divisible rows with weights \(\mu(d)\) over \(d\mid n\). For other \(e\) the primitive row is empty. Multiply by \(\mu(e)\), sum over \(e\le V\), then remove the unit. The proper-power terms cost at most a constant times \(2^{\omega(n)}\sqrt n\sum_{e\le V}e^{-1/2}\le 2^{1+\omega(n)}\sqrt{nV}\). This gives the stated bound and preserves all three sharp endpoints. The corresponding discrete center for the uncentered prime prices is \(\sum_{e\le V,(e,n)=1}\mu(e)\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)F_{ed}\).

The discrete center differs from \(c_0\varphi(n)\sum_{e\le V,(e,n)=1}\mu(e)/e\) by \(O(V\,2^{\omega(n)}\log(4n))\). This follows from \(F_{ed}=c_0n/(ed)+O(\log(4n))\) and the totient divisor identity. Thus the original totient center can also be read directly after the projection.

If cofactors are unrestricted, omit the \(d\mid n\) sum. The unit still gives \(-D(n)\); the centered projected price then has error \(O(VQ+\sqrt{nV}+V\log(4n))\). At the square-root prime cut and fourth-root cofactor cut this is \(O(n^{3/4})\). The primitive price above retains its factor \(2^{\omega(n)}\).

**Survivors.** If \(p>V\) divides \(v\le V^2\), every divisor of \(v\) through \(V\) divides \(v/p\le V\). Hence the coefficient equals one for \(v=p\) and zero otherwise. For smooth \(v\) whose radical is at most \(V\) the complete Möbius sum vanishes. At the first gate, \(V=\lfloor n^{1/3}\rfloor\) also gives an admissible error for every \(t>5/6\). At \(1/2<t<1\), any \(V\ll n^\delta\) with \(\delta<t-1/2\) preserves the target error. At \(V=\lfloor n^{1/3}\rfloor\), the complementary divisor identity \(\kappa_V(v)=-\sum_{h\mid v,\,h<v/V}\mu(v/h)\) puts the remaining sum on products \(pbh\), with \(b>V\), \(h\ll n^{1/6}\) and \(p>Q\). The coefficient on \(b\) is exactly \(-\mu(b)\). Since \(p^2>4n\), one has \(p\nmid b\), so this is \(\mu(pb)\). For each \(h\) the prime-weighted part is the signed count of squarefree \(k=pb\) with \(Q<P^+(k)<k/V\), weighted by \(\log P^+(k)\), with \(hk\) in the original two intervals and \((hk,n)=1\). Here \(P^+(k)\) denotes the largest prime factor. The upper bound for this joint sum is open. **Disjoint ranges of ordinary primes.** For any integer \(k\ge1\) set \(B_j=(4n)^{j/(k+1)}\). For \(j=1,\ldots,k\) use \(Q_j=\lfloor B_j\rfloor\) and \(V_j=\lfloor4n/B_{j+1}\rfloor\). Every contributing ordinary prime satisfies \(B_j<p<B_{j+1}\): it exceeds \(Q_j\), and \(p\le4n/v\) with \(v>V_j\). Each of these \(k\) disjoint prime ranges, with its own clipped lengths and cofactor weights, recovers the **same** \(D(n)\) to \(O_{k,\varepsilon}(n^{k/(k+1)+\varepsilon})\). Indeed \(V_jQ_j\le(4n)^{k/(k+1)}\) and \(V_j\le(4n)^{(k-1)/(k+1)}\). The cut \(Q_j\le2n\) holds for all sufficiently large \(n\); the finitely many smaller endpoints are absorbed into the constant. No averaging over \(n\) enters. At \(k=5\) there are five separate ordinary prime ranges at the first gate. At \(k=2\) the two ranges meet at \((4n)^{2/3}\) and recover the midpoint to \(O_\varepsilon(n^{2/3+\varepsilon})\). More generally, fix \(1/2\le b<1\). Every translate \(((4n)^a,(4n)^{a+1-b})\) with \(1-b\le a\le b\) has the same \(O_{b,\varepsilon}(n^{b+\varepsilon})\) error, uniformly in \(a\). Take \(V=\lfloor(4n)^{b-a}\rfloor\). Thus one may select a different favorable range at each integer. An independent upper bound for one such range at every \(n\) is still open. [Verified ordinary-prime range theorem](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExceptionalLayerTheory/SquareConductors/DisjointOrdinaryPrimeRangesAtTheSameMidpoint.lean)

**The same semiprime in two measurements.** Set \(X=4n,\ P=\lceil X^{1/4}\rceil,\ R=P^2\). Exchange the cuts \((Q,V)=(P,R)\) and \((R,P)\). Both errors are \(O_\varepsilon(n^{3/4+\varepsilon})\). The two prices share exactly the semiprimes \(k=pq\) with \(p>P,\ q>R,\ (k,n)=1\) in the two original intervals. Indeed \(k\le X\le R^2\) forces \(p<R<q\). In the low-prime measurement every surviving cofactor \(u>R\) satisfies \(u\le R^2\); if \(q>R\) divides \(k=pu\), then \(q\mid u\), hence \(u=q\). In the high-prime measurement the cofactor \(v=k/q<R=P^2\); a prime \(p>P\) dividing \(v\) forces \(v=p\). Lean verifies that both projected coefficients survive exactly in this semiprime case, when both equal one. [Exchanged cuts](../../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExceptionalLayerTheory/SquareConductors/SignedDivisibilityAveragesOfTheReflectedCofactors.lean#L121) Their contributions add to \(\log k\) and differ, high minus low, by \(\log(q/p)\), with the same interval sign on each product. The other low-price products have all prime factors at most \(R\). Each other high-price product has one prime above \(R\) and a \(P\)-smooth cofactor with radical above \(P\). These two classes are disjoint as sets of integers. This identifies the physical overlap of the two prime ranges; a joint upper bound for the three classes remains open.

**Proof status.** Lean verified the divided factorial identity, the primitive Möbius projection, and its one-sided exponent criterion at every integer. At the original prime cut, one may discard all proper cofactors through \(\lfloor n^{1/3}\rfloor\) at the \(5/6\) milestone. Lean also verified the uniform translated-range estimate and the criterion allowing a different prime range at each midpoint.
