# The complete Weil inverse cost

Let $A_N(c)$ be the literal complete Weil matrix of the tilted unit spline,
and let $E(c)$ be its completed conductor-one energy. For every fixed $c>0$,
$E(c)>0$, every eigenvalue eventually exceeds $E(c)-\varepsilon$, and some
eigenvalue lies within $\varepsilon$ of $E(c)$. The unique ground window
captures the normalized pole vector. These are source theorems in
`TheCompleteWeilGroundStateIsThePole`.

The expected sharp completion payment is
$$C>E(c)^{-1}\Longrightarrow
  0\le b^{\mathsf T}A_N(c)^{-1}b\le C\|b\|^2
  \quad\text{eventually, for every }b.$$
For $C<E(c)^{-1}$, a ground eigenvector violates the upper bound eventually.
Thus the reciprocal completed shell energy is the asymptotically optimal
mixed-block cost. The exact threshold $C=E(c)^{-1}$ requires information
about the side from which the finite ground eigenvalue approaches its limit.

The proof uses the source spectral theorem, the identity
$A^{-1}v=\lambda^{-1}v$ on a positive eigenvector, and the complete-form
inverse comparison. No estimate for an unproved source remainder is assumed.
For every fixed upper overlap $K$, the uniform source floor gives one
finite inverse cost and one horizon for all $0<c\le K$. A prescribed
growing overlap needs a joint estimate relative to its vanishing reserve.
