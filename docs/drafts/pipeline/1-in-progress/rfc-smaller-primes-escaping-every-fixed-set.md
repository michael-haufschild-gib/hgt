# The smaller split primes must escape every fixed set

- **Stage** 1-in-progress
- **Question** Can a density-one sequence of short exchanges use a fixed finite set for the smaller split prime while both other primes range freely?

For a finite prime set \(S\), put \(P=\prod_{p\in S}p\). Give a squarefree integer \(n\) the second sign \(\chi_S(n)=\mu((n,P))\). Its mean on a sharp octave, divided by \(X\), tends to the positive number
\[
  b_S=\frac{6}{\pi^2}\prod_{p\in S}\frac{p-1}{p+1}.
\]
Indeed, in the fibre \((n,P)=d\) one has \(n=da\) with \(a\) squarefree and coprime to \(P\). Its limiting density is \(d^{-1}(6/\pi^2)\prod_{p\in S}p/(p+1)\). Summing the signs gives the product. Within each second-sign class the positive and negative ordinary Möbius signs have equal limiting populations by the coprime Möbius mean.

Suppose \(a=cp\), \(b=cqr\), \(q<r\), are endpoints of an exchange in \((X,2X]\) with displacement at most \(\delta X\). Distinct integers \(p,qr\) differ by at least one, so \(c\le\delta X\). Both changed factors exceed \(1/\delta\). For \(S\subseteq[2,Y]\) and \(\delta\le1/Y^2\), this gives \(p>Y,\ r>Y\). Thus the second sign flips precisely when \(q\in S\). In any such matching, the second-sign total equals the signed sum over unmatched vertices plus twice the signed number of edges preserving the second sign. Consequently the unmatched count plus twice the number of edges with \(q\notin S\) is at least \((b_S-o(1))X\). In particular any density-one short matching uses at least \((b_S/2-o(1))X\) edges outside each fixed smaller-prime set. The common cofactor and both large primes are unrestricted.

This obstruction concerns the smaller split prime alone, whereas the full prescribed-prime obstruction restricts every changed prime. Together with the bounded-multiplicity obstruction, the two necessary features are growing support and growing degree. The separate sharpness question is whether the actual restricted graph can attain the parity lower bound, even for \(S=\{2\}\).

**Candidate attaining matching.** For each \(p\in S\), pair auxiliary donor primes \(r\) to distinct primes \(v_r\) close to \(pr\), with all auxiliary primes distinct across all pairs. Short cells in separated multiplicative intervals supply arbitrarily large \(H=\sum_r1/r\) with \(v_r\ge3r/2\). Give each pair one letter \(1_{r\mid n}-1_{v_r\mid n}\). In a complete period these prime divisibility indicators are pairwise independent. The word total has mean at least \(H/3\) and variance at most \(2H\). Hence the fraction of integers with nonpositive total is at most \(18/H\).

On a squarefree integer divisible by \(p\), scan the prefix sums of that word including its initial zero. If the maximum is positive, switch the donor at the **first maximum** from \(pr\) to \(v\). This lowers every subsequent prefix sum by two. The last maximum of the new word occurs just before the switched letter. On an integer without \(p\), switch the letter immediately after its **last maximum**, if there is one, from \(v\) to \(pr\). All letters at other positions remain unchanged. These two rules are inverse, take one scan and use only short genuine edges. A missed vertex divisible by \(p\) must have total increment at most zero.

Use the first eligible \(p\in S\), with disjoint auxiliary ranges for different \(p\). Its switch preserves every other word and occupation bit. Thus outside an arbitrarily small exceptional fraction every integer divisible by some \(p\in S\) is paired. Truncation to the sharp octave costs only the two narrow endpoint strips. The unmatched vertices are therefore coprime to \(S\) apart from \(o(X)\). Their second sign is then positive, so conservation gives a matching leaving exactly \((b_S+o(1))X\) squarefree vertices, with displacement \(o(X)\). This would identify the sharp deficit for the actual prescribed-smaller-prime graph, including \(S=\{2\}\), while allowing either large prime to vary without bound. The variance estimate concerns all integers and requires no independence assumption about Möbius signs.
