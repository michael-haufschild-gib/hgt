# Which prime readings pass beyond the hyperbola?

- **Stage** 1-in-progress
- **Question** How far can the cofactor domain shrink at half-plus precision, and which prime weights prevent further shrinking?

**A single adverse prime mask.** Suppose the sieve wall tends to infinity and is eventually below half the head height \(T(X)\), which also tends to infinity. Choose increasing \(X_j\) so that \(T(X_{j-1})\le y(X_j)<T(X_j)/2\). Mark precisely the ordinary primes in the union of \((T(X_j)/2,T(X_j)]\). At observation \(X_j\) the sieve erases all earlier marked primes and every later one is above the head. The remaining primes admit only the unit cofactor there. The signed inverse-coefficient head of this one fixed reading is exactly \(\vartheta(T(X_j))-\vartheta(T(X_j)/2)\sim T(X_j)/2\). Thus a polynomial head of exponent greater than one half has no uniform half-plus payment over fixed Mangoldt-dominated weights. The obstruction is in the signed head itself.

**Structured weights.** For the complete rough Mangoldt reading the same head is the negative logarithmic rough Möbius prefix. The unconditional strip with boundary \(\beta_0=20999/24000\) gives the bound \(O_s(E_s(y)T^s\log(2T))\) for each \(s>\beta_0\), by smooth dilation and partial summation. Passing to ordinary primes removes a kernel supported on proper powers. Its \(s\)-mass is bounded for \(s>1/2\) by summing \(\sum_{p,k\ge2}\log p/p^{ks}\). At the log-square wall one may therefore take \(T=\lfloor X^{1/(2\beta_0)}\rfloor\) for both complete readings, leaving cofactors of exponent \(1-1/(2\beta_0)\) with half-plus head price. The bound used for the input Möbius sum has exponent strictly above the known strip boundary. The interior reading at the smaller cofactors is the open estimate.

For the full rough Mangoldt reading, the paid head equals \(-\sum_{d\le\min(X,T)}\mu_y(d)\log d\). For ordinary primes its coefficient is \(\sum_{p\mid d,\ p>y}\mu_y(d/p)\log p\). On squarefree rough integers this again equals \(-\mu(d)\log d\). Its only further support is \(d=p^2r\) with \(p>y\), \(r\) squarefree rough and \(p\nmid r\), where the coefficient is \(-\mu(r)\log p\). Every other term vanishes because removing a single prime leaves a square or a forbidden prime. This identifies the signed correction when seeking to push the same cutoff beyond the hyperbola using the available weaker-exponent Möbius bound.

The structured-weight argument allows an arbitrary signed change on every proper prime power simultaneously. At ordinary primes keep the rough Mangoldt weight; at every composite choose any value of magnitude at most \(\Lambda\). At height \(T\) transpose the change into \(\sum_{q\le T}(a(q)-\Lambda_y(q))M_y(\lfloor T/q\rfloor)\). Its absolute value is at most \(4C_s E_s(y)T^s\sum_{q\text{ composite}}\Lambda(q)q^{-s}\), uniformly in all choices, even choices varying with the observation. For fixed \(s>\beta_0\) this last series converges. In particular, at \(T=\lfloor X^{4/7}\rfloor\) take \(\beta_0<s<7/8\). The combined signed head is \(O(X^{1/2-\delta})\) for some \(\delta>0\), leaving only cofactors below \(X^{3/7}\). The strictly negative smooth center still appears separately when comparing with the original reading.

**Which power can be removed?** Write \(\Theta\) for the supremum of the real parts of the nontrivial zeta zeros. The natural cutoff for a uniform half-plus bound on the removed heads of all genuine prime readings is \(\alpha\Theta\le 1/2\), where \(T\le X^\alpha\), \(0<\alpha\le1\). For sufficiency one uses the Möbius estimate at any exponent above \(\Theta\) only at the smaller height \(T\).

For necessity it suffices to bound the single full Mangoldt head only at the last index \(\lfloor X^\alpha\rfloor\) of each observation. At each integer height \(n\) choose \(X=\lceil n^{1/\alpha}\rceil\) and \(T=n\). Then \(\lfloor X^\alpha\rfloor=n\). Indeed, \(n^{1/\alpha}\le X<n^{1/\alpha}+1\le(n+1)^{1/\alpha}\), since \(1/\alpha\ge1\). The frozen wall \(b(X)\) is nondecreasing in \(n\). The head is exactly \(-\sum_{d\le n}\mu_{b(X)}(d)\log d\). Replace its unit weight by \(\log 2\), costing one constant. Weighted moving-wall inversion restores \(M(n)\) at a price of two Euler products. At \(b(\lceil n^{1/\alpha}\rceil)\) both products together cost \(n^{o(1)}\) at every exponent above one half. A uniform head price \(O_\varepsilon(X^{1/2+\varepsilon})\) therefore gives \(M(n)=O_\eta(n^{1/(2\alpha)+\eta})\) and \(\Theta\le1/(2\alpha)\). Only the complete group of removed terms is priced in this equivalence. The square-root bound on the surviving interior remains separate.

**Recovery from one perturbed terminal reading.** At the sampled height \(n\), write \(Y=b(\lceil n^{1/\alpha}\rceil)\), and let \(e_n\) be the difference from the genuine rough Mangoldt weight. The signed head changes by \(\sum_{q\le n}e_n(q)M_Y(\lfloor n/q\rfloor)\). If \(e_n(1)=0\), every argument is strictly smaller. For each fixed \(s>1/2\), genuine ordinary primes and arbitrary admissible proper powers give \(\sum|e_n(q)|q^{-s}\le 2\sum_{q\text{ not prime}}\Lambda(q)q^{-s}=O_s(1)\).

More generally, suppose that at all large endpoints this mass is at most \((1-\delta)\log n\) for some \(\delta>0\). In an induction at the current wall, write \(|M_Y(k)|\le H k^s\) at smaller arguments. Partial summation bounds \(|M_Y(n)\log n-L_Y(n)|\) by \(2Hn^s\): use \(k^s\le n^{s-1/2}\sqrt k\) and sum \(1/\sqrt k\). The perturbation costs at most \((1-\delta)Hn^s\log n\). The remaining \((\delta\log n-2)Hn^s\) pays the observed head. Extending the wall by Euler deletion proves the inductive bound at every higher cutoff. One further Euler product restores \(M(n)\). Thus even a **single prescribed family** of signed proper-power choices, changing at every terminal observation, retains the zero abscissa. The same induction allows ordinary-prime edits that meet the stated mass budget. The constant one here is a sufficient absorption threshold; its optimality is open.

The [structured-weight transfer](../3-formalized/qfsb-shrinking-the-cofactor-range-for-genuine-prime-readings.md) is formalized. The fixed mask and the converse from a single perturbed terminal reading remain in progress.
