# Strict gain over the best ordered-fibre matching

- **Stage** 1-in-progress
- **Question** Can a cross-fibre transport increase total matching size while paying for all displaced edges?

In the ordered fibre at one, every old edge joins a prime to a semiprime. Reassign its prime endpoints to semiprimes with smallest factor in \(\{2,3,5\}\). The reciprocal sum \(1/2+1/3+1/5>1\) supplies independent room on a large dyadic block; the three sets are disjoint there. This frees every semiprime with least factor seven while retaining the number of old root edges. All other ordered fibres are untouched.

Merge the largest two primes of \(7qr\) into one prime \(t\) in the same quotient interval. Balanced \(q,r\asymp\sqrt{X/7}\) give \(\gg X/\log^2X\) negative vertices in empty ordered fibres, with distinct free positive endpoints \(7t\). This would prove a strict gain of that size over the maximum ordered matching. The wider cube-root band has enough merger capacity and may yield a gain of order \(X/\log X\) at cofactor seven via a lower reciprocal-prime sum over an exponent interval.
