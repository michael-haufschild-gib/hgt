# Boundary separation via algebraic continuation

- **ID:** boundary-separation-algebraic-continuation
- **Stage:** 1-in-progress
- **Type:** CANDIDATE THEOREM
- **Source:** Q6.2 connected proof; `u0-phi-independence`; Haiku chat id=1698
- **Statement:** Algebraic dependence over a meromorphic side-field forces analytic continuation of a
  natural-boundary germ through any crossing point where the side-field is analytic and the algebraic
  discriminant is nonzero.

## Goal

Extract the structural theorem used twice in the current Böttcher work:

> Algebraic dependence is a continuation machine; a natural boundary blocks finite algebraic generation.

This is not the false principle "natural boundary implies hypertranscendence." Natural boundaries do not
certify differential transcendence in general. The proposed theorem is narrower: if all other functions in
the relation already live analytically across a selected boundary point, then an algebraic relation makes
the natural-boundary germ algebraic over an analytic field there, hence locally continuable there outside a
finite bad locus.

The hard work in applications is therefore separated into two independent gates:

1. **boundary obstruction gate:** prove the chosen `F` has no analytic continuation through the target
   boundary set;
2. **crossing gate:** prove the companion field has a path from the germ domain to a boundary point outside
   the algebraic bad locus.

Q6.2 and `u0` differ mainly in the crossing gate. The algebraic-continuation core is the same.

## Candidate theorem

Let `V` be a connected one-dimensional complex manifold, `V0 subset V` a nonempty connected open set,
and `F` a holomorphic function on `V0`. Let `Sigma subset partial V0 cap V` be a set through which `F`
has no analytic continuation at any point.

Let `K` be a finitely generated field of meromorphic functions on `V`. Assume `K` is generated over a
base field `k` by meromorphic functions `G_1,...,G_m` on `V`. Suppose there is a point `z0 in Sigma`
and a path `gamma:[0,1]->V` such that:

- `gamma([0,1)) subset V0`, `gamma(1)=z0`;
- the generators `G_i` are holomorphic along a neighborhood of `gamma([0,1])`;
- after substituting `G_i`, the coefficient poles and discriminant zeros of the minimal polynomial below
  do not meet `gamma([0,1])`.

Then no relation

`P(F)=0`, with `P(T) in K[T] nonzero` and `deg_T P > 0`,

can hold on `V0`.

Equivalently, if such a relation exists, the algebraic implicit function theorem gives a holomorphic branch
of `F` across `z0`, contradicting the natural-boundary hypothesis.

### Pure-side exception

If the relation has degree zero in `F`, the theorem says nothing. Applications must kill that case by a
separate side-field result, such as Becker-Bergweiler for a single nonexceptional Böttcher coordinate.

## Proof core `[PROVED HERE, modulo source/significance gate]`

Assume a relation of positive degree in `F`. Replace it by the monic irreducible minimal polynomial

`m(T)=T^d+a_{d-1}T^{d-1}+...+a_0 in K[T]`.

On a neighborhood where the elements of `K` are meromorphic, let `Bad` be the union of:

- poles of the coefficients `a_i`;
- zeros of the discriminant `Delta_m`;
- poles introduced by the chosen side-field parametrization.

If the side-field is generated by analytic functions `G_1,...,G_m`, these bad sets are locally discrete
unless a nonzero rational function in the `G_i` vanishes identically; that exceptional case must be killed
separately as a pure side-field algebraic dependence.

By hypothesis, the path `gamma` avoids `Bad` and ends at `z0`. Choose a small disk `U` around `z0` with
no point of `Bad`. On `U`, all coefficients `a_i` are holomorphic and `Delta_m` is nonzero, so the algebraic
function defined by `m(T)=0` splits into `d` distinct holomorphic branches

`r_1,...,r_d : U -> C`.

Since `gamma([0,1)) subset V0`, the relation gives

`F(w) in {r_1(w),...,r_d(w)}`

for `w in U cap V0` near the endpoint. Let `C` be the component of `U cap V0` containing a tail of
`gamma([0,1))`. For each `i`, the set `{w in C : F(w)=r_i(w)}` is closed. It is also open: if
`F(w*)=r_i(w*)`, the roots `r_j(w*)` are distinct, and by continuity `F` must remain in the same separated
root branch in a smaller neighborhood of `w*`. Since `C` is connected and the union of these finitely many
open-closed sets is `C`, exactly one branch, say `r_i`, equals `F` on all of `C`.

Thus `r_i` is a holomorphic extension of `F` from the side of `V0` to the full disk `U`, in particular through
`z0 in Sigma`. This contradicts the defining natural-boundary/no-continuation hypothesis on `Sigma`.

This is exactly the local algebraic-function theorem in standard complex analysis: away from coefficient
poles and discriminant zeros, the roots of an irreducible algebraic equation split into holomorphic
branches, and a branch can be continued along arcs avoiding those excluded points.

## Instantiations

### `u0` versus `phi_b`

- `F = u0`, with natural boundary `|z|=1` by Ostrowski-Hadamard.
- `K = Qbar(b)(z, phi_b(z))`.
- For `b in M`, the crossing gate is `Omega_b cap |z|=1 != empty`.
- For `b notin M`, the crossing gate is the Green crossing
  `max_{|z|=1} G_b > G_b(0)`, giving a point in `Delta_b={G_b>G_b(0)}`.
- Pure side-field dependence is killed by Becker-Bergweiler for nonexceptional `b`.

### Q6.2 connected Böttcher independence

- `F = phi_{c1}`, with no continuation through `J_{c1}` by Eremenko-van Strien smooth-piece rigidity.
- `K = Qbar(c1,c2)(z, phi_{c2}(z))`.
- The crossing gate is `J_{c1} cap Omega_{c2} != empty`, proved by the capacity energy test plus
  same-filled-Julia-set rigidity.
- Pure side-field dependence is killed by Becker-Bergweiler for `phi_{c2}`.

## Source boundary

The local algebraic-continuation step is classical algebraic-function theory. Ahlfors, *Complex Analysis*,
Chapter 8, Section 2, proves the standard version: the discriminant marks the multiple-root values, and away
from leading-coefficient and discriminant zeros an algebraic equation splits into holomorphic branches; the
corresponding function elements continue along arcs avoiding the excluded points.

Search phrases around `"natural boundary" "algebraic dependence" "analytic continuation"`,
`"natural boundary" "algebraic over" "meromorphic functions"`, and `"algebraic function" "natural boundary"
"meromorphic coefficients"` find many uses of this principle in Mahler, automatic, and G-function settings,
but no source yet that packages the above as a reusable HT boundary-separation criterion with explicit
crossing gates and side-field bad-locus bookkeeping.

Current grade target: **structural BRIDGE**, not deep-new mathematics. The theorem's value is to isolate a
native mechanism for HT objects: finite algebraic relations cannot cross a genuine boundary unless the
companion field supplies singularities or branch locus at every crossing point.

Kill condition: produce a printed theorem already stating this boundary-separation principle in comparable
generality, including side-field analytic crossing and pure-side algebraic-dependence exceptions.

The theorem shape is correct as a reusable proof schema, but the continuation core is classical algebraic-function theory. Away from coefficient poles, leading-coefficient zeros, and discriminant zeros, an algebraic branch splits into holomorphic branches and continues along arcs. That part should be cited, not claimed.

Promotion constraint: this card should not be graded as a new mathematical theorem unless the source search finds no comparable packaged criterion *and* the packaging itself does work beyond naming a textbook local branch theorem. Current grade should be **library lemma / structural bridge**, useful because it forces each application to expose two real gates:

1. a boundary obstruction for the target germ;
2. a crossing lemma proving the companion field is analytic at some boundary point outside the algebraic bad locus.

For Q6.2 and `u0`, the novel work is in the crossing geometry plus the native mixed objects, not in the algebraic-continuation lemma itself.

Hypothesis tightening still needed before any promotion:

- State the theorem in terms of chosen meromorphic coefficient representatives for the minimal polynomial, not only generators of `K`.
- Make the positive-degree-in-`F` assumption explicit after separately killing pure side-field relations.
- Require the crossing path to lie in a domain where those coefficient representatives and side functions are holomorphic and where the leading coefficient/discriminant do not vanish.
- Replace any wording suggesting "natural boundary implies HT" with the narrower claim: finite algebraic dependence over a side-field analytic at a crossing point forces local continuation there.

Theatre-test verdict: the team already knows the local theorem. The card is valuable only if it prevents hidden crossing assumptions in future proofs.
