## Goal

Answer two explicit questions left open in current recurrence research:

1. What are tight recurrence laws for dynamics generated by physical local
   noncommuting Hamiltonians?
2. What happens to the late-time arithmetic of a locally interacting free or
   commuting system under an interacting perturbation?

The proposed answer is a singular law. Eigenvalues vary continuously with the
perturbation, but the number of rationally independent projective energy
directions jumps from at most polynomial in system size to the full Hilbert
dimension minus one. That rank is the exponent governing how rarely the
entire evolution returns.

## Exact setting

Let \(G=(V,E)\) be any finite connected graph with \(n=|V|\geq2\), and let
each vertex carry \(\mathbb C^q\), \(q\geq2\). Put \(D=q^n\). Let
\(\mathcal L_G\) be the real vector space of traceless Hermitian Hamiltonians
of the form
\[
 H=\sum_{v\in V}h_v+\sum_{\{u,v\}\in E}h_{uv},             \tag{1}
\]
where \(h_v\) and \(h_{uv}\) are arbitrary one-site and edge-supported
Hermitian terms. Lebesgue-null and full-measure statements on
\(\mathcal L_G\) are basis independent.

For a simple-spectrum \(H\), order its eigenvalues
\(E_1(H)<\cdots<E_D(H)\) and define the projective spectral rank
\[
 \rho(H)=
 \dim_{\mathbb Q}\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Q}
 \{E_j(H)-E_1(H):2\leq j\leq D\}.                         \tag{2}
\]
This is invariant under energy shifts and nonzero rescalings and satisfies
\(0\leq\rho(H)\leq D-1\).

Define the global projective return distance
\[
 \Delta_H(t)=
 \max_{2\leq j\leq D}
 \left|e^{-it(E_j-E_1)}-1\right|.                         \tag{3}
\]
It is an anchored form of return of \(e^{-itH}\) to a scalar unitary and is
equivalent, up to universal constants, to projective operator-norm return.
For \(0<\varepsilon<2\), define its time density when the limit exists,
\[
 d_H(\varepsilon)=
 \lim_{T\to\infty}\frac1{2T}
 \left|\{t\in[-T,T]:\Delta_H(t)<\varepsilon\}\right|.      \tag{4}
\]
Let \(\tau_H(\varepsilon)\) be the first positive return after the orbit has
exited the \(\varepsilon\)-target.

## Frozen conjecture `[CONJECTURE]`

### I. Generic local maximality

For every finite connected \(G\) and every \(q\geq2\), Lebesgue-almost every
\(H\in\mathcal L_G\) has simple spectrum and
\[
 \boxed{\rho(H)=D-1=q^n-1.}                               \tag{5}
\]
Equivalently, no rational relation among the projective energy gaps survives
a generic symmetry-free local perturbation.

### II. Exact recurrence rarity

For every \(H\) satisfying `(5)`,
\[
 \boxed{
 d_H(\varepsilon)=
 \left(\frac{2\arcsin(\varepsilon/2)}{\pi}\right)^{D-1}.
 }                                                        \tag{6}
\]
Thus the reciprocal fraction of time spent in a global
\(\varepsilon\)-return is
\[
 d_H(\varepsilon)^{-1}
 =
 \exp\!\left[
 (q^n-1)
 \log\frac{\pi}{2\arcsin(\varepsilon/2)}
 \right],                                                 \tag{7}
\]
which is doubly exponential in \(n\) at every fixed
\(0<\varepsilon<2\).

For Lebesgue-almost every \(H\in\mathcal L_G\), the first post-exit
continuous-time return also has the sharp metric-Diophantine exponent
\[
 \boxed{
 \lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}
 \frac{\log\tau_H(\varepsilon)}
      {\log(1/\varepsilon)}
 =D-2.
 }                                                        \tag{8}
\]
The stroboscopic exponent is \(D-1\) for almost every tick size.

### III. Commuting local compression

If
\[
 H=\sum_{a=1}^{m}K_a,\qquad [K_a,K_b]=0,                  \tag{9}
\]
and every \(K_a\) is supported on at most \(k\) sites, then
\[
 \boxed{\rho(H)\leq m(q^k-1).}                            \tag{10}
\]
For bounded-degree finite-range systems, \(m=O(n)\), so the commuting
recurrence exponent is at most linear in \(n\), rather than exponential in
\(n\).

### IV. Singular perturbation law

The maximal-rank set in `(5)` is full measure and dense in
\(\mathcal L_G\). Consequently every neighbourhood of every commuting local
Hamiltonian contains local Hamiltonians with rank \(q^n-1\). A generic
arbitrarily weak noncommuting perturbation therefore changes the
high-resolution recurrence exponent discontinuously:
\[
 O(n)\quad\longrightarrow\quad q^n-1.                    \tag{11}
\]
This jump is invisible to every finite-order perturbative approximation that
retains the additive commuting spectral relations.

## Public meaning

> **The smallest generic quantum interaction can postpone repetition by a
> double eternity.**

This means global projective recurrence of the complete finite evolution. It
does not say that every local observable, subsystem, person, black hole, or
the universe realizes the full-rank phase.

The physical shift is that double-exponential recurrence is not attributed
merely to Hilbert-space size. It is attributed to activation of exponentially
many rationally independent spectral clocks. Finite dimension makes
recurrence possible; maximal projective spectral rank makes it doubly rare.

## Hostile falsifiers

Retire or replace the frozen conjecture if any of the following occurs.

1. A connected graph \(G\) for which every \(H\in\mathcal L_G\) obeys a
   nontrivial rational projective spectral relation.
2. A symmetry-free open subset of \(\mathcal L_G\) whose projective spectral
   rank is strictly below \(D-1\).
3. A nonzero-measure set of local Hamiltonians with maximal rational rank but
   a different density law from `(6)`.
4. Failure of the almost-everywhere exponent `(8)` because the local spectral
   manifold is Diophantine-degenerate.
5. A commuting \(k\)-local counterexample to `(10)`.
6. A prior theorem equivalent to generic local maximality plus the exact
   recurrence-density and singular-perturbation consequences.
7. A proof that maximal global operator recurrence has no independent
   consequence for local many-body physics, equilibration, spectral form
   factors, or black-hole recurrence estimates.

Failure of `(8)` does not license silently deleting the waiting-time claim.

## `[OPEN CORE]`

The first open core is `(5)` for an arbitrary connected bounded-degree graph.
It requires proving that the local spectral manifold lies in no nontrivial
rational affine hyperplane after quotienting the trace direction.

The second open core is the almost-everywhere fixed-Hamiltonian exponent `(8)`
on that same local spectral manifold. Rational independence alone is
insufficient because Liouville spectra can recur anomalously quickly.

No Lean construction is authorized before both cores have dependency-closed
paper proofs and the rediscovery attack passes.

## Post-freeze bridge proved

`../2-fully-proven/complete-graph-local-projective-spectral-maximality.md`
proves clause `(5)` for the complete interaction graph for every local
dimension \(q\geq2\). The explicit diagonal two-site plus one-site-transition
subfamily
\[
 \sum_{i,r}a_{i,r}P_i^r
 +\sum_{i<j,r,u}b_{ij,r,u}P_i^rP_j^u
 +\sum_{i,r<r'}x_{i,rr'}X_i^{rr'}
\]
has no branchwise affine spectral relation beyond trace: second derivatives
in \(x_{i,rr'}\) produce linearly independent reciprocal poles indexed by
every configuration of the other \(n-1\) sites. A symmetric-polynomial
product over eigenvalue permutations then places each rational relation in a
nonzero polynomial zero set. Countable union gives dense full-measure maximal
projective rank \(q^n-1\).

This is a genuine paper-proved bridge, not closure of the frozen conjecture.
The proof uses all-to-all diagonal couplings to distinguish every remote spin
configuration. Higher mixed perturbation coefficients are still required to
propagate that separation through an arbitrary bounded-degree connected
graph.

## Post-freeze bounded-degree core proved

`../2-fully-proven/nearest-neighbor-chain-projective-spectral-maximality.md`
proves generic maximal projective rank \(2^n-1\) for the full space of
one-site and nearest-neighbour two-site qubit Hamiltonians on every open
one-dimensional chain.

The proof inducts by adding one endpoint qubit. A maximal-rank shorter chain
has distinct ordered nonzero gaps. Its endpoint local-observable transition
graph can simultaneously be chosen connected and non-bipartite. Second-order
edge perturbations make every putative affine relation satisfy the equality
graph of the bipartite double cover; non-bipartiteness makes that cover
connected, so every coefficient is equal and only trace survives. A
symmetric-polynomial null-set argument then gives dense full-measure
maximality.

This closes the hostile bounded-degree objection for qubit chains. It does
not yet prove the frozen clause for every connected graph or every
\(q\geq2\).
