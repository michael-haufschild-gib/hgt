## Exact killing theorem

Every finite simple graph `H` is realized by nonnegative profile pairs.  Put one source atom at
each vertex.  For every ordered adjacent pair `(x,y)`, use the task

\[
 u_{x,y}=\delta_x,\qquad v_{x,y}=\delta_y.
\]

The difference is positive only at `x`, negative only at `y`, and zero elsewhere.  Its only
conflict is the edge `{x,y}`.  Taking all adjacent pairs therefore realizes exactly `H`.

Consequently no special graph theorem follows from positivity, finite support, or deterministic
coarse-graining alone.  Perfect-graph, matroid, antimatroid, Helly, chordal, bounded-degeneracy,
and uniform chromatic claims all fail on suitable realized graphs.

## Why the proposal failed

Positivity restricts each individual task to a bipartite sign cut, but an arbitrary finite graph
is a union of single-edge bipartite cuts.  Allowing an unrestricted task family destroys every
global graph restriction.  The mistake was seeking geometry in the static union of task conflicts
rather than in the resource law governing observers that must respect them.

## Strongest positive survivor

For a fixed task family, lossless deterministic observers are exactly proper colorings of its
conflict graph.  Minimal observer range is exactly chromatic number, and every monochromatic edge
provides an explicit strictly positive total-variation loss certificate.  This is proved in

`docs/drafts/pipeline/2-fully-proven/universal-law-field/failure-kernel-transversal-mechanics/total-variation-conflict-coloring-and-observer-width-law.md`.

The cofinal survivor is sharper than the failed graph-class claim: unbounded chromatic number is a
finite observer-width obstruction, while de Bruijn--Erdos compactness proves that a uniformly
bounded abstract coloring problem has no further combinatorial obstruction.  Any remaining global
failure must be analytic admission or coherence.

## New insight

The field should not classify static obstruction graphs.  It should classify two mechanisms:

1. how much observer alphabet is required to preserve a declared task doctrine;
2. why an abstractly adequate observer fails admission in a topology, symmetry, or completion.

This width/admission split is the positive replacement.
