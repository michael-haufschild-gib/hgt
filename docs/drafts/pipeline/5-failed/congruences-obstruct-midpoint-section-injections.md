# Midpoint injections preserving integer congruences

**Refuted.** On an unconditional positive two-atom ray $b$, no injection $S(3mb)^2\hookrightarrow S(4mb)\times S(2mb)$ can preserve congruences at two fixed coprime bases at every precision for all large $m$. Preserving the narrow coordinate alone gives the obstruction. Coordinates here are displacements from the left endpoints of the integer section intervals.

**Proof.** Write $n,L,s$ for the middle, wide and narrow lengths. Their logarithms divided by $m^2$ tend respectively to $9Q(b),16Q(b),4Q(b)$. Thus $L<n^2$ while $Ls/n^2$ grows exponentially. If a narrow coordinate has two coprime input periods $P,Q$ with $P+Q\le n$, every row is constant. Extend the first $P$ entries periodically. The $Q$-translate of every representative is still in the interval, so the extension has both periods. Coprimality gives constancy, first on rows and then on columns. The wide coordinate alone cannot hold the square.

**Positive survivor.** For any one prescribed base $p>1$, let $P$ be the largest power of $p$ at most $s$ and $W=P(\lfloor n/P\rfloor+1)$. The map $(x,y)\mapsto(y+W\lfloor x/P\rfloor,x\bmod P)$ is injective and respects every power of $p$ in both outputs. It fits in the rectangle for every sufficiently large $m$. For any embedding, losses of $i,j$ digits at two fixed coprime bases $p,q$ satisfy $i\log p+j\log q>\delta m^2$ eventually for every $\delta<Q(b)$.

**Sharp denominator growth.** Let $D$ be the least positive scalar making the narrow coordinate of a given injection preserve all integer congruences. All but at most one prime between $X$ and $2X$, $X=\lfloor n/4\rfloor$, divide $D$. Each missing prime at least $s$ gives a period; two would sum to at most $n$. Their primorial and the prime number theorem give $n/20\le\log D$ eventually. Conversely $\mathrm{lcm}(1,\ldots,n-1)$ clears every map, so $\log D\le(\log4+4)n$. Uniformly over all injections, $\log\log D/m^2\to9Q(b)$. Any scalar denominator in an integral polynomial formula for the narrow coordinate must obey the same lower bound. The unrestricted midpoint comparison for general displacements remains open.
