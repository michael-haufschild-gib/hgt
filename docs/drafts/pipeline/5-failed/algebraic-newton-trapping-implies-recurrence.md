# Algebraically Trapped Newton Transitions Do Not Imply P-Recurrence

- **ID:** algebraic-newton-trapping-implies-recurrence
- **Stage:** 5-failed
- **Type:** FALSIFIED INTERMEDIATE / COUNTEREXAMPLE
- **Scientific status:** `[PROVED COUNTEREXAMPLE] [BRIDGE]`
- **Failed statement:** If the normalized Newton transition clusters of a super-Gaussian entire series lie in a fixed proper algebraic locus, then its Taylor coefficients satisfy a finite-width polynomial recurrence.

## Reason for retirement

Transition geometry records which Taylor monomials are competitive, but it does not record the algebraic complexity of their amplitudes. Even the simplest possible transition locus—consecutive exponents with equal normalized amplitudes—supports coefficient sequences of infinite transcendence degree and hence hypertranscendental, in particular non-P-recursive, series.

Any live recurrence theorem must use the full coefficient identities of an assumed ADE, including its lower faces. Newton trapping alone is insufficient.

## Counterexample theorem `[PROVED HERE]`

There exists a transcendental entire series

`f(z)=sum_(n>=0) a_n z^n`

with all of the following properties:

1. for every `A>0`, `|a_n|<=C_A exp(-A n^2)`;
2. every sufficiently large index is active for the maximum term;
3. the logarithmic Newton plateau width at index `n` tends to infinity;
4. every transition is between consecutive exponents `n` and `n+1`, and after maximum-term normalization the two amplitudes are equal;
5. `(a_n)` is not P-recursive over `C`;
6. `f` is hypertranscendental over `C(z)`.

Thus all normalized two-term transition parameters lie in the fixed proper algebraic locus

`s_2-s_1=1`, `u_2-u_1=0`,

but no finite polynomial recurrence exists.

## Construction

Choose positive real numbers

`t_n in [1,2]`

that are algebraically independent over `Q(exp(-1))`. Such a sequence exists: after finitely or countably many choices, the algebraic closure of the generated countable field is countable, while `[1,2]` is uncountable.

Set

`a_n=t_n exp(-n^3)`                                                           `(1)`

and

`b_n=-log a_n=n^3-log t_n`.                                                   `(2)`

## Proof

### 1. Entirety and super-Gaussian decay

Since `1<=t_n<=2`,

`0<a_n<=2 exp(-n^3)`.

The power series therefore has infinite radius of convergence. For fixed `A>0`, `n^3>=A n^2` once `n>=A`; finitely many remaining indices are absorbed into `C_A`. This proves property 1.

### 2. Every Newton transition is adjacent and the plateaux widen

The discrete second difference of `(b_n)` is

`b_(n+1)-2b_n+b_(n-1)`

` = 6n-(log t_(n+1)-2log t_n+log t_(n-1))`

` >= 6n-2log 2 > 0`                                                          `(3)`

for `n>=1`. Hence `(b_n)` is strictly convex. Every index is therefore a vertex of its lower Newton hull, and the transition slopes are

`tau_n=b_(n+1)-b_n`.                                                          `(4)`

The plateau of index `n` is `[tau_(n-1),tau_n]`, with width

`W_n=tau_n-tau_(n-1)=b_(n+1)-2b_n+b_(n-1)`.                                 `(5)`

By (3), `W_n->infinity`. At `t=tau_n`, the terms of exponents `n` and `n+1` have equal modulus and all other terms have smaller modulus. Thus every transition has exponent gap one and equal normalized amplitudes, proving properties 2-4.

### 3. The coefficients are not P-recursive and the series is HT

Scaling algebraically independent elements by nonzero elements of `Q(exp(-1))` preserves algebraic independence. Hence the family

`{a_n:n>=0}`

is algebraically independent over `Q`; in particular, the field generated by all coefficients has infinite transcendence degree over `Q`.

Suppose `(a_n)` were P-recursive. Then for some `r>=0` there would be polynomials

`p_0(N),...,p_r(N) in C[N]`, `p_r!=0`,

such that

`sum_(j=0)^r p_j(n)a_(n+j)=0`                                                 `(6)`

for all sufficiently large `n`. After excluding the finitely many integer zeros of `p_r`, equation (6) expresses each new coefficient rationally in the preceding `r` coefficients and the finitely many complex coefficients of the `p_j`.

Therefore all `a_n` lie in the field generated over `Q` by:

- the finitely many coefficients of the recurrence polynomials;
- finitely many initial values of the sequence.

That field has finite transcendence degree over `Q`, contradicting the algebraic independence of the infinite family `(a_n)`. Thus `(a_n)` is not P-recursive, proving property 5.

For property 6, put

`F(s)=f(exp(-s))=sum_(n>=0) a_n exp(-ns)`.

If `f` were DA over `C(z)`, composition with the DA function `exp(-s)` and differential-algebraic transitivity would make `F` DA over `C(s)`. Ostrowski's finite-basis theorem for convergent Dirichlet series would then place all `a_n` in a field generated over `Q` by finitely many complex numbers. Such a field has finite transcendence degree, again contradicting the algebraic independence of `(a_n)`. Hence `f` is HT. `square`

## What survives

The finite secant-detection theorem remains valid: arbitrary clusters of bounded rank detect every nonzero jet face. What fails is the inference from one sequence being trapped in a proper cluster locus to linear recurrence.

The counterexample is therefore stronger than required: the trapped Newton geometry is compatible with HT itself, not merely with failure of linear recurrence. The corrected live target must assume an ADE and use all of its coefficient equations:

> `[OPEN CORE]` If a super-Gaussian sequence satisfies a nonlinear polynomial differential recurrence and its leading transition clusters remain algebraically trapped, prove that successive lower faces force either finite support or a contradiction.

This is strictly stronger than transition geometry and is not refuted by the counterexample, whose coefficients were chosen precisely to avoid every ADE.
