# What would close each route to RH?

Three arithmetic tests illustrate where the work lies. An upper bound for one difference of adjacent prime intervals would close ACS without estimating either of its two large conductor heights. For the critical state, two large opposite discrepancies can persist on every subset of the same block of consecutive numerators. For a lone quartet of zeros, classification from ordinary-prime data already works with arbitrary background zero divisor. Each is a different demand on ordinary primes. A theorem about data supplied as a premise counts as a reduction. “Work” means finite operations or deductions with identified inputs. ## Subproblems and the limits of the inventory

**AAL, ZAL and landing.** AAL forbids a nonempty finite strict right tail beyond any depth h≥0. ZAL asks for finitely many exceptions beyond each positive depth. Together they force RH. The alternative three-part reduction needs ZAL, landing from ZAL to finite total off-line support, and elimination of finite off-line support. Excluding exactly one quartet solves a proper piece of the third demand. These are additional quantitative readings of the zero census and the prime error. The Deninger three-term implication requires a cup product, Hodge data and a faithful ξ point spectrum. Those premises describe a candidate realization. The cubic detector and its angular lifts supply strip machinery for several routes.
