# Research programme for a formal proof of RH

**Best full-proof prospect.** Work on one ordinary-prime second difference, D(n)=2θ(3n)−θ(2n)−θ(4n). It is the excess of prime logarithms in (2n,3n] over (3n,4n]. The important freedom is in *how to estimate it*. At 5/6 there are five disjoint prime ranges that each read the same D(n) after an explicit factorial subtraction. At full RH there is still a cofactor sum with signed integer coefficients and three exact endpoints. I would combine this arithmetic with the project's finite divisor-correlation identities and reciprocal prime estimates. The missing theorem controls their **joint** error. The [full programme](#preferred-route) states the first block to attack and what would invalidate the proposed mechanism. ## 2.4 A breakthrough on a smaller domain than a new strip

**Can one beat the known absolute-summation price on the first surviving cofactor octave at one fixed band?** Take x=4n, b=5/6, a=1/2, Q=⌊x^(1/2)⌋, V=⌊x^(1/3)⌋. For V<v≤2V coprime to n the projected coefficient is exactly −μ(v). Let R(v) be the difference between the prime log masses of (3n/v,4n/v] and (2n/v,3n/v], with p>Q and p∤n. Ask for

$$\left|\sum_{V<v\le2V,\ (v,n)=1}\mu(v)R(v)\right|\ll_\varepsilon n^{8/9+\varepsilon}.$$

For large n the lower cutoff lies below both intervals, so the two continuous lengths cancel on this octave. Applying a Θ′-power ordinary-prime error at each v and then summing absolute values gives exponent 1/3+2Θ′/3, about 0.91664 when Θ′ approaches the corpus bound. Under Θ≤5/6 the same calculation gives 8/9+ε. Thus a 5/6 strip would imply this pilot estimate, while the pilot asks for only one octave of one band. The exclusion of p dividing n costs at most O(V log n). The displayed target asks for a gain on the *combined* row. An independent 8/9 bound would beat the current separate-endpoint estimate. The stronger target 5/6 on this octave would price that part at the final exponent. Other cofactor octaves and the complete projected center would still have to be controlled. A coherent large first octave could instead identify where cancellation with the other octaves is unavoidable. There is no established reverse implication from this octave alone. ## 2.9 Would RH require a new field?

One independent estimate for D(n) could suffice. The reductions give no mathematical necessity for a new field. What the existing methods lack in common is a theory that keeps the *signed discrepancy of a divisor sum through a change of observation scale*. A worthwhile field would study integer intervals, divisibility relations, oriented endpoint measures, and the exact operators that change cutoff, prime order or conductor. Its elementary objects would be a finite signed row, its deterministic center and its boundary under a specified change. Addition, divisor convolution, restriction and transfer would carry explicit error exponents. [Model row](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExceptionalLayerTheory/SquareConductors/TheFactorialPriceOfDivisibleCofactorRows.lean#L31) · [Change of cutoff](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExceptionalLayerTheory/SquareConductors/TheContinuousPrimeMassAtTheCofactorEndpoint.lean#L363) · [Boundary](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/RestrictedFactorizationCalculus/TheCenteredReadingBelowTheHyperbola.lean#L33)

The questions are whether an error cancels after two transfers, which main term survives if the order is reversed, and when several readers see exactly the same undamped mode. Tools should include signed divisor-correlation formulas at sharp endpoints, countermodels for missing arithmetic information, and composition theorems that state the resulting constant and exponent. A first test is to derive a power saving on an ordinary-prime/cofactor block outside the range obtained by estimating the factors separately. The RH test is to preserve that saving through all conductors and apply an existing all-endpoint RH implication. An abstract calculus that supplies only equivalences leaves the first research question untouched. ## 2.10 A field for proving or falsifying AAL

AAL is an arithmetic assertion about the outermost part of an infinite divisor. Its negation specifies a threshold h≥0 and a nonempty **finite** set of actual zeros strictly to the right of 1/2+h, with all other zeros at or left of that boundary. An actual off-line zero, a large finite computation, or a model with an isolated top would each leave the global upper bound unproved. An infinite lower background is allowed. [Failure witness](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ZeroConfigurationGeometry/AbscissaLaws/TheFailureOfTheAbscissaLawIsAFiniteLayer.lean#L33) · [Model boundary](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExceptionalLayerTheory/TheFiniteLayerWallCuspPayoff.lean#L328)

A specialized theory of exceptional strata could start with an outer finite divisor, its multiplicities, its gap from a chosen inner strip, and test functions that annihilate the other frequencies. The corpus already gives a contracting prime filter with diverging response at a selected top zero and a normalized jet sequence recording its multiplicity. The next tool would compare this *selected* response to an independent ordinary-integer fluctuation while retaining uniform dependence on gap, degree and damping. To prove AAL, contradict such a witness for every possible layer. To falsify it, rigorously locate the finite nonempty actual layer and prove the global bound for its complement. A first discriminating experiment is to reproduce the filter on a planted Euler model and exhibit an integer-divisibility relation lost under the planting. A theory insensitive to the planting cannot establish the needed distinction. ## 2.11 A field for an RH proof with no positivity or spectral input

Develop a quantitative **signed sieve with moving quotient endpoints**. Its objects would be signed multisets of factorizations, relative divisor classes, and pairs of neighboring intervals. Its morphisms would be ordinary division, reflection k↦6n−k on eligible integer pairs, and restriction to a quotient cell. Its basic invariant would be the signed boundary left after an involution or a finite Möbius inversion. Tools would include exact integral transport identities, nested boundary recurrences and a signed estimate stable under recombination. A signed price on reflected prime factors already differs from the log cofactor ratio by between 0 and log 2 on one opposing pair. Summing that harmless-looking constant over order n pairs is too expensive. [Per-pair error](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExceptionalLayerTheory/SquareConductors/OppositeCofactorPricesAcrossTheFirstRemainingPrimeOctave.lean#L300) · [Exact inversion](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExceptionalLayerTheory/SquareConductors/ThePrimitivePrimePriceBeyondAMovingCofactorCut.lean#L81)

The first research question is whether adjacent-cell boundary terms can be made to telescope with a uniform gain when ordinary primes enter or leave through either endpoint. Test the rule on incomplete terminal cells and under dyadic refinement. Reject it if it assumes a Mertens bound on the surviving cofactors or loses an uncontrolled cost per pair. ## 2.19 The one smaller question with the largest shared payoff

**Does one negative prime-displacement witness for actual ζ force unbounded negative index?** Equivalently for this purpose, can a nonempty finite off-line ζ divisor be ruled out while leaving every infinite configuration available? Failure of RH already gives negative two-prime displacements beyond every cutoff. Disjoint support gives linear independence of the vectors. It gives no control of the *mixed Gram entries*. A finite-dimensional negative space can project negatively onto infinitely many separate probes. That is the precise place to seek new arithmetic. **A concrete sufficient experiment.** Given one adverse witness, try to build for each m a set of m high-prime probes whose complete Gram matrix is negative definite. Normalize their negative diagonals to −1. A sufficient, stronger pilot is that the sum of absolute off-diagonal entries in each row be <1. First try two probes, bounding their mixed interaction by arithmetic separation of prime contacts and the real jet in the same normalization. For the two-probe test the exact requirement is |G₁₂|²<G₁₁G₂₂. For m increasing, allow a change of basis before estimating the residual interactions. A common low-rank mode shared by all the probes would defeat the simple separation strategy. Correlation near equality would tell us to identify that mode and examine the quotient, rather than merely move the probes to larger primes. [Mixed arithmetic terms](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/SignedArithmeticCorrespondences/TheCompletedWeilIntersectionOfTwoConductorSections.lean#L114) · [Approximation of individual probes](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/CountingHolography/Sections/SharedMidpointEncodingOnPrimeDisplacementPlanes.lean#L1136)

## 2.20 Best prospect for a full formal proof

**I would prioritize the signed divisibility projection of the prime midpoint.** It identifies the input down to individual pairs (p,v), specifies which part is already affordable, and has an RH implication without any limiting geometric object to construct. Three known obstructions test the proposal. An untwisted exceptional wave can pass the reflected identity, coherent excursions can repeat on many cofactors, and two costlier routes can each report exactly the same large D. This makes it possible to reject a mechanism early. Those tests and the direct implication to RH motivate this choice. [Compatible waves](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExceptionalLayerTheory/SquareConductors/ReflectedCompositeWaveTransmission.lean#L1580) · [Repeated excursions](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/RestrictedFactorizationCalculus/RepeatingAPrimeExcursionAcrossRoughCofactors.lean#L67) · [RH criterion](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExceptionalLayerTheory/SquareConductors/ThePrimeMidpointInTwoAdjacentGcdHorizons.lean#L342)

**Proof object.** Fix n,Q,V. Write $\tau_V(w)=\sum_{d\mid w,\ d\le V}\mu(d)$. Define

$$A(u)=\sum_{V<w\le u,\ (w,n)=1}\tau_V(w),\qquad
F(n,V)=\sum_{e\le V}\mu(e)\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)\,f_n(\operatorname{lcm}(e,d)),$$

where e ranges over positive integers and fₙ(r)=log(⌊4n/r⌋!)−2log(⌊3n/r⌋!)+log(⌊2n/r⌋!). By finite transposition the centered band price is

$$\sum_{Q<p\le4n,\ p\nmid n}\log p\,[A(4n/p)-2A(3n/p)+A(2n/p)]-F(n,V).$$

Here p ranges over ordinary primes. The common endpoint is essential. The proof of the band transfer prices floor residues and proper powers. What it does not price is the signed interaction of a prime with a cofactor near the three edges. A new estimate must preserve the sign of the factorial subtraction. [Cofactor row](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExceptionalLayerTheory/SquareConductors/ThePrimitivePrimePriceBeyondAMovingCofactorCut.lean#L26) · [Remaining sum](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExceptionalLayerTheory/SquareConductors/ThePrimitivePrimePriceBeyondAMovingCofactorCut.lean#L158) · [Factorial center](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExceptionalLayerTheory/SquareConductors/ThePrimitivePrimePriceBeyondAMovingCofactorCut.lean#L76) · [Known cost](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExceptionalLayerTheory/SquareConductors/DisjointOrdinaryPrimeRangesAtTheSameMidpoint.lean#L18)

**Proposed mechanism.** Keep the three jumps together under dyadic subdivision of the cofactor range. Expand $\tau_V$ on two neighboring cells before bounding their error. For each pair of divisibility conditions the admissible residues are fixed by gcd and lcm. The incomplete-period discrepancy is a signed floor at the two moving endpoints. Combine these endpoint differences over the dyadic prime block. The hope is that ordinary-prime reciprocal-sum cancellation prices the remaining floor fluctuations after the coherent density term has been removed. The corpus can already do these two operations separately. It does not yet estimate the weighted, jointly truncated expression. A further test uses rough triples. Choose Y with 3n<(Y+1)³ and 4n<(Y+1)⁴. A product through 4n of three nonunit Y-rough factors is necessarily a product of three primes in the right interval, with repetition allowed. The logarithm identity identifies the other two rows whose signed contribution must be retained. Try a dyadic bilinear decomposition of those three rows together. The corpus already factors selected sieve boxes into two factors with a divisor bound. A transfer would have to identify the coefficients, logarithmic weights and midpoint endpoints exactly, then estimate the joint residual. Concentrating on the prime-triple count alone drops those compensating rows. The decisive deliverable is the joint block inequality with its endpoint and divisor costs. A new criterion, a finer classification of remaining coefficients or another computation of the same abscissa becomes progress toward a full proof when it eliminates a term from that inequality or supplies a bound at its required exponent.
