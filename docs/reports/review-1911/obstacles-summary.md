# A research programme for a formal proof of RH

**The strongest full-proof prospect is to bound** the excess of prime logarithms in (2n,3n] over (3n,4n]. Two gcd horizons give exactly this question to square-root precision. Passing to reflected composites pays for the small prime divisors and isolates a sum over a large prime and its small cofactor. **The strongest intermediate target is to decide** whether critical-line crowding under a finite off-line divisor is compatible with the *measured coefficient* of the first ground state's logarithmic cusp. A contradiction would eliminate every finite exceptional configuration without requiring a better zero-free strip. The first target has an exact RH gate. The second has a precise partial payoff and can explain why a family of exclusion arguments fails. ## 2.4 A breakthrough smaller in scope than a zero-free strip

**Can ordinary integer arithmetic exclude a lone *simple* off-line quartet?** Allow every multi-quartet configuration, every infinite off-line divisor, and even one quartet of higher multiplicity. This conclusion would change the list of possible failures without advancing Θ by any given amount. It is narrower than a strip theorem. No complexity comparison is assumed.

A unit-multiplicity quartet predicts a specific wave in ψ−id to square-root/log² accuracy, a specific odd-composite wave, and corresponding prime radii. The midpoint multiplier at that zero cannot vanish. Insert the predicted wave into the *reflected composite factorization* of §2.18 and compute the coefficient the large-factor cells would have to transmit. Try to rule out that coefficient using joint prime/cofactor divisibility. Recovering the same wave by partial summation is the control calculation. A proof must obtain an incompatible coefficient or a smaller remainder independently. Prime edits can imitate a radius. ## 2.7 A physical phenomenon and a way to test it

**Physical hypothesis.** The modular cusp loses returning wave amplitude at a universal rate because repeated arithmetic traversals dephase. A compact incident pulse leaves a delayed return. RH corresponds to quarter-rate amplitude decay up to ε for every such pulse. The project can supply decay below 3001/48000 already. Passive arithmetic twins can reproduce prescribed traversal data to any chosen finite depth and still have a slow return. This locates the physical question in the *late coherent return* of the actual cusp. **Test the mechanism independently.** For the reflected signal r define tail energy $E_h(T)=\int_T^\infty |r_h(u)|^2du$. Seek a propagation length L and a late-time decrement for $E_h(T+L)/E_h(T)$ at the predicted energy rate, first on a pair of pulses and their superpositions. Vary relative phase. Polarization reads the cross term. Derive the decrement from path returns with the travel times and repeated-prime amplitudes fixed by the modular surface. Prove a local regularity or receiver estimate before inferring pointwise field decay from tail energy. ## 2.9 Might RH need a new field?

A full proof needs **one** successful mechanism. There is no evidence that every route's obstruction must be solved, or that existing mathematics is incapable of solving one. The gap across several routes suggests a coherent subject at the interface of analytic number theory and arithmetic intersection theory: inequalities for *completed signed arithmetic correspondences*. Its objects would be integer graphs with signed multiplicity, prime-power contacts, the two pole degrees, an archimedean metric, and maps between cutoff families. Its operations would be restriction, composition, change of window, completion and comparison with explicit prime sums. ## 2.10 Might AAL need a new field?

A smaller and more plausible subject is **arithmetic detection of finite outer zero layers**. AAL says every strict right tail beyond ½+h, h≥0, is empty or infinite. A falsifier needs an actual zero in such a tail **and** an upper bound on the number of all zeros there. A model finite layer, or one zero detected at finite height, gives neither conclusion for ζ. A finite outer tail may coexist with infinitely many shallower exceptions. [Exact falsifier](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ZeroConfigurationGeometry/AbscissaLaws/TheFailureOfTheAbscissaLawIsAFiniteLayer.lean#L33)

Its objects would be an outer exponential sum with multiplicities, the remainder at smaller depth, and prime samples of their combined lag. Its basic questions are whether a finite outer signal must recur with a quantitatively stable phase, and whether ordinary primes forbid that recurrence. A finite visible top gives growth on each sufficiently late log-window of any fixed positive length in the sampling theorem. A sufficient route to AAL is a normalized small reading at *all* primes of a suitable arbitrarily late window, for every eligible positive top. Tools should distinguish sampling density, amplitude and multiplicity, propagate an error on the lower background, and flag aliasing. Test them on both an attained infinite top and a separated finite top. For falsification one must also certify the remainder cannot hide further zeros above the chosen depth. The required tools might all fit within present harmonic analysis. The missing theorem is arithmetic selectivity at the actual top. ## 2.12 A field for a geometric proof

The natural subject would be a quantitative geometry of **noncompact branched covers arising from arithmetic heat flows**. Its objects are the two-variable heat surface, finite spatial windows of varying degree, their contour moment polynomials, discriminant sections and transition functions on the moving boundary. Its invariant must count collisions with integer multiplicity and remain computable from the original θ kernel when zeros leave a spatial window. Local holomorphic geometry already supplies the fold and multiplicity formula. The missing tools are a gluing theorem across height, effective exterior estimates and a time exhaustion that can approach zero. ## 2.18 Best prospect for a full proof

**I would prioritize the reflected-composite prime midpoint, with ACS as its exact measuring instrument and ordinary factorization as the proposed producer.** It has one source, a known center, two adjacent intervals and an explicit half-power gate. **Proof object.** For n>1 put $I_-=(2n,3n]$, $I_+=(3n,4n]$, and let “difference” below mean right minus left. Every prime in these intervals is coprime to n. Let C(n) be the difference of log weights of the composites in the two intervals coprime to n. Let A(n) be the corresponding difference over *all* integers coprime to n. Then the prime midpoint has the opposite orientation and satisfies

$$
 D(n)=2\theta(3n)-\theta(2n)-\theta(4n)=C(n)-A(n),\qquad
 |A(n)-(10\log2-6\log3)\varphi(n)|
 \le \sum_{d\mid n}\frac{|\mu(d)|}{n/d}.
$$

The rightmost error is at most the divisor count. Thus the deterministic center already has far more precision than the proof requires. This is a stronger reduction than guessing the average slope of C(n). $$
