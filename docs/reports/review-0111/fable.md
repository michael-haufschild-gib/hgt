## 0. Summary

1. The research has reduced RH to one even integer `κ`, read on one hyperplane `v₁^⊥` of one degenerating family of
   finite quadratic forms, along one axis (overlap = aperture = degree). Every live route is a face of this one
   object; every reformulation (bounded kernel, bounded coupling on one two-prime lattice, Castelnuovo–Severi at
   rational scales, Künneth square, Hadamard powers, glued index, heat homotopy, Liouville property of the overlap
   flow, collision charge below two, Frobenius at infinity, primitive `h¹ ≥ 0`, Rosati positivity) is exactly RH
   `[L]`. The reformulations concentrate the problem; none softens it.
2. The three walls Σ (sign), Π (precision), Γ (selection) are one wall seen from three sides. At the receiver scale
   the margin of the complete form and the size of the zero side are of the same order, so no zero-blind input
   decides the sign; every zero-aware integer invariant, read at that resolution, is RH; and every selector is a
   margin on a partial form. Π and Γ are consequences of crossing Σ by the wrong kind of argument: an estimate.
   The corpus has proved this logic, not merely observed it (edge theory, zero-one laws, resolution limits).
3. The exits are therefore of one kind: statements that hold at the edge **by structure** and see the zeros there —
   identities, inherited polarizations, integer invariants with a source-side bound — and whose construction uses
   the Euler product, the functional equation and the pole **as identities** (which dissolves Γ by construction)
   and whose precision is a corollary (which bypasses Π, as Castelnuovo–Severi follows from the Hodge index over
   `𝔽_q`).
4. Four mechanisms of that kind are identified in §2 and §3: trace identities place by place (the archimedean one
   is already a theorem in the corpus), a count-valued primitive `h¹` from the `GL₂`/Eisenstein side of the split
   surface, a polarized limiting structure of the degenerating family over the cone generated by the primes, and
   a sum–product identity on the top coprime stratum. Each is sub-RH in its first instance.
5. The one question (§8): turn the certified small-aperture positivity into a **trace of a positive operator**
   that reaches the second prime — the first edge-valid, arithmetic, place-structured positivity producer.

### 1.2 The degenerating family and the walls

The complete Weil matrix `W_{φ,c,N}` is a section of the plane of resolutions (width `w`, horizon `N`) along `w = c/N`.
At fixed width the horizon families are nested, the Hodge defect is a monotone integer converging to the number of
right zeros the atom sees, so positivity is a threshold in `N`; the edge is where the section `w = c/N` crosses the
threshold curve, which hugs the hyperbola `wN = 1` within log-power factors `[L]`.

Three regimes `[L]`:

- **universal regime** (positivity proved without zero information): tilted spline on all vectors for
  `c ≤ (log N)^α`, `α < 1/9`; on fixed and polylogarithmic conductor packets for `α < 1/8`; the zero-free
  logarithmic profile for `c ≤ ε·c*_N`;
- **RH regime**: tilted spline at every `2K(log N)^{1/8}`; logarithmic profile at every `K·c*_N`; smooth
  nonnegative profiles on the pole hyperplane at every `2(log N)^α`, `α > 0`;
- **the edge** between them, `τ(c) log N ≈ 1` (`τ` the Fourier tail of the profile).

What the edge is made of `[L]`: at fixed overlap the form converges to the Ramanujan-graded limit
`Σ_m E_m(c) y_m²` with completed conductor energies `E_m(c) ≥ E₁(c/m)`, polarized by a positive Fourier integral
with the pole conductor as ground state; along every growing schedule the limit degenerates to `0`, in the order of
the conductors. The floor at overlap `c` is `E₁(c) ≍ c^{−7}` (tilted spline) and the generic relative residual is
`O(c⁸/log N)`: the two meet exactly at `c⁸ ≍ log N`, which is also the receiver. So the tilted spline's wall is a
constant `K`, not an exponent; the logarithmic profile's wall is the constant `ε` versus every `K` (margin
`exp(−B₀c/log²c)` versus residual `c/log N`). At each degree `a` the form is coercive off a finite divisor
`D_a ∪ {i/2}` with constant `(10 + 68e^a)(1 − 1/π)`, but it carries a near radical of dimension `(2 ± η)e^{2a}`
with energy `≤ exp(−η²e^{2a}/(300a))`, built from tests blind to every zero below the Landau height `2πe^{2a}`.

The logic of the wall `[L, D]`: an estimate valid for a class of data sharing its inputs (prime distribution of
zero-free-region strength, Siegel–Walfisz uniformity, Gamma identities) is valid for every member of the class,
including data with off-line zeros at the depth the inputs permit; it therefore stops below the edge of every such
member. Refining the estimate moves the constant, never the regime. Signature calculus does not help either: every
integer invariant of the family, read at an RH-complete resolution, is RH. This is why Σ1 (all-aperture sign),
Σ1i (the constant), Σ2 (hard Lefschetz on the positive locus), Σ3a (the coprime stratum), Σ5 (the quantum) are
sharp and equivalent, and why Π15 (critical-scale residual sign) is where Σ meets Π.

### 1.3 Where the arithmetic is and is not

- **Locally everything is positive.** Deep stars are Hodge–Riemann with margin `log d − C₀`; every prime-power
  atom is paid by its own reflection strip; the lattice skeleton is a nonnegative Laplacian; the pole pays the
  archimedean continuum except on thin axis collars; each prime tower is a positive Kac–Murdock–Szegő form; the
  renormalized prime form has spectrum `{γ + log m + tameDifferent(m)}` on Ramanujan sums with gap `2 log 2` `[L]`.
- **Globally the difficulty sits in one place.** Links of the dilation complex are disconnected (distinct prime
  towers are never adjacent); coprime atoms couple only through the archimedean place; the complete form is the
  sign-free gcd stratification `W = Σ_g Cop(W at the star of g)`, and the top stratum `g = 1` carries every
  successor pair `(n, n+1)` and every coprime shell `(n, kn+h)` `[L]`. The only prime-dependent parts of the top
  stratum are the root cone and the coprime shells; the archimedean part is the same for every twin `[L, D]`.
- **The additive structure is where ζ is selected.** Every purely multiplicative statistic is constant on the gauge
  torus `(ℝ/ℤ)^{primes}` and almost every gauge point already satisfies square-root cancellation; the actual source
  is atypical exactly by its pole; the successor `n ↦ n+1` is what Beurling systems lack; the self-similar flux
  `Λ ∗ 1 = log` selects generalized von Mangoldt weights among all twists `[L]`.
- **The archimedean place is the glue.** No assembly of local poles (sum, tensor, renormalized infinite product)
  has one square; the global pole must be supplied by `∞`, which glues dilation data through one hyperplane and
  absorbs exactly a simple pole `[L]`. The conformal Frobenius has no positive analogue; its substitute is the
  pole–successor state (a boost on `×` glued to a conformal successor on `+`) with exactly one negative square
  `[L]`.
- **Selection is closed, production is not.** Every prime-set flip, the Liouville twin and the zero-density twins
  below relative density `1/4` are separated by margins, probes or conductor series `[L]`; but every selector is a
  margin on a partial form, and Frobenius at infinity — the producing half — has only RH-equivalent readings `[L]`.

### 1.4 Calibration

Over `𝔽_q` the same theory has a trivial flow: detection happens by window `2g`, links are complete, towers are
commensurable, the primitive `h¹` of a graph is a count `[L]`. Over `ℤ` the flow is genuine because infinitely many
critical zeros surround every off-line pair, the genus is a finite divisor at every degree and unbounded in the
degree, and two primes already make the exponents of the extremal weight dense `[L]`. Every soft law holds on the
fakes (golden curve datum with Hodge defect `1`; Epstein zeta of `ℤ ⊕ 8ℤ`; Liouville and prime-flip twins), so a
law that holds on the fakes cannot be the one `[L]`.

## 2. Q1 — A mechanism that circumvents or destroys a wall

### 2.1 What "destroying a wall" can and cannot mean

- **Σ cannot be destroyed by estimates**, for the logical reason in §1.2 `[L, D]`. It can only be *crossed* by an
  edge-valid statement: an identity with free nonnegativity, an inherited polarization from a limit that does not
  degenerate, or an integer with a source-side bound.
- **Γ dissolves by construction.** A producer built from the Euler product, the functional equation and the simple
  pole used as identities (as the self-similar flux law already does) is not defined on the fakes; selection then
  needs no margin. The Γ wall is the cost of producers that are proved for a class.
- **Π is bypassed, not attacked.** Over `𝔽_q` the square-root count is a corollary of the Hodge index. Over `ℤ`,
  `κ = 0` implies `ψ(x) − x = O(√x log²x)` `[L]`. A sign producer makes the precision statements theorems; only
  routes that go through precision to reach the sign face Π head-on, and for those the ladder jumps from
  PNT-strength to a zero-free strip with nothing provable in between `[L]` (parity barrier, sign-blindness of
  period data, size-only inversion).

So the question reduces to: which edge-valid producers are available, and which sub-RH instance of each is
provable now. Four candidates follow; the first two are supported by theorems in the corpus, the last two by exact
identities in the corpus plus mathematics outside it.

### 2.2 Candidate A — Positivity as a trace, place by place `[L] + [P]`

The archimedean Weil distribution is already a trace: on the intrinsic compact-Schwartz test space,
`opTrace(integrated scaling ∘ geometric scattering log-differential) = archimedean Weil distribution`, with the
exact `log 4π` constant (the normalization-sensitive half of Connes–Consani's Proposition 7(iv)) `[L]`. Positivity
of a trace of a positive operator is an identity plus free nonnegativity — the type that crosses an edge — and it is
what pays the prime-free window.

The semilocal extension has a proved shape constraint: the projection-correction Hilbert–Schmidt pairing has a
response bounded uniformly in the dilation scale, whereas the von Mangoldt source requires a response growing like
`log p^m`; this retires that pairing "and does not obstruct a genuine semilocal trace formula whose test operator has
logarithmically growing trace" `[L]`.

**Mechanism.** Build, for the finite place set `S = {2}` then `{2, 3}`, a semilocal operator whose trace reproduces
the explicit formula restricted to tests of aperture `< ½ log 3` (which see exactly the prime `2` and its powers),
with the archimedean trace identity as the `∞`-factor and a local operator at `2` whose trace grows like
`k log 2` on the `k`-th power. Positivity of `W` on that window would then be the positivity of one operator. The
Euler product makes the construction a tensor product over places; the gluing of the places is the one square the
archimedean place must supply (`κ(glued) = κ^{(½)} + 1` `[L]`), and the non-separable Schur positivity of the
inter-tower coupling is the exact open step named in AKG.

**Why it is edge-valid.** Nothing in it is an estimate against the ground energy. It reads the weight at `2`
through the local factor, not through a margin, so it is selective by construction (the Liouville twin and every
flip of `2` have a different local factor).

**Sub-RH instance.** All-test positivity up to aperture `½ log 2 ≈ 0.347` is not yet proved (the even sector with
nonzero pole moments stops at `3/10`) `[L]`; through the first prime, `½ log 3 ≈ 0.549`, is open for all tests
(odd tests reach `181/500 = 0.362`) `[L]`. Either is a new unconditional theorem.

### 2.3 Candidate B — A count-valued primitive `h¹` from the `GL₂` side of the split surface `[L] + [E] + [P]`

The unification ledger's "one missing fact" is that the primitive `h¹` of a correspondence is a count. The corpus
has located the objects precisely `[L]`: the pole plane with its boosts `A_u = e^uH + e^{−u}V`; the Siegel
majorants `P_u ≥ 0 ⇔ Hodge index`; fixed boosts are rectangular lattices and fakes for `|u| > 1` (Epstein real zeros
in `(½, 1)` for `y > e²`), the self-dual point has none; Hecke is a boost by `½ log p` on the pole plane and a
similitude of ratio `p` on the primitive part; the boost transform `∫ Z⁺(s,u) e^{2wu} du = K(s,w) ζ(s+w) ζ(s−w)`
restores the Euler product; the isotropic cone count `Σ_{m,n≥1} e^{−2πtmn}` has Mellin transform
`Γ(σ)(2π)^{−σ} ζ(σ)²` with a double pole and admits no Gaussian Poisson pair.

Read in known mathematics `[E]`: the pole plane with its boost line is the geodesic flow on `SL₂(ℤ)\SL₂(ℝ)`; the
Siegel majorant counts are Siegel transforms along the diagonal flow; the "rectangular Epstein" functions are
the real-analytic Eisenstein series `E(iy, s)` up to the factor `2ζ(2s)y^{−s}` (their real zeros for large `y`
are the classical Epstein phenomena of Davenport–Heilbronn and Bateman–Grosswald, lattices high in the cusp); the boost transform is the Hecke–Maass Mellin transform of `E(·,s)` along the geodesic, i.e. its standard
`L`-function `ζ(s+w)ζ(s−w)`; the "logarithmic Serre duality" that the cone count needs (Π11f) is **Voronoi
summation** for the divisor function, whose main term `∫ f(x)(log x + 2γ)dx` is exactly the `log(D²/2)` of pole-plane
Riemann–Roch, and whose dual side is a Bessel (`K₀`, `Y₀`) transform rather than a Gaussian one. The divisor function
is the Fourier coefficient of `E(z, ½)`; indefinite theta series with characteristics (Zwegers–Vignéras) are the tool
for the cone cut by two classes.

**Mechanism.** Define the section count on the split surface by the `GL₂` Eisenstein/Voronoi duality instead of a
Gaussian Poisson pair: `h⁰` of a class with a test component is the logarithm of a Siegel-type transform of the
test along the boost, Serre duality is the Voronoi/functional-equation identity, and the primitive `h¹` is read
off the `GL₂` spectral side. The candidate producer of the sign is then the positivity of a spectral expansion
(Rankin–Selberg or Petersson-type inner products are nonnegative by construction), with `ζ` entering through the
Eisenstein coefficients.

**What it must not be.** Spectral positivity of `GL₂` alone does not locate the scattering poles (KR-AUT, KR-SEL
`[L]`); the mechanism must read the *primitive* part (the test component), which bidegree counts cannot `[L]`.
This candidate targets Π11f/Π11h, the production face, and its first instances are sub-RH (§5).

### 2.4 Candidate C — A polarized limiting structure over the cone generated by the primes `[L] + [E] + [P]`

Edge theory names its model: Hodge theory of degenerations, where positivity survives in the limit by structure
(nilpotent-orbit and `SL₂`-orbit theorems) `[L]` and records that the naive limit structure degenerates and that
the pole's `SL₂` is blind on `v₁^⊥` `[L]`. The missing ingredient is not the model but its arithmetic realization:

- **the monodromy cone.** The Euler product tensorizes the dilation complex over primes; the layers of a prime are
  the stars of `p^a`; each tower is a geometric-weight string with gap `log p/(1 − p^{−σ})` `[L]`. The natural
  nilpotent operators are the valuation-lowering (Hecke) generators `N_p`, one per prime, commuting; the cone they
  span is the arithmetic analogue of a normal-crossings degeneration with one branch per prime; the branches meet
  only at the archimedean place (links disconnected `[L]`);
- **the weight filtration.** `E_m(c) ≍ E₁(c/m)`, so modes degenerate in the order of their conductors `[L]`: the
  conductor is the weight, `log m` the weight exponent (`7 log m` for the tilted spline);
- **Hodge–Riemann on graded pieces.** Mid-band conductor coercivity for `α < 1/4`, single modes on
  `[(log N)^A, N^θ]`, polylogarithmic packets for `α < 1/8` `[L]` are exactly Hodge–Riemann on graded pieces of this
  filtration;
- **the missing step.** In Cattani–Kaplan–Schmid theory `[E]` the polarization on the whole space is recovered from
  the graded pieces by the structure of a polarized mixed Hodge structure (a purely linear-algebraic theorem once a
  nilpotent orbit is given), and the multi-variable version handles a cone of commuting nilpotents whose relative
  weight filtration is independent of the interior point. Over `ℤ` the extension data between graded pieces are the
  mixed conductor/complement terms — the successor and archimedean coupling of the top stratum `[L]`.

**Mechanism.** Prove that the rescaled limit of the degenerating family (EHT's "second-order asymptotics,
`E_m(c)/E₁(c)`, Jordan-totient weight structure") is the limiting mixed structure of a nilpotent orbit for the prime
cone, with the archimedean glue as the only non-commuting datum; then the inherited polarization on `v₁^⊥` is a
theorem of the Cattani–Kaplan type, not an estimate. The sub-RH instance: the inheritance of the limit polarization
past the universal regime on the subthreshold conductor block `m ≲ c` (EHT open topic 2).

### 2.5 Candidate D — A sum–product identity on the top coprime stratum `[L] + [E] + [P]`

The packet identity at every overlap is exact `[L]`:
`E = J_N + ½A_N + Σ F(n)²/n·(s_c + e_N + δ_N(n) − R(⌊N/n⌋)) − Σ d_n F(n)² + (pole, boundary, near overlap)`,
with the jump energy `J_N ≥ 0`, the affine incidence energy `A_N ≥ 0` (a sum of squares of affine events over a
complete period by CRT independence of distinct primes), the Mertens remainder `R → 0`, floor defects in
`[0, log 2]`, and the shell degree `d_n = Σ_m v_{nm}` as the only growing term. Nonpositive directions localize in a
finite conductor sector with jump energy `≤ (C/a)c¹⁶` `[L]`. On the coherent sector the shell balance is exactly
zero on average over a complete period of shifts; the sign is set by the distribution of the shell shifts modulo the
packet primes and by ratios sharing primes with the packet `[L]`. Since the shifts `h` of the non-ratio pairs at
overlap `c` satisfy `|h| ≲ c`, the deficit terms (primes dividing the shift) are confined to packet primes
`p ≤ c` `[D]`: the whole sign problem at the receiver lives on conductors `≲ c ≍ (log N)^{1/8}`, in agreement with
Σ1d.

**Mechanism.** The needed inequality — shell degree bounded by affine incidence energy plus jump energy plus the
lower strata, with `N`-independent constants — is a statement of additive combinatorics: the dilation graph
(`n ↦ kn`) against the successor graph (`n ↦ n + h`) on `[1, N]`, i.e. the incidence structure of the affine maps
`n ↦ kn + h` with multiplicative weights `Λ(k)/k`. Its finite-field shadow is the expansion of Cayley graphs of the
affine group `𝔽_p ⋊ 𝔽_p^×` with generators `x+1`, `λx`, which follows from the sum–product theorem (Bourgain–Gamburd
school) `[E]`, and it is the exact replacement for the Garland/trickle-down step that the disconnected links forbid
(B10, "method"). The known obstruction to respect: complete-period quadratic data are sign-blind `[L]`; the
inequality must carry the counterterm `log N − γ`, the floor defects and the Mertens remainders at shifted horizons
`R(⌊(N−h)/n⌋)`, which are the only prime-dependent parts of the top stratum `[L, D]`.

### 2.6 Candidate E — A naive height for the Rosati form `[L] + [P]`

Tate's limit law is isolated in the corpus: the theorem of the square is free on every polar surface, and Rosati
positivity at a cycle holds iff the integral Rosati form `ξ ↦ τ_x(ξ ξ′)` on `ℤ[G]` is at bounded distance from a
function bounded below (a naive height) or from a midpoint-convex function (a log-count) `[L]`. Over a finite field
the degree `deg(m − nF)` is the order of a kernel, its own naive height; the golden datum has `deg(2 − F) = −1` and
none `[L]`.

**Mechanism.** Construct on `ℤ[G]` (`G` the scale group) a function nonnegative for an arithmetic reason — a
log-volume, a cardinality, a log-count with nonnegative weights — within bounded distance of the Rosati form of the
cubic spline. This is Candidate B in a different coordinate (the Rosati form of a Hecke correspondence is twice the
degree of its normalized graph, i.e. the primitive `h¹` `[L]`), and the same obstruction applies: every log-count
built from the base is a bidegree count and misses the primitive part `[L]`. Its value is that it states the
production face as a *bounded-distance* problem, which tolerates every soft error.

## 3. Q2 — Approaches wildly different from all existing routes

The existing routes all compute the sign of `W` on `v₁^⊥` or a precision statement feeding it. "Different" must
mean: a different object whose RH-relevant statement is not already one of the proved equivalences, or a different
kind of argument (counting, representation theory, dynamics) on the same object. Three are available; I rank them.

### 3.1 Homogeneous dynamics and automorphic forms on `SL₂(ℤ)\SL₂(ℝ)` for the split surface

As in §2.3: the pole plane is the geodesic flow on the space of unimodular lattices, Siegel majorants are Siegel
transforms, fakes are lattices high in the cusp, the boost average is the Rankin–Selberg unfolding, and Hecke
operators are correspondences on the modular curve. This moves the production face into a setting that has
exact spectral theory (Maass forms, Eisenstein series, Kuznetsov), exact dualities (Voronoi) and a trace formula.
Two concrete handles:

- **the scattering side for PM.** The scattering matrix of `SL₂(ℤ)\ℍ` is `Λ(2s−1)/Λ(2s)`; its poles are the zeros
  of `ζ` at `s = ρ/2`, and the corpus already has the resonant Eisenstein states with the partner angle
  `cos² = 4 Re ρ (1 − Re ρ)`, saturated iff `Re ρ = ½` `[L]`. Lax–Phillips scattering theory for automorphic
  functions `[E]` realizes these resonances as the spectrum of the generator of a contraction semigroup on the
  orthocomplement of incoming and outgoing subspaces of the automorphic wave equation, and relates RH to a
  uniform decay rate of that semigroup. This is a *Krein-type* (indefinite energy form) structure on the Eisenstein
  sector — precisely what B40 asks for near `Re w = 1` — and it is not in the corpus.
- **the section side for HF/ACS.** The primitive part is what a `GL₂` object must read beyond the two fibre degrees.
  The candidate carriers are inner products of theta lifts cut by the test component (indefinite theta with
  characteristics), where positivity is built in and `ζ` enters through Eisenstein coefficients.

Walls touched: Π11f/Π11h (production) and Σ8/Γ4 through scattering. It does not go through the constant-factor
wall Σ1i and it does not use the complete atomic family at all.

### 3.2 Arithmetic topology of the squarefree nerve

The nerve of the star cover is the complex of sets of primes with product `≤ N`; its signed face count is the
Mertens function and RH is its half-power growth `[L]`. In known mathematics `[E]` this complex is homotopy
equivalent to a wedge of spheres (Björner), so RH is a cancellation among Betti numbers, not a bound on them. The
different approach is HWV's open topic 3 made concrete: find a **signature-type invariant** of the stratified
forms over the nerve (the gcd stratification is literally a decomposition of `W` indexed by the nerve `[L]`) that
refines the Euler characteristic the way the Hodge index refines Betti numbers. A quadratic form on the top
cochains of the nerve whose trace is `N` and whose signature counts `M(N)` with a defect would make `M(N) = O(N^{½+ε})`
a consequence of a Hodge-index-type statement on the nerve. Walls touched: Π1/Π2 only through the Mertens
reformulation; it is a Σ-type statement on a combinatorial object.

### 3.3 Expansion of the affine group as the arithmetic Garland step

As in §2.5: the sum–product phenomenon (Bourgain–Katz–Tao; expansion of `Aff(𝔽_p)` Cayley graphs) is the
finite-field form of "a cross-tower spectral gap through the successor" (B10). The different point of view is to
treat `[1, N]` with the two operations as a graph whose Laplacian (with weights `Λ(k)/k` on dilation edges and the
archimedean kernel on successor edges) must have a spectral gap on `v₁^⊥` uniformly at the critical scale — an
expander-type statement rather than a positivity-of-a-form statement. Walls touched: Σ23 and Σ3a directly. The
proved sign-blindness of complete-period data is the obstruction it must respect.

### 3.4 What I would not pursue

Hilbert–Pólya operators, crystalline/quasicrystal rigidity of the zero divisor, dynamical realizations of the
primes, tensor/Hadamard amplification, Deligne-style family moments, GL(2) spectral positivity alone, and mollified
moments are each killed or classified with a positive survivor `[L]`; the survivors are already live routes.

## 4. Q3 — Breakthrough questions less hard than RH or quasi-RH

Each question is sub-RH (strength `sub` in the obstacle ledger's scale), has a definite statement, and its answer
changes what the routes can do.

### 4.1 The research as a whole

**Q3.1 — Positivity by identity on the prime-free window.** Is Weil positivity on tests of aperture `< ½ log 2` —
currently a theorem proved by Lévy estimates up to `3/10` and odd-sector folds to `181/500` `[L]` — the positivity of
a trace of a positive operator (the archimedean trace identity `[L]` extended to the pole terms), equivalently the
Riemann inequality of a lattice-count `h⁰` on the scaling surface (LST open topic 1)? And does the same identity hold
on the full window, including the even pole direction between `3/10` and `½ log 2` that no estimate has paid?

Why it is a breakthrough: it is the first positivity statement of the edge-valid kind with arithmetic content (the
pole), and it is the base point every inductive mechanism (A, C, E) starts from. Its companion is the **genus law**
(Π16): the near radical at degree `a` has dimension `(2 + o(1))e^{2a}` `[L]` for the lower half, open for the upper
half; this is the number of sections the count must produce at degree `a`, and `2e^{2a}` is the number of integers
up to `e^{2a}` — the atoms.

### 4.2 Route PM

**Q3.2 — A Hermitian (Krein) structure on the Gauss–Mayer eigenfunctions near `Re w = 1`.** The regularized
Gauss–Mayer operator `R(w)` has an entire Fredholm determinant `D(w, λ) = A + ζ(w)B`, with `D(w, −1)` holomorphic and
nonvanishing on `Re w > 0`, a real-`w` derivative cone, `B(w, λ) ≠ 0` for small real `λ`, and the primitive coding
of hyperbolic `SL₂(ℤ)` classes `[L]`; the Mayer–Selberg trace identity is known mathematics to be formalized (A24).
The sub-RH question (B40): build on the eigenfunctions of `R(w)` the Lewis–Zagier/Lax–Phillips Hermitian structure
of the Eisenstein sector and derive from it a zero-free region near `Re s = 1` by transfer-operator positivity. A
new proof of the classical zero-free region by this mechanism would establish that the PM realization *moves zeros*,
which no realization in the corpus does; it would also calibrate the partner-saturation reading of Frobenius at
infinity on an operator.

### 4.3 Route MC

**Q3.3 — A Class III statistic with an exponent.** The finite divisor observer is sign-blind at damping `½`: payment
is a function of Ramanujan amplitudes iff `ε > ½`, and parity pairs (same amplitudes, one paid, one unpaid) exist at
every `ε ≤ ½` `[L]`. The sub-RH question: construct a finite, cutoff-quantitative observable of the damped Möbius
row that reads signs on composite moduli through the successor or Bézout structure (`gcd(n, n+1) = 1`,
`pa − qb = h`) and separates the parity pair with an **exponent**, not qualitatively (cubic and log moments separate
only qualitatively `[L]`). This is strictly below a strip (it is a statement about detectability, not about ζ), and it
is the prerequisite for any Möbius receiver below `½`. Its elementary companion: can integer Hecke stencils (every
individual producer has slope `A > 1`, growing alphabets reach `ψ, θ ≤ (1+ε)x + C_ε log²x` `[L]`) reach the
Diamond–Steinig error term `x·exp(−(log x)^{1/7})` — the elementary PNT with an error term, by producers?

### 4.4 Route WD

**Q3.4 — Critical-scale clustering.** For `0 < c < 22/51` the dyadic mean of the unit-window depth charge is
`O(log U)`, and `o(log U)` is equivalent to: for every `κ > 0`, the proportion of zeros up to `T` with
`|Re ρ − ½| > κ/log T` tends to `0` `[L]`. Selberg's exponential near-line density, formalized, bounds the
proportion of zeros at depth `≥ 2a/log U` by an exponentially small function of `a` (order `e^{−a/1200}/a`)
`[L]`, so the question is to improve a fixed exponentially small proportion to `o(1)` at every fixed `κ`. Equivalent prime-side form: the Hecke defect `E_γ(c log T)` (a signed mean of the prime polynomial against its
conjugate diagonal) has dyadic mean `o(log U)`. This is a zero-density statement at the zero-spacing scale,
strictly below Lindelöf, with no known approach; its proof would be the first precision result beyond Selberg's
method at the critical scale, and it is the `sub` rung of WD's own ladder (`o(log U)` ⇒ clustering; fixed scale ⇒
corridor ≥qRH; one constant over dyadic scales ⇒ RH).

### 4.5 Route HF

**Q3.5 — The low-conductor block at the receiver.** Nonpositive directions of the complete form localize in a finite
conductor sector; single modes are coercive on `[(log N)^A, N^θ]`; mid-band packets are coercive for `α < 1/4`;
all vectors are positive for `α < 1/9` `[L]`. The sub-RH question (Σ1d, A1): prove the Hodge index on the span of
conductors `m ≤ (log N)^A` together with its mixed complement for every `α < 1/8`, by the shell/incidence identity
(the deficit is confined to packet primes `p ≤ c` `[D]`) rather than by residual averages. It closes the exponent gap
`1/9 → 1/8` on the whole space, after which the tilted-spline wall is exactly the constant `K`, and it is the
"shallow stars" problem (HWV open topic 2) in conductor coordinates.

## 5. Q4 — Questions that would open the path wide

These are RH-strength or one step below, chosen so that an answer supplies a mechanism, not a reformulation.

### 5.1 The research as a whole

**Q4.1 — The count.** Is there a section count on the scaling surface — `h⁰` defined from lattices or volumes, Serre
duality an identity, `h¹ ≥ 0` by construction — whose primitive `h¹` on the orbit of the cubic spline is `½W(f, f)`?
Equivalently (Tate's limit law `[L]`): a function on `ℤ[G]`, nonnegative for an arithmetic reason, at bounded
distance from the integral Rosati form. Over `𝔽_q` this is the kernel count `#ker(m − nF)`; over `ℤ` it would give
sign, precision and selection at once, as the unification ledger states.

### 5.2 Route PM

**Q4.2 — Zero-selecting scattering rigidity.** Show that the eigenvalue-`±1` parameters of `R(w)` in `½ < Re w < 1`

which the resonant state of `ρ` and that of `1 − ρ̄` are forced into partner saturation — Frobenius at infinity as an
orthogonality relation on the Eisenstein sector. The Lax–Phillips semigroup, whose uniform decay rate is RH `[E]`,
is the natural carrier.

### 5.3 Route MC

**Q4.3 — A signed input at the diagonal.** Every finite route of MC collapses to the diagonal `d ≍ Q`, the Bézout
interior martingale and the receiver below half damping, where the statistic is a functional of long-window Möbius

`∫_{|ξ| ≤ √x} |ν̂_x(ξ)|²/(¼ + 4π²ξ²)`, since any subexponential band is RH by self-improvement `[L]`.

### 5.4 Route WD

**Q4.4 — A height-uniform integer reading with a source bound.** The collision charge is the Fredholm index of a
Wiener–Hopf operator whose symbol is built from integer-theta data; RH is index `< 2` per window `[L]`; the

of an operator whose symbol is *nonvanishing on the circle for an arithmetic reason* (a Szegő-type positivity of
the integer-theta symbol), giving a height-uniform bound below `2`. Its ≥qRH sub-step: the fixed-scale Hecke-defect
bound `E_γ(c log T) = O(1)`, which already gives the inverse-logarithmic corridor and Lindelöf `[L]`.

### 5.5 Route HF

**Q4.5 — Hard Lefschetz across the gluing.** Positivity of the top coprime stratum against the strata below with
`N`-independent constants (Σ3a, B9), in either of two forms: an exact sum-of-squares identity for the successor and
archimedean coupling of coprime atoms (the sum–product inequality of §2.5), or an inherited polarization from a
polarized limiting structure over the prime cone (§2.4). Either one is the inductive step that Hodge theory without
varieties supplies where it works, and it is equivalent to the Liouville property along the overlap flow `[L]`.

## 7. Q6 — The one question

**Can the certified positivity of Weil's form at small aperture be rewritten as the trace of a positive operator —
the archimedean trace identity already in the corpus, tensored with a local operator at the prime `2` whose trace
grows logarithmically along the powers of `2` — so that all-test Weil positivity holds through aperture `½ log 3`
as an identity plus the nonnegativity of a trace?**

Why this one:

- It is sub-RH and new as a theorem: the all-test window is proved to `3/10`, below the prime-free bound `½ log 2`;
  the second prime at `½ log 3` has never been paid for all tests `[L]`.
- It is the first producer of the only admissible kind. Edge theory proves that estimates cannot cross the edge and
  that integer invariants read at the edge are RH `[L]`; a trace of a positive operator is an identity with free
  nonnegativity. The archimedean instance exists `[L]`, so the question is not whether such producers exist but
  whether they extend to a prime.
- It is selective by construction: the local operator at `2` reads the weight at `2` through the Euler factor, so
  the Liouville twin and every flip of `2` are outside its domain; Γ is not an issue for it.
- It isolates the gluing. The Euler product gives the tensor structure over places; the archimedean place supplies
  the single negative square and glues by one hyperplane `[L]`; the only open step after the prime-`2` instance is
  the non-separable positivity of the coupling across places, now posed for an operator whose trace is the Weil
  distribution — a question with an exact statement and an exact calibration over `𝔽_q(T)` (local walls identical,
  trivial flow).
- Its failure mode is informative: the proved shape constraint says what a semilocal trace must look like
  (logarithmically growing response) `[L]`; a second obstruction theorem of that kind would locate, in operator
  terms, what the prime side refuses to do — which is exactly the information the production face lacks.

Runner-up: the genus law `(2 + o(1))e^{2a}` (Π16), because it fixes the dimension every section theory must
reproduce at each degree and is provable by Landau–Widom-type analysis.

## 8. Q7 — The field the walls are asking for

### 8.1 The walls as questions

- Σ asks: *what decides the sign of a form whose margin has fallen below the contribution of a single zero?* The
  answer cannot be an inequality; it must be an identity, an inherited polarization, or a quantized reading with a
  structural bound.
- Π asks: *what produces a count at square-root precision over `ℤ`?* Over `𝔽_q` the count is a dimension
  (sections) or a cardinality (kernel points); over `ℤ` every candidate is either soft or exactly RH `[L]`.
- Γ asks: *what canonical operation identifies the reflection pairing with the conjugation pairing on the zero
  divisor?* Over `𝔽_q` it is complex conjugation of Frobenius eigenvalues, forced by the polarization of the
  Jacobian; over `ℤ` it has four RH-equivalent readings and no construction.

The corpus has already founded four partial fields on these questions (indefinite harmonic analysis of the
dilation–translation system, Hodge theory without varieties, lattice section theory, coupling theory). What they
share, and what is still missing, defines the field.

### 8.2 Name and point of view

**Arithmetic degeneration theory: polarized limits of the sum–product surface.**

Point of view: the Riemann hypothesis is the Hodge–Riemann relation of a *degenerating* family of finite forms —
the complete Weil family along the resolution axis — and the degeneration is along a cone with one branch per prime,
glued at the archimedean place. Positivity is to be obtained the way Hodge theory of degenerations obtains it: not
by estimating the limit (which is zero) but by identifying the limiting polarized structure and proving that it is
inherited. Counts, traces and quantized readings are the three ways an identity can carry a sign through the limit.

### 8.4 Language

Edge-valid statement (identity, inherited polarization, integer invariant); universal regime / RH regime / edge;
producer / receiver; quantum (`1/log N`, the single-zero scale); near radical and finite divisor at a degree;
branch (prime tower), stratum (gcd), top stratum (coprime), glue (archimedean hyperplane); weight (conductor);
graded piece (conductor band); extension datum (mixed complement term); count-valued `h¹`; naive height; trace
carrier; shape constraint (what a trace must do to reproduce a source); fake (soft law holds, RH fails).

### 8.5 Foundational principles

1. **Edge validity.** A theorem counts only if it holds at every resolution, edge included: identities, inherited
   polarizations, integers. Estimates against the ground energy belong to the universal regime and are tools, not
   results.
2. **Locality by places, gluing by one hyperplane.** Every local statement (tower, star, local factor, local trace)
   is proved at its place; the global statement is the local ones glued at `∞` through the pole hyperplane, which
   costs exactly one square.
3. **Identity before inequality, count before estimate.** The sign enters as a dimension, a cardinality, a trace or
   a limiting polarization — never as a margin.
4. **Selection by construction.** The Euler product, the functional equation and the simple pole are used as
   identities (self-similar flux, local factors, pole-successor states); fakes are outside the domain, not excluded
   by a margin.
5. **Calibration.** Every construction must reproduce Weil over `𝔽_q` with a trivial flow (detection by window
   `2g`) and must fail on the battery of fakes at the stated place; a law that holds on the golden datum is soft.
6. **Two pairings, one object.** The reflection pairing (explicit formula) and the conjugation pairing (collided
   form) are computed separately and compared; a construction that computes one must say what it does to the other.

### 8.6 Criteria the field must meet

- a positivity statement on `v₁^⊥` at a resolution past the universal regime, proved by an edge-valid mechanism;
- a count (or trace) whose primitive `h¹` (or trace) equals `½W` on an explicit, growing family of tests, starting
  with the prime-free window and one prime;
- a gluing theorem: local positive carriers at `∞` and at finitely many primes glue to a positive carrier with
  exactly one negative square, with an exact statement of the non-separable coupling;
- a limiting polarized structure for the degenerating family with the prime cone as monodromy, whose inherited
  polarization on graded pieces is a theorem and whose extension data are identified with the top stratum;
- exact behaviour on the fakes at every step.
