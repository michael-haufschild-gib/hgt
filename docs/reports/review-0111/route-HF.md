For HF the building blocks run along the overlap axis, from the range already proved to the receiver scale. The main path:

1. Exponent 1/9 to 1/8 on all vectors, sub-RH. Lower the split cross payment from order c⁹ to c^(8+o(1)), so that tilted-spline positivity covers every vector up to the eighth root of log N. Packets already reach 1/8, and collective packets to 1/4 are in progress.
2. The low-conductor block, sub-RH. Prove the Hodge index on the span of conductors up to (log N)^A, together with its mixed complement, for α < 1/8. In star language this is local Hodge–Riemann on shallow stars, of depth up to about c.
3. Joint shell, pole and Gamma payment, sub-RH. Pay the shell degree, the pole and the non-arithmetic Gamma graph together on growing packets, without estimating each piece separately. The confinement exponent of nonpositive directions must come down to 8 or below.
4. The top coprime stratum. Bound the successor and archimedean coupling of coprime atoms by the affine incidence energy, the jump energy and the strata below, with constants independent of N. The bound must use the von Mangoldt weights of the shells. It must also pay the odd sinh polar mode with the full prime sum, which the semilocal result showed is never paid locally.
5. The constant-factor wall. Identify the exact leading term of the bilinear residual at the receiver scale, where c⁸ is comparable to log N. Equivalently, identify the limit as N → ∞ at a fixed multiple K as Weil's form at radius ½ log K plus a coercive conductor part. Each fixed K is sub-RH.
6. Closing step. Prove positivity at every fixed multiple K of the receiver scale. This is the same as hard Lefschetz on the positive locus along the overlap flow, and it is equivalent to RH.

A production branch can replace blocks 4–6:

- a. The genus law, sub-RH. Prove that the near radical at degree a has dimension (2 + o(1))e^{2a}; only the lower half is proved.
- b. Logarithmic Serre duality, sub-RH. Complete the isotropic cone count, which has Mellin transform ζ² with a double pole, by a Voronoi-type duality. This duality has to carry the logarithm of the pole-plane Riemann–Roch.
- c. A count-valued primitive h¹. Build a lattice or GL₂ section count whose primitive h¹ equals half of Weil's form. Do it first on prime-free tests, then one prime at a time. Nonnegativity of this count gives the Hodge index, which is RH.
