SC's exact index identities are already proved. Glued at ∞ or at any prime, Weil's correlation has index κ + 1. On boost-mass-zero vectors the archimedean form splits as the dilation form plus shell squares. Coupled valuation pullbacks and height-matched windows reproduce the actual Grams exactly.

The main path:

1. The link to the complete family. This is known mathematics; the remaining work is a formalization. Realize the ratCorr Grams at overlap K·c* as smooth-bump packets of the complete Weil family. Glued positivity on the hyperplane then becomes the same statement as positivity at a fixed multiple K.
2. The cone case with unattained width, sub-RH. Show that bounded coupling on the cone a, b ≥ 0 implies RH outright. The open input is a lower bound for the spline weights of zeros whose real parts approach the supremum, set against their spacing modulo 2π/log p and 2π/log q.
3. The zero–one law, sub-RH. Rule out a finite nonzero index, so that κ is either 0 or ∞. Together with block 5 this would give RH.
4. Non-separable Schur positivity. Transfer the uniform finite-sieve Hodge positivity, one positive direction on every finite sieve partition, to the inter-tower coupling form. The semilocal result shows that every truncation to finitely many places is negative. So this step must use the global compensation of the pole by all primes, not local pieces.
5. An arithmetic index ceiling, ≥qRH. Prove from the source that κ(K_ζ) is finite. This gives finite off-line support and Lindelöf. The same rung appears as a bound on the coupling rate: rate σ < ½ is equivalent to zero width at most σ.
6. An exact sum-of-squares identity. Write Weil's dilation form on boost-mass-zero vectors of one two-prime lattice as a sum of squares of coupling differences. Equivalently, find a shell identity for the dilation form itself, not only for its successor part.
7. Closing step. Prove glued index at most 1 at some place, which is positivity of every ratCorr Gram on the boost-mass-zero hyperplane. Equivalently, prove bounded coupling on one lattice ⟨p⟩ × ⟨q⟩. This is equivalent to RH.

The heat branch:

- a. Heat calibration. This is known mathematics; the remaining work is a formalization. Express a certified de Bruijn–Newman upper bound as a heat time at which the glued state has index one.
- b. A subcritical heat count, sub-RH. Prove a uniform count N(t) ≤ exp(A·t^(−a)) with a < 1.
- c. A uniform heat count, ≥qRH. A count bounded uniformly in t is equivalent to bounded collision charge, and it gives finite off-line support.
- d. Closing step. Show that the index does not jump anywhere on (0, 1/8]. This is equivalent to RH.
