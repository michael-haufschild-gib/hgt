## 0. The frontier in ten statements

1. **Positivity on a carrier certifies blindness, half-blindness or isotropy, never forcing.** A sharp-stable positive convolution ideal lies in the maximal blind ideal; a positive ideal without sharp stability is blind to one point of each off-line pair; channels coupled by divisor multipliers on a point-stable carrier are positive only if their fibres at `ρ` and `1 − ρ̄` are isotropic for the coupling matrix. The blind ideal is synthesized by critical jets and the Poisson boundary; its exhaustion, and that of every such carrier, is RH.
2. **Multiplicative data cannot see the actual source.** Multiplicative observation is the projection onto gauge orbits of the torus `(ℝ/ℤ)^{primes}`. The Möbius half-power law holds at Haar-almost every gauge point, fails at the Liouville twin, and at the point `0` is RH [S]. Every statement proved from multiplicative data alone holds on the whole orbit.
3. **Every amplification over `ℤ` tested loses linearly.** A rate needs a loss law `b_k = o(k)`. Gauge Jensen makes moment amplification lose at least linearly; product receivers of every order gain on depth only through their total length; relative powers of `ζ` are `ζ`. Over `𝔽_q` a fixed budget forces purity; the golden datum, with all Künneth counts positive, loses linearly.
4. **Multiplicative positivity is complete on the boundary and capped in the interior.** Four invariants force `D = δ₀`; a pole of order `k` carries `⌊k/2⌋` boundary zeros, every placement realized by integral Euler products over the rational primes; in completed positive systems the same `⌊κ/2⌋` holds on finite-height collars of width `≍ 1/(d log T)`, and at `κ = 1` the collar is zero-free. Interior gain comes only from growth input.
5. **No route has a rung between Vinogradov–Korobov and quasi-RH.** Every graded cancellation receiver, every Vaughan power improvement, admission at fixed damping, one-sided Lefschetz bounds at two primes, and multiplicative forcing have a fixed zero-free strip as first gain.
6. **The invariants determine ζ; the simple pole is the rigidity threshold.** Positivity, the FE, the simple pole and finite order force the coefficient measure, stably in an `L¹` FE defect. Pole order one admits no positive relative deformation of ξ, pole order two every off-line type; every completed positive system satisfies `5d < 9κ`.
7. **Pole order pays only for inserted zeros.** The index–pole conservation identity is exact, the absolute budget is false, and ζ's own zeros are paid, if at all, by oscillation of the zero sum.
8. **Positivity holds on average over short windows and at almost every zero ordinate.** Averaged positivity of the complete Weil form holds on every window `[Γ, Γ + H]`, `Γ^{99/100} ≤ H ≤ Γ/8`; the negative part at a height is read by the off-line field `Φ`, small outside a test-independent set of measure `CH/(η log Γ)`; nonnegative tests are positive at all but `CH/η` zero ordinates. A zero's witness persists only on the reciprocal aperture, so exclusion of an individual zero needs exceptional sets below the zero spacing.
9. **The receiver side of Weil's proof exists over `ℤ`.** One test's Lefschetz sequence `c_m = W(T_{p^m} g, g)` at one prime grows exactly like `p^{Δm}`; RH is Weil's inequality `|c_m| ≤ c_0`; two primes make one-sided bounds sufficient. Weil's input, a fixed budget from correspondences, is missing; the Hecke graphs of `ℤ` satisfy no theorem of the square.
10. **On finite windows, cofactor escape states are translated zeros.** The renormalized cofactor acts on power rows by `ζ(s + ε)`; its power-log eigenspace on any finite exponent window has dimension `Σ ord_{s+ε} ζ`. Whether actual escape modules are generated by these eigenstates is open.

## 3. The three questions to answer next

**Selection criteria.**

1. The question is weaker than RH, so it can be answered.
2. Either answer changes the frontier: a positive answer adds a mechanism; a negative answer is a sharp no-go naming the missing input.
3. Together the questions cover the three walls, each in its own currency.
4. The question respects the exhaustion map: it does not reduce to a class already mapped (gauge-invariant data, point-stable positivity, multiplicative certificates, pole order, averaging).

### Q1. Escape completeness: are actual escape modules generated by translated zeros?

> *At a subcritical damping `ε`, is every actual cofactor escape module generated, in its hull, by the translated-zero power-log eigenstates, so that escape at `ε` is exactly the presence of uncancelled zeros right of `1/2 + ε`?*

- **Why.** Cancellation routes have no graded rung below quasi-RH; their only handle that is not a rate is the escape alternative. The finite half is proved: on every finite exponent window the eigenspace of the renormalized cofactor is the direct sum of blocks at translated zeros, of dimension `Σ ord_{s+ε} ζ`. What remains is completeness for infinite-dimensional modules.
- **Route.** W8 first: the Plancherel form of the cofactor Gram through `G(1/2 + iτ)`. Then: the eta transform reduces the moving tail to dilation regularity; the Euler laws at `2` and `3` and the twisted tied-exponent law fix exponents; record minima, contraction calculus and eigencharacter moduli constrain the remaining states.
- **Outcomes.** *Yes:* PM, CTF, BVI and MC lose escape as a separate handle; it becomes the strip rung B2.1, and U1 reduces completely to rates. *No:* actual escape modules carry states not driven by zeros; they are arithmetic objects a source estimate can target, and the first non-rate handle of U1.

### Q2. Positivity beyond isotropy: does any carrier escape the isotropy law?

> *Is there an unconditional positivity theorem for the Weil or Meyer form on a carrier that is not closed under point convolution — the Rankin–Selberg source `Λ ⋆ Λ`, carriers built from affine maps or near-incidences `ab − cd = h`, cones without convolution closure — whose fibres at some off-line pair are not isotropic? Or does every positive carrier have isotropic off-line fibres?*

- **Why.** Every quadratic class the corpus has mapped ends in blindness, half-blindness or isotropy, and the proof of isotropy needs closure under diagonal point convolution. The gauge principle says that a ζ-specific sign must use gauge-charged data; affine and near-incidence carriers are gauge charged and not point-stable. This is the only quadratic class left open.
- **Selectivity test.** Fail on the `CompletedPositiveSystem 2 1` models and the Liouville twin; reproduce `κ = 1` rigidity; state the gain's scaling with arithmetic length (W9).
- **Outcomes.** *Yes:* the first positivity theorem in the corpus that is neither blindness, half-blindness nor isotropy, and a candidate forcing input for B1.6. *No:* a universal isotropy theorem closes quadratic positivity, and forcing must come from a section count or a base.
- **Tools.** The coupling matrix `K_ρ` and its proof; the left-blind ideal; the strip Bochner–Weil form; the Rankin–Selberg Bessel law of MPG; ACG's ratio-energy and near-incidence calculus; the two-prime Bochner receiver.

### Q3. A theorem of the square over `Spec ℤ`

> *Is there a ring of correspondences on the scaling surface, containing the dilation graphs `T_{p^m}` and closed under composition and transpose, whose classes satisfy a theorem of the square (a parallelogram law modulo fibres) — or is every such ring, under natural axioms, without one?*

- **Why.** Over `𝔽_q` the fixed budget behind Weil's inequality comes from the intersection theory of correspondences on `C × C`: a theorem of the square, Riemann–Roch and the Hodge index on the spans `⟨Δ, Γ_m⟩`. Over `ℤ` the receiver side is complete (§1.7 of the complete report): one prime, one test, `|c_m| ≤ c_0` ⇔ RH, one-sided at two primes. The loss law (B1.9) cannot come from multiplicative data, moments or products. The theorem of the square is the first algebraic property of a correspondence ring that carries a budget, and it is weaker than RH: it constrains the classes, not the source.
- **Calibration.** Genus-one graph classes satisfy it (`genusOne_hasse_tfae`); the Hecke graphs of `ℤ` do not (`heckeCorrespondence_no_square_relation`). An enlargement must keep the golden datum failing (it has positive Künneth counts and no budget) and reproduce Weil with budget `2g` on curves.
- **Outcomes.** *Yes:* the first piece of an intersection theory with a budget over `Spec ℤ`, and a target for a section count (B3.5) on its classes. *No:* Weil's route through correspondences is closed over `ℤ`; the budget must be analytic (an oscillatory capacity) rather than geometric, and B1.9 must be sought from gauge-charged analysis (B3.20, B3.24).
- **Tools.** The scaling-surface pairing; graph degrees and defects (`TheDefectOfAGraphIsTwiceItsDegree`); λ-ring and Adams operations; the Frobenius-span tower identification; the Lefschetz receivers; the Hecke and successor states of APD as candidate correspondences that are gauge charged.

The three questions are the forcing problem in each wall's currency: what escape is made of, the last non-rate handle on the rate (Q1, Wall 1); what positivity can see on gauge-charged carriers (Q2, Wall 14); and whether a budget can be carried uniformly across all `m` by correspondences, the shared-resource question of Wall 3 (Q3, realized as Wall 14 face 14.16).

**Considered and not chosen.** Shorter averaging windows (W6 to `Γ^{1/2+ε}` is known mathematics, below needs B2.10, and no window length decides selection); selection below the zero spacing (RH-strength, B1.4); the coalescent surplus at the gauge point `0` (B2.5); the κ = 1 class collapse (W11).

## 4. Would the B-tier obstacles require a new field of mathematics?

### 4.1 Assessment

No theorem shows that a new field is necessary. The corpus has a complete map of the known forcing mechanisms, each with a sharp limit:

- **Quadratic positivity on carriers certifies blindness, half-blindness or isotropy.** Single-channel and point-stable coupled carriers cannot leave the isotropy class; exhaustion of each is RH.
- **Multiplicative positivity forces the boundary and its capacity, nothing more without growth.** On `Re s = 1`: four invariants, capacity `⌊k/2⌋`, every configuration realized. In completed systems: the same capacity on collars of width `1/log T`. In the interior: the width `(√B − √A)²/G`.
- **Linear rigidity determines ζ but does not see zeros.** Positivity, the FE, the simple pole and finite order fix the coefficient measure, stably; zero location is nonlinear in that measure.
- **Pole order pays only inserted zeros.** The conservation identity is exact; the simple pole is the rigidity threshold.
- **Multiplicative data see only gauge orbits.** Whatever they prove holds at Haar-almost every twist, the Liouville twin's orbit included; the actual source is one point.
- **Amplification over `ℤ` loses linearly.** Moments, tensor products and relative powers gain on depth exactly as fast as they pay.
- **Averaging over heights selects windows, not zeros.** Exclusion of a zero needs exceptional sets below the zero spacing.
- **Positivity cannot couple the successor to the archimedean place.** Positive-definite dilation functions split into archimedean and Kronecker-null parts; arithmetic successors see only the null part.
- **Weil's proof transfers on the receiver side only.** Over `ℤ` the Lefschetz sequence at one prime reads the zero width exactly, but no correspondence ring with a theorem of the square, no section count on the scaling surface and no base with tensor powers is known; at pole order one the completed positive class is expected to be `{ζ}`.

The missing ingredient is a **positivity of gauge-charged correspondences with a fixed budget**. It must be at least quadratic, not isotropic, sensitive to more than pole order, independent of the conclusion, not depth-homogeneous, and it must couple the archimedean place to the finite places in a way that positivity on `ℚ_{>0}` does not. No existing toolkit over number fields supplies one.

### 4.2 The field: arithmetic correspondence positivity

The corpus already holds the two halves of a field. **Arithmetic Positive Duality** ([APD](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ArithmeticPositiveDuality/README.md)) is the rigidity and capacity theory of positive Poisson pairs, with [MPG](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/MultiplicativePositivityGeometry/README.md) as its boundary side and [SBWF](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/StripBochnerWeilForcing/README.md) as its strip side; it supplied the exhaustion map above. **Arithmetic Correlation Geometry** (ACG) supplies the symmetry: the gauge torus, its charged and invariant statistics, the loss law. What neither has is the object Weil used: correspondences with an intersection pairing and a budget. The field the B-tier points to is their join with that object, here called **arithmetic correspondence positivity** [R].

- **Phenomenon to explain.** Over `𝔽_q` one structure, the intersection theory of correspondences on `C × C`, supplies the sign (Hodge index), the budget (Riemann–Roch, `2g`), the base (the curve) and the tensor powers. Over `ℤ` the corpus has the pairing, the receivers and the exhaustion of every substitute. The field should explain why ζ, and not its gauge twins or its pole-order-two relaxations, has budget zero for its off-line charge, and why positivity on the multiplicative group alone cannot see it.
- **Scope.** Positive functionals on algebras generated by dilations (gauge invariant), translations and affine maps (gauge charged), and the archimedean flow; their degrees and defects; positivity on spans `{g, C g}` for correspondences `C`; the capacity of such a positivity for intrinsic charge.

### 4.4 Questions it must answer

1. **What replaces `C × C`?** Which space carries correspondences whose Lefschetz numbers are the sequences `c_m^{(p)}`, with an intersection pairing that has the Hodge index on spans?
2. **Is there a theorem of the square over `Spec ℤ`?** (Q3.)
3. **What is the budget?** Which integer plays the role of `2g`, and is it a section count, a torsion order or an oscillatory capacity?
4. **Which positivity is gauge charged and not isotropic?** (Q2.)
5. **How are the archimedean and finite places coupled?** Positivity on `ℚ_{>0}` splits them; which structure keeps them coupled under positivity (B3.23)?
6. **What pays ζ's own zeros?** Pole order pays inserted zeros; what positive structure measures oscillatory payment, and does it carry a capacity?
7. **What are escape states?** (Q1.)
8. **What is the base?** Which coefficient systems over `Spec ℤ` leave the conductor-one completed positive class and still have nonnegative even tensor powers with a computable pole part?
9. **How does an average become a point?** Which selection principle reaches below the zero spacing?
10. **How are shared resources paid?** What nonlocal calculus pays frustrated prime incidences from one budget?

### 4.5 Tools it would develop

APD and ACG have built the Fejér-tent pairing, Hamburger rigidity and its stability, lattice and `ℚ_{>0}` Bochner, Kronecker push-forward and decomposition, induction from subgroups, sign characters, Bohr projection, the quantum budget, the critical-jet topology and synthesis, the coupling matrix, gauge Jensen, ratio energy and the near-incidence form. Not yet built:

- **Correspondence intersection theory over `ℤ`.** Classes of dilation, Hecke and affine graphs on the scaling surface; degrees, defects, a theorem of the square, Castelnuovo–Severi on spans.
- **Gauge-charged positivity.** Positivity theorems for forms built from additive relations and the archimedean line, with their isotropy or non-isotropy laws.
- **Loss-law calculus.** Bounds `b_k = o(k)` for zero-forced families, with the gauge-Jensen obstruction as its sharp boundary.
- **Oscillatory capacity.** A positive structure whose integer counts intrinsic charge, keeping dominated and oscillatory payment apart.
- **Base constructions** outside conductor-one completed positive systems.
- **Escape-state classification** for cyclic critical-Wiener modules through their spectral profiles.
- **Archimedean–finite coupling** on the prime torus beyond the Kronecker split.
- **Selection lemmas** at the zero-spacing scale.
- **An arithmetic Stepanov method**: auxiliary sections over `ℤ` whose forced vanishing bounds spectral counts (a characteristic-zero shifted-subgroup Stepanov lemma exists in `HTGeometry`).

Each milestone is a theorem of a type the corpus does not contain: completeness, charged positivity, a square theorem, a coupling law, rather than a blindness or receiver statement. Under APD's standard each must name its positive side, spectral side, gap and integer, and under ACG's standard its gauge charge. If several succeed, the third forcing instance of APD has its first pieces; if they fail, each failure is a sharp no-go that narrows where a forcing law could be.
