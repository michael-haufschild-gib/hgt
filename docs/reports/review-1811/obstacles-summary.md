# Research priorities for a formal proof of RH

**Best full-proof target.** Bound one signed ordinary-prime spline sum at the square conductors by Oε(n^ε), for every ε>0. The ACS theorem converts a bound of order n^(2s) into w≤s, where w is the rightmost zero abscissa minus ½. Consequently logarithmic losses are affordable all the way to RH. The strongest available decomposition for attacking this target is the paired arithmetic of Vaughan cofactors, with the small first primes, repeated factors and a reciprocal band already paid. The missing estimate concerns the long coprime products after the two dilations have been combined. This is a proposed proof programme. [Exact exponent transfer](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/CountingHolography/Sections/TheDyadicPrimeWeightOfSquareConductors.lean#L177) · [Remaining prime products](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExceptionalLayerTheory/RemovingSmallOrdinaryPrimesAtBothWidths.lean#L192)

**Best smaller target.** Under a single off-line quartet the ordinary prime error must obey a second-order dilation recurrence with a square-root remainder. Test whether prime arithmetic permits that recurrence simultaneously at 2 and 3. Exclusion would remove the two-right-zero stratum from ACS/HF, ECS and ZCG. It would leave open the possibility of a larger off-line set. Section 2.17 gives a test whose failure is also informative. [Exact payoff](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExclusionOfTheCriticalState/Index/SingleQuartetSecularAndWallRigidity.lean#L2574) · [Arithmetic readout](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExceptionalLayerTheory/TheSingleQuartetWaveInAdjacentPrimeCounts.lean)

**Evidence convention.** A Lean implication supplies a verified target at the level of source statements. Its premise remains a research task unless there is a separate production theorem. Sections 2.3–2.18 distinguish deductions from proposed constructions. The corpus states a fixed bound Θ≤20999/24000. It is stronger at sufficiently large height than the published explicit Vinogradov–Korobov region in the comparison below. That comparison concerns the statements of two bounds. It makes no claim of external validation or priority for the corpus. No new build is used. ## 2.4 A breakthrough below a new zero-free strip

**How large is the extra Hall deficit when the cofactor restriction is removed but every move must still exchange one prime for two?** Let H(X) be the Hall excess on the full global one–two graph on squarefree integers in (X,2X]. Ask whether H(X)=o(X/log³X). This tests the very order of the ordered-fibre obstruction. With the corpus Mertens bound, the inherent parity imbalance is already o(X/log³X), so an affirmative answer would show that allowing cross-fibre transport genuinely removes the known loss. The target is much larger than X^β for every fixed β<1, hence does not deliver a new fixed strip. A negative answer with a family of deficient cuts rules out RH-scale matching on this graph irrespective of the truth of RH. [Fibre debt and degree](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/QuantitativeFactorizationMatching/GlobalDyadicGraphAndCofactorFibres.lean) · [Excess](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/QuantitativeFactorizationMatching/OreDeficiencyAndCascadeConservation.lean) · [Mertens bound](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExponentDynamicsOfTheCriticalStrip/TheAbscissaAvoidsAnIntervalBelowSevenEighths.lean)

Start with the ordered-fibre witnesses and expose their neighbors across the first changed cofactor. Compute the deficit after one, two and arbitrarily many augmenting layers. For an upper bound the expansion must cover **every** competing cut; rescuing the original witnesses is insufficient. A cut stable under arbitrary global exchanges is the useful negative outcome.

## 2.5 What could produce positivity?

**Can the inevitable p-adic precision loss in a midpoint section map be exported to its other output coordinate at subquadratic *net* logarithmic cost?** For a fixed positive background b, write S(v) for its finite small-section set. The desired arithmetic operation sends two sections near kb to sections near kb+a and kb−a. If its largest fibre has size exp(o(m²)) at multiplicity m, the logarithmic product inequality survives division by m². At sufficiently large k the signed limit is 2 deg(a). Proving this for the required displacements and unbounded multiplicities would produce the sign. A first experiment can use one signed displacement with separated prime contacts. Specify the map by integral arithmetic before calculating any of the three cardinalities. [Signed comparison](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/CountingHolography/Sections/MidpointProductsOfSmallSections.lean#L86)

There is a sharp design constraint. On a positive ray with degree d, simultaneous losses i,j in one output coordinate at coprime places p,q have i log p+j log q>δm² eventually for every δ<d, in the specified injection model. Improving a constant in single-coordinate rounding cannot suffice. Try carrying the lost digits in the first output and count the resulting collisions across **both** outputs. A quadratic lower bound for net fibre loss in that enlarged class would retire this construction; an explicit reversible two-output rule on one genuinely mixed family would be a producer prototype. ## 2.6 A breakthrough with positivity and spectral methods excluded

**Can binary carries give a cancellation law for distinct ordinary prime divisors of the central binomial coefficient?** At base cutoff k set n=⌊k/2⌋. For p above the square-root cutoff the ordinary-prime contribution is the last quotient class, determined by the parity of ⌊k/p⌋ after accounting for the sampling endpoints. Consider its first total after subtracting the same class of the discrete 1/log density. Seek a signed combinatorial identity which groups gains and losses between the two adjacent quotient intervals and bounds the unmatched *signed* remainder by Oε(X^(3/2+ε)). The exact fixed-depth theorem would then finish RH directly. The argument can use divisibility, integer division, finite differences and rearrangement of finite sums throughout its production step. Whenever 2n<(y+1)², the primes p>y dividing the central binomial contribute exactly 2Ψ(n,y)−Ψ(2n,y). The difference from counting *all* its distinct divisors that are prime sums to at most Xπ(y) over k<X, n=⌊k/2⌋. Thus the first-total estimate for the smooth counts, with the identical reciprocal-logarithmic class subtraction, is a named RH criterion. It permits a second attack through division trees of smooth integers at the same y. An uncentered change of periodic quotient profile cannot give a power gain unless the profiles agree on positive integers. ## 2.7 A physical phenomenon and a decisive experiment

**Hypothesis: RH describes the rate at which a localized disturbance escapes through the cusp of the arithmetic modular surface.** In the corpus model a compact pulse produces an image field; after subtracting its explicit mean, the slowest observable decay over all pulses and points reads the zero-free strip. The physical proposal is that coherent propagation through the modular identifications fixes the quarter-rate. Generic passivity is already too weak: a positive passive Euler twin may have off-line zeros. [Observable and rate](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/BlackHoleRingdown/TheRingdownRateIsTheZeroFreeStrip.lean) · [Control system](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/BlackHoleRingdown/ThePositiveEulerTwinsArePassive.lean)

**Test the cause of the damping.** Derive a finite-time return or escape identity from propagation through specified modular cells, with a computable error bound and no ζ zeros in its input. Pick two compact pulses, one exciting a chosen slow channel and one canceling it by its known pulse factor; predict both fields at a fixed observation point from the same identity. Repeat the transfer calculation with a passive Euler twin. If the alleged mechanism gives identical quarter-rate predictions on the twin, one of its hypotheses or its channel decomposition is inadequate. If it distinguishes the sources and proves a decay improvement for a class of pulses that provably detects the offending poles, it gives a mathematical advance. The full theorem still requires every compact pulse and admissible ε, or a proved reduction to a detecting family. The physical interpretation remains a hypothesis even if its transfer identities succeed.

## 2.8 A geometric breakthrough question

**Can an arithmetic label distinguish the two sheets of an actual heat-cover fold all the way around its vanishing cycle?** On a small time–height box containing a simple double collision, the local turn exchanges two sheets. A pair of collisions can have trivial aggregate parity. Seek labels obtained at the undeformed Euler boundary and transported by an independently defined arithmetic correspondence. Require that the two labels separate on entry, return individually around the box, and stay separated through transport. This would forbid that fold. A label assigned by following the zero is free to exchange with it and gives no obstruction. This sets a concrete topological task beyond counting a winding number.

## 2.9 Does RH need a new field?

There is no necessity theorem for a new field. **Arithmetic comparison of signed counts at different scales** is a plausible research subject if the existing analytic estimates continue to pay the two scales separately. Its objects would be integer-indexed measures, ordinary-prime and quotient-class counts, signed graph contacts and maps between neighboring dilations. Its basic operations would compose a dilation with a cofactor filter, center it by its explicit density, and bound the residual with constants uniform in the outer cutoff.

It should answer three quantitative questions. Which transformations preserve a right-zero singularity? Which erase only the density term? Which yield an error smaller on the actual ordinary primes than on an edited system? Useful tools would include identities for mixed carry classes, estimates for sums of correlated floor remainders and a calculus carrying the error exponent through finite cofactor changes. A first acceptance test is a new bound on one unresolved long-prime band. A stronger one is any independent ACS bound with s<8999/24000. Re-deriving the common width in several coordinate systems would not validate the proposed field as a route to RH. ## 2.10 Does AAL need a new field?

**The narrower subject would be the arithmetic rigidity of isolated leading oscillations.** With E(x)=ψ(x)−x, failure of AAL gives a finite nonempty top separated in real part from the remaining zeros. In the prime error this produces a nonzero finite trigonometric polynomial in log x times x^Θ, with a power-saving remainder. Its frequencies and coefficients are constrained by the zero ordinates and their integral multiplicities. AAL allows w>0 when the tail reaches the same abscissa. Its objects would be finite frequency sets with multiplicities, actual prime-jump measures and an explicit error class stable under fixed dilations. Tools would extract frequencies without assuming separation constants uniform over all sets, track coalescing ordinates, and test a predicted phase against independent factor counts. Fixed finite experiments can decide whether one estimator distinguishes two close frequencies at its resolution. The first theorem worth seeking is exclusion of a single isolated frequency pair with any multiplicity. The full proof must exclude every finite separated top even when lower off-line support is infinite. Falsification requires an actual ζ zero above a cut **and proof that the nonempty part above that cut is finite**. A planted Beurling pair, a finite height search or a good finite trigonometric fit does not do this. Such a falsification would in particular disprove RH. ## 2.11 A field for a sign-free, nonspectral proof

**Develop quantitative combinatorics of changing factorizations.** Use squarefree divisors, parity, prime-exchange graphs, carry intervals, finite signed chains and their integer boundaries. A certificate lists rewrites of one product into another within the dyadic interval, its inverse on matched elements and the exceptions. Composition must track boundary vertices and the number of representations exactly. No inner product, operator sign or distribution of eigenvalues is needed to state a certificate.

The essential questions are whether changing cofactors repairs the X/log³X fibre debt, whether a finite-depth cutoff can be removed without losing a positive fraction of the integers, and whether the **absolute** unmatched set has half-power growth. Tools would include arithmetic augmenting paths, congestion bounds from divisibility, and dual deficient-set certificates. Test a proposed calculus against the proven bounded-arity and prescribed-prime obstructions. Passing the smaller Hall-excess test in §2.4 would justify it; producing Oε(X^(½+ε)) unmatched integers on every dyadic interval would give the Mertens bound by signed cancellation and then RH. This programme does not require identifying the optimal unrestricted matching first. ## 2.12 A field for a geometric proof

**Study arithmetic transport on ramified families of zeros with real structure.** The data would be a branched cover, real conjugation, multiplicities, oriented local turns, restriction between time–height boxes and an arithmetic fibre attached at the undeformed boundary. A morphism must respect both gluing and the prime labels. A theory with only the underlying real cover also covers the model with the unwanted turns.

The tools would be a local intersection number for each marked pair, a gluing law preserving the ordered turn word, and quantitative continuation bounds that remain meaningful as height grows and time decreases. The first test is a pair of cancelling transpositions: the invariant must detect each or prove by an arithmetic constraint why they cannot occur. A second is a repeated quartet whose copy count passes the divisibility-by-eight test. Success means proving the actual real action trivial on every positive nonbranch fibre, or independently excluding every positive branch time. The classical heat equation and known nonnegativity of the de Bruijn–Newman constant constrain which direction of time matters. ## 2.17 The one smaller question with cross-route consequences

**Can a super-square-root prime error obey the two single-frequency dilation recurrences at both 2 and 3?** Write E(x)=ψ(x)−x. For a trial ½<β<1, γ>0 and a=2 or 3, define

$$
R_a(x)=E(a^2x)-2a^\beta\cos(\gamma\log a)E(ax)+a^{2\beta}E(x).
$$

**Proposed theorem.** If limsup |E(x)|/x^β>0, then for at least one of those two a the ratio |R_a(x)|/(√x log²x) is unbounded at large x. This demand concerns the shape of a signal *if it exists*. It gives no general upper bound on Θ. The two clocks avoid relying on one possibly aliased sampling lattice; no equivalence between this test and single-quartet exclusion is assumed.

**Why success matters.** Under a single quartet ρ=β+iγ, the main term is a real linear combination of x^ρ and x^conj(ρ) with nonzero amplitude. The displayed difference annihilates both powers, since its characteristic roots are a^ρ and a^conj(ρ). The remaining three errors are each O_a(√x log²x). The limsup premise holds along a sequence of phases, so the proposed theorem gives a contradiction, for any multiplicity. Lean already converts absence of a single quartet into “RH or at least four distinct right zeros.” This finite-rank dichotomy leaves Θ unrestricted on the second branch. [Wave law](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExclusionOfTheCriticalState/Index/SingleQuartetSecularAndWallRigidity.lean#L2230) · [Payoff](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExclusionOfTheCriticalState/Index/SingleQuartetSecularAndWallRigidity.lean#L2574)

**How to attack it.** Rewrite E at all three scales using one common adjacent-octave identity. Cancel the known density and square-root-priced pieces algebraically. Instead of separately lower-bounding either sign of E, seek a conditional lower bound on the residual where |E(x)| is large, using the incompatible carry boundaries at powers of 2 and 3. Label every cofactor correction so that a change of width cannot manufacture a phase defect. Test the claim first on a planted single wave plus a square-root perturbation: it must fail there, so any proof must use ordinary-prime input absent from that control. Proving weak upper bounds on both residuals alone would establish neither a quartet nor its frequency; a genuine arithmetic lower bound would remove the index-two, two-rung and single-wave possibilities at once. ## 2.18 Best prospect for a full formal proof

**My choice is the signed dyadic ordinary-prime comparison from ACS, attacked through the long coprime product of PVC.** The reason is the location of the unknown: one fixed profile and one conductor variable, an exact theorem translating *any* improved exponent, an explicit list of removable terms, and no requirement to guess a new space over 𝔽₁ or first prove that off-line support is finite. It is a research judgment. There is no demonstrated estimate on the decisive term. [Target](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/CountingHolography/Sections/TheDyadicPrimeWeightOfSquareConductors.lean) · [Arithmetic decomposition](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ExceptionalLayerTheory/RemovingSmallOrdinaryPrimesAtBothWidths.lean)

**1. Fix the arithmetic bill.** Let A be the actual spline autocorrelation, x=n², and let P_n be the finite ordinary-prime window in the ACS definition. Write

$$
H_n(y)=\sum_{p\in P_n}\frac{2\log p}{\sqrt p}\,A(\log(p/y)),\qquad
D_n=2H_n(x)-H_n(4x).
$$

This keeps the identical cutoff in both sums. D_n is exactly the dyadic prime contact. In continuous logarithmic coordinates its density cancellation is visible: the integral of e^(t/2)A(t−log 4) is twice that of e^(t/2)A(t). Hence the large main term disappears in the combined expression. The error can still carry an off-line mode. The two uncombined positive masses are a poor target; the analogous diagonal generator already has numerator and denominator logs individually of cubic size. Establish an arithmetic reduction with a published error budget.** Partial summation expresses the fixed smooth reading through centered prime counts; retain any cutoff boundary terms explicitly. On a multiplicatively bounded window y≈x, partial summation of a count error O(√y log^k y) against a smooth weight y^(−1/2)A(log(y/x)) costs O(log^k x): the derivative is O(x^(−3/2)) over length O(x) and either endpoint costs the same order. Prime powers can be removed at such precision. Choose one of the cofinal adjacent first-octave pairs where the difference recovers y−ψ(y) on multiples of 2t+1. Apply the all-width PVC remainder decomposition to each count at that *fixed* pair. This use of adjacent first octaves is separate from the paired-square-width RH criterion. Keep the cofactor norm, the divided horizon, varying logarithmic cutoffs and the signs at x,4x. Formalizing this combined smoothed identity is work once those explicit terms are specified; no saving is furnished by partial summation.
