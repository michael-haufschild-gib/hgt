# Which routes ask for the same theorem?

**Three problems carry all routes.** **P** asks for one exponent: an upper bound for one signed arithmetic remainder that reads Θ. **R** asks for one sign: positivity of the complete explicit-formula pairing at every aperture, using a property of ζ that its twins lack. **U** asks for one stratum: exclusion of exceptional configurations that escape the observations; its core is the asymptotic abscissa law. Within P the identification is exact: most observables are exact meters of Θ, so a complete estimate for one proves all others. Within R and U the routes share their open input, and a partial result transfers only where a theorem below says so. R and P are joined by a chain of theorems through the critical state. [Walls](walls-ledger.md) · [Route statements](route-obstacles.md)

Entries are of three kinds. **Equivalent:** a Lean theorem, or a composition of Lean theorems, transfers the complete statement both ways. **Reduction:** one statement implies the other or is a branch of it. **Shared obstruction:** a common difficulty without a transfer of partial results.

**Consequence.** No transfer between rows lowers Θ; the unconditional face of each row is the strip. A bound for one row by a method that does not already contain a bound on Θ is a producer, and it pays every row at once. The eleven rows differ only in which arithmetic is visible: adjacent prime intervals, the halving increment, conductor sections, balanced composites, gcds on power grids, rough heads, scattering paths, floor alphabets, Möbius polynomials, Mertens samples.
