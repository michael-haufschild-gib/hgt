# Which routes ask for the same theorem?

**Three problems carry all routes.** **P** asks for one exponent: produce an upper bound for one signed arithmetic remainder that reads Θ. **R** asks for one sign: show the complete explicit-formula pairing is nonnegative, using a property of ζ that its twins lack. **U** asks for one stratum: exclude exceptional configurations that escape the observations. Its core is the asymptotic abscissa law, since RH ⇔ approach ∧ AAL. Within P the identification is exact: most observables are *exact meters* of Θ, so a proof of any one complete estimate proves all the others through Θ. Within R and U the routes share their open input, but a partial result transfers only where a theorem below says so. [Walls](walls-ledger.md) · [Route statements](route-obstacles.md)

Three kinds of entries follow. **Equivalent:** a Lean theorem, or a composition of Lean theorems, transfers the complete statement both ways. **Reduction:** one statement implies the other or is a branch of it. **Shared obstruction:** a common difficulty without a transfer of partial results.

**Consequence.** No transfer between rows lowers Θ. A bound proved for one row by a method that does not already contain a bound on Θ is a producer, and it pays every row at once. The 5/6 column holds eleven faces of one theorem. ## Cross-family links

| Link | Content |
|---|---|
| **P ↔ R** | Every R-sign ⇔ RH ⇔ every P meter at b=1/2. No partial transfer: a P bound at b>1/2 gives no R sign, and an R sign at one aperture gives no strip. |
| **R ↔ U** | The probe index (R3) counts distinct right zeros. Its all-rank amplification is U1, and its full sign is RH. |
| **P ↔ U** | The halving increment, PMI and PDR are clean RH tests once single quartets are excluded (PDR) or without that step (PSM, PMI). Finite-layer exclusion makes no P bound easier. |
