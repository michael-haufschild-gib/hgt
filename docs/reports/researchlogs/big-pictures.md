# Big pictures of the research programmes

Section 10 ("Big picture") of the 54 programme READMEs in `formal/GaloisForLFunctions/<Programme>/README.md`,
preceded by a synthesis of all of them. Numbering follows `docs/reports/research-programmes.md`.

## Synthesis: the big picture of the big pictures

### 1. There is one barrier, and every programme reaches it

Fifty-four programmes, started from different mathematics (incidence, criticality, intersection theory, gauge
theory, electrostatics, Krein spaces, dynamics), end at the same place. Its names differ: half damping, the
square-root threshold, the zero width as an exponent, the first-prime layer, the Nyquist or spacing scale, the
growing-overlap limit, the diagonal `d ≍ Q`, the long-modulus Möbius tail, the index of the Weil kernel. Every
programme that carried its reformulation to the end proved these are coordinates on one line. RH-equivalent
statements are therefore abundant and cheap. A new equivalence is a change of coordinates, not progress.
Unconditional inputs (PNT, de la Vallée Poussin, Vinogradov–Korobov, zero density, pair correlation) move
constants and sub-exponential factors and stop exactly at the barrier.

### 2. Seeing zeros is solved; forcing them is not

Detection is complete. Every off-line zero is seen by an explicit finite object: a grid tent, a tower window, a
two-prime box, an atomic Gram matrix, a theta reading of the collided form, a negative square of the Weil kernel
with an explicit certificate. The divisor can be read from arithmetic with polynomial resources, and finite
sections reach the index exactly. Over `F_q` every framework is faithful (admission, index and collision charge
reproduce Weil's theorem). The problem is never visibility. It is forcing the visible quantities to take their
critical-line values.

### 3. Positivity is abundant locally and fails in the exhaustion

Positivity is proved wherever a scale, a window or a finite piece is fixed: short apertures, odd windows through
the first prime, every single prime tower, every Euler factor, every finite sieve or character, and the literal
complete Weil matrix at every fixed overlap. In the atomic route it is selective there: it already separates `ζ`
from Liouville, from flipped primes and from pole-free gauges. What fails is always one exhaustion: overlap,
rank, prime cutoff, conductor count, height, or the coupling between transverse prime towers. Each programme
that measured it found a reserve that degrades at a definite rate and payments that arrive at their own rate.
RH is a rate problem: payment must beat reserve decay uniformly along the exhaustion. No change of
normalization, gauge, completion or coordinate alters the rates.

### 4. Two filters decide which arguments can work

**Sign filter.** Arguments that use only size (absolute values, norms, budgets, positive majorants, sieves,
unsigned large-sieve averages, amplitudes, complete periods, rank counts) saturate at half damping. This is
proved repeatedly as a theorem, not observed. The true answer at the barrier is a small signed number produced
by cancellation, and every unsigned bound overshoots by a constant factor.

**Twin filter.** Arguments whose hypotheses also hold for a twin of `ζ` prove statements about the twin class,
and that class contains counterexamples. The twins appear in every guise: Beurling systems, Liouville and sign
gauges, flipped Euler factors, fake curves (golden datum), `n^{−1/2}` perturbations, the κ ≥ 2 axiomatic class,
the gauge orbit (where almost every point satisfies RH and `ζ` is atypical), the Bohr closure. Euler product,
functional equation, positive coefficients, integrality of prime data, Hecke covariance, local positivity and
moment positivity are all twin-blind.

A candidate route must name what it reads, read signs, keep every prime's exact weight, and fail for at least
one twin. Most machinery in the corpus passes neither filter and is transfer, not proof.

### 5. Selectivity lives in a small place

Where `ζ` is special is sharply located and small. The selective advantage sits on the pole: the pole hyperplane
margin, carried by the prime 2 and the correlation of `Λ` with the pole, is the one positive square RH permits
and the only thing that separates the arithmetic point from its gauge neighbours. Selectivity enters one prime
at a time, as finite arithmetic checks, at atomic or spacing scale. Additive structure is the other selective
ingredient. The successor, the unit gap of the integers, non-ratio lags and log-cells are what pin the Euler
point. Purely multiplicative structure is gauge-blind and stops at the 1-line. The decisive cancellation is
therefore joint: primes against pole, multiplicative against additive, prime place against archimedean place.
Paying any of these pieces separately is provably too expensive; paying them together has been cheap on every
family tested.

### 6. Weil's proof over ℤ: the parts exist, the engine does not

Over `ℤ` the corpus has built most of Weil's machine: intersection numbers of torsion cycles, projection formulas,
hyperbolic fibres, Lefschetz and adjunction identities, Bézout for effective cycles, a Hodge index that holds in
large unconditional pieces, measures of its failure that count right zeros and zero width, and a prime side that
is genuine intersection theory. What is missing is narrow and the same in every formulation: an effectivity or
section theory (an arithmetic Riemann–Roch) proved without the sign; a regularized diagonal that keeps both
Hecke covariance and sign; a correspondence carrying Rosati sufficiency. The function-field engines do not
transplant. Stepanov's auxiliary pays its multiplicity exactly in support length, because `ℤ` has no identity like
`x^q = x`. Deligne's amplifier works at finite rank, and over `ℤ` orthogonality costs exactly what it gains. Transfer-operator
realizations are forced to be prime-length and are cut down by integrality, unique factorization and the Gamma
factor. The explicit formula computes only reflection pairings; the same-point side needs a non-holomorphic
input. Every engine that produces RH over `F_q` relies on finiteness (rank, genus, Frobenius identity, discrete
spectrum) that `ℤ` lacks.

### 7. The missing input has one description

Read across all programmes, the missing input is one object, described consistently:

- **signed**: a cancellation statement, not a size bound or a positivity of a carrier;
- **exact**: an identity of `Λ` (or `μ`) kept alive through the limit, not an estimate of its size;
- **at square-root precision**: one-sided prime counts, long-window Möbius sums, signed prime-pair correlations,
  or the Chebyshev error between scales, at the scale where zeros and primes are in uncertainty balance;
- **joint**: primes paid together with the pole and the archimedean place, additive together with multiplicative;
- **uniform**: valid along the whole exhaustion with constants that do not degrade;
- **selective**: false for every twin.

The concrete targets are different coordinates on this input. The joint signed shell–pole payment at growing
overlap is the atomic Weil-matrix route, the most quantitatively advanced. The other targets are: prime–pole
cancellation in the first-prime layer with uniform control; long-modulus Möbius cancellation below half damping;
a producer (an auxiliary vanishing on one parity class to growing order); sections of the scaling surface; and a
non-holomorphic same-point identity with controlled depth term.

### 8. What this means for the research as a whole

The research has mapped RH rather than reduced it. Its lasting output is a precise map: one barrier, located
exactly, with every route's stopping point proved and its positive survivor formalized. Four consequences follow.

1. **Stop producing equivalences.** Each new reformulation lands on the same line. A route earns work only when
   it states a new inequality that passes the sign and twin filters.
2. **Concentrate on joint signed cancellation at the barrier.** Every programme that got furthest ended at a
   cancellation between two large terms that are individually unpayable (shells and pole, first-prime head and
   pole square-root term, Möbius tail and prime-harmonic debt). The atomic route has the most explicit rates
   and the most formal infrastructure, so it is the natural place to test any new cancellation mechanism.
3. **Look for a new arithmetic identity, not a sharper estimate.** The function-field proofs work because of an
   identity or a finiteness that `ℤ` lacks. A proof over `ℤ` needs an identity of comparable force tying additive
   and multiplicative structure at the square-root scale. Positivity is the last step, after such an identity
   has computed the index.
4. **Use the twins and the finite field as standing tests.** Every proposed mechanism should be run on Beurling
   systems, Liouville and flipped-prime twins, fake curves and curves over `F_q` before it is developed. The
   corpus already contains these test objects, and most routes the programmes closed were closed by exactly
   these tests.

## Programme big pictures

### 01 arithmetic-positivity-theory

Weil positivity is cheap wherever a scale is available: the archimedean channel grows like `log M`,
every arithmetic channel is ℓ²-bounded, and on linear windows the primes even pay a positive
subsidy. So the arithmetic side is not the enemy on structured families. The difficulty of RH sits
entirely where no scale exists: fixed-aperture receivers, the odd sector, low modes. On that side
APT has a complete unconditional detection theory (every off-line zero is seen by an explicit grid
tent) and positivity only up to aperture about `0.35`.

The critical family cannot tell `ζ` from Beurling systems with off-line zeros. Any proof that runs
through scale-driven positivity plus a size bound `|w| ≤ CΛ` proves false statements. A route to RH
needs an input that is exact in `Λ` and works at bounded aperture without a scale parameter. The
astronomically large thresholds say the same thing quantitatively: the log-gain mechanism is not the
RH mechanism.

### 02 BiScaleUncertainty

RH is an uncertainty principle between the additive log grid and the multiplicative origin: a
correlation that vanishes at every prime power and carries no pole mass must pay archimedean energy
at least `|Ω(0)|` times its mass. BSU proves every shape of this principle unconditionally — collar
constants, logarithmic windows, linear log modulus, inverse-linear tails, blind polar sources costing
`log N` — but never with the constant `|Ω(0)|`. The constant is the theorem.

Which nodes matter is decided exactly: prime powers, each with price `Λ(m)/√m`, adding over primes
like an Euler product and coupling within a tower through the local Euler factor. Density is
irrelevant above the square-root threshold; incidence is everything. Composite nodes matter only
through lags of joint tests. So positivity is a statement about `Λ`, not about how many constraints
are imposed.

When blindness is dropped, the prime side collapses to one object: the Chebyshev error `ψ(x) − x` seen
in the gauge `e^{−t/2}`, i.e. the critical-line band `|t| ≤ √x` of `ζ'/ζ`. Every packet, every
producer, every window bound reads this band, and every growth rate of it is a zero-width rate. RH is
"linear band", "bounded packet", "window ceiling `e^{L/2} + O(L)`", "bounded critical deficit" — the
same statement in different coordinates. The programme's unconditional results reach exactly the
classical PNT strength (dlVP, VK, zero density) and stop at the square-root barrier.

The observation side says that the RH-relevant quantities are unstable: polar balance is not closed,
finite-order cycle data never suffice, exact incidence breaks under perturbation, sieve phases need
degree six. Positivity cannot be obtained by limits, compactness or finite observation; it has to come
from an exact arithmetic identity at the square-root scale.

### 03 TransHorizonIncidenceMechanics

The finite divisor observer is rich. It has complete invariants, exact recovery, white-noise
statistics, and sharp sign theorems far beyond its horizon. None of this pays the RH receiver. That
information is either sign-blind or linear-algebraic, and both kinds stop at damping `1/2`.

`ε = 1/2` is one line seen from many sides: absolute convergence of the class, sign-blindness,
horizon-limit commutation, the critical Wiener hyperplane law, primitive-energy boundedness, and the
unconditional Möbius receiver. The quarter law locates the same line inside one Euler factor: a local
exponent of `1/2` at each height. This is the square-root barrier, stated as an observation theorem.

Above the line the receiver is ζ itself. Its energy is the boundary energy of `ζ(s)(C(s) − m)/s`.
Every paid/unpaid dichotomy for structured weights reads the zero divisor of ζ, a local Euler exponent,
or a singularity's integrability. Payment can therefore only be proved from an input as strong as zero
location. The available unconditional inputs (`ζ ≠ 0` on `Re s = 1`, zero-free regions, VK) settle
half damping and nothing below it.

For the RH programme: truncated-Möbius, Nyman and mollifier energies are exact instruments, not
estimation devices. Finite-horizon combinatorics supplies conservation laws and calibrations, but no
cancellation. Progress needs a new signed input that reads `μ` on composite moduli through successor
or Bézout structure (Class III) and carries an exponent. The odd sector gives the same picture: every
unconditional estimate pays local, low-prime pieces, and what remains is a global cross-lobe statement.

### 04 BezoutValuationIncidence

BVI shows where the RH difficulty is, and that it does not move under reformulation. Everything finite
and structural is in hand: rows are determined by polynomially many values at any phase, the
Nyman–Beurling Gram is an explicit cotangent sum, the Báez–Duarte residual splits into a readout and a
centred energy, the receiver splits into pieces whose drifts are computed, and cross-scale correlations
are dispersed unconditionally at every exponent below the diagonal using only the additive structure
of the integers.

Every remaining gap collapses to one place: the diagonal `d ≍ Q`, the Bézout interior martingale, the
receiver below half damping. There the statistic is a functional of long-window Möbius partial sums and
nothing else, so its quality is exactly the zero-free region: power savings there are `Θ < 1`, endpoint
rates are RH. Quadratic, period and diagonal data are provably sign-blind, so no positivity argument
built from them can see the zeros. A proof must inject a new signed input at the diagonal — a
cancellation of Möbius on long windows stronger than the zero-free region provides — and BVI's value is
that it has reduced every finite route to that single, sharply identified statement.

### 05 ArithmeticCriticalityTheory

ACT asked whether RH has a positive ground state, the way nonnegative Schrödinger operators do. It does not:
the arithmetic operator reads any positive profile only through two moments, and both have the wrong sign.
What remains after removing the poles is a single gauge-free form that is exactly critical — no direction has
slack, and the obstructing directions are precisely the theta-span combinations with zeros erased. Inside that
form the archimedean place is harmless and strictly positive at every scale; all difficulty sits on the prime
anti-diagonals, where reflected jumps cross the origin. Positivity can be proved, unconditionally and with
explicit constants, on windows up to `3/10`, and these certificates are genuinely selective: an `L`-function
with the same Euler product and functional equation but off-line zeros fails on every window. Yet they live
below the first prime, where the form is purely archimedean. The lesson for the RH programme: reformulations
are cheap and all equivalent; gauges, shifts, superpositions and averages only move bookkeeping; the live
content is a prime-side inequality between anti-diagonal and same-side atoms, which no window certificate has
yet reached.

### 06 SquareRootThreshold

- Weil positivity is **faithful but blind at finite resolution**. The full form determines the zeros;
  any bounded aperture is fooled, and being fooled is exact mimicry by an on-line configuration.
- Everything measures **one number**, the zero width, as an **exponent**. Exponents are tail
  invariants: no finite window, finite prime set, finite computation or bounded aperture can bound
  them. RH ⇔ statements are abundant and worthless as progress; what is missing is an **estimate**
  with exponent $<\tfrac12$, and any subexponential estimate would already be enough.
- Unconditional prime information (PNT, dlVP, VK, Chebyshev) moves constants and sub-exponential
  factors but never the exponent; the first prime is paid, the second is not.
- For $\zeta$, positivity on a finite lag range already forces rigid structure: off-line zeros can hide
  only as approximate multiple zeros or by escaping to large height and small amplitude. The remaining
  enemy sits at the square-root threshold (pair correlation of zeros / signed prime pairs with height
  phase) and at infinity (escape beyond every horizon).
- Consequence for an RH proof: it must inject information that is uniform in height and reaches past
  $X\asymp T$ with phase — not another reformulation, not another finite certificate, and not a sign
  argument at a fixed aperture.

### 07 ArithmeticIncidencePositivity

AIP shows that the Weil form is overwhelmingly positive **locally**. The lattice skeleton is a nonnegative
Laplacian; the pole pays the archimedean continuum except on exponentially thin axis collars; every prime-power
atom is paid by the surplus of its own reflection strip; and odd windows are certified through the first prime
up to 0.362. Positivity is also **selective**: it already distinguishes ζ from the shifted pair, the Liouville
twist and geometric Beurling systems at small apertures, so it reads zero positions, not merely Euler product,
functional equation and Dirichlet structure.

The failure is **globalization**. Strips are nested and share one surplus only inside a square-root light cone
with logarithmic capacity; odd-part allocation provably cannot cover all atoms; the pole grows like e^r and
cannot be paid separately from the prime channel. When everything is kept together, the identity
Ẑ = budget − cost collapses the whole global problem to one functional: the two-pole Chebyshev defect
E(s) = e^s + s − 1 − ψ(e^s) paired against the test's lag curvature — equivalently one spectral symbol. Every
test-geometric saving (oddness, support, half law, ladder, bipartite halving) is a constant factor; the rate
is fixed by the size of ψ(x) − x. For the RH research this means: incidence geometry can organize and localize
the problem completely, but it cannot supply the decisive input — that input is a signed, cancellation-based
estimate of the prime–polar balance at PNT precision, concentrated on multiplicatively aligned tests and on
the smallest moduli. Any route that bounds pieces separately, or by absolute values, is already ruled out here.

### 08 ArithmeticContinuationAndCriticalEnergy

ACCE recasts RH as a question of survival rather than sign: finite energy at an easy end must
survive transport to the critical end, uniformly in the horizon. Every exact identity the
programme proves — derivative, angle, dilation, Dirichlet form, prime-pair level sum, shell flux —
shows the same thing from a different side: the easy ends are paid, the cutoff is free, the short
moduli are paid, the scalar moment is paid, and what remains is one signed cancellation in the
long-modulus Möbius tail, at relative precision $1/T$, against a positive prime-harmonic debt. The
transport machinery does not supply that cancellation; it locates it, prices it in the width unit,
and refutes every route that ignores signs (majorant), ignores exact prime weights (Class III,
frozen primes) or estimates terms separately (indefinite kernel, divergent dilation series at ½).
The function-field model shows the mechanism itself is sound — with exact higher-degree Möbius
cancellation there is nothing to transport — so the number-field difficulty is that cancellation
and nothing else. For the RH proof this means: continuation frameworks are complete as
bookkeeping, and the only admissible new input is a sign-reading estimate on the long-modulus
quadrature (equivalently the prime-shell correlation) below half damping.

### 09 ScaleSignedIncidenceCriticality

SSIC says the RH problem is not short of positivity statements but of the right observable. Every
sign-type certificate at a bounded scale is exactly as informative as no certificate; every
magnitude above the Nyquist scale is exactly informative. The width lives at infinity or in a size,
and enters quadratically, so any argument must control a deficit to precision $\beta^2L^3$ at the
scale of the configuration, and must do so for every prime individually rather than on average. The
two available unconditional ζ schedules fall on the wrong side of this line — one below Nyquist,
one with an exponentially large deficit — which locates the missing input precisely: a
trans-Nyquist magnitude estimate with sub-cubic precision, equivalently control of the local
off-line count or of the signed first-power comb charge. For the RH programme this is a filter: a
route is worth building only if it names its reading, crosses Nyquist, reads magnitudes, and keeps
every prime's weight.

### 10 ArithmeticCompensationTheory

ACompT shows that the arithmetic sources of the RH programme are full of genuine cancellation —
the comb is a translation, prime atoms cancel the polar density, the large-prime sector cancels its
complement, two cutoffs cancel the divisor remainder, a sieve corrector cancels residue-class bias —
and that every such cancellation, measured honestly, stops exactly at the zero width: the exponent of
the compensated ledger is the width, the curve receiver is admitted exactly on zero chains, and a
power saving in any single-scale residual is a zero-free strip. Cancellation buys log powers and
relative rates unconditionally; it buys powers only between two representations that share the
same hard term. The lesson for the RH proof is that a decisive estimate will not come from making
one description smaller but from a compatibility between two descriptions that constrains the
residual differently, kept signed, kept anchored to the actual source, and paid for every change of
scale, origin and norm. Function fields confirm that the receiver framework is faithful (admission
⇔ Weil); over the integers the missing piece is the coercivity that curves get from a discrete
spectrum and integers do not have.

### 11 ArithmeticCastelnuovoSeveri

ACS shows that $\mathbb Z$ already has almost all of Weil's machine: an intersection pairing,
hyperbolic fibres, exact Lefschetz and adjunction identities, unconditional Bézout for effective
cycles, a nondegenerate pairing, a cardinal and a metric measure of failure that equal the number of
right zeros and the zero width, and a prime tower that sees every off-line zero. What is missing is
narrow and sharply located: an effectivity whose ampleness and Riemann–Roch are proved without the
sign. Every source-defined cone tried either reduces RR to a coercive floor, or buys Bézout and
ampleness at the price of RR, and no finite-information cone can be Bézout. The fake curves show why
data cannot force the sign — only effectivity (sections) does — and genus one shows that Weil/Hasse
use a ring of correspondences, while $\mathbb Z$ only has a free monoid of Hecke graphs. Positivity
does exist in large unconditional pieces: short windows, low-frequency-blind subspaces, arithmetic
and Haar sections, the blind ideal, almost every height. Each piece fails exactly at a stated
boundary (finitely many low frequencies, a signed potential, the Fejér barrier, a wall, a one-prime
Lefschetz bound). The message for the RH programme: the proof must construct sections — an
arithmetic Riemann–Roch on the scaling surface — rather than refine estimates of the form; once the
finitely many low-frequency directions at each degree are governed by such sections, the rest of the
Hodge index is already in place.

### 12 SignedArithmeticEnergyIncidence

SAEI separates what a positivity proof can get from *structure* from what it must get from
*cancellation*. Structure is powerful but bounded: orientation (holonomy) decides exactly when local
bookkeeping suffices; Kron reduction pushes all difficulty onto a few frustrated terminals; trains,
boxes and companions give sharp capacities. In the arithmetic ledgers the frustration is real and is
created only by primes, and the obstructions are always one step beyond the structure: the prime
triangle is frustrated, the smooth-number support saves across primes, the residue modes are not a
Riesz family. On the analytic side the programme pins the barrier precisely: every sign-blind
argument stops at half damping, everything up to and including half damping is unconditional (with
PNT-strength input), and continuation below it cannot be an operator estimate — it has to read the
Möbius source and needs $1/\zeta$ inside the critical strip, which is RH-strength. The lesson for the
RH programme: positivity frameworks that only manage budgets, norms or sizes will saturate at the
same threshold; the missing ingredient is a signed cancellation statement about $\mu$ (equivalently
about zeros), and SAEI supplies the exact interfaces — terminal conductances, polar dual norms,
companion counts, aperture-uniform compactness — where that statement would have to enter.

### 13 TransThresholdPairCorrelation

TTPC turns a vague statement — "the explicit formula cannot see past `X = T`" — into a single,
explicitly named quantity. Everything an analytic method can pay for is paid: the archimedean side, poles,
end effects, grid aliasing, far prime pairs, sum frequencies, prime powers, the prime 2. What is left is the
signed correlation of odd primes at even gaps within relative distance `T^{−1}`, measured against a kernel
whose sign oscillates. That quantity is exactly the pair-correlation/Hardy–Littlewood content of the primes
at the scale where zeros and primes are in uncertainty balance. The programme's lesson for RH research:
the obstruction is not missing analysis but missing arithmetic — size-only tools (sieves, Hilbert
inequalities, positive majorants, survival-cone positivity) provably cannot reach it, because the true
answer is a small negative number (`−1/144`) produced by repulsion, and every unsigned bound overshoots by
a constant factor. Any proof that moves past `2/3`, and a fortiori any RH route that runs through the
explicit formula, must supply signed prime-pair information at the square-root scale; TTPC tells it the
exact form, unit, and budget.

### 14 ArithmeticOrientationTheory

AOT says the RH problem is a problem of orientation, not of size. Every reading that throws away sign
information — amplitudes, complete periods, rank counts, finite apertures — provably cannot decide the
question, and every reading that keeps it pays a precision cost quadratic in the depth it certifies.
The prime side of the Weil form is not mysterious locally: each prime tower is a Cholesky energy minus a
mass, the frustrating part is one rank-one square per prime, and the archimedean diagonal already
dominates all towers at once except for a bounded head and a continuum loss exponential in the aperture.
The real difficulty is global coupling, not any single prime.

The Wiener-algebra side shows that payment has a genuine index theory: unpayability is located on a
compact spectrum, counts zeros of $\zeta$ with multiplicity, and is invariant under units. But every
proved statement is a lower bound; the matching upper bound — that the obstruction is exactly the
off-line zeros — is RH-strength. Likewise, every effectivity or section formulation, once made exact,
returns the Hodge index unchanged. The lesson for the programme: reformulations are coordinates on one
width line, and frustration is abundant yet paid. Progress requires an estimate that keeps orientation
through the limit — a selective count, a source energy not equal to the defect, or control of the
continuum coupling between towers.

### 15 ArithmeticOrientationGeometry

AOG shows that the sign Weil needed is, cycle by cycle, positive definiteness of one function on the scale
group, and that this function is an exponential sum over the zeros. Every geometric identity of Weil's
proof survives on impure models, so none can supply the sign; every soft arithmetic property (integrality,
functional equation, positive prime counts) survives on the golden curve, so none can either. What decides
orientation is purely spectral: a cycle is oriented exactly when it is blind to the off-line zeros, its
growth rate is the depth of the deepest zero it sees, and its negative index counts the right zeros it sees.

For the RH programme this collapses many formulations into one explicit target: the translation orbit of
the cubic spline. RH is equivalent to Hasse–Weil along that orbit, to subexponential growth of one function
computed from primes, Gamma factor and pole, and to positive semidefiniteness of its Gram matrices on a
two-prime Hecke orbit. Weak bounds self-improve (any eventual bound, any damped bound gives the sharp one),
so the needed input is qualitative, not a sharp constant. The obstruction is that every unconditional
source of positivity found here — the archimedean collar, finite Euler data, base Riemann–Roch — orients
only finite pieces. A proof must bring arithmetic information about infinitely many primes that is visible
on one orbit and fails on the golden curve.

### 16 ArithmeticLocalDivisorGeometry

ALDG localizes the RH heat question to one finite polynomial per height and answers every purely
polynomial question about it sharply: exact repair regions, exact heat thresholds, the sharp Hermite
barrier with its extremizers, and a configuration-free degree law. The outcome is a clean separation.
Everything that depends only on degree and root radius is now unconditional, and through zero counting it
already yields heat positivity of every finite head and every heated slice coefficient at large height.

What it cannot do is reach the detection scale. The barrier and the cluster model show the true local heat
rate is depth² divided by local count; a polynomial argument that ignores where the zeros are can only see
radius, and an adverse pair masked by enough central multiplicity is invisible to heat at the same order.
The remaining RH content is therefore a statement about how many zeros of `ξ` can crowd near a point and how
deep they can sit — arithmetic input on local clustering, not further polynomial positivity.

### 17 SignedSuccessorArithmetic

SSA shows precisely what the successor buys and what it does not. On the sign side, `+1` against `×` yields exact,
quantized, sharp density laws — the one-third floor, conductor-quantized biases, a deficit that is four times a
count — all attained by pretentious twins. These are density-scale facts; the square-root scale that RH needs never
appears over `ℤ`, although its finite-field shadow is literally a count.

On the form side, the programme separates pole from zeros cleanly. Prime-supported Möbius forms diverge below the
1-line for a reason that has nothing to do with zeros, and are bounded on it; everything provable at `σ = 1` comes
from the 1-line alone. The one object that carries zero information is the size-truncated form, now characterized
completely — it is the receiver, its Möbius weights are the unique solution of a sieve functional equation, and its
difficulty is isolated in one interior integral. The RH content left here is a single uniform bound strictly inside
the strip; no successor identity proved so far reaches it.

### 18 ArithmeticDefectRigidity

ADR turns "the receiver energy might diverge" into a concrete object and asks what that object must look
like. The source keeps a lot: the forcing is paid by its own pole, the escaping energy is antipodal to its
cofactors, and every escape state is a cyclic module whose spectrum sits inside the obstruction hull. But
the same abstract constraints are satisfied by white noise and other countermodels, so geometry of Grams
alone never forces RH.

The decisive finding is a two-level picture. At the level of rows, the renormalized inversion law plus two
Euler factors (primes 2 and 3) is a zero detector: power-like solutions exist exactly at translated zeros of
ζ, counted with multiplicity, and vanish on zero-free lines. At the level of escape states, those Euler laws
are not yet derived. RH through this route reduces to one bridge: show that a divergent receiver produces a
row obeying Euler laws at two primes. Everything on either side of that bridge is proved.

### 19 BiarithmeticGeometry

BG asks what the integers' two operations, kept together at a finite horizon, can certify. The answer is
mostly structural: the action is rigid (scalar commutant, polynomial-size, unipotent in finite rank) but it
cannot pick an analytic normalization, cannot see signs that complete periods erase, and its integral
invariant is a degree minus a logarithmic torsion rather than a dimension. Structure alone does not orient.

The calibration laboratory shows what payment really is: membership in an operator graph, exact for
geometric towers, with a sharp `√5` threshold. At critical contact payment stops being a closed condition;
the reduced obstruction is one number (a total evaluation) while the unreduced defect is a continuum of
power-tail directions. For RH this says the decisive estimate is quantitative and lives exactly at the
critical boundary: a uniform cofactor-energy bound for the actual source, which no closure argument or
finite index can replace.

### 20 MultiplicativePositivityGeometry

MPG closes the book on what nonnegative Euler coefficients can do. On the line `Re s = 1` the answer is exact and
complete: positivity is equivalent to positive definiteness of the boundary divisor, every positive-definite
configuration with mild regularity is realized by an honest Euler product over the rational primes, and the four
invariants together force exactly `ζ(1+it) ≠ 0` and nothing more. Integrality of prime data, choice of progression,
number of channels and degree of the certificate add nothing.

For RH this is a clean negative compass. Multiplicative positivity cannot see the interior: realized Euler products
satisfy every boundary invariant and vanish inside the strip, and a real zero on a self-conjugate member is never
excluded. Every interior gain must therefore come from the growth input or from a new interior object, not from a
cleverer positive certificate. The dlVP–VK line of zero-free regions is one inequality between pole weight, zero
weight and growth; any RH route that leans only on coefficient positivity is bounded by the universal width cap.

### 21 ArithmeticPositiveDuality

APD shows that positivity is a strong forcing engine exactly where it is paired with self-duality on the opposite
side and an integer: it recovers the integers, the primes and ζ itself from Riemann's functional equation, and does so
stably. But on Weil's own pair the gap and the positivity sit on the same (prime) side, so the same engine produces
only density statements and receivers. Every positive carrier tried — lattices, the prime torus, the affine group,
convolution ideals, coupled channels — either reduces to the dilation Gram (adding nothing) or, when given genuine
arithmetic structure, becomes incompatible with Weil's correlation.

The affine programme makes this precise: arithmetic successors live on the Kronecker-null, Haar-singular part of the
prime torus, Weil's correlation lives on one archimedean line, and the two never meet. The critical line appears as
a positivity threshold (Hecke s ≥ ½, Bost–Connes β = 1, Kakutani wall) of arithmetic states, not of Weil's
correlation. For the RH research this says: positivity of a carrier is not the missing ingredient; the missing
ingredient is a quantized identity that counts the zero set's own charge, and none of the positive structures built
here can supply it.

### 22 StripBochnerWeilForcing

SBWF extracts everything height positivity and a functional equation can say about zeros, and the answer is
sharp and modest: a zero-free collar of width ≍ 1/log T with at most ⌊κ/2⌋ exceptions, exactly matching what pole
charge can pay for. At pole order two the axioms already admit off-line zeros; at pole order one they admit only
real multiples of ζ. So the axiomatic class "positive Euler-type function + completed FE" is either too large (κ ≥ 2,
RH false in the class) or exactly ζ (κ = 1, RH is the original problem).

The lesson for the RH programme: no argument that uses only height positivity, pole order and the functional
equation can prove RH, because such an argument would apply to the whole class. A proof must use a property of ζ
that distinguishes it inside its own κ = 1 class — and since that class is ℝ^×·ζ, it must use arithmetic structure
beyond these axioms, not a sharper estimate within them.

### 23 ArithmeticCorrelationGeometry

ACG names the reason so many routes stall: everything provable from multiplicative structure holds for the whole gauge
orbit of the source, and almost every point of that orbit already satisfies RH-type and Chowla-type cancellation. The
actual source is one point of a full-measure set that is also meagre, with atypical twins arbitrarily close in every
finite resolution. Any argument that does not use something specific to the point 0 — additive structure, the
archimedean pole, an explicit correspondence — proves a statement about the orbit, not about $\zeta$.

The positive message is a precise address for the missing input. The prime source is atypical exactly by its pole; the
critical line is the unique weight where dilation boundaries carry finite multiplicative mass; an off-line zero would show
up as escaping energy with no gauge spectrum. RH is thus a visibility statement — every escape state is seen by the
gauge torus — and a proof must supply gauge-charged information (singular series, correspondences with a fixed budget,
boundary structure) rather than sharper multiplicative estimates.

### 24 ArithmeticKunnethTheory

Every exhaustion theorem in the corpus has the same shape: what a mechanism gains on a zero's depth it pays in
arithmetic length. AKT shows this is not a weakness of particular constructions but a property of multiplying the
spectrum with itself: products, moments, Künneth and Adams powers are all homogeneous, and their positivity content over
finite fields is mere point-count positivity, which impure fake curves satisfy.

The lesson for RH is a sharp division of labour. The term that turns positivity into a rate in the function-field
proofs is a fixed budget of a correspondence (Weil) or a fixed base loss (Deligne); neither is a product of the spectrum.
Over $\mathbb Z$ the search has to target correspondences on the missing surface or a genuinely non-abelian base,
not higher moments or tensor powers of $\zeta$.

### 25 ArithmeticCorrespondencePositivity

Weil's proof over $\mathbb F_q$ factors into layers, and ACP locates each over $\mathbb Z$. The identities, graphs and
the budget all exist and are free: fake curves satisfy them. Every correspondence $\mathbb Z$ owns has a local type at an
off-line pair: multiplicative ones are hyperbolic exactly there, so their positivity ends in blindness or isotropy;
the successor is unipotent and invisible; affine maps have genus zero. Each quantitative input that would suffice
(section counts, one-sided prime counts, a fixed tensor loss, spectral positivity of symmetric squares) turns out to
be RH itself. The sign is not hidden in a missing layer; it is the one layer, Rosati sufficiency, that no existing
correspondence carries.

The second half replaces positivity by bookkeeping of energy. Rows of arithmetic have symbols whose poles sit at the
zeros; energy budgets pay residues, and Abel escape is read pole by pole without contour shifts. This yields the
$\mathbb Z$-analogue of $2g$: the archimedean genus $2+\gamma-\log4\pi$, which the prime error's energy slope always
exceeds and equals exactly under RH with simple zeros, as a curve's slope equals $2g$ exactly for a pure simple
spectrum. The research lesson is that an RH proof along these lines needs an unconditional upper bound for a
quantity whose lower bound and conditional limit are now known; the difficulty concentrates in mass at infinite
height, not in finite windows.

### 26 ArithmeticPolarization

Weil's argument needs a form that is positive and equivariant at once. Over $\mathbb Z$ the test space of $\zeta$ carries
two forms, each with exactly one property: Weil's form is equivariant and its positivity is RH; the collided form is
positive, faithful, coercive at every aperture and equal to a logarithmic Sobolev norm, and its equivariance is RH. RH is
the statement that the two coincide. The polarization exists; what is missing is a way to compute it, because the
collided form is the global collision of conjugate zeros, which no analytic object carries and the explicit formula does
not see.

Every attempted repair runs into a structural parity. Localizing at an off-line pair without hyperbolicity forces the
point reflection, which is a twist: it costs a conductor and gauge charge, and only its odd half can force. Positive
completions are couplings: they collide evenly and cannot remove a lifted quartet. For the RH programme this pins the
target: a non-meromorphic, two-variable or measure-valued structure that computes the off-line defect, or that collides
conjugate zeros with odd multiplicity. More positivity built from symmetry or completion cannot reach it.

### 27 ArithmeticEffectivityTheory

AET asks where an $h^0$ for the arithmetic surface could come from, and answers with a sharp split. Every quantity that can
be written down from counting turns out to be either soft (the theta polarization, convexity of log-counts, cardinalities —
true for every datum, hence silent about $\zeta$) or exactly as strong as RH (an effectivity function, a naive height, a
log-torsion law at square-root precision, a fixed tensor loss). Nothing intermediate appears. The single integrality
inequality of the base, $|n|\ge1$, reaches Chebyshev-size statements and no further.

At the same time the prime side of Weil's formula turns out to be genuine intersection theory: von Mangoldt is the
intersection number of torsion orbits on the circle, computed by cyclotomic resultants and equivariant under Hecke, and the
Weil prime kernel is the renormalized limit of a positive energy — the sign is lost precisely in the renormalization. And at
each finite level square-root cancellation is a count (Gauss sums, Weil on curves, Burgess). For the RH programme this says
that the missing object is uniformity across levels at the arithmetic point: a structure that counts $\psi(N)-N$ or
regularizes the torsion energy without spending the sign. The complete 𝔽₃ base shows what such a structure has over a
function field: a positive-genus curve, a gap domain of covolume one, and a zeta function whose zeros survive as poles.

### 28 BiarithmeticGaugeGeometry

The programme turns positivity into a deformation theory. Adding positive prime mass to $\zeta_\gamma$ is irreversible in a
precise sense: it keeps every zero off the critical line, can only lower the central order, and on the critical line can
only move zeros in a pattern governed by a positive-definite function, with each created zero paid for twice at the centre.
Strong multiplicity one follows below the square-root deficit, and the surplus at the $\sqrt x$ scale is quantized by the
critical multiplicity. All of this is unconditional.

What it shows about RH is equally sharp. Every restricted form of the central ceiling — rational, positive — is exactly
equivalent to the absence of off-critical zeros at that height, so the deformation theory gives exact reformulations, not
leverage. The remaining freedom is deficits: deformations that remove prime mass of square-root size. RH is the statement
that removing such mass can never raise the central order. The multiplicative side alone is gauge-blind; the additive
successor and the square-root deficit are where the arithmetic of $\zeta$ must enter.

### 29 ConjugateDiagonalGeometry

CDG settles the question "can the conjugate-diagonal side be computed from arithmetic?" in the affirmative and with
quantitative force: the collided form, every depth moment, the Hecke defect, local zero counts on discs shrinking polynomially
with height, and the horizontal collision charge are all limits of positive integrals of literal theta data, with polynomial
resources and explicit error certificates, uniformly over moving windows and growing apertures.

What this tells the RH effort is where the difficulty is not. Reading the divisor is solved; the depth information is
present in computable positive quantities. What is missing is a mechanism that forces those quantities to take their
critical-line values — an upper bound for the Hecke defect from the prime side, or a structure identifying the conjugate
pairing with the reflection pairing. Positivity of the collided side is free; its translation invariance is RH. The
conductor-forcing gap and two-scale shell rigidity show that local forcing and moment comparisons alone do not close it.

### 30 SelectivePositivityAndThresholdLaws

Positivity of Weil's form on short windows is not evidence about ζ until the window is long enough to see a prime, and
the first prime it sees is read only through its weight. SPTL proves that the first selective step is real: past ½log2
the form is positive exactly for weights with the right value at 2, and fake weights are caught by explicit tests. This
makes each additional exposed prime a separate, certifiable event, and shows that window-by-window positivity is a
sequence of finite arithmetic checks rather than a single analytic inequality; it does not by itself approach RH.

On the global side, every face of the square-root threshold reproduces the same picture: escaped Möbius saving,
sections beyond the large sieve, subquadratic heights, gauge visibility and pole-neutralized tower floors are each
exactly equivalent to a zero-free strip or to RH, while the methods that ignore sign or parity (unsigned large-sieve
averaging, parity-blind dispersion, unneutralized continuum towers) provably fail. The lesson for the RH programme:
any successful route must carry sign and parity information through the threshold; blind methods stop exactly at it.

### 31 ArithmeticElectrostatics

AES shows that RH has an exact electrostatic form: the zeros are in transverse equilibrium, and equilibrium is
quantized, so a single bounded quantity (energy below one quantum, window energy below two, field below a fixed
threshold) would decide it. Everything that can be computed is computed: energies are logarithmic per zero, source work
equals energy in the limit, and the explicit formula reads the field from the primes. What is missing is always the same
kind of statement: an upper bound for the field or a sign for the work, not a new identity.

The renormalization results say where positivity lives. Removing the diagonal costs a counterterm that grows like log N,
forced by the arithmetic point; the archimedean place pays it exactly while the tests are narrower than a power of the
atom spacing, and stops paying at the spacing itself. At that resolution the prime form cannot distinguish ζ from its
sign twins, so separation must come from archimedean interaction or from finer cancellation. The heat-flow dissipation
law is the one global monotone quantity proved; it lacks an initial value. The programme maps RH onto a definite missing
inequality at a definite scale.

### 32 ArithmeticIntersectionTheory

Over ℤ three of Weil's four ingredients exist and are proved: intersection numbers of torsion cycles are logarithms of
orders of finite rings, power maps satisfy the projection formula, and the self-intersection of a torsion orbit is the
discriminant of its cyclotomic field. The diagonal, however, only exists after a regularization, and every
regularization tried either breaks Hecke covariance (the anomaly log t) or, once covariance is restored, loses the sign.

The sign itself splits cleanly: at each prime it holds (a positive Kac–Murdock–Szegő tower), but the towers of
different primes are transverse and their bounded local forms do not sum; on narrow log-atom tests Weil's prime
functional is exactly this sum, so RH becomes one inequality in which the archimedean self-energy and the pole must pay
for all towers at once. The finite intersection cannot tell ζ from its sign twins, and rotations see only the
archimedean factor, so the missing input must couple the archimedean place to the multiplicative structure across
primes. AIT turns "prove RH" into "prove the tower inequality at the widest admissible atoms".

### 33 RenormalizedArithmeticPotentialTheory

Every divergence of the explicit formula is understood: its rank is set by the regulator, Weil's own horizon regulator is
Hecke covariant with a polar (tensor) counterterm, and what remains is a computed finite part built from Euler's constant,
local tower forms and logarithmic derivatives of L-functions at 1. The renormalized prime form has a clean spectrum — the
integers, with energies log m + tame different, attained by Ramanujan sums — and the archimedean place pays it down to
the atom spacing. Positivity of the literal complete Weil matrix is therefore proved at every fixed overlap, with an
explicit completed spectrum whose bottom is a positive Fourier integral.

What it tells us about RH: the obstruction is not hidden in any single prime, character or finite sieve — all of these are
computed and positive. It is a rate problem. The positivity margin E(c) decays to zero as the overlap grows, flips of
larger primes need larger overlaps, and RH is exactly positivity at overlap (log N)^α. A proof must control the shrinking
margin against all primes simultaneously; finite-dimensional arithmetic (sieves, gauges, packets) is fully understood
and cannot supply it on its own.

### 34 RenormalizedBiarithmeticGeometry

RBG explains why positivity is so hard to carry from finite objects to the limit over ℤ: the prime place keeps no
positivity through its divergence at all, the archimedean place keeps it only on one hyperplane, and only coupling to
the arithmetic point (the pole) restores it. Matching the prime place requires a twin carrying the same Ramanujan
modes, and when one is built the primes reappear as digamma values — the inequality is re-expressed, not yet proved.

It also sorts tools into soft and selective. Amplification, rigidity and moment positivity hold for every positive
configuration, so they can only transport a bound that must come from elsewhere; every such target is exactly a
zero-free strip. Multiplicative (square-class) criteria cannot tell ζ from its Liouville twin; only the successor,
seen through log-cells, pins the Euler point. A proof of RH must therefore inject a selective, additive–multiplicative
input at one scale; everything else in the machinery is transfer.

### 35 SpacingScaleHarmonicAnalysis

SSHA pins down where the difficulty of RH sits on the scale axis. At widths below a fixed fraction of the atom spacing
positivity is a theorem — and it is universal: it holds for every twist of the primes, Liouville included, so it
contains no arithmetic. At any overlap growing faster than (log log N)^{1+ε}, narrow atoms reproduce every fixed test,
so positivity there is exactly RH, and every non-positive twin is detected. Between them lies bounded overlap, where
the prime form begins to read signs through non-ratio lags and where twist-blind methods provably stop.

For the RH programme this means two things. First, the archimedean place can pay the prime place only up to the
Nyquist scale; beyond it, positivity must come from the pole (the one negative square RH permits) together with a
selective, sign-reading input from non-ratio lags. Second, the gap between theorem and RH is not polynomial in N but
iterated-logarithmic in the overlap: any argument that controls positivity at slowly growing overlap with constants
uniform in N would be a proof, and any argument blind to the signs of the primes cannot do it.

### 36 ArithmeticLorentzianGeometry

ALG asks whether the Hodge index of the scaling surface can be produced the way Lorentzian theory produces it: local
signature, a sign hypothesis, and trickle-down. The answer splits the problem cleanly. The sign hypothesis is real for
`ζ`: it is a gauge-fixing performed by the archimedean place along the successor, it selects the Euler point among all
twins, and it holds exactly up to atomic resolution. The positive square cannot come from any symmetry over `ℤ`, because
every available symmetry is an isometry; it comes from the pole plane. The multiplicative closure (Euler products) is
Lorentzian but blind, so arithmetic cannot enter there. What is left is the trickle-down inequality itself, which over
`𝔽_q` is the square-root count of points.

For `ζ`, trickle-down is a theorem on a fixed fraction of the spacing, and the hyperbolicity there is twin-blind: it holds
at the Liouville point. The programme therefore locates the RH content precisely: between the Robin–Euler threshold and the
spacing, on the pole complement, where a positive square of the Markov form along the pole vector has to be absorbed by the
pole plane. Absolute-value payment of the pole cannot reach it; the sign of the pole term and a non-atomic basis are needed.
The constructive tools (trickle-down engine, polar gluing, Hardy inequality with the prime defect) are the parts other
programmes reuse.

### 37 IncommensurableGaborPinching

IGP is a zero-proportion result, not an RH route. Its lesson is methodological: the best unconditional counting
arguments throw away Gram geometry, and keeping it pays. The Montgomery–Taylor window cannot make dense on-line zeros
orthogonal because its kernel zeros are not on a lattice, so the trace bound is never attained and a definite fraction of
energy is recovered.

For the RH programme this marks what structural information alone can and cannot do. It improves constants in counting
arguments that are blind to arithmetic, and the improvement is tiny (`10⁻⁸`) and certified only for the shortest
configurations. It does not touch off-line zeros. Arithmetic positivity has to come from elsewhere; IGP shows that
existing zero-density machinery is not saturated and that its slack is Gram geometry.

### 38 ArithmeticIndexDescent

AID says the RH problem, in index form, has a sharp seam. Everything except the first-prime coarse layer is paid by the Gamma
energy: higher powers, squares, finite prime sets, all detail self-interactions, and the full pole with only one or two scalar
readouts lost. The index descends exactly through sections, completions and limits, so no information is lost in the
presentation itself. On the zero side, every negative square has a quantized price in energy, work and critical rate.

The seam is the first-prime layer. Its cost grows like `√Y`, so Gamma can pay heads only up to `(log A)²`, and a growing head
alone creates unbounded index. That growth is exactly the pole's square-root term: with every prime power in the window, the two
cancel. The open problem is this cancellation with uniform control, a square-root prime count matched against the pole. Any RH
proof along these lines must exploit arithmetic cancellation between primes and pole; positivity of separate pieces cannot
suffice.

### 39 SignedArithmeticHodgeTheory

SAHT shows that the cell space carries a canonical dyadic structure in which the prime two acts as a shift and every Haar
coefficient is a literal physical readout. In these coordinates one original Gamma reserve pays every layer of the Euler
product that has finite absolute coefficient mass, and also the even powers by signed compensation, without truncating any
translation level. The complement of the positive carrier retains exactly the global null directions, so nothing is lost by
removing the carrier.

The lesson for the RH programme is a sharp split. Everything with summable coefficient mass, and the squares, is now paid on
objects that keep all translation levels. What remains is the first-prime layer together with the two exponential poles, and
the sign on the retained orbits. These cannot be reached by absolute estimates; they require cancellation between primes and
poles on infinite-dimensional, non-compact orbit spaces.

### 40 SelectiveMultiscaleArithmeticPositivity

SMAP sharpens the target. Anything the twins of `Λ` also satisfy cannot prove RH, and most positivity built so far is of that
kind. The place where `Λ` is special is the pole hyperplane, and the size of its advantage is a definite constant, `2 log 2`,
carried by the prime two and by the correlation of `Λ` with the pole. That advantage is real but small and lives at spacing
scale.

The multiscale analysis shows where it must be spent. Across dyadic levels everything is paid or removable (Gamma decays, the
pole vanishes on one hyperplane) except one quantity: the signed Chebyshev error along the dyadic orbit. Tower positivity on
pole-free generators is exactly RH at any overlap, and interval-type prime information cannot decide it. RH research must
therefore find cancellation in the prime error between scales, and use the hyperplane margin as the selective reserve that
pays it.

### 41 ArithmeticCountingPositivity

ACountP turns a qualitative separation into a complete calculation. For every completely multiplicative prime-sign twin, its
selective gap is now determined by divisibility counts: an Euler-product density, Ramanujan conductor weights, and one
arithmetic secular equation that matches the actual finite prime matrices exactly. Counting is powerful enough to compute
the discrete margin to the last constant.

The finding for RH research is negative but sharp. The discrete margin, even at its exact maximum and combined with
multiplicativity, polar matching and the true size of every Weil coefficient, does not identify `Λ`: infinitely many twins
match it and still fail Weil positivity. Exhaustion through prime cutoffs is non-uniform, and any fixed tolerance in a
selecting probe is defeated by remote primes. A proof of positivity cannot come from the hyperplane gap alone; it must use
continuous overlap, Gamma and the poles, or an invariant finer than any finite count of prime signs.

### 42 HeckeKreinToeplitz

HKT turns the RH problem into an invariant of one object: a Hermitian kernel on the positive rationals whose negative squares are exactly the zeros right of the line. Everything structural is now known about this invariant. It is closed in the right topology. It is carried by two primes, by integer windows and by every receiver. It is priced by energy and by heat collisions. It equals a one-sided square-root prime count. Over finite fields it reproduces Weil. None of this decides its value. Each equivalence relocates the problem without shrinking it, and finite index alone already sits at Lindelöf depth.

The programme also shows where a proof cannot come from. Twin-blind structure (aliasing, closedness, decompositions, index ladders) cannot decide, because sign gauges act by congruence. The selective content at atomic resolution is a single square, carried by the pole. Every arithmetic perturbation of `Λ` (a flipped prime, a Liouville twist, an `n^{−η}` error) has infinite index, so `ζ` sits at an isolated point. A proof has to supply a global law that is exact rather than approximate: a count or a Riemann–Roch-type identity, with the Euler product and functional equation entering as identities. Positivity is then the last step, after the index has been computed.

### 43 ShallowCauchyPrimeEnergy

SCPE shows what unconditional zero statistics can do for an energy-based proof. Lorentzian pair correlation, multiscale zero counts and Selberg's near-line density together bound the shallow off-line energy by its own diagonal. The `log T` loss disappears and the deep zeros become an arbitrarily small fraction. The result is sharp: unconditional prime-side inputs reach the diagonal and stop there.

For the RH programme this marks a boundary. Energy or Ward arguments that need the shallow Cauchy energy to be small compared with its diagonal cannot be fed by pair-correlation or density estimates, because those inputs see ordinates, not depths. A zero at depth `1/log T` and a zero on the line look alike to every tool used here. Closing the gap needs an input that is sensitive to depth itself: an index, a positivity, or a structural identity.

### 44 ArithmeticKreinLangerTheory

AKL establishes that the local structure of the Weil kernel is fully understood and completely uninformative about its index. Restrictions to towers decide the index when the spectrum is faithful, and the logarithmic spectrum of `ζ` is faithful. Fine towers and two primes carry everything. Euler factors are positive. Yet tower-supported data alone have index `0` or `∞`, every local statement is blind to twins, and every pointwise-closed statement that survives vertical shifts also survives the Liouville twin. The index is a global invariant, and its value is set by one global law: an upper count for one parity class at square-root precision.

The function-field comparison is exact and shows where `ℤ` differs. Over `𝔽_q` the parity twin is geometric, Frobenius gives multiplicity `√q`, and the atomic ceiling has no error term. Over `ℤ` the twin is geometric only in the Bohr closure, integrality gives multiplicity one, and Euler's constant is the archimedean defect of the ceiling. What RH needs is a producer: an auxiliary object that vanishes on the odd Liouville class to growing order, in a sense finer than divisibility. None of the known constructions is of that kind.

### 45 ArithmeticOfSquareRootPrecision

ASRP shows that the information RH needs is never missing from the arithmetic of `ℤ`. The Chebyshev identity is exact. The integrality lattice of the horizon is exact, and its floor is attained. Tower positivity sees every coefficient of `Λ`. The count itself, by contrast, sees only a square-root class: it cannot distinguish `Λ` from any finite modification, a flipped Euler factor, or an `n^{−1/2}` perturbation. All the classical estimates (PNT, short intervals, any error law weaker than square root) are shared by weights that fail the count, so none of them can decide.

Precision is lost at identifiable places: Möbius inversion of the Chebyshev identity, the analytic ceiling that certifies a small integral, and the sawtooth part of Stirling's error. A proof of RH along this axis must keep the identities of `Λ` alive through one of these steps, and that cannot be a size estimate. It needs an arithmetic certificate of square-root size: a Stepanov-type auxiliary over `ℤ`, cancellation in `μ` against the sawtooth, or a positivity statement that, like tower positivity, carries the identities of `Λ` and not just its size.

### 46 CriticalArithmeticCorrelationTheory

The programme shows that the literal Weil form, with nothing modelled away, is positive on every atomic family whose overlap stays bounded, and that it separates ζ from Liouville and from every cofinite sign flip at the same overlap. Positivity is not an accident of one profile: any smooth profile whose Fourier transform has no real zeros works, and the ground state is always the pole. The mechanism is always the same triangle: a prime-defect reserve confines low energy to finitely many conductors, actual prime sums realize each conductor's energy as an approximate eigenvalue, and a Fourier tail gives every conductor a positive floor.

What this says about the RH proof: positivity through the arithmetic of finitely many conductors is now routine, so the difficulty sits entirely in growth. As overlap grows, the floor decays polynomially, the residual costs grow polynomially, and the conductor cutoff grows exponentially. Fixed packets survive to `(log N)^{1/48}`; the full matrix does not yet survive any growing schedule. An RH proof along this route needs either a conductor-count bound that grows slowly with overlap or a family that exhausts the test space without paying conductor confinement.

### 47 TransMetzlerWeilPositivity

The programme fixes the two ends of the atomic route. At bounded overlap, positivity of ζ's complete form is real, unconditional, and selective: it survives past the Metzler wall where no sign gauge helps, while Liouville, every pole-free gauge, and even a single flipped prime fail at the same or smaller overlap. The selectivity is driven by exact pole matching and by the arithmetic of the first prime correlations, not by zero information, which alone cannot decide anything at fixed overlap.

At growing overlap, RH is exactly a statement about the overlap `L(log L)²` (or any divergent multiple of it), and a bounded multiple sees only a window of logarithmic radius. Between the two ends sit three proved obstructions: the non-ratio prime correction becomes unbounded as soon as `c > 1`, the Gram floor collapses, and the remaining obligation after paying a prefix is a Schur complement that retains every arithmetic and archimedean term. An RH proof along this route must show positivity of that Schur complement at overlap `≈ log log N (log log log N)²`, and it must do so with an argument that fails for every twisted source.

### 48 EffectiveArithmeticHodgeTheory

The programme shows that the index of the Weil kernel is a finite, effective object: every zero off the line below height `T` is caught by an explicit tower window, by a two-prime box of size equal to the spectrum, and by atomic Gram matrices along explicit overlap schedules, with explicit finite inequalities certifying each negative square. Nothing in the transfer is lossy; the sections reach the index exactly. So the difficulty of RH is not in seeing zeros in finite sections. Any off-line zero would be visible in a finite computation.

The auxiliary half delivers the opposite message. The zero estimate over `ℤ[Γ]` is exact and sharp, and integrality turns approximate vanishing into exact divisibility, but a dimension count then gives exactly Chebyshev's bound and nothing better. Over `𝔽_q` Stepanov's method wins because `x^q = x` makes vanishing at all points of one degree cheap; over `ℤ` each prime has its own progression and no such identity is known. A producer route to RH therefore needs a genuinely new arithmetic identity on the primes; the geometry of sections around it is complete.

### 49 CompletedArithmeticForms

The programme shows that every RH-equivalent complete form studied here is positive on every fixed finite
piece, with explicit and usually sharp horizons, and that the remaining difficulty is always a single
exhaustion: rank for the theta hierarchy, overlap for the Weil matrix, cutoff for Euler deletion, frequency
near the constant mode for Chebyshev energy. Exact completions show where the unpaid part lives; it is
never a hidden defect of the method but a precisely located compact region or near-constant band.

The repeated lesson is that reserves degrade at a definite rate (fraction (2/3)^N, floor c^{−7}, failing rank
between √q and q) while the sources supply payments at their own rates (quadratic degree cost, 1/√log N
residuals). RH-strength conclusions require the payment rate to beat the reserve decay along the full
exhaustion. The programme makes both rates explicit and shows that, where they currently fail to meet, no
change of normalization or coordinate (clocks, truncations, single atoms) closes the gap by itself.

### 50 ArithmeticGaugeTheory

Gauge theory recasts RH as a typicality question for a single point on a compact group whose almost every
point already satisfies the square-root law. The programme establishes the full landscape around that point:
the typical value is exactly ½, the trivial point can only err upward, its whole natural class shares its
exponent, and the special locus reads GRH point by point while being typical on average. Every Haar average,
every gauge-invariant certificate and every finitely determined certificate is provably blind to the
difference between 0 and its atypical neighbours.

The lesson for an RH proof is that the decisive input must be pointwise at 0 and must use additive structure
on signed directions: the pole (coherence) and periodicity (cancellation) are the only additive mechanisms at
special points, and neither produces the typical value. Multiplicity-based producers, conductor averages and
level of distribution all stop exactly one point short. Up to overlap two the Weil form's shells are invisible
to every conductor, so the sign there is a property of 0 alone; this localizes the open problem without
supplying it.

### 51 ArithmeticHarmonicAnalysis

The programme tests whether a Stepanov-type auxiliary can exist over ℤ by computing its exact price. The
answer is rigid: vanishing to order r on the natural torsion analogue of the Frobenius-fixed points forces the
auxiliary to reach integer support 2^{qr}, and a trivial polynomial already attains this. Unique factorization
is both the reason the cost is finite and the reason it cannot be beaten by torsion data alone.

For the RH effort this closes off the most direct transplant of the finite-field proof: multiplicity bought on
Adams loci is paid for exactly in support length, with no gain. Any gain must come from coupling the additive
structure (successor relations, which reconstruct phases only logarithmically and only below a cutoff) with
the multiplicative one, or from analytic jets along the logarithmic flow, where transcendence theory controls
first-order data but not yet higher orders.

### 52 HilbertianExplicitFormulas

The programme separates the RH problem into two sides with a precise boundary. Everything an explicit formula
can compute is the Weil form; the same-point form differs from it by the line-reflection defect, pair by pair,
and no holomorphic test removes that defect. On the same-point side the defect is quantized: off-line zeros
contribute an even integer collision charge, at least 2, and over F_q this charge is exactly Weil's theorem in
disguise. Holomorphic clocks see the quantum only as an infinite excess; it is finite only on the
antiholomorphic side.

For the RH effort this means the decisive input cannot be a cleverer explicit formula; it must be a
non-holomorphic identity that reaches the same-point side. The resolvent identity shows such identities exist
and have a prime side right of the strip, with a nonnegative depth term that vanishes exactly under RH. The
open problem is to control that depth term near the critical line, or to find a source-realizable carrier that
reads the quantum with finite loss.

### 53 SuccessorShellArithmetic

The successor shells are where additive and multiplicative structure meet inside the Weil form, and the
programme shows that the fixed-overlap positivity is not a prime phenomenon but an integer one: the unit gap of
the shift lattice pays the logarithm, and a continuum of shifts would leave the form unboundedly negative. This
locates the discreteness that RH-strength positivity must use.

It also shows why growing-overlap positivity has been stuck at polynomial loss: paying the shells by themselves
is provably expensive, while paying them together with the pole is cheap on every family tested so far. The
divisor-jet work exposes the finite algebra behind that cancellation as a positive divisor-graph energy with
explicit spectrum, positive even after arbitrary signed smoothing. The remaining gap is identification: proving
that the true complete Weil shell block is such a completion, including its horizon boundary, for vectors off
the conductor span. That joint signed estimate is the single input the confinement route needs.

### 54 ArithmeticResonanceTheory

ART asks whether the two non-positive mechanisms that prove resonance bounds elsewhere, oscillation and
amplification, can be brought to `ζ`. The answer is a sharp map of obstructions. Every realization of `ζ` by a
dynamical system is forced to be prime-length, additive ones collapse to the trivial commutative one, finite ones
are excluded by the archimedean place, and the regularity of any Hölder realization is bounded by integrality
along every cycle. Such a realization would immediately give a zero-free strip, which is why the obstructions are
so strong: the difficulty is not the oscillation but the existence of the system.

For amplification, the finite-field proof works because rank is finite; over `ℤ` orthogonality costs exactly what
it gains, so any amplifier must evaluate its moments by an arithmetic identity rather than by orthogonality. For
the RH effort this says that neither a transfer-operator picture of the primes nor a Deligne-style family moment
is available off the shelf; both require a genuinely new arithmetic input (a non-additive realization or an
identity with fixed loss), and integrality, unique factorization and the Gamma factor are the precise constraints
it must respect.
