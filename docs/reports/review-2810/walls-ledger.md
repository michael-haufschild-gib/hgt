## The three families

| Family | Mathematical question | What would cross it |
|---|---|---|
| **Wall 1 — Critical arithmetic sign** | What bounds the actual signed prime/Möbius source, complete Weil form, or computed defect at the critical scale? | A new arithmetic inequality meeting an existing receiver, with all tails and quantifiers intact. |
| **Wall 3 — Transfer and exhaustion** | Does an estimate survive the required norm, source realization, shared budget and simultaneous limit? | A source-faithful comparison in the needed topology, with enough uniformity and an input that is actually proved. |
| **Wall 14 — Realization and forcing** | What independently constructed arithmetic structure forces that inequality? | A faithful object plus a proved selective law, rather than a calibration or structure field containing the desired sign. |

These families overlap causally. A positive finite-prime operator is a Wall 14 construction; its all-prime comparison is Wall 3; the resulting full-form sign would cross Wall 1. They are not three independent conjectures that must each be solved separately.

The organizing identity remains

\[
\operatorname{Re}W(f,f)=D(f,f)-\mathfrak d(f).
\]

Here \(W\) is the full equivariant signed Weil form, \(D\) the positive collided form, and \(\mathfrak d\) the off-line defect. The collided form is computable; its required invariance is RH-equivalent. Positivity of \(D\), a Gårding inequality for \(W\), and computation of \(\mathfrak d\) are three different facts. None alone is a zero-defect upper bound.
