\operatorname{Re}W(f)=D(f)-\Delta(f),\qquad
D(f)=\sum_\rho m_\rho|\widehat f(\rho)|^2\ge0.
\]

Here the Weil form uses functional-equation reflection, while the collided form uses conjugation. Their difference is a computed nonnegative defect. Arithmetic now computes much more of `D`, its depth moments and its source currents than the earlier programmes could. The missing comparison is still `Δ(f)≤D(f)` on the required tests. M(f_x)\ge
\bigl(2\theta'(0)+\log(1/w)-\log N-C\bigr)\|f_x\|_2^2.
\]

Consequently `M≥0` at `w≤c₀δ_N`, uniformly in `N`. This uses the constant-one self-energy and the tilted prime rows; it is not merely the earlier gain from shifts below the spacing. The source statement is [markovEnergy_atomicTest_ge_spacing](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/SpacingScaleHarmonicAnalysis/TheStabilityLawAtTheSpacingScale.lean#L198).

f(u)=\sum_{n\le N}G_n(u-\log n),\qquad
\operatorname{supp}G_n\subset[0,w],\qquad w\le c_{\rm full}\delta_N,

with arbitrary real `C²` profiles, the current source proves

\[
W(f,f)\ge\int f(u)^2\,du.
\]

No sinh-neutrality condition remains. The retained reserve has weights

**The strongest proposed route is an arithmetic descent of the negative index.** “Descent” here means transferring the specified form through finite presentations while accounting for mixed terms and limits. It does not mean defining a new positive form and identifying it with the target by assumption.

The exhaustive endpoint is unusually concrete. Let `B` be the corpus's fixed cubic spline, and for a finite set `S⊂{log n:n≥1}` let

\[
\mathsf W_S=
\bigl(W(B(\cdot-s),B(\cdot-t))\bigr)_{s,t\in S}.
\]

The supremum of the Hermitian negative indices of these matrices counts the distinct zeros right of the critical line. This is [zeta_splineAtomic_negIndex_law](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/SpacingScaleHarmonicAnalysis/UnboundedOverlapIsTheRiemannHypothesis.lean#L142). The common-denominator argument transports every finite rational Hecke orbit into the integer-log family; this removes the need to invent another complete testing criterion.

There is also a useful quantization: one right zero brings its distinct conjugate. The corpus proves that a global bound of **one** negative square on the spline orbit already forces RH: [riemannHypothesis_iff_splineOrbit_negIndex_le_one](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ArithmeticOrientationGeometry/TheSplineOrbitDecidesTheCriticalLine.lean#L237). Together with the integer-log transfer, this gives a precise target:

\[
\boxed{\quad \nu_-(\mathsf W_S)\le1
\quad\text{for every finite }S\subset\{\log n:n\ge1\}.\quad}
\]

This target is RH-strength. Its value is that it asks a source construction to preserve an index budget, rather than a positive coercivity constant independent of aperture. The corpus already records the decay of aperture ground energies; a fixed global positive floor is the wrong resource to demand. A finite bound `K>1` would still be major progress, but would only bound the number of off-line zeros. It would not eliminate them.

The local input is the new section-space theorem. The composition input is ALG's gluing law. For real symmetric forms `Q_i` with a common positive anchor `e`, write

\[
a_i=Q_i[e]>0,\quad b_i(x)=Q_i(e,x),\quad
A=\sum_i a_i,\quad b(x)=\sum_i b_i(x),
\]

\[
R_i(x)=\frac{b_i(x)^2}{a_i}-Q_i[x],\qquad
V_e(x)=\sum_i a_i\left(\frac{b_i(x)}{a_i}-\frac{b(x)}A\right)^2.
\]

Then

\[
R_{\sum_iQ_i}(x)=\sum_iR_i(x)-V_e(x).
\]

The exact hyperbolicity criterion is `V_e(x)≤Σ_iR_i(x)` for every `x`. The canonical correction subtracts the dispersion matrix and makes local reserves add. That corrected form is generally smaller than the required form; its signature does not prove the signature of the original. Both facts are explicit in [PolarGluingAndDispersion](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ArithmeticLorentzianGeometry/PolarGluingAndDispersion.lean#L125).

The proposed advance is to derive a decomposition and a payment from the **actual** prime lags, archimedean kernel and global poles. The normalized polar rows, their dispersion and any remaining cross term must be computed from those data. Local hyperbolicity cannot be inserted as a premise of an “arithmetic link” when it is precisely what remains unproved.

An equivalent way to organize a finite extension is by its Hermitian block matrix

\[
G=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\B^*&C\end{pmatrix}.
\]

When the already controlled block `A` is positive definite, the new negative directions are those of `C−B* A⁻¹B`. The Schur/inertia calculus is already present in [HTGeometry](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/HTGeometry/FiniteRankBoundarySchurIndex.lean). The new mathematics would be a source estimate for this mixed expression on a growing overlap family. Bounding `A`, `B` and `C` separately by their largest absolute sizes can discard exactly the compensation one needs.

The two signature conventions must remain distinct: ALG controls the positive index of an intersection form; the spline criterion controls the negative index of the Weil Gram. The scaling-surface bridge relates them. Merely calling both “Lorentzian” would lose the pole-plane accounting.

Why is this a possible circumvention of the walls? It changes three requirements that failed in earlier versions:

- It permits the prime counterterm to have growing rank. The proposed bounded quantity is the negative index of the **completed form after payment**, not the rank of a counterterm for the prime form alone.
- It retains mixed signed entries during assembly. It does not infer positivity of a sum from positivity of its pieces, or discard polar dispersion.
- It uses finite-Gram convergence and a closed index bound as the limiting objective. It need not preserve one coercivity constant across all apertures.

None of these observations pays a mixed term. They identify a mechanism outside the scope of several existing no-go arguments. ALG already proposes arithmetic gluing; RAPT already interprets Gårding bounds as finite-rank survival; AOG already supplies the index receiver. The proposed contribution is their **source-controlled composition on overlapping exhaustive tests**, not a claim to have invented those ingredients.

**Question 1: can the new positive arithmetic sections be joined without creating an uncontrolled number of negative directions?**

This is my highest priority for an RH route. The narrow-section result and fixed-spline receiver now make both ends explicit. The mathematical gap is between them.

The first experiment should use a specified family of translated or interlaced section spaces whose union introduces non-ratio prime lags. Keep four matrices simultaneously: the overlap Gram defining the actual `L²` norm, the signed prime-lag matrix, the archimedean cross matrix, and the two pole rows. An estimate in the coefficient Euclidean norm is not automatically an estimate in the overlapping-test norm; near-linear dependencies make that distinction decisive.

A useful first theorem would enlarge the proved family and control its mixed Schur expression or polar dispersion by a source-defined reserve. The bound must hold for every coefficient vector in that family, with the original poles included. A result confined to a fixed finite dimension is a local advance; an extension rule with a stable index budget is the desired structural advance.

There are two plausible approaches. One is a refinement of SSHA's tilted-weight argument that keeps the overlap Gram rather than reducing immediately to scalar rows. The other is a section/incidence construction whose relations force the needed mixed compensation, with ALG's reserve identity recording its cost. Neither should begin by requiring every individual prime piece to be positive. The existing success at the spacing already depends on a joint estimate across places.

The first gauge-sensitive terms have been located. For a completely multiplicative sign gauge, their factor is

\[
\omega(k)-\omega(m)\omega(n),
\]

which vanishes at exact ratios `n=km` or `m=kn`. Only near-incidences that are not exact ratios can contribute to this prime-side gauge defect. See [the lag defect identity](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/SpacingScaleHarmonicAnalysis/TheGaugeEntersThroughNonRatioLags.lean#L119). Finding such a term is not finding its favorable sign. It gives a precise place to look for the arithmetic information missing from the separated-atom estimate.

The eventual transfer should be stated for each fixed finite spline family: construct approximating presentations, prove convergence of all mixed Weil entries, and retain the required index bound. Increasing dimension alone is not exhaustion. Nor does positivity on all tiny windows imply positivity of their linear span: the mixed pairings are absent from that assertion. A claim that widths `N^{-θ}`, `θ<1`, suffice already needs the continuity and approximation theorem identified as open in SSHA.

Success would be a proved extension beyond the existing narrow spaces, with a genuinely reusable signed estimate. A full source construction reaching every finite spline family with index at most one would settle RH. A new definition of an index budget, a corrected artificial form, or an assumed mixed inequality would not advance this question.

**Question 2: can the signed prime surplus at bandwidth `4/3` be paid at the actual arithmetic point?**

This is the strongest concrete target for substantial quantitative progress before a full RH mechanism is available. TTPC has already reduced a useful conclusion to a specified arithmetic inequality. Its simple-critical certificate is

\[
\left(\frac{29}{36}-32\pi\varepsilon_1-\varepsilon\right)N(T,2T)
-\mathcal S_{u,\eta}(T)+M_{\rm off,mult}(T,2T)
\le N_0^{\rm simple}(T,2T).
\]

The off-line multiple mass is nonnegative. The formula, including the collar cost and common profile, is in [zeta_coalescent_certificate_four_thirds_simple_and_offLineMultiple](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/TransThresholdPairCorrelation/ParitySensitiveRamanujanStabilizerTransfer.lean#L1288). The prime-only reduction and its quantifiers are in [PrimeCoalescentSurplusAtMost](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/TransThresholdPairCorrelation/CoalescentPrimeReductionAndParitySieve.lean#L707).

The target is to choose admissible profiles, with collar losses tending to zero, for which the same signed surplus satisfies

\[
\mathcal S_{u,\eta}(T)\le(\beta+o(1))N(T,2T).
\]

Then the receiver yields the lower proportion `29/36−β`. The budget `β≤1/18` gives `3/4`. To improve the latest IGP result through this certificate alone, the relevant threshold is

\[
\beta<\frac{29}{36}-\bigl(H_D(1)+10^{-8}\bigr)
\approx0.13305484.
\]

The older target `β<5/36` only beats `2/3`; it does not automatically improve the current corpus result. This distinction changes which partial estimates are worth pursuing.

The same-profile requirement matters. One cannot optimize the zero-side certificate with one smoothing profile and prove a surplus estimate for another, then combine them. The objects, collar, damping range and eventual quantifiers must match.

The arithmetic object is a signed weighted sum of near prime pairs, with parity and stabilizer structure retained. A bound obtained by summing absolute local errors over the positive kernel lobe is already ruled out in the stated parity-blind class: its cost grows faster than the zero-count budget above the threshold. Haar-average smallness on the prime gauge torus, or zero mean of a noncongruent discrepancy under character averaging, also does not bound the Euler point.

A focused approach is to keep the exact Ramanujan/stabilizer decomposition through the oscillatory summation and seek cancellation in its actual discrepancy. This combines the existing incidence arithmetic with the test kernel that the receiver really consumes. An estimate for generic coefficients is not required if the actual arithmetic coefficients supply additional relations; conversely those relations must appear in the proof.

IGP suggests a complementary line of attack. Its disjoint-pair rotations retain

\[
2\sum_{\{x,y\}}\langle v_x,v_y\rangle_{\mathbb R}^{,2}
\]

while preserving the relevant operator. Non-lattice kernel geometry makes a macroscopic amount of this quantity unavoidable. Larger blocks or several compatible windows might retain more slack. The precise research problem is to prove a joint Gram inequality with all overlaps, tails and shared normalization accounted for. Adding separately optimized scalar improvements risks counting the same resource twice.

I would pursue such refinements as an immediate source of unconditional progress, while keeping the harder surplus question as the trans-threshold target. IGP's slack is a zero-side quantity; no current theorem identifies it with a saving of the required size in TTPC's prime surplus. That bridge, if found, would itself be a significant new mechanism.

Even `3/4`, or any fixed proportion below one, leaves the exceptional-zero problem untouched. Question 2 is valuable because it asks for a genuine signed arithmetic estimate with an explicit measurable payoff, not because its present receiver proves RH.

**Question 3: what arithmetic inequality can exclude one transverse-energy quantum?**

This is the essential complementary question. It prevents a research programme from mistaking increasingly strong density results for an argument about every zero.

For distinct zeros, AES defines a nonnegative transverse energy from

\[
\frac{(\operatorname{Re}\rho-\operatorname{Re}\rho')^2}
 {|\rho-\rho'|^2}.
\]

An off-line zero and its reflected partner have the same ordinate and contribute a full quantum independently of their distance from the critical line. Consequently a window containing one such pair has energy at least two. Here window energy sums the **full interaction row** of each zero in the window, including interactions with zeros outside it. It is not merely the energy of the internally truncated configuration. The exact criterion is

\[
\mathrm{RH}\iff \mathcal E(T,1)<2\quad\text{for every real }T.
\]

See [the window definition and quantum law](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/ArithmeticElectrostatics/QuantizationTurnsWindowsIntoPoints.lean#L46).

The corpus also provides two other readings of the same obstruction. The transverse field at right zeros has RH-strength upper thresholds, including `≤1` and a specified square-root-logarithmic threshold. And the Laurent residues of the arithmetic logarithmic-derivative square, with damping and reflection pairing, exhaust the multiplicity-weighted global energy. These are usable receivers with the diagonal and exterior terms already addressed. They do not yet provide their required upper bounds.

The existing unconditional estimate is logarithmic in the height for the energy of one zero. The field is a different quantity: near-coincident charges create inverse-distance singularities. An energy estimate cannot be silently promoted to a field estimate. Likewise, the distinct-point quantum normalization and the multiplicity-weighted source-work normalization require their explicit comparison.

The first substantial target I would set is **a source-derived bound on damped transverse work uniform as damping is removed**. Such a bound would control the total energy and hence force finiteness of off-line support. This is already far beyond an identity or a density estimate. It would still leave finitely many exceptions; finite energy alone is not RH. A stronger local budget below the quantum, or a separate mechanism excluding the finite remainder, is required.

A second possible route within this question is terminal-conditioned forcing. RBG supplies a terminal lens and Harnack propagation of an outward lower reserve. AES supplies exterior prime anti-alignment and moment estimates at zero ordinates. The missing statement must connect these on a common domain with the correct background and singular terms. The current interior terminal reserve is not automatically a prime detector of size above the exterior `½ log|γ|` background. Spreading a lower bound by Harnack does not manufacture the missing upper bound.

The mathematical tool to seek is a boundary-to-interior estimate for the **regularized arithmetic field or work**, retaining the local pole subtraction, the reflected partner, the Γ-term and the signed prime source. One must prove its validity on the actual terminal geometry. An arbitrary reflection-closed charge configuration satisfies the soft virial identities, so those identities alone cannot supply such an estimate.

Selberg's source is a particularly concrete place to investigate:

\[
\Lambda_2=\Lambda\log+\Lambda*\Lambda\ge0,
\qquad L(\Lambda_2,s)=\frac{\zeta''(s)}{\zeta(s)}\quad(\operatorname{Re}s>1).
\]

Its counterterm changes the local double coefficient from `m²` to `m(m−1)` while retaining the field residue `2mR`. The source distinguishes sign gauges coefficient by coefficient. The needed advance is a justified conversion from that arithmetic information to an upper bound on the regularized work inside the critical strip. Nonnegative coefficients outside the strip do not make that conversion by themselves.

Alternatively, one could prove a propagation law: one off-line defect forces sufficiently many disjoint charged tests or sufficiently much work to contradict an independently proved total budget. The amount forced must exceed the budget in the same scale and normalization. A power-sparse exceptional set still accommodates a single zero. A detector persistence interval must be compared with the exceptional measure at that interval's scale, rather than with an unrelated global density.

A partial result should state exactly which defects it excludes: a depth range, a height window, an attained terminal, or an escape class. Infinite-height escape must remain in the statement until an exhaustion theorem removes it. Classifying the escape module, or locating its spectral hull, is not an exclusion of that module for the actual source.

Polar neutrality does not turn this into the signed mass of the profile. **The field I would develop is arithmetic index descent.** This is a proposed organizing discipline extending the existing programmes, especially ALG, SSHA, RAPT, RBG and AES. It is not a claim that a wholly new field is logically necessary, or that the present names conceal no mathematics.

Its central question would be: **when do local arithmetic identities and positive reserves produce a controlled signature of the completed global pairing, without losing the source, the mixed signs or the limiting domain?** The distinctive object is a family of source-defined forms together with their transition costs. Neither an isolated positive Hilbert space nor an isolated arithmetic correspondence contains enough information.

Its point of view would be that positivity is a property to be transported with a budget. A local sign has mathematical value only together with the directions on which it holds, the boundary data it uses, the mixed pairings with other pieces, and the errors incurred when it is transferred. Index is the coarse invariant; signed reserve, polar dispersion and charged incidence are the quantities that can produce it.

The objects and language should be explicit:

| A completed form | Prime lags, archimedean kernel, diagonal normalization and both pole moments, with an exact identity or correctly directed comparison to `W` |
| A local certificate | A proved reserve or index bound, its domain, anchor and dependence on width, horizon and height |
| A transition between presentations | Pullback of forms, all mixed entries, boundary terms and a quantified discrepancy |
| A charged incidence structure | Dilation and successor/cell relations, with the action of prime gauges recorded |
| A renormalization | Regulator, divergent form, its rank and survival space, finite part, and correspondence anomaly |

The primitive words should distinguish “computed,” “bounded,” “selected,” “transported” and “excluded.” They should also distinguish a prime counterterm rank from a full-form negative index, and an asymptotic zero proportion from a bound on the number of exceptions. These are different mathematical resources.

The foundational principles would be the following.

1. **Identity before transfer.** Construct the object and identify its form before using its signature. A comparison must point in the direction needed for the conclusion.
2. **Global poles are shared resources.** Allocate the pole plane once. Local pieces cannot each spend the full global reserve.
3. **Mixed terms belong to the object.** Orthogonality, diagonal dominance and absolute values are deductions with costs, not default simplifications.
4. **Gauge selection and sign production are separate.** Source rigidity can identify the Euler point without controlling its energy. A quantitative charged estimate must bridge them.
5. **Renormalization carries its domain.** State what survives subtraction and why. Counterterm matching alone is not positivity of the finite part.
6. **Transfer preserves quantifiers.** One profile, all vectors, each fixed finite family, every horizon and every zero are different requirements. No averaging operation upgrades them for free.
7. **Use the weakest sufficient invariant.** A small index may be enough even when a uniform coercive floor is unavailable. Conversely, a finite index is not a zero index.
8. **An obstruction has a scope and a survivor.** A failed construction should yield an exact boundary, decomposition, rigidity law or replacement input before retirement.
9. **Endpoints are not producers.** An RH-equivalent condition is a receiver. Progress requires an unconditional estimate, construction or transfer feeding it.

A first foundational project would be a calculus of completed quadratic forms under arithmetic refinement. It would combine Schur complements, polar gluing and actual overlap Grams, and track the effect of refinement on inertia. Its first arithmetic theorem should recover the proved narrow-section result and extend it to a specified interacting family. Reproducing the abstract variance identity would not count as the extension.

A second project would study charged near-incidence forms: how the relations `n≈km`, successor cells and congruence stabilizers enter signed kernels. The key outputs would be inequalities at the Euler point, with finite-prime flips as calibration where they belong to the comparison class. The existing coefficient margin and cell-holonomy defect are inputs to this project, not its final estimate.

A third project would develop limits in the completed energy norm. It should quantify how a profile decomposition's absolute-coefficient error compares with its actual signed Gram norm, and which refinements preserve that comparison. This would connect fixed-class finite-part theorems to growing arithmetic spaces without assuming the desired uniformity. Escape to infinite height would be treated as a possible boundary component, not silently discarded.

A fourth project would develop arithmetic rigidity of Gram equality. IGP shows that equality in a scalar spectral estimate can demand an impossible configuration of kernel zeros. The next questions are which larger blocks, mixed windows and arithmetic relations force a macroscopic strict inequality, and which gains survive the finite-grid and prime-side transfers. This supplies an independent source of inequalities rather than a new description of positivity.

A fifth project would develop a quantitative relation between index and transverse work. Both have defect thresholds, but their normalizations, multiplicities and localization differ. A useful theorem would transport an arithmetic work budget to an index bound, or show that a negative direction forces a controlled amount of source work with all exterior interactions retained. It must give more than two separate RH equivalences.

A sixth project would study terminal boundary-to-interior estimates for the renormalized logarithmic derivative. The known lens, Harnack law and source residues provide the geometry and the computed quantities. The research problem is the missing upper estimate or propagation cost. Its tests should include finite off-line configurations, multiple charges, and unattained depth suprema.

The field would also need finite-field calibration. A valid arithmetic construction should recover the curve case from actual section or incidence structure and explain the bounded genus cost. The golden and complex fake data show why nonnegative counts and Frobenius-shaped identities alone are insufficient. Starting from already assumed purity is useful calibration of notation, but supplies no independent source of the Hodge sign.

Success criteria should be tied to explicit theorems:

- A larger arithmetic section family with a proved full-form lower or index bound.
- A signed mixed-term estimate whose constants survive repeated gluing.
- A source-defined finite part with a nontrivial controlled domain and a proved transfer to the intended tests.
- A signed surplus bound at the actual arithmetic point with a better certified zero proportion.
- A source-work bound controlling finite off-line support, followed by an extinction theorem for the remainder.
- Ultimately, a construction reaching the fixed-spline criterion or the window-energy criterion without inserting its conclusion as a hypothesis.

The selectivity tests should be applied to objects satisfying the construction's stated assumptions: the relevant sign twins, finite-prime flips, shifted pairs, planted quartets, pole-budget relaxations, finite-field fake data and escape models. A counterexample that violates the construction's pole or completion assumptions is not a refutation. A proposed mechanism that accepts a matching counterexample must explain what additional arithmetic property it will use.

I would give the signed-overlap/index problem primary attention, keep the explicit surplus problem as the main source of measurable analytic progress, and maintain transverse-work extinction as an independent route against exceptions. The advances already made suggest that a breakthrough is most likely to come from a new **joint arithmetic inequality** preserving information across a transfer. The report's proposed field is a way to construct and organize such inequalities. Its decisive contribution would be the first new payment theorem on the actual source, not the terminology.
