# RH research priorities

**Full-proof programme:** construct an arithmetic count for the Weil form on signed integer spline probes. The source already specifies the quantity the count must recover, including its mixed terms. Convergence along each fixed integer ray suffices. The decisive problem is to realize the renormalized *complete* form, including the reflection debit, as the growth of actual finite objects. Section 2.18 gives a staged programme and rejection tests. ## 2.4 A bounded question with consequences for the whole programme

**At three distinct coprime primary primes, does the normalized cubic-dilate residue factor into the three pair interactions, or is there a genuine three-prime term?** For two primes the source obtains the individual double pole by peeling the local coefficients before continuing. Relative to the product normalization the correction is (1+χ)/2 for χ=(p/q)₃. Its modulus is one when χ=1 and one half otherwise. In particular the naïve product of independent one-prime factors is already wrong. [Individual two-prime residue](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/OrderSixMetaplecticTheory/TheCompositeDilateCorrelationHasADoublePole.lean)

Fix three primes before any averaging. Predict the Laurent coefficient at 4/3 from their one-prime factors and three oriented pair symbols, making the reciprocity convention explicit. Compute the trace in two orders of peeling. If both give the pair product, test whether a compositional law survives a fourth prime. If they give a new term, isolate its phase and determine whether the signed family sees it. This question asks for a fixed-conductor local calculation, without any zero-free half-plane or growing-conductor error. A solution would decide whether the analytic programme should average pair characters or first develop a genuinely higher-order residue calculation. A fixed triple would not yet deliver that average.

## 2.6 Breakthrough question with positivity and spectral methods excluded

**Can the actual local prime–semiprime matching be extended across squarefree factor counts with a controlled number of unmatched integers?** In a sufficiently large dyadic block (X,2X], the corpus pairs every prime to a distinct semiprime in the same log-power cell, then pairs the unused squarefree semiprimes to distinct triprimes. The matched integers cancel exactly in the unweighted Möbius sum. The argument uses the small factors 2,3,5 and a strict surplus of eligible targets. The rest of the squarefree integers still contributes. [Prime-to-semiprime matching](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/RHTower/MobiusPrimeSemiprimeCellMatching.lean) · [Two-layer matching](../../../formal/GaloisForLFunctions/RHTower/MobiusSemiprimeTriprimeCellMatching.lean)

Seek disjoint opposite-parity pairs generated by specified changes of prime factorization. First allow a common squarefree cofactor and exchange a small prescribed group of primes for a group of opposite parity. Keep track of targets consumed in *all* earlier layers. The first genuinely informative test is to prove a common supply estimate as both the common cofactor and the number of prime factors grow. Exhibiting a deficient family of sources for these permitted moves would rule out that rule and identify the extra move needed. Pairing arbitrary positive and negative terms after computing their totals would put the desired cancellation into the construction.

The full theorem would bound the unmatched population by Oε(X^(1/2+ε)) in every dyadic block. Summing the block bounds gives the same estimate for M(x); partial summation gives convergence of the Möbius Dirichlet series for Re s>½, and its identity with 1/ζ on Re s>1 excludes zeros in that half-plane by continuation. The functional equation then gives RH. Even a power strictly below 20999/24000 for this particular matching would make a new strip available. This programme uses factorization, counting, sign reversal and ordinary complex continuation. It needs no quadratic-form inequality, operator model or spectral interpretation. ## 2.7 A physical phenomenon and a falsification test

**Hypothesis: critical relaxation of a pulse escaping a modular cusp.** The candidate phenomenon is a universal decay rate for late-time returns after a compact perturbation of the cusp. The mathematical identification is unusually precise: a strip ending at 1−2δ is equivalent to decay at every δ−ε, for all compact pulses and positive observation heights. The RH endpoint is ¼. This is a statement about the model field; the corpus does not establish an independent physical law selecting that rate. Prescribe the propagation law and observable first. Compare the modular cusp with a passive Euler twin that agrees on the chosen finite prime prefix. Predict a difference in the late return amplitude or in the repeated-return correlation, then prove the predicted bound for the true evolution. ## 2.8 A topological or geometric breakthrough question
