## 1. The picture in one page

**One kernel.** Every live route measures the Hecke orbit kernel of the Weil form,
\[
K_g(u)=W(g,\tau_{\log u}g)=\sum_\rho\omega_\rho(g)\,u^{\rho-1/2},\qquad u\in\mathbb Q_{>0},
\]
on the Hecke lattice \(\Gamma=\mathbb Q_{>0}\cong\bigoplus_p\mathbb Z\). **One invariant.** Its negative index is the number of distinct zeros with \(\operatorname{Re}\rho>\tfrac12\), even, and RH is \(\kappa\le1\) [S]. **One count.** \(\kappa=0\) iff \(\psi(x)-x\le C\sqrt x\log^2x\) eventually [S]; the count of the odd parity class \(\{\lambda(n)=-1\}\) alone suffices [S, AKL]. **One lattice (new).** The integrals of integer polynomials of degree \(\le n\) over \([0,1]\) form exactly the lattice \(\operatorname{lcm}(1,\dots,n+1)^{-1}\mathbb Z\), whose least positive element is \(e^{-\psi(n+1)}\) [S, ASRP]. So the count has a third face: RH iff for large \(n\) some integer polynomial has \(0<\int_0^1P\le e^{-(n+1)+C\sqrt{n+1}\log^2(n+1)}\) (the Siegel half, a lower bound for \(\psi\)) iff every integer polynomial with positive integral has \(\int_0^1P\ge e^{-(n+1)-C\sqrt{n+1}\log^2(n+1)}\) (the Liouville half, the upper bound, which is the HKT global law \(\kappa=0\)) [S]. Index zero of the Weil kernel, the one-sided prime count and a Liouville-type inequality for integrals of integer polynomials are the same statement in three languages, each equivalence formalized (inference from the three dictionaries).

**What is proved.** Receivers are complete and exact in every currency. Below RH the strata are exact: \([\text{open}]<[\ge\text{QRH}]<[\text{LH}]<[\text{FS}]<[\text{RH}]<[\text{RH}+]\), with finite off-line support Lindelöf-deep [S], and now with a fourth proof of that depth: a finite index is a count with finitely many exceptional waves [S, AKL]. The deciding input is characterized by theorem, not by principle: every property of weights that implies the count is parity-breaking, precision-faithful, aperture-unbounded, and has stabilizer inside the square-root class [S, ASRP]; the prime number theorem, Guth–Maynard's short-interval law and every error term weaker than square root are inherited by weights that fail the count [S]. Unconditional positivity of the actual complete Weil form is proved for one smooth near-indicator profile at one horizon for every overlap ratio \(0<c\le1\), with both poles and the boundary prime star retained [S, CACT]; at fixed \(c<1\) it is [L]. Selectivity, precision rigidity, the census at \(2/3+10^{-8}\), the three exchange rates to \(3/4\) and the shallow Cauchy energy bound \(E_{1/\log T}\le CT\log^2T\) stand as before [L].

**What is not proved.** No producer. No positivity at any overlap ratio \(c>1\). No certificate for a small positive integer-polynomial integral beyond the linear one, hence no zero-free strip. No upper count for the odd parity class. No displacement-sensitive bound at depth \(1/\log T\), hence no proportion beyond \(2/3+10^{-8}\). The touching chain is unaudited and unwired [S].

**The one sentence.** RH is a global sign of an assembly of local constituents none of which is positive, and no finite set of places can carry a finite nonzero index [S]; the information needed is parity-breaking and square-root precise; the only structures on \(\mathbb Z\) with both properties are integrality, which is now known to be lossless, the Euler product and functional equation as identities, and the successor; the precision is lost in the analytic ceiling that certifies an auxiliary and in Möbius inversion [S]; and the first place where additive structure enters the positivity currency is the overlap ratio one (inference, §3.1).

## 2. Anatomy of the obstruction

### 2.1 The deciding-input theorem

What the morning version called three necessary properties is now `deciding_input_necessary_properties` [search]: for any property \(S\) of weights \(a:\mathbb N\to\mathbb R\) with \(S(a)\Rightarrow\) Count\((a)\), unconditionally, \(S\) fails for the Liouville twin; no weight satisfies \(S\) together with \(a+\varepsilon n^{-\eta}\), \(0\le\eta<\tfrac12\); every finite window of every weight extends to a weight failing \(S\); and \(S(a)\wedge S(a+\delta)\) forces \(\delta\in\mathcal Q\), the class of perturbations with partial sums \(O(\sqrt x\log^2x)\). The battery is a classification theorem (`battery_classification`): visible moves are the Liouville twin and its cofinite tails, every mixture \((1-t)\Lambda+t\lambda\Lambda\) with \(t\ne0\), every scaling \(c\Lambda\) with \(c\ne1\), and \(\varepsilon n^{-\eta}\) with \(\eta<\tfrac12\); invisible moves, for which Count is again RH, are finite modifications, rescaling or deletion of finitely many Euler factors, and \(\varepsilon n^{-\eta}\) with \(\eta\ge\tfrac12\) [S]. The count therefore selects \(\Lambda\) only modulo \(\mathcal Q\); SMAP's selection theorem (positivity on an RH-complete family selects \(\lambda=\Lambda\) exactly) is a strictly finer statement about positivity, not about the count. The power threshold is sharp at \(\tfrac12\) inclusive. Each property retires a class: parity retires sieves, majorants, mean values, multiplicative positivity; precision retires density and short-interval statistics as sign inputs, now including Guth–Maynard's theorem itself [S]; aperture retires finite computation.

### 2.2 The gauge picture and the structure ladder

Multiplicative observation has the gauge torus \(G=(\mathbb R/\mathbb Z)^{\text{primes}}\) as symmetry group; square-root cancellation holds at Haar-almost every gauge point, fails at the Liouville point, and at the arithmetic point is the conjecture [L]. AKL sorts the battery by the level of arithmetic structure that excludes each member: **sign** (positive local coefficients) excludes the Liouville twin and the flips; **support** (an Euler product) excludes \(\Lambda+\varepsilon n^{-\eta}\) and Davenport–Heilbronn; **normalization** (pole and Gamma matched to the prime mass) excludes \(c\Lambda\); only the **ring** level (additive structure, \(\gcd(n,n+1)=1\), the curve's equation) excludes over-regular Beurling systems and the golden datum [S]. Kernel-level positivity of each local constituent sits below sign: every unitary twist of the Euler kernel is positive definite [S]. A producer must act at the ring level.

### 2.3 Depth homogeneity and the two places where precision is lost

Every amplification over \(\mathbb Z\) gains on depth as fast as it pays in length (AKT, ACG, RBG); Weil and Deligne break homogeneity with a term that does not scale, which \(\mathbb Z\) lacks [L]. ASRP adds the elementary form of the same fact. The Chebyshev transform \(T_\Lambda(n)=\sum_d\Lambda(d)\lfloor n/d\rfloor=\log n!\) is exact and identifies \(\Lambda\) (precision class \(\{0\}\)); it is known to precision \(\log n\) by Stirling; the count is its Möbius inversion; and inversion is unbounded at every precision: for every \(\theta\ge0\) and \(x\) there is an error \(|E(y)|\le y^\theta\) whose inversion \(\sum_{k\le x}\mu(k)E(x/k)\) is at least \(\pi(x)\) [S]. No size bound on the error of an identity transports through the inversion; only cancellation specific to the Stirling error can. The second loss is in the ceiling (§2.5).

### 2.4 The index theory: locality is a property of the spectrum

HKT states Walls 1, 3 and 14 as one object (index ceiling, index-carrying topology, producer of the count). AKL proves the local-to-global laws [S]. **Cyclic Hasse principle:** on every group a positive-definite kernel plus finitely many hyperbolic pairs is positive if positive on every cyclic subgroup; without torsion aliasing one cyclic subgroup carries the whole index. **Towers:** coordinate towers decide only tower-faithful spectra (a Krein–Langer kernel of index two on \(\mathbb Z^2\) vanishes on both axes); logarithmic spectra are tower-faithful, which is why two primes decide for \(\zeta\). **Fine towers:** the orbit kernel is continuous in the logarithmic scale, so the cyclic subgroups \(\langle(n+2)/(n+1)\rangle\), fine grids of \(\mathbb R\), decide every orbit kernel with any number of pairs; RH iff the \(\zeta\) kernel is positive on each of them (`riemannHypothesis_iff_zetaKernel_consecutive`) [search]. **Zero-or-infinity law:** an axis-supported kernel on \(\mathbb Z^S\), \(|S|\ge2\), has index \(0\) or \(\infty\); no finite set of places carries a finite nonzero index; finite index needs the off-axis constituent, which for \(\zeta\) is Gamma. **Wave formula:** along a separating tower the index is the least number of exponential waves whose subtraction leaves the count bounded, so every finite ceiling for \(\zeta\) proves Lindelöf. **Continuity:** the index is continuous under pointwise convergence of Krein–Langer data off the discriminant (vanishing weights, collisions, coalescence onto the line). **Compressions are receivers:** the characteristic polynomial of Frobenius annihilates the trace kernel regardless of purity; the Euler compressor \(\delta_1-c\delta_p\) with \(|c|\le p^{-1/2}\) is index-neutral; a producer must vanish at the primes, on the point side, as Stepanov's polynomial vanishes at rational points.

### 2.5 Integrality is lossless; the ceiling is the problem

The Stepanov–Nair mechanism over \(\mathbb Z\) was believed, in the morning version and in AET, to reach linear precision only. ASRP shows where the belief was wrong. The integrality step is exact: the least positive Nair integral in degree \(\le n\) is \(1/\operatorname{lcm}(1..n+1)\), attained, by Bézout over the horizon [S]. The Siegel half at scale \(\varphi\) (existence of \(P\) with \(0<\int P\le e^{-(n+1)+C\varphi(n+1)}\)) is exactly \(\psi(N)\ge N-C\varphi(N)\); the Liouville half (every positive \(\int P\ge e^{-(n+1)-C\varphi(n+1)}\)) is exactly \(\psi(N)\le N+C\varphi(N)\); at power scales both halves hold for \(\theta>\Theta\) and fail for \(\theta<\Theta\); \(\Theta<1\) iff the Siegel half holds at some power \(\theta<1\) [S]. Integer Dirichlet polynomials \(D(s)=\sum_{n\le N}a_nn^{-s}\), \(a_n\in\mathbb Z\), carry integrality at \(s\in\mathbb Z\) only: the values at \(s=m\ge1\) form the lattice \(\operatorname{lcm}(1..N)^{-m}\mathbb Z\) with the floor attained, \(D(-k)\in\mathbb Z\), a nonzero \(D\) has fewer than \(N\) real zeros and is \(\ge1\) in modulus at some \(s\in\{0,-1,\dots,-(N-1)\}\), and Siegel's lemma supplies small \(D\) vanishing on the trivial lattice [S]. Integrality never speaks at a nontrivial zero. What is missing is a *certificate*: a way to exhibit \(P\) with a small positive integral whose smallness is verified without knowing \(\psi\). The classical certificates go through \(\int|P|\) or \(\sup|P|\), and those are capped (inference, constants cited from memory): the integer Chebyshev constant of \([0,1]\) is about \(0.42>1/e\), so \(\sup_{[0,1]}|P|\ge0.42^n\) for every nonzero integer \(P\) of degree \(n\), and an absolute ceiling can never certify \(\int P\le e^{-n}\); Nair's \(x^m(1-x)^m\) and the Gorshkov–Montgomery polynomials give \(\psi(n)\ge cn\) with \(c<1\) and nothing more. The precision-preserving ceiling must use the sign changes of \(P\) on \([0,1]\), that is, cancellation in \(\int P\) itself.

### 2.6 What all unconditional positivity knows, through touching

The morning statement stands: the information content of every unconditional positivity theorem below the Metzler threshold is \(|\lambda|\le\Lambda\) plus one bit, the carrier of the first negative square of the atomic kernel \(D\delta_1-P\), with margin \(2\log2\) set by conductor two [S]. The touching theorem [S, CACT] adds the following exact anatomy for \(0<c\le1\). At the touching ratio the only non-ratio prime lag is the boundary star: for atoms supported in \([0,1/N]\),
\[
\operatorname{NR}(g,N,x)=2G_g(d_N)\,x_N\sum_{\substack{km=N+1\\k\ge2,\ m\le N}}\frac{\Lambda(k)}{\sqrt k}\,x_m,\qquad d_N=\log\tfrac{N+1}{N},
\]
normalized \(O_\varphi(\log N/N)\) uniformly for \(c\le1\); every other prime entry is a ratio lag \(\Lambda(j/n)\) with \(n\mid j\) [S]. The complete matrix is exactly \((\log N+F_\varphi(c)-\gamma+o(1))I-P_N+\) center \(-\) cross error \(+\) pole error \(-\) non-ratio, the cross error is \(\le4C_\varphi c^2\|x\|^2\) because distinct integer logarithms are more than \(1/N\) apart, the strict charge \(q_\varphi=(\int\varphi)^2/\int\varphi^2<1\) leaves a reserve \(\tfrac{1-q_\varphi}4\cdot\)poleDefect, and finite Ramanujan coordinate detection with width-uniform norm quasimodes excludes every eigenvalue below the conductor-one energy \(B_\varphi/M+\alpha_\varphi-\log2\pi\to2-\log2\pi>0\) [S]. (inference) Through touching the form is therefore read on multiplicative data only (ratio lags, the Ramanujan spectrum, the pole), and the proof uses no sign of \(\Lambda\) at a non-ratio lag. Whether the theorem survives the Liouville twin is not in the corpus; it is exactly ASRP's open topic "precision of positivity", and the answer decides whether the regime \(c\le1\) carries any arithmetic bit beyond the margin (§7, question 1).

### 2.7 The complete source: additive debt against multiplicative reserve

On the Haar sections of the complete logarithmic cells the Gamma contribution is now exact [S]: with \(u_a=\sqrt{2a+1}\,d_a\),
\[
\Gamma=\sum_a\operatorname{row}_a u_a^2-\tfrac12\sum_{a,b}K_{ab}(u_a-u_b)^2,\qquad K_{ab}\ge0,
\]
a row potential minus a nonnegative additive graph energy (positive Laplace representation of every cross term, including touching cells). The arithmetic side is exact too [L]: in valuation coordinates \(x_a=d_a/\sqrt{2a+1}\), \(D_H+B=\tfrac12\sum_e\text{occupation}_e+R\) with \(B,R\ge0\), the occupation energy running over the product basis of the source's primes. The open sign is the comparison of a graph Laplacian on the cell index (additive) with an occupation energy on the prime lattice (multiplicative), with the outgoing cost \(B\) and the first-prime, coarse and polar terms retained. (inference) This is the same shape as the parity obstruction: the debt is additive, the reserve is multiplicative. On the generator side the even-power head error is \(\le\varepsilon(\Gamma(h)+\Gamma(t))+C_\varepsilon\Gamma(f)/(1+\log A)\) [K], and the missing input is \(\Gamma(h)+\Gamma(t)\le K\Gamma(f)\) uniformly [S].

## 3. Question 1 — mechanisms that circumvent or destroy a wall

Ranked by how much of a wall they would remove and by how far the corpus supports them. Two of the morning's mechanisms have been absorbed by the corpus (see §3.3 and §3.4) and one has been downgraded (§3.5).

### 3.1 Cross the Metzler threshold: the first additive term in the positivity currency (the frontier mechanism)

The exact frontier is now \(c=1\). Below and at it, positivity is proved with a trickle-down or compactness argument [L]/[S]; above it, for every \(c>1\), the atoms \(N-1,N,1\) form an odd triangle and no diagonal unitary gauge makes the Markov form Metzler [S, ALG]; and at unbounded \(c\) positivity is RH [L]. What enters at \(c>1\) is exact (inference from `exists_nonRatio_pair` and the touching decomposition): a non-ratio prime lag between atoms \(n\) and \(j\) at ratio \(c\) requires \(|j-kn|\le c\) for some \(k\ge2\) with \(\Lambda(k)\ne0\); at \(c=1\) the only such pair inside the horizon is the boundary star \(N,N+1\); for \(1<c<2\) every pair \((kn,kn\pm1)\) with both members \(\le N\) enters, weighted \(\Lambda(k)/\sqrt k\) and the overlap \(G_g\) at lag \(\log(1\pm1/kn)\). The first term beyond touching is therefore the correlation of the multiplicative lattice with its unit translates, \(\sum_k\frac{\Lambda(k)}{\sqrt k}\sum_n x_n x_{kn\pm1}G(\cdot)\): the successor, at every scale, inside the Weil form. This is the ring level at which AKL says a producer must act, and it is where the gauge enters (SSHA). The mechanism to find is a cancellation mechanism for this signed successor correlation against the diagonal reserve \(\log N+F_\varphi(c)-\gamma\) and the charge reserve; the Metzler gauge is excluded, so it must be either a genuine Schur-complement argument with one reserve (CACT's "one reserve for interacting directions"), or an arithmetic estimate of the successor correlation on average over \(n\) with the atom profile as weight. What it buys: the first positivity theorem on a frustrated family and the first arithmetic bit beyond the margin; what it does not buy: a fixed \(c>1\) is still far below finite off-line support, since one zero weighs at most \(O(c)\) times the diagonal [L]. The breakthrough briefing (`breakthrough-question.md`) states the question with its sub-questions; its part Q0 is now answered [S].

### 3.2 The precision-preserving ceiling (proposal; ASRP open topic 1)

Produce, for large \(n\), integer polynomials \(P_n\) of degree \(\le n\) with \(0<\int_0^1P_n\le e^{-(n+1)+C(n+1)^\theta}\) for some \(\theta<1\), certified without using \(\psi\). By `zeroReSup_lt_one_iff_siegelHalf` this is a zero-free strip; at \(\theta=\tfrac12\) with logarithms it is RH [S]. The absolute ceilings are capped by the integer Chebyshev constant (§2.5), so the certificate must exploit sign changes of \(P\). Over \(\mathbb F_q\) the ceiling is a degree bound (number of zeros \(\le\) degree) and the auxiliary is built by linear algebra on vanishing conditions; over \(\mathbb Z\) Siegel's lemma builds \(D\) vanishing at \(s=0,-1,\dots,-(M-1)\) with height \((N^M)^{M/(N-M)}\) [S], and the open step is a ceiling for \(\int_0^1P=D_P(1)\) (the value at the pole) from such vanishing data. Two directions: (a) quadrature: if \(P\) vanishes to high order at the nodes of a positive quadrature rule exact to degree \(n\), then \(\int P\) is a short signed sum controlled by the remainder, not by \(\sup|P|\); the nodes are algebraic, so the vanishing conditions are integer conditions on the coefficients through the minimal polynomials, and the count of conditions against the degree is the ceiling; (b) the Liouville half as a transcendence measure: every positive \(\int P\) is at least \(e^{-(n+1)-C\varphi}\) is a lower bound for a nonzero integer combination of the numbers \(1/(k+1)\), which is exactly the Bézout floor and is attained, so the Liouville half cannot be improved by analysis, only read off; the content is entirely in the Siegel half. The test battery is built in: the construction must fail on the golden datum (no integer-count structure) and for \(\lambda\Lambda\), whose horizon value is a reciprocal torsion [S]. The no-go version is also valuable: a theorem that every ceiling of a stated analytic form (absolute, or one-signed) is linear with constant \(<1\) would be the first precision no-go with an explicit survivor (the Bézout floor).

### 3.3 Index ceilings from local data (status; AKL)

AKL has made the index-ceiling problem precise and smaller. No finite set of places carries a finite nonzero index, so any ceiling for \(\zeta\) must use the Gamma constituent; every finite ceiling is Lindelöf-strength; and the structure theorems are in place (wave formula, continuity, Hasse principles). The remaining structural question is coarse: prime-tower locality for kernels with infinitely many pairs (the fine towers \(\langle(n+2)/(n+1)\rangle\) decide, the prime towers alias). This is the one place where the morning's proposal survives: Naimark's theorem on commuting \(\pi\)-unitaries in a Pontryagin space (cited from memory, hypotheses unverified) is the natural tool for the abstract problem, but the arithmetic case no longer needs it. I no longer rank this as a lever; the lever is the ceiling itself, and AKL's first-evidence criteria are the right ones: a ceiling for truncated Weil kernels from prime-side data that fails for the golden datum.

### 3.4 Baker-type one-prime certification (status; ASRP open topic 6)

Unchanged in content, now listed by ASRP as an open topic: the corpus holds a formalized Baker-type theorem on linear forms in logarithms (`BakerGeneralProof.false_of_long_extrapolation`, qualitative) [search]; the quantitative form would turn "two primes carry the index" into "one explicit prime certifies every right zero of height \(\le T\)", a Wall-3 locality statement. It carries no sign.

### 3.5 The Hecke defect at logarithmic translation (downgraded)

The morning version proposed the Hecke defect \(E_f(L)=\sum_\rho m_\rho(2\cosh(2\delta_\rho L)-2)|\hat f(\rho)|^2\) at \(L=c\log T\) as the displacement statistic for the census, with its theta-prefix computation [search]. Two facts reduce it (inference). With the formalized Selberg density, at most \(C(N e^{-\alpha/28}/\alpha+\log^2T)\) zeros in \([T,2T]\) have depth \(\ge\alpha/\log T\) [L, SCP], and the weight is \(\le e^{2c\alpha}\), so for a height-localized test \(E_f(c\log T)=O(N)\) for \(c<1/56\) (for \(c<1/8\) with Selberg's classical slope) follows from density alone: an upper bound there carries no information beyond density. Zeros with \(\eta\to0\), the ones the dichotomy cannot exclude, are weighted \(4c^2\eta^2\) and are invisible. The defect is a displacement-sensitive statistic, but its arithmetic upper bound is a door only at \(c\) beyond the density range or with a constant-precise bound, both of which are the same parity-level estimate as the prime-pair surplus. It remains the correct *currency* for stating the displacement problem, not a mechanism.

## 4. Question 2 — approaches different from the existing routes

Every live route is a quadratic form whose positivity is RH. A different approach must get its sign from a source other than a form's definition, or must prove a count directly. I grade five directions.

### 4.1 Transcendence-method arithmetic, re-aimed (proposal)

The morning version proposed auxiliary Dirichlet polynomials with integer coefficients and zero estimates at the points \(\rho-\tfrac12\). ASRP and AKL have removed that aim: integrality speaks only at \(s\in\mathbb Z\) [S], and any compressor vanishing at zero characters is a receiver [S]. The template survives with its vanishing conditions moved to the point side. The objects are integer polynomials on \([0,1]\) (equivalently integer Dirichlet polynomials evaluated at the pole, since \(\int_0^1P=D_P(1)\)) and the target is the Siegel half (§3.2). The analogy with Stepanov is now exact in the corpus's own terms: both methods have an integrality floor that is lossless (over \(\mathbb F_q\): a nonzero polynomial value is a nonzero field element; over \(\mathbb Z\): the Bézout lattice) and both need a ceiling; Stepanov's ceiling is the degree, and Frobenius flatness makes the number of vanishing conditions independent of the size of the counted set. Over \(\mathbb Z\) nothing is flat, so the ceiling must come from cancellation. Concrete first problems: (a) the signed integer Chebyshev problem: the asymptotics of \(\min\{\int_0^1P>0\}\) is known exactly, so study instead the smallest positive integral *certifiable* by a stated class of arguments, and prove the first no-go for the absolute class; (b) quadrature-based certificates (§3.2a); (c) the first rung named by AKL, beating the sieve's parity constant \(2\) on intervals of length \(x^\varepsilon\) by an integrality argument, a statement far below a strip but already parity-breaking. Risk: a certificate that works may be one that encodes \(\psi\) through the back door (the lattice is \(\operatorname{lcm}^{-1}\mathbb Z\), so any exhibited small positive integral is a lower bound for \(\psi\); the question is whether the exhibition can be verified independently).

### 4.2 Exactness of the integer count: the successor as a precision source (direction; ASRP open topic 5, AKL ring level)

Over-regular Beurling systems have integer counts at square-root precision and prime counts without a zero-free strip; the golden datum has positivity, an Euler product and the right normalization. Both pass every level of the structure ladder except the ring level [S]. What \(\mathbb Z\) has and they lack is an exact identity: \(N(x)=\lfloor x\rfloor\) exactly, hence \(T_\Lambda(n)=\log n!\) exactly (precision class \(\{0\}\)) [S]. The question is which consequence of exactness a proof can use. Candidates in the corpus's language: the integrality lattice itself (Beurling systems have no lcm); the reciprocal-torsion identity for the twin; Bézout-selective orientation laws (AOG, open). A theorem of the form "statement \(X\) about \(\Lambda\) holds for \(\mathbb Z\) and fails for every Beurling system with \(N(x)=x+O(\sqrt x)\)" would be the first precision-faithful, parity-breaking input of ring type, whatever \(X\) is. This is the most different direction available: it treats the integers as a ring rather than as a multiplicative semigroup with a measure.

### 4.3 Reflection positivity as a construction (direction; unchanged)

RH is reflection positivity of the Cayley–Li ladder [L]. The open target is a source-defined ferromagnetic system (couplings from \(\Lambda\), functional equation as reflection) whose partition function converges to Riemann's measure; the Bost–Connes system with \(\beta\mapsto3-\beta\) is the natural candidate (ALG). The battery test: the Liouville twin must fail to be ferromagnetic. Every attempted system so far has landed in a receiver.

### 4.4 A covariant regulator on the characteristic circle (direction; unchanged)

Torsion orbits on \(\mathbb G_m/\mathbb Z\) carry integer intersection numbers and the local Hodge index on every \(p\)-ladder; the global sign fails because the regularization of the diagonal is not covariant (Hecke cocycle \(\log t-\sum\log p/(p-1)\)) [L]. A covariant regulator with nontrivial null cone is the most geometric unsolved problem in the corpus; ASRP lists its precision class as open.

### 4.5 A logical observation on families (not a route; unchanged)

A zero-free strip for all Dirichlet characters modulo \(q\) including \(\chi_0\) is a strip for \(\zeta\). The external claim \(1/8<\operatorname{Re}\rho<7/8\) [E] either excludes the principal character or concerns almost all characters; the first check in extracting its mechanism (W08) is whether the argument survives \(\chi=\chi_0\).

## 5. Question 3 — quasi-RH with improved values

**Facts.** No zero-free strip \(\Theta<1\) is proved, in the corpus or the literature; the best unconditional region is Vinogradov–Korobov, formalized with an explicit constant [search]. The interval \(1/8<\operatorname{Re}\rho<7/8\) is an external claim about Dirichlet \(L\)-functions [E]; the intake has detector and density components and no capstone [search]. The corpus's strip dictionaries are exact and now include a new currency: \(\Theta<1\) iff a balanced short-window Möbius or prime power saving [L], iff the growing-endpoint escaped Möbius law [L], iff \(M(x)\ll x^{1-\delta}\), **iff the Siegel half holds at some power \(\theta<1\): some integer polynomial of degree \(\le n\) has \(0<\int_0^1P\le e^{-(n+1)+C(n+1)^\theta}\) for all large \(n\)** [S]. Density envelopes never force a strip [L]; a strip is precision-faithful only at level \(1-\Theta\), so every statement surviving \(\varepsilon n^{-\eta}\) with \(\eta<1-\Theta\) cannot give it [S].

**What produces strips.** Only multiplicative positivity plus growth has ever produced a zero-free region; MPG gives the exact width law and the Fejér barrier [L]; a fixed strip from it needs a growth input of Lindelöf strength on a line inside the strip. The afternoon adds the integrality mirror: the Chebyshev–Nair method is stuck not at integrality but at the ceiling, and the absolute ceilings are capped below linear precision by the integer Chebyshev constant (§2.5). So the two classical producers fail for dual reasons: positivity lacks a \(\delta\)-dependent growth input; integrality lacks a sign-sensitive ceiling.

**Assessment.** There is no opportunity in the corpus to prove any fixed zero-free strip for \(\zeta\) with present tools. The realistic work: (a) finish the W08 extraction and run the \(\chi_0\) test; (b) the signed integer Chebyshev problem (§3.2), whose first deliverable is a no-go for absolute ceilings and whose positive form is a strip; (c) state the VK step as a range-limited growth inequality and ask which \(\delta\)-dependent input would move it. Improved quasi-RH values from positivity alone, or from density, remain excluded by theorem.

## 6. Question 4 — three quarters of the zeros on the line

**What is exact [L].** (a) Prime surplus \(\le(1/18+o(1))N\) at bandwidth \(4/3\) gives \(75\%\) simple critical zeros; Hardy–Littlewood predicts \(-1/144\), Poisson without repulsion gives \(+1/16\) and fails. (b) Zero-side slack \(\ge N/12\) at \(\lambda\in[3/4,1)\) is supplied by displaced pairs iff \(\eta\ge1/6\); the slack hypothesis is false for every \(\lambda<3/4\). (c) `PairCorrelationFourThirds` gives \(3/4\) simple and on the line [search]. The dichotomy: \(3/4\) critical in every dyadic block, or at least \((1/6-\varepsilon)N\) simple off-line zeros there [search]. The intermediate rung \(70\%\) follows from \(E(1/9)\le4\) [L]. Shallow off-line zeros have \(\sum m_j^2\le CT\log T\) and bounded clustering at scale \(K/\log T\) [L, SCP].

**Why each is blocked.** (a) is a signed coalescent prime-pair correlation at shifts \(h\le T^{1/3}\) whose budget lives in the Hardy–Littlewood repulsion term; one-sided sieves lose the height phase, positive majorants give \(8/27\) against \(1/9\), higher moments force \(k\lambda\le1\) [L]: the parity problem at square-root precision. (b) needs a displacement-sensitive lower bound at depth \(1/\log T\); value moments are depth-blind [L]; the Hecke defect is density-controlled at small translation (§3.5). (c) is Montgomery beyond \(\alpha=1\). The dichotomy shows the whole obstruction is the possible presence of \(N/6\) simple zeros at depth \(\lesssim1/\log T\), which no density theorem and no energy bound excludes; the shallow Cauchy bound is sharp enough to be consistent with exactly that configuration.

**What can be attempted.** (i) The exact value of the Gram pinching slack (IGP): a pure Gram-geometry computation, the only route to a larger unconditional proportion that needs no arithmetic; it will not reach \(3/4\). (ii) The centred stabilizer discrepancy (parity-locked Ramanujan stabilizer, TTPC): a two-sided estimate on average over even shifts is the cleanest additive statement in the corpus and CACT's named target. (iii) Nothing in the touching chain or in ASRP touches the census: positivity at \(c\le1\) is a statement about the form, not about zero locations, and the count statements are global.

**Assessment.** \(75\%\) is exactly as hard as a square-root-precise two-sided prime-pair estimate at bandwidth \(4/3\), equivalently a displacement-sensitive zero-density bound at depth \(1/\log T\). \(70\%\) has the same obstruction with a smaller constant. Not reachable with current tools.

## 7. Question 5 — three open questions to focus on

1. **Cross the Metzler threshold.** Prove that for some fixed overlap ratio \(c>1\) and some profile \(\varphi\), the complete Weil matrix \(\mathcal W_{\varphi,c,N}\) is eventually positive definite; locate the ratio at which the zeros first enter; and determine the precision class of the touching theorem (does positivity at \(c\le1\) survive the Liouville twin?). Why: it is the exact frontier [S], the first term beyond it is the successor correlation inside the Weil form (§3.1), the Metzler gauge is excluded by theorem so a new mechanism is forced, a single zero weighs \(O(c)\) so the statement is far below RH, and the twin question decides whether the whole regime \(c\le1\) was dictionary or arithmetic. Expected first outcome: either the first frustrated positivity theorem or a proof that fixed \(c>1\) positivity is finite-support-equivalent, a new no-go with the touching theorem as survivor. The briefing is `breakthrough-question.md`; its Q0 is done.

2. **The precision-preserving ceiling.** Decide the signed integer Chebyshev problem: which ceilings for \(\int_0^1P\), \(P\in\mathbb Z[x]\), certify precision beyond linear. First deliverable, a no-go: every absolute or one-signed ceiling is linear with constant \(<1\) (the integer Chebyshev constant), with the Bézout floor as positive survivor. Second deliverable, the positive form at any \(\theta<1\): a zero-free strip in a currency where the object is an integer polynomial and the obstruction is a classical extremal problem, not a sign. Why: ASRP has shown the integrality step is lossless and that this is the only place in the Chebyshev–Nair route where precision is lost; it is the one producer-shaped question in the corpus whose unknown is a construction rather than an inequality about zeros; and its battery is automatic (golden datum, reciprocal torsion of the twin).

3. **Displacement at depth \(1/\log T\), in the currency of the centred stabilizer discrepancy.** Prove a two-sided estimate for the coalescent prime-pair discrepancy from its Ramanujan model, on average over even shifts \(h\le T^{1/3}\), with the logarithmic weights and end corrections of the census; or prove that every such estimate obtainable from density inputs is bounded away from \(1/18\). Why: it is the only open estimate whose partial results are proportions (\(70\%\), \(75\%\)) rather than nothing; its obstruction is exactly the parity problem at square-root precision, so any success is a producer; and the Hecke-defect reformulation (§3.5) shows that the zero-side currencies are density-controlled, leaving the prime side as the only side with an arithmetic constant.

The index ceiling with the Lindelöf test (the morning's first question) is subsumed: AKL has proved the structure, shown that a ceiling needs the Gamma constituent and is Lindelöf-strength, and set its first-evidence criteria; it continues as AKL's own programme. The Hecke defect and the Naimark route are retired as focus items.

## 8. Question 6 — the field that supplies the tools: assessment of the start

The morning version proposed *arithmetic of square-root precision* as the field: arithmetic quantities that are integers or log-orders of finite groups, the identities constraining them, and the methods extracting square-root-precise estimates from them; positivity last, after index and count; every statement named with the twin, the perturbation and the gauge it fails for. The field now exists as three programmes with distinct roles: **AKL** (index: local-to-global), **ASRP** (precision: classes, integrality, where precision is lost), **CACT** (estimates: signed comparison at the critical scale), on the foundations of HKT and ACS.

**What the field has already delivered** [S]. The deciding-input theorem and the battery classification (the principle became a theorem). The exact integrality lattice and the attained floor. The integrality exponent equals the zero width, in each half; a strip is integrality at a fixed precision below one. Integer Dirichlet polynomials speak only at integer points. The Liouville twin is a reciprocal torsion. The Chebyshev ledger: identity exact, Stirling precision \(\log n\), inversion unbounded. On the index side: Hasse principles, the zero-or-infinity law, the wave formula, continuity off the discriminant, compressions are receivers, the parity form of the global law, the twist tower over \(\mathbb F_q\). On the estimate side: positivity through touching, the logarithmic ceiling for completed source work, square-root-horizon Ramanujan frames. Each of these is a theorem about the *precision* or the *locality* of a statement, not another equivalence; that is the field doing what it was proposed to do.

**What it has corrected in its own founding document.** The limit of integrality is not linear (the ceiling is the obstruction); the structural half of Wall 3 needs continuity, not a fixed-point theorem; auxiliary functions must vanish at primes and integers, not at zeros.

**Foundational theorems still to establish**, in order of leverage.
- The precision-preserving ceiling, or its no-go for absolute ceilings (§3.2, §7.2).
- The precision class of the touching theorem and of every unconditional positivity theorem in the corpus: which twins survive at which ratio; the first theorem that fails for the Liouville twin at a fixed ratio is the first arithmetic bit beyond the margin (§2.6, §7.1).
- The precision class of a finite index on two towers (Lindelöf as the test) and of the Hecke defect at logarithmic translation (density as the test).
- The ring-level input: an identity of \(\mathbb Z\) (exact integer count, lcm, Bézout) that fails for over-regular Beurling systems and the golden datum, in the form of a statement about \(\Lambda\) (§4.2); AKL's first rung, beating the sieve constant \(2\) at length \(x^\varepsilon\).
- Prime-tower locality for infinite spectra (the coarse-tower problem), and one-prime certification in height by quantitative Baker.
- The successor correlation inside the Weil form at \(1<c<2\) as an object in its own right: its exact decomposition, its Ramanujan model, and its centred discrepancy (§3.1), joining the census discrepancy (§7.3) as the two faces of one additive–multiplicative comparison.

**Criteria a contribution must meet** (now the field's own, from AKL and ASRP): unconditional; built from arithmetic data, never from zero-indexed objects; keeps Gamma, both poles, every prime power and all mixed entries; states its precision class and its parity behaviour; says what it gives for the battery; separates identity, floor, ceiling and inversion, and names the step where precision is kept or lost; names the level of the structure ladder at which it acts; calibrates over \(\mathbb F_q\) (Weil by counting, golden datum by precision exponent); records the positive survivor of every no-go.

**Principles**, as the programmes now state them. Precision is a class, not a constant. Identities are exact; precision is lost in extraction. A statement that survives a visible move cannot decide. Integrality is exact at the floor; the ceiling is the problem. Locality is a property of the spectrum; two primes decide because the spectrum is logarithmic. No finite set of places carries a finite nonzero index. The index is the number of exceptional waves; finite index is Lindelöf-deep. A positive part never pays a pair. Index, then count, then sign. Calibrate over \(\mathbb F_q\).

**What the field is not.** It is not another route and it has not produced a producer. Its success criterion is unchanged: one unconditional inequality about \(\zeta\) whose precision class lies in \(\mathcal Q\), which fails the battery, and which lands above \([\text{open}]\): first a fixed strip (integrality at precision below one, or positivity at a ratio where the twin fails), then the count.

## 9. Closing assessment

The programme has an exact theory of what RH is equivalent to, an exact theory of what cannot prove it, and now the beginning of an exact theory of *why*: the deciding input is characterized by theorem; locality is a property of the spectrum; integrality over \(\mathbb Z\) is lossless and the loss is in the certificate; the first additive term enters the positivity currency at overlap ratio one. The next move requires the first signed estimate of an additive correlation inside the Weil form, or the first certificate of a small positive integer-polynomial integral by cancellation. Both are parity problems at square-root precision, now stated as concrete objects rather than as walls. The honest expectation is that the next real result is a no-go of a new kind, for absolute ceilings or for fixed-ratio positivity, with a positive survivor, rather than a sign; the honest hope is that the successor correlation at \(1<c<2\), being finite, explicit and computable, is the first place where an arithmetic cancellation can be seen before it is proved.
