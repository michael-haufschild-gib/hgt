## 1. The picture in one page

**One kernel.** Every one of the 31 live routes and 17 candidates measures the same object: the Hecke orbit kernel of the Weil form,
\[
K_g(u)=W(g,\tau_{\log u}g)=\sum_\rho \omega_\rho(g)\,u^{\rho-1/2},\qquad u\in\mathbb Q_{>0},
\]
a Hermitian kernel on the Hecke lattice \(\Gamma=\mathbb Q_{>0}\cong\bigoplus_p\mathbb Z\). On-line zeros are unitary characters of \(\Gamma\) with nonnegative weight; each off-line zero and its reflection form a hyperbolic pair. **One invariant.** Its negative index \(\kappa(K_\zeta)\) equals the number of distinct zeros with \(\operatorname{Re}\rho>\tfrac12\), is even, and RH is \(\kappa\le1\) [S]. **One count.** \(\kappa(K_\zeta)=0\) iff \(\psi(x)-x\le C\sqrt x\log^2x\) eventually; for curve data over \(\mathbb F_q\) the same theorem reads \(\kappa=0\) iff the one-sided square-root point count, which Stepanov produces [S]. The entire programme has reduced RH to producing that count over \(\mathbb Z\).

**What is proved.** Receivers (RH-equivalent reformulations) are complete and exact in every currency: width, damping, aperture, lag, index, effectivity, heat time, collision mass, Báez–Duarte distance, Li coefficients, recurrence, escape visibility. Dictionaries between currencies are exact. Below RH the strata are exact: \([\text{open}]<[\ge\text{QRH}]<[\text{LH}]<[\text{FS}]<[\text{RH}]<[\text{RH}+]\), with finite off-line support identified as RH above a height, hence Lindelöf-deep [S]. Unconditional positivity is proved exactly where it is twin-blind: aperture \(181/500\) (prime 2 only), atomic tests at a fixed fraction \(c_0\delta_N\) of the spacing, narrow windows to a quarter of the Robin–Euler threshold, the separated-regime complete-Weil spectrum with its Ramanujan eigenlines [L/S]. Selectivity is a theorem: the margin \(2\log2\) on the harmonic hyperplane separates \(\Lambda\) from Liouville, is attained by every single-prime flip \(p\ge11\), and does not select \(\zeta\) [L]. Precision rigidity is a theorem: \(\Lambda\) and \(\Lambda+\varepsilon n^{-\eta}\), \(\eta<\tfrac12\), are never both tower-positive [L]. The census stands at simple critical zeros \(\ge0.67250071\), simple-or-critical \(\ge0.8876\), and three exact exchange rates to \(3/4\) [L].

**What is not proved.** No producer: no zero-free strip \(\Theta<1\), no power saving in any Möbius or prime short-window sum, no index ceiling, no displacement-sensitive zero-density bound at depth \(1/\log T\), no proportion beyond \(2/3+10^{-8}\). The corpus has formalized the classical inputs (Vinogradov–Korobov, Guth–Maynard \(30/13\) and \(17/30\), Montgomery's \(F(\alpha)\) on \(0<\alpha<1\), de Bruijn–Newman \(\Lambda\ge0\), Weil's Kloosterman bound, Stepanov over \(\mathbb F_q\), a Baker-type theorem on linear forms in logarithms) [search], and has shown by theorem that none of them, nor any statement stable under relative error \(o(1)\), can decide the sign.

**The one sentence.** The problem has been located, not moved: RH is a global sign of an assembly of local constituents none of which is positive; every mechanism that respects multiplicativity sees only gauge orbits; the information needed is parity-breaking and square-root precise; and the only structures on \(\mathbb Z\) with both properties are integrality, the Euler product and functional equation used as identities, and the successor.

## 2. Anatomy of the obstruction

### 2.1 Three necessary properties of any deciding input

The corpus has turned the "walls" into theorems about what an input must do. (i) **Parity-breaking**: it must fail for the Liouville twin \(\lambda\Lambda\), which shares every multiplicative identity, every complete-period quadratic datum, every majorant and every gauge-invariant correlation (BVI, THIM, ACG). (ii) **Square-root precise**: it must fail for \(\Lambda+\varepsilon n^{-\eta}\), \(\eta<\tfrac12\), so no short-interval prime theorem with relative error \(o(1)\) (Guth–Maynard included) can supply it (SMAP, HKT). (iii) **Unbounded aperture**: every bounded-aperture certificate has width limit \(\tfrac12\) (SSIC); one-prime tower positivity is RH at every generator size (ACS, SMAP); the finite-aperture ground energy \(\mu(A)\) has a doubly exponential ceiling (ACT).

Each property retires a class: (i) retires sieves, majorants, mean values, multiplicative positivity (boundary only, MPG); (ii) retires density and short-interval statistics as sign inputs; (iii) retires finite computation as a proof strategy.

### 2.2 The gauge picture

Multiplicative observation has a symmetry group, the gauge torus \(G=(\mathbb R/\mathbb Z)^{\text{primes}}\). Multiplicative data determine a source up to its orbit; additive relations are gauge-charged. Square-root cancellation (RH, Chowla) holds at Haar-almost every gauge point, fails at the Liouville point, and at the arithmetic point \(0\) is the conjecture; the exceptional set is null and comeager [L] (ACG). Heights trace one line in \(G\); the square-root threshold \(X\asymp T\) is the price of that line. The archimedean place is the only glue between the prime towers: dilation boundaries tend to \(\log a\) exactly at \(\sigma=\tfrac12\) [L].

### 2.3 Depth homogeneity and the missing non-scaling term

Every amplification over \(\mathbb Z\) (tensor powers, moments, family averages over \(\chi\bmod q\), harmonic twists, dyadic amplification) gains on depth exactly as fast as it pays in arithmetic length (AKT, ACG, RBG). Weil's and Deligne's proofs break homogeneity with a term that does not scale: the fixed budget \(\tau(0)=2g\) of a correspondence, and the fixed base loss of a tensor power. Over \(\mathbb Z\) the genus is infinite, the only families definable from \(\zeta\) are abelian twists (moments), and a fixed tensor loss is RH itself [L]. The scaling surface has Weil's identities (i)–(iv) exactly and lacks (v), Riemann–Roch: effective cones are tautological, the base theta \(h^0\) has linear RR on every surface and quadratic RR on none, and a surface \(h^0\) must have the size of a dimension of length-\(N\) Dirichlet polynomials (AET).

### 2.4 The index-theoretic unification (the novelty of this review period)

HKT states Walls 1, 3 and 14 as one object: Wall 1 is a selective index ceiling, Wall 3 is an index-carrying topology (bounded index is closed under pointwise kernel convergence, not under \(L^2\) convergence of tests), Wall 14 is a producer of the count. Proved [S]: the index formula and hyperbolic-plane law; the aliasing law (one prime hides exactly equal \(p^{\rho-1/2}\), at most \(\binom\kappa2\) blind primes, two primes decide, infinitely many positive towers force index zero); the Pontryagin decomposition (finite index = positive semidefinite minus \(\kappa\) squares spanned by translates); index is energy (\(50m^2-3m/2\)); heat collisions count the index; compressions never raise the index and kill a pair iff their Dirichlet polynomial vanishes at one of its characters; the quasi-analytic boundary of the receivers at Gevrey class one. The finite stratum is Krein–Langer and Lindelöf-deep.

### 2.5 What all unconditional positivity knows

The sum of the selective results is sharp and sobering: the information content of every unconditional positivity theorem in the corpus is the bound \(|\lambda|\le\Lambda\) plus one bit. The one bit is the *carrier* of the first negative square of the atomic kernel \(D\delta_1-P\): for \(\zeta\) it lies on the pole direction down to \(\log N-\gamma-2\log2\); for Liouville it never does below \(\log N-\gamma\) [S]. Everything else (spacing-scale stability, Haar cell floors, Metzler sign below \(K=1\), high-detail reserves, uniform finite index of every prime layer except \(k=1\)) holds for every twin. The margin \(2\log2\) is the width of the index-one window of that atomic kernel, set by conductor 2 (\(E_2-E_1=2\log2\)), and the exact single-prime classification (\(\delta_p=\min(2\log2,\alpha_p)\), maximal iff \(p\ge11\)) shows a positive gap is a parity statement, not a \(\zeta\) statement.

### 2.6 The census picture

Three routes to \(3/4\) are proved equivalent to one problem (unification I): displacement at depth \(1/\log T\). (a) Prime side: the coalescent surplus of odd primes at even shifts within \(T^{-1+\eta}\) must be \(\le(1/18+o(1))N\) at bandwidth \(4/3\) [L]; Hardy–Littlewood predicts \(-1/144\), Poisson without repulsion gives \(+1/16\) and fails, so the budget *uses* arithmetic repulsion [L]. (b) Zero side: slack \(\ge N/12\) at \(\lambda\in[3/4,1)\) needs displaced pairs with \(\eta\ge1/6\); the \(3/4\) slack hypothesis is false for every \(\lambda<3/4\) [L]. (c) The dichotomy `three_quarters_or_simple_offLine` [search]: in every dyadic block either \(3/4\) of the zeros are critical, or at least \((1/6-\varepsilon)N\) simple off-line zeros exist; density estimates cannot exclude the second branch because the off-line zeros may sit at depth \(\lesssim1/\log T\), where value moments are blind (mimicry) and the shallow Cauchy majorant counts all zeros.

### 2.7 The corpus relative to the literature

The corpus is ahead of the literature in exactness of equivalences and in the classification of what cannot work; it is level with the literature in producers (it has reproved VK, GM, Montgomery, Rodgers–Tao, Weil–Kloosterman, Stepanov, Baker, Alpöge–Furman's \(2/3\) as import, and added \(10^{-8}\) by Gram geometry). It has produced no new unconditional estimate about zeros of \(\zeta\) of strength above the literature. This is the correct diagnosis of forty programmes: the receivers are finished.

## 3. Question 1 — mechanisms that circumvent or destroy a wall

Ranked by how much of the wall they would remove, and by how far the corpus already supports them.

### 3.1 The converse Krein–Langer theorem on \(\Gamma\) by a Pontryagin-space fixed-point theorem (proposal; closes HKT open problem 1)

HKT's open structural step is: for a Hecke-covariant kernel of finite index \(\kappa\) on \(\Gamma\) with infinite spectral support, find a \(\kappa\)-dimensional negative subspace invariant under *all* prime translations, so that its joint eigenvectors are the characters of the hyperbolic pairs. The README calls this "a fixed-point theorem beyond finite dimension". That theorem exists in the literature of indefinite inner product spaces: **Naimark's theorem** (1963) states that a commutative group of \(\pi\)-unitary operators on a Pontryagin space \(\Pi_\kappa\) has a common invariant \(\kappa\)-dimensional non-positive subspace (Azizov–Iokhvidov, *Linear operators in spaces with an indefinite metric*; Iokhvidov–Krein–Langer). This is cited from memory; the exact hypotheses (in particular whether the group may be any commutative family of \(\pi\)-unitaries, as needed here) must be checked against the source before the step is used. The reproducing-kernel Pontryagin space of a finite-index Hermitian kernel on \(\Gamma\) is a \(\Pi_\kappa\); Hecke covariance makes every translation \(\pi\)-unitary; the translations commute. Naimark's theorem then supplies the invariant negative subspace; commuting operators on a finite-dimensional space have a common eigenvector, and iterating on quotients gives the \(\kappa\) characters. The corpus has no Pontryagin-space theory [search], so this is new input, but it is classical and formalizable.

What it buys: Wall 3's "transfer" half becomes a structure theorem (finite index on \(\Gamma\) *is* positive plus finitely many hyperbolic pairs of characters, with no separation hypothesis), the subtracted-lag receiver becomes the decomposition, and the whole finite stratum acquires a canonical spectral datum. What it does not buy: the finite stratum is LH-deep; this is structure on the stratum, not entry into it. It destroys the structural part of Wall 3 and leaves Walls 1 and 14 intact.

### 3.2 The Hecke defect at logarithmic translation as the displacement statistic (proposal; joins CDG to the census)

The collided form \(D(f)=\sum_\rho m_\rho|\hat f(\rho)|^2\) is positive by construction, Hecke-invariant iff RH, and not computed by the explicit formula. Its translation law is exact [search]:
\[
D(T_Lf)=\sum_\rho m_\rho\,e^{2(\operatorname{Re}\rho-\tfrac12)L}\,|\hat f(\rho)|^2,
\qquad
E_f(L):=D(T_Lf)+D(T_{-L}f)-2D(f)=\sum_\rho m_\rho\,(2\cosh(2\delta_\rho L)-2)\,|\hat f(\rho)|^2 ,
\]
with \(\delta_\rho=\operatorname{Re}\rho-\tfrac12\), and \(4L^2\sum m_\rho\delta_\rho^2|\hat f|^2\le E_f(L)\) [search]. At \(L=c\log T\) a zero at depth \(\delta=\eta/\log T\) is weighted by \(2\cosh(2c\eta)-2\approx4c^2\eta^2\): zero on the line, quadratic in the displacement at exactly the scale \(1/\log T\) the census cannot see. This is the displacement-sensitive zero statistic that mimicry, shallow Cauchy energy and zero-side slack all lack (unification I). Moreover CDG proves [search] that literal theta prefixes compute \(E_f(L)\) in the limit, uniformly for \(|L|\le2\log(n+1)\) and twists \(|\tau|\le n/2\): the quantity is arithmetic-side at the logarithmic translation scale, with no zeros used.

The target becomes: an **arithmetic upper bound** \(E_f(c\log T)\le\varepsilon\,N(T,2T)\) for a height-localized test, which would bound \(\#\{\rho:\eta_\rho\ge\eta_0,\ T\le\gamma\le2T\}\le\varepsilon N/(4c^2\eta_0^2)\) — a zero-density estimate *at depth \(1/\log T\)*, which the Guth–Maynard envelopes cannot give (they never drop below 1). Such a bound controls the *displaced* half of the census problem (pairs with \(\eta\ge\eta_0\)); it says nothing about pairs with \(\eta<\eta_0\), which are exactly the ones the zero-side slack cannot see, so on its own it does not give \(3/4\). It is a census input only together with a statement about the shallow population. Status of the bottleneck: CDG's readings recover the collided side exactly but prove no upper bound; an upper bound on \(E_f\) is the "prime-side upper bound for the Hecke defect at \(L\asymp\log T\)" listed open in CDG. It is a Wall-1 statement in a new currency, but one where the unknown is a *positive* quantity with an exact arithmetic formula, not a sign.

### 3.3 Baker-type Diophantine input for one-prime certification (proposal; partial on HKT open problem 4)

The Nyquist law says the tower of \(p\) sees every right zero in ordinate windows shorter than \(2\pi/\log p\) and aliases only zeros with equal real parts and ordinates \(2\pi\mathbb Z/\log p\) apart [S]. Two zeros alias at \(p\) and \(q\) simultaneously iff they coincide, by irrationality of \(\log p/\log q\). The corpus holds a formalized Baker-type theorem [search]: `BakerGeneralProof.false_of_long_extrapolation`, "a solved algebraic relation among chosen logarithms is impossible", with the full auxiliary-polynomial, house-bound and extrapolation machinery. The formalized statement is qualitative (no relation exists); the quantitative form, effective lower bounds for \(|a\log p-b\log q|\), is not in the corpus and would have to be added on top of this machinery. Quantitative Baker turns "no common aliasing" into an explicit statement: one prime \(p\) certifies every right zero of height \(\le T\) once \(\log p\) exceeds an explicit function of \(T\) determined by the minimal distance between \(\gamma\log p/2\pi\) and the integers over the finitely many ordinates. This replaces "two primes carry the index" by "one explicit prime at each height", a Wall-3 locality statement made quantitative. It carries no sign; it sharpens the local-to-global law.

### 3.4 Structural precision: integrality as the parity-breaking, precision-faithful input (direction; AET open problems 4–5, HKT topic 9)

The only inputs that fail the precision battery are identities: the Euler product, the functional equation, and integrality. AET makes integrality exact: \(\psi(N)=\log|\mathbb Z/\operatorname{lcm}(1..N)|\), the box Euler characteristic \(E(\log(N+1))=N-\log Q_N\), and RH iff \(\log|\mathbb Z/\operatorname{lcm}(1..N)|=\chi(\sqrt N A)+O(\sqrt N\log^2N)\) [L]. The perturbation \(\Lambda+\varepsilon n^{-\eta}\) destroys the integrality of \(\operatorname{lcm}\); the Liouville twist destroys it too. So integrality is the right kind of input. The known extraction (Chebyshev, Nair: \(\operatorname{lcm}(1..n+1)\int_0^1P\in\mathbb Z\) gives \(\psi(2m+1)\ge m\log4\)) reaches linear precision only. The decisive question is whether any auxiliary construction over \(\mathbb Z\) can reach square-root precision, or whether the integrality floor is provably linear. Over \(\mathbb F_q\) Stepanov reaches the sign because the coefficients are Frobenius-flat (\(x^q=x\)); over \(\mathbb Z\) no coefficients are flat (AET). I treat this as the central open mathematical question of the whole programme (see §7, question 3), not as a mechanism in hand.

### 3.5 What I do not recommend

More receivers or dictionaries (the width functor is full and faithful; a new unit is not a proof). More finite-aperture or fixed-rank certificates (width limit \(\tfrac12\)). Markov or Metzler gauges past overlap ratio 1 (odd triangles frustrate every gauge). Positive majorants of any kind (a positive part never pays a pair). Multiplicative-only constructions (gauge-blind by theorem). Tensor, family or harmonic amplification of \(\zeta\) alone (depth-homogeneous by theorem). Function-field calibration as a source of positivity (curves reproduce the prime support, not the archimedean continuum).

## 4. Question 2 — approaches wildly different from the existing routes

Every live route is a quadratic form on a test space whose positivity is RH, and every candidate is a construction landing in such a form. A different approach must either not be a quadratic form, or get its sign from a source other than a form's definition. Three directions qualify; I grade them honestly.

### 4.1 Transcendence-method arithmetic: auxiliary functions, Siegel's lemma and the Liouville inequality on the Hecke lattice (proposal)

Weil's \(\mathbb F_q\) proof by Stepanov is a Thue–Siegel–Dyson-type argument: an auxiliary polynomial vanishing to high order on a known set, a degree bound, and integrality. On \(\mathbb Z\) the corresponding discipline is transcendence theory (Gelfond, Schneider, Baker): Siegel's lemma for small integer solutions, an auxiliary function with many zeros, an analytic upper bound, and the Liouville inequality \(|n|\ge1\) read as the product formula — which AET identifies as the *one* intrinsic integrality inequality over \(\mathbb Z\), from which every known sign from integers derives. The corpus has the machinery: Stepanov over \(\mathbb F_{p^n}\), hyperelliptic Hasse–Weil by descent, Baker's general theorem, Siegel-lemma-type auxiliary matrices [search]. It has never pointed this machinery at the zeros.

Bridge to the Weil kernel: HKT's annihilation law says a compression of the Weil kernel by \(P=\sum p_\alpha\delta_{y_\alpha}\) is positive definite iff the Dirichlet polynomial \(\hat P\) vanishes at one character of every off-line pair; so a "Stepanov certificate" is a Dirichlet polynomial vanishing off the line without presupposing the zeros [S]. The transcendence template produces exactly such objects: Dirichlet polynomials with controlled integer coefficients vanishing at prescribed points, by Siegel's lemma. The missing step, and the risk, is the counting direction: transcendence proofs bound a single number (a linear form in logarithms), while the sign needs a count at square-root precision. Siegel's theorem on exceptional zeros is the one classical place where a transcendence-style argument did locate zeros (ineffectively), which suggests the template is not foreign to the problem. Concrete first problems: (a) AET open 4, the limit of integrality over \(\mathbb Z\) — prove or refute that every bound from \(\operatorname{lcm}\cdot\int P\in\mathbb Z\) is linear; (b) a zero estimate for Dirichlet polynomials with integer coefficients on \(\Gamma\) (how many prescribed vanishing conditions at points \(\rho-\tfrac12\) of bounded height a length-\(N\) polynomial of height \(H\) can satisfy), using Baker-type separation of \(\log p\); (c) a Liouville-type lower bound for \(|\sum_{n\le N}a_nn^{-s}|\) at arithmetic points. A first target is a fixed zero-free strip, not RH.

### 4.2 Reflection positivity as a construction, not a criterion (direction; W1C landing, ALG candidate)

RH is reflection positivity of the Cayley–Li ladder [L]: the Li coefficients are the two-point function of a lattice system whose Osterwalder–Schrader positivity is RH. In statistical mechanics reflection positivity is not proved from the two-point function; it is a consequence of a construction (a reflection-positive measure, a transfer matrix, a ferromagnetic Lee–Yang structure). The corpus has the Lee–Yang theorem, Asano contraction, prime Ising trees and the Ising-limit receiver [L/search], and has killed the obvious couplings (balanced prime trees, harmonic site classes, uncentered normalization: KR-ISG). The open target is a source-defined ferromagnetic system (couplings from \(\Lambda\), FE as reflection) whose partition function converges to Riemann's measure; ALG names KMS states of \(\mathbb N\rtimes\mathbb N^\times\) (the Bost–Connes system, whose partition function is \(\zeta\)) with the reflection \(\beta\mapsto3-\beta\). This is a Hilbert–Pólya programme in which the operator is *reconstructed* (OS reconstruction) rather than guessed, and positivity comes from ferromagnetism rather than from the form. Risk: every attempted source-defined system has landed in a receiver; the test is the battery (Liouville twin must fail to be ferromagnetic).

### 4.3 A covariant regulator on the characteristic circle (direction; AIT open problem 1)

AIT exhibits the one place over \(\mathbb Z\) with integer intersection numbers: torsion orbits \(O_n\) on \(\mathbb G_m/\mathbb Z\), \(I(n,m)=\log\#(\mathcal O_{\mathbb Q(\zeta_n)}/(\Phi_m(\zeta_n)))\), \(\Lambda(n)=\log\#(\mathcal O/(\zeta_n-1))\), power maps as Hecke, adjunction as the different [L]. The local Hodge index holds on every \(p\)-ladder; the global sign fails because the regularization of the diagonal is not covariant (Hecke cocycle \(\beta(n,t)=\log t-\sum\log p/(p-1)\), not a bounded coboundary), and the covariant spacing regulator leaves a scalar counterterm with trivial null cone. By the survival law, a sign through the limit needs a covariant regulator with a nontrivial null cone. This is an Arakelov-type question (an archimedean Green's function that absorbs the anomaly \(\log t\) of \([t]\)) and is the most geometric unsolved problem in the corpus. Risk: \(\mathbb P^1_{\mathbb Z}\) carries no Hodge-index positivity to gain; the archimedean place must supply it, and the "arithmetic point \(z=1\) carries the divergence" [L].

### 4.4 A logical observation on families (not a route)

A zero-free strip for *all* Dirichlet characters modulo \(q\), including the principal one, is a zero-free strip for \(\zeta\): \(L(s,\chi_0)=\zeta(s)\prod_{p\mid q}(1-p^{-s})\). The family average over \(\chi\bmod q\) is, by AKT's homogeneity analysis, expected to lose linearly (open problem 3 there asks for a formal no-go). The external Chen–Gupta–Li claim [E] therefore either excludes the principal character or concerns almost all characters; whichever it is, the first check in extracting its vanishing mechanism (W08) is whether the argument survives \(\chi=\chi_0\). If it does, it is a quasi-RH producer for \(\zeta\); if it cannot, the point of failure is the exact definition of what a family supplies that a single \(L\)-function lacks, which is a sharper form of AKT's open problem.

## 5. Question 3 — quasi-RH with improved values

**Facts.** No zero-free strip \(\Theta<1\) is proved for \(\zeta\), in the corpus or in the literature; the best unconditional region is Vinogradov–Korobov, formalized with an explicit constant [search]. The interval \(1/8<\operatorname{Re}\rho<7/8\) is an external claim about Dirichlet \(L\)-functions [E]; the 279-module intake has detector and density components and no capstone [search]. Every corpus quasi-RH statement is a dictionary: \(\Theta<1\) iff a balanced short-window Möbius or prime power saving, iff a growing-endpoint escaped-Möbius power law \(\theta=1-\Theta\), iff Carathéodory positivity of \(\operatorname{Re}\xi'/\xi\) on \(\operatorname{Re}s>\Theta\), iff a uniform Vaughan power gain; \(\Theta\le3/4\) iff the normalized Chebyshev Cesàro mean-square energy is \(\ll N^{3/2+\varepsilon}\) [L]. Density envelopes never force a strip, at \(30/13\) as at \(8/3\) [L].

**What produces strips.** Only one mechanism has ever produced a zero-free region: multiplicative positivity plus a growth input. MPG makes its limit exact: exclusion iff \(\sqrt A+\sqrt{G\eta}<\sqrt B\), certified width \((\sqrt B-\sqrt A)^2/G\), where \(G\) is the growth of \(\operatorname{Re}(-\zeta'/\zeta)\) from Hadamard; with \(G\asymp\log t\) the width is \(\asymp1/\log t\), and the Fejér barrier \(a_1<2a_0\) caps every finite multi-frequency certificate [L]. A fixed strip from this mechanism needs \(G\) bounded on a vertical line inside the strip, which is a growth statement of Lindelöf strength at that line. So improved quasi-RH values from positivity alone are excluded by theorem; a new growth input \(G(\delta)\) that genuinely depends on \(\delta\) is MPG's open problem 3 and the only lever left on this side.

**The other side.** The second conceivable producer is a vanishing mechanism combined with the density machinery: an off-line zero forces a large value of a detector polynomial, large values are counted, and the count is below the trivial one. This is the shape of the Chen–Gupta–Li argument as far as the intake shows, and it needs a family (orthogonality over characters) to make the count beat the diagonal — the resource \(\zeta\) alone does not have (§4.4).

**Assessment.** There is no opportunity in the corpus to prove any fixed zero-free strip for \(\zeta\), let alone an improved one, with present tools. The realistic work is (a) finish the W08 extraction and run the \(\chi_0\) test; (b) state the VK step as a range-limited growth inequality and ask which \(\delta\)-dependent growth input would move it; (c) treat §4.1 (transcendence template) with a strip as its first target, since a strip is the weakest statement in the ladder that is still parity-breaking.

## 6. Question 4 — three quarters of the zeros on the line

**What is exact.** Three routes, all [L]: (a) prime surplus at bandwidth \(4/3\): the odd-prime even-shift coalescent surplus within \(T^{-1+\eta}\) at most \((1/18+o(1))N\); (b) zero-side slack \(\ge N/12\) at \(\lambda\in[3/4,1)\), which displaced pairs supply iff \(\eta\ge1/6\); (c) `PairCorrelationFourThirds` (\(\|\hat G\|_F^2\le(5/4+\varepsilon)N\)) gives \(3/4\) simple and on the line [search]. The dichotomy theorem gives a fourth face: \(3/4\) critical in every dyadic block, or at least \((1/6-\varepsilon)N\) simple off-line zeros there [search]. The intermediate rung \(70\%\) follows from \(E(1/9)\le4\) at bandwidth \(1+\delta\) [L].

**Why each is blocked.** (a) is a signed prime-pair correlation at shifts \(h\le T^{1/3}\) whose Poisson main term cancels exactly against the dyadic kernel and whose budget lives in the Hardy–Littlewood repulsion term \(-\tfrac12\log H\); positive spectral majorants give \(8/27\) against \(1/9\), one-sided sieves lose the height phase, higher moments force \(k\lambda\le1\) [L]. This is the parity problem at square-root precision. (b) needs a displacement-sensitive lower bound at depth \(1/\log T\); value moments are blind by mimicry [L]. (c) is Montgomery's conjecture beyond \(\alpha=1\), equivalent under RH to (a). The dichotomy face shows the whole obstruction is the possible presence of \(\ge N/6\) simple zeros at depth \(\lesssim1/\log T\), which no density theorem excludes. Mollifiers do not help: \(3/4\) needs mollifier length \(\theta\ge4^{1/3}-1\) even conditionally [L].

**What can be attempted.** (i) Quantify the Gram pinching slack (IGP): the \(10^{-8}\) is a structural gain from the non-lattice zeros of the cosine kernel; its true macroscopic value is unknown and is a pure Gram-geometry computation (span \(\le3\) triples only were used). This is the one route to a larger unconditional proportion that needs no arithmetic; it will not reach \(3/4\). (ii) The Hecke-defect statistic of §3.2 is the displacement-sensitive input (b) and the dichotomy need; its arithmetic upper bound is the open estimate. (iii) The parity-locked Ramanujan stabilizer reduces (a) to one centred discrepancy [L]; a two-sided estimate for it on average over even shifts is the cleanest additive statement in the corpus and the natural test bed for any parity-breaking method.

**Assessment.** \(75\%\) is not reachable with current tools; it is exactly as hard as a displacement-sensitive zero-density bound at depth \(1/\log T\), equivalently a square-root-precise two-sided prime-pair estimate. \(70\%\) has the same obstruction with a smaller constant. The honest intermediate deliverable is the exact value of the Gram slack and the exact arithmetic formula for the displacement statistic.

## 7. Question 5 — three open questions to focus on

1. **An index ceiling for the Weil kernel on two prime towers from local data, with the Lindelöf hypothesis as the test.** Order of work: (a) the converse Krein–Langer theorem on \(\Gamma\) via Naimark's theorem (§3.1), making finite index a canonical spectral datum; (b) a bound on the canonical critical energy \(\mathcal E_{\rm crit}\) or on the two-prime index from the prime-two dyadic Chebyshev sequence and the first prime layer against Gamma (the only layer not yet Gamma-small); (c) every claimed bound checked against LH, since any finite index proves LH. Why this: it is the one place where Walls 1, 3 and 14 are literally one statement, the structural half is within reach, and a falsifiable intermediate (LH) exists. Expected first outcome: a structure theorem; expected hard outcome: the first layer.

2. **The displacement problem at depth \(1/\log T\), in the currency of the Hecke defect at \(L\asymp\log T\).** Prove an arithmetic upper bound for \(E_f(c\log T)\) from the theta-prefix readings (CDG) or from the prime side, or prove that every such bound is a zero-density statement no stronger than Guth–Maynard. Why this: it is the only open estimate whose target is a *positive quantity with an exact arithmetic formula*, it is the common blind spot of the census, WD and SCP, and partial results are proportions (\(70\%\), \(75\%\)) rather than nothing.

3. **The limit of integrality over \(\mathbb Z\) (arithmetic Stepanov).** Decide whether an auxiliary construction over \(\mathbb Z\) (integer Dirichlet polynomials, \(\operatorname{lcm}\) integrality, Baker-type zero estimates on \(\Gamma\)) can produce a one-sided count at any precision beyond linear, first in the form of a fixed zero-free strip. Why this: integrality is the one input that is both parity-breaking and precision-faithful; it is the only known producer mechanism (Stepanov) over \(\mathbb F_q\); and a definitive negative (the integrality floor is linear) would be the most important no-go in the corpus, redirecting the search for producers away from \(\mathbb Z\)-integrality toward the functional equation as an identity.

The separated-regime step (ledger Q1: the bottom of the complete-Weil spectrum past Robin–Euler, selective against Liouville) remains the correct incremental frontier and should continue as "just work"; it is not listed above because it is already precisely posed and resourced.

## 8. Question 6 — the field that would supply the tools

The walls, read as questions, are: *what bounds a signed arithmetic source at square-root precision using a property \(\Lambda\) has and its twins lack; in which topology does a sign or index survive the limit; which independently built object produces the inequality.* The previous review answered with HKT, the indefinite harmonic analysis of the Weil kernel; HKT has since built the structure theorems and located its own open step (§3.1). The field that would supply what HKT cannot is one level down, where signs come from integers.

**Name and point of view.** *Arithmetic of square-root precision* (precision arithmetic): the study of arithmetic quantities that are integers or log-orders of finite groups, of the identities that constrain them (Euler product, functional equation, successor, product formula), and of the methods that extract estimates at square-root precision from those identities. Its point of view is that a sign is a comparison of two computations of one integer (AET's principle), that positivity is the last step after index and count (HKT's principle), and that every estimate must be stated with the twin, the perturbation and the gauge it fails for (the battery).

**Objects.** The Weil kernel on \(\Gamma\) and on the idele class group with its Krein–Langer structure; the log-torsion \(\psi(N)=\log|\mathbb Z/\operatorname{lcm}(1..N)|\) and the box class \(\sqrt N\,A\) of the scaling surface with \(\chi=N\); integer Dirichlet polynomials of length \(N\) and height \(H\) as auxiliary functions, with the annihilation law as their action on the kernel; torsion orbits on \(\mathbb G_m/\mathbb Z\) with their integer intersection numbers and the regulator anomaly; the gauge torus and the arithmetic point; the collided form and its Hecke defect as the displacement currency.

**Language.** Index, hyperbolic pair, aliasing, survival cone, counterterm and anomaly, gauge charge, log-torsion, Euler characteristic, zero estimate, house and height of an auxiliary polynomial, Liouville inequality, precision class (which perturbations a statement survives), stratum.

**Foundational principles.** (1) Positivity is the last step: index, then count, then sign. (2) A positive part never pays a pair; an index ceiling comes from a structure, a count or an identity. (3) A statement that survives all twins is a dictionary; one that survives \(n^{-\eta}\) perturbations cannot decide the sign. (4) Locality fails; two primes decide; the archimedean place is the glue. (5) Finite index is Lindelöf-deep; LH is the diagnostic of every claimed ceiling. (6) Integers over \(\mathbb Z\) are log-torsions; the product formula is the Bézout of the base; linear RR is free, quadratic RR is the content. (7) Calibrate over \(\mathbb F_q\): reproduce Weil by counting, fail on the golden datum and the complex fake.

**Criteria a theorem must meet.** Unconditional; built from arithmetic data, never from zero-indexed objects; keeps Gamma, both poles, every prime power and all mixed entries; names its precision class and fails the battery (Liouville, \(c\Lambda\), quadratic mixtures, flips \(p\ge11\), cofinite flips, \(\Lambda+\varepsilon n^{-\eta}\), golden curve on the lattice, over-regular Beurling systems, Davenport–Heilbronn); states its stratum; records the positive survivor of every no-go.

**Foundational theorems to establish.**
- Structure: the converse Krein–Langer theorem on \(\Gamma\) and on \(\mathbb A^\times/\mathbb Q^\times\) (Naimark route), and an index-carrying completion at the spacing scale with the explicit schedule \((\log\log N)^\alpha/N\).
- Zero estimates: for Dirichlet polynomials with integer coefficients on the Hecke lattice, with Baker-type separation of the prime logarithms; an explicit one-prime certification law in height.
- Riemann–Roch: a section count on the scaling surface with quadratic Euler characteristic, box-class size \(N\), growth laws proved from its definition; or a proof that every such count with the Riemann inequality is bounded by a large-sieve count in degree zero (the emptiness test of AET).
- The limit of integrality: the exact precision reachable by auxiliary constructions over \(\mathbb Z\) (linear, or beyond).
- The displacement statistic: an arithmetic upper law for the Hecke defect at logarithmic translation.
- The covariant regulator: an archimedean Green's function on the characteristic circle whose counterterm has a nontrivial null cone, or a proof none exists.

**Research topics.** (i) Pontryagin-space harmonic analysis of Hecke-covariant kernels. (ii) Transcendence methods for Dirichlet polynomials: Siegel lemma, zero estimates, Liouville inequalities at arithmetic points, and their action on the Weil kernel by compression. (iii) Log-torsion cohomology on the scaling surface. (iv) Covariant renormalization of the diagonal on \(\mathbb G_m/\mathbb Z\). (v) Displacement-sensitive zero statistics computed from theta prefixes. (vi) The gauge-charged content of the successor (Bézout-selective laws with constants). (vii) The classification of precision classes of analytic statements (which theorems survive which perturbations), making the battery a theory rather than a checklist. (viii) Reflection-positive constructions on the Bost–Connes system as the operator-algebraic face of the same count.

**What the field is not.** It is not another route. Its success criterion is a producer: one unconditional inequality about \(\zeta\) that fails the battery and lands in a stratum above \([\text{open}]\), first a fixed zero-free strip, then a finite index on two towers, then the count.

## 9. Closing assessment

The programme has achieved a complete and exact theory of what RH is equivalent to and of what cannot prove it, and has unified forty programmes into one kernel, one invariant and one count. It has not produced a single unconditional estimate about the zeros of \(\zeta\) beyond the literature. The ledgers' own priorities (the separated-regime step, the census displacement, the Stepanov producer) are correct; this review adds three concrete levers — the Pontryagin fixed-point structure theorem, the Hecke defect at logarithmic scale as the displacement currency, and transcendence-method auxiliary constructions as the one parity-breaking, precision-faithful mechanism on \(\mathbb Z\) — and one logical test for the external quasi-RH claim. The honest expectation is that the next real progress will be a no-go of a new kind (the precision reachable by integrality over \(\mathbb Z\)) or a structure theorem (finite index on \(\Gamma\)), not a sign.
