Three wall families carry the frontier. **Σ** is the critical sign of the window form, **Π** the precision and production of the prime count, **Γ** the selection of ζ, the reflection of its zeros and the transport of its zeros in height. **How the three families split one problem [D].** No test is compactly supported on both sides of the explicit formula. Σ fixes a compact prime side (windows); the zero side then has infinite rank and only an exact sign decides. Π localizes the zero side (Gaussian or band-limited weights, geometric scales of a tower, the prime degrees of the smoothed Frobenius graphs); a crude bound then suffices, and Landau's theorem for nonnegative Dirichlet series together with the absence of real zeros of ζ in the strip turns a one-sided constant into RH [L] — but the prime side needs square-root precision. Γ is the involution `ρ↦1−ρ̄`, the selection of ζ and the vertical transport of its zeros. The curve proof has finite rank (elimination is algebra, ZAL in tower form is automatic) and zeros periodic in height (AAL is automatic); ζ has neither [L]. The prime degrees remove one of the two freebies: one-sided bounds at sampling sets of primes give AAL without periodicity, and power-saving gaps give attainment for free [L].
