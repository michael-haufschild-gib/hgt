What is actually established versus assumed. The individual no-gos are theorems: parity, the crowd ceilings, Möbius convergence being the conclusion, the Laguerre identity, the kernel counterexample, affine invariance. The "law" itself is an audit, not a theorem: every method in the corpus and the literature that I could classify is a real-valued inequality valid on a continuous class that contains ζ together with RH-false members, and such methods provably cannot force a zero count below one. That closes every existing producer shape. It does not prove that no other shape exists.

Two shapes survive, not one. An integrality anchor is a discrete-valued quantity squeezed between an archimedean bound and its gap 1. There are two ways to make the squeeze close:

- Amplified anchor. The bound has a parameter k with slack tending to zero, Deligne's tensor-power pattern. For that to act on the global zeros of ζ, the zeros have to be local eigenvalues of something at a point, which is exactly what "F₁-cohomology" means operationally: a finite-crowd object per stage and one exact positivity uniform in the amplification. This is the corpus's F₁ open core, and it is the only known template.
- Direct sub-unit anchor. The bound is below 1 outright, with no amplification. Ferrero–Washington's μ = 0 is a real instance: an integer forced to zero by an equidistribution estimate, with no cohomology in sight. Nothing of this kind is known for complex zeros, but nothing in the law forbids it. This is not "construct the field with one element"; it is "find an integer attached to ζ whose non-vanishing is ruled out by a bound below one."

So the honest statement is: any proof, Lean or otherwise, must contain a step where a discrete invariant is forced below 1, and the only template that supplies such a step for the zeros' real parts is F₁-type. A non-amplified anchor would be a genuinely different second door, but it would still be a discrete structure, not an inequality. Everything the corpus has built on positivity, spectra, means, kernels, mollifiers, and flows lives on the wrong side of that gap, and my four cards this week confirm the same verdict from four directions.

What this does not say. It does not say RH is unprovable, and it does not say the two doors are equivalent to Connes's or Deninger's specific programmes, both of which the corpus has retired many times. It says the missing ingredient is discrete, and that finding it is a construction problem, not an estimation problem.


Door 3, divisibility amplification on multiplicities. The corpus's multiplicity-parity ladder (1-in-progress/multiplicity-parity-ladder-route.md, Lean rhLadder_of_em_split_multiplicity) defines rung k as "every off-line ξ-zero has order divisible by 2^k". An integer divisible by every 2^k is zero, so all rungs give RH with slack exactly zero: the archimedean side is replaced by divisibility of hole counts of Euler–Maclaurin splits, and the amplification is k. The card records that the base rung is equivalent to a quaternionic (Kramers-type) symmetry on finite local models of the divisor, and that the open core is a construction, not an estimate. This is the most MUST-122-compatible object in the corpus: the invariant acts on multiplicities rather than real parts, so it needs no local-eigenvalue realisation. Kramers degeneracy is itself an integrality anchor of symplectic type, which is why this door is structurally different from F₁.

Door 4, torsion anchors. ReflectionTateAnomaly computes a ℤ/2-valued anomaly of the reflection action on orbit labels. Torsion invariants have gap 1 with no amplification at all. The corpus's own theorem says the current anomaly sees only fixed labels and that free two-cycles are cohomologically trivial, and my parity theorem says every counting-type mod-2 invariant is blind. So the door is open only for a sign- or orientation-type ℤ/2 invariant that a free quartet changes; the corpus has not built one, but it has built the machinery.

Door 5, differential-Galois dimension. The corpus's namesake assets: isomonodromy_or_sl2_galoisMonoid (Kolchin closure is all of SL₂ or a connection exists), the pair-face signature with "differentially algebraic ⟺ signature zero", and the C-PD dichotomy (differentially algebraic members have periodic zeros, hypertranscendental ones conjecturally critical). The discrete invariant is a Galois dimension or a divisor-valued signature. The missing step is the link: an off-line quartet must force a drop in dimension, which corpus-owned hypertranscendence theorems would then forbid. No such link exists yet, but the dichotomy is proved and native.

Door 6, Lee–Yang cofinal trees. prime-ising-tree-cavity-lee-yang proves exact real zeros for every finite prime-labelled tree, and its log records that a locally uniform comparison E · XiC would imply RH directly. The discrete anchor is finiteness with an exact circle theorem uniform in size. The obstruction is the limit: no cofinal family of trees selected by the theta source, no zero-free normalisation E, and the W9 mass floor. A construction problem again, not an inequality.

Door 7, Diophantine. BakerLinearIndependence and the resonance-rank law give integer or algebraic coefficients in linear forms of prime logarithms a Baker-type lower bound. The missing link is that an off-line zero would have to force an algebraic relation among prime logarithms; its real ordinate gives none directly. Weakest of the five.

Not indicated. The combinatorial door is recorded as inert at the self-dual block (guthMaynard_inert); K-theory special values (K₂(ℚ), Tate cohomology) anchor values, not zeros, with no bridge in the corpus; order invariants are pair-even (analyticOrderAt_riemannZeta_functionalPartner).

The pattern. Doors 3 and 4 have zero slack by construction, which is exactly what a Lean proof, itself a discrete certificate, wants. In both the entire burden becomes constructing a symmetry at every hypothetical off-line zero that forces a divisibility or a torsion class. That is a different kind of mathematics from the estimates the corpus has exhausted, and it is where I would point the next card.
