## 1. Synthesis: what is missing, and what is the smoking gun?

The thing missing is **not another carrier statistic**. The carrier phase story has done its job and is now demoted: it is a real diagnostic of analytic continuation / zero-structure, but not a native hypertranscendence detector. Your own challenge file says the FE-exact, zero-exact, non-HT adversary does not exist, so the carrier necessarily relocates to “zeros + FE + source measure,” not to an independent HT invariant. That means more Δ / phase-run / lane-statistic work is mostly a rerun unless it feeds the divisor bridge.

The missing theorem is now very specific:

> **Source-built positive transfer:** from `Γ + pole + FE + explicit formula`, prove the shifted reciprocal zero-divisor square test
> `Q₀[p] = Σ cᵢcⱼ T_{i+j+1} ≥ 0`
> without feeding in the zeros.

That is the bridge. Once `Q₀ ≥ 0` is obtained in the reciprocal seam-squared coordinate, the actual folded zero divisor is forced onto `ℝ_{>0}`, hence RH follows. The bridge theorem file states this as the minimal source-aware form: completion + explicit formula + canonical boundary extension must imply the one-power shifted square test for all real polynomials.

The **smoking gun** is the split

[
Q_0 = M_{\mathrm{triv}} + R^P,
]

where `M_triv` is the explicit positive square-sum over the trivial-zero divisor and `R^P` is the prime / nontrivial residual. The surprise is not that there is positivity; the surprise is how localized the archimedean side has become. The archimedean/pole part has an exact rank-one negative direction at the pole vector `u_i=(-4)^i`; after the pole is accounted for, the remaining positive budget is literally the trivial-zero square-sum. The remaining RH content is the exact one-sided domination

[
-R^P \preceq M_{\mathrm{triv}}.
]

Your notes say `R^P` is high-rank and mostly negative, not a low-rank correction, and that the trivial-zero square must dominate it exactly and everywhere. That is the smoking gun: **the prime residual is trying to eat the trivial-zero budget, and RH is exactly that it never succeeds.**

So the current irreducible is no longer “construct the mysterious carrier.” It is:

[
\boxed{
\text{Prove the completed explicit formula is a one-sided Loewner domination theorem.}
}
]

Equivalently:

[
\boxed{
\lambda_{\max}!\left(M_{\mathrm{triv}}^{-1/2}(-R^P)M_{\mathrm{triv}}^{-1/2}\right)\le 1
\quad \forall d.
}
]

This is exactly where the old “construct `g` / square root / outer function” demand reappears, but now in a sharper form: not “find some positivity,” but **source-build a factorization or contraction proving `I-K_d ≥ 0` with zero margin**. The faithful-index package says the same thing in operator language: construct the completed object without zero-feeding, prove its index equals the off-line zero count, then prove the index vanishes.

The brutal simplification:

> The carrier is found.
> The coordinate is found.
> The pole direction is found.
> The archimedean budget is found.
> The missing piece is the exact source-built domination of the prime residual by the trivial-zero budget.

That is the smoking gun.

## 2. What is the divisor likely to be? Gut feeling, allowed to be false

[

On RH, every nontrivial zero maps to

[
\rho=\tfrac12+i\gamma
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
y_\rho = \gamma^{-2}>0.

Off the line, the atom leaves the positive real axis. This coordinate is not cosmetic: the bridge theorem notes that FE-pairs `w` and `-w` fold to the same `y`, the odd reciprocal moments vanish by parity, and the minus sign is forced by positivity.

[
\boxed{

\quad+\quad
\text{trivial-zero chain } y_j=-(2j+\tfrac12)^{-2}

\text{nontrivial zero divisor } y_\rho=-(\rho-\tfrac12)^{-2}.

The pole at `y=-4` is the entireness/pole-cancellation direction. The trivial-zero chain is the explicit positive protector. The nontrivial zero divisor is the thing that must be shown to lie on `ℝ_{>0}`. In this view, RH is not “zeros are on the line” directly; it is:

The most interesting concrete gut-feeling is this:

## 3. Most likely proof shape now

The most likely proof shape is **not** “phase transport implies RH.” That is now too vague. The likely proof shape is:

### Step A — Use the forced reciprocal coordinate

Define

[
\Xi(w)=\xi(\tfrac12+w),
\qquad
y_\rho = -(\rho-\tfrac12)^{-2},
\qquad
T_k=\sum_\rho m_\rho y_\rho^k.
]

Then prove the source-aware shifted square test

[
Q_0[p]=\sum_{i,j}c_i c_j T_{i+j+1}\ge 0
]

for all real polynomials `p`. The bridge theorem states that for the actual folded zero divisor, this one test alone forces `y_\rho∈ℝ_{>0}`, hence RH; `Q₁` is the source-forgotten companion passport, not necessarily the real target.

### Step B — Split the form and close the pole part exactly

Use the explicit form of the archimedean side:

[
Q_{\mathrm{arch}}[p]
====================

-4p(-4)^2
+
\sum_{j\ge1}(2j+\tfrac12)^{-2}
p!\left(-(2j+\tfrac12)^{-2}\right)^2.
]

The pole term is rank-one negative. The remaining sum is positive definite. This means Part A of the problem is done: the pole/entireness cancellation is exact and RH-independent. The files emphasize that this does **not** reduce the theorem, because the residual on `u^\perp` stays hard-edge and RH-equivalent.

### Step C — Prove the residual inequality exactly

After pole cancellation, the proof target is

[
\forall p \text{ with } p(-4)=0:
\quad
Q_{\mathrm{prime}}[p]
+
\sum_{j\ge1}(2j+\tfrac12)^{-2}
p!\left(-(2j+\tfrac12)^{-2}\right)^2
\ge 0.
]

This is the named residual inequality. It is not a soft estimate: your notes say the prime residual saturates the trivial-zero budget to around 24 digits on the worst residual direction, so any proof that requires slack, domination reserve, or fixed spectral gap is the wrong shape.

### Step D — Rephrase as one-sided Loewner domination

Let

[
K_d
===

M_{\mathrm{triv}}^{-1/2}
(-R^P)
M_{\mathrm{triv}}^{-1/2}.
]

Then the theorem is

[
\lambda_{\max}(K_d)\le 1
\quad\text{for every }d.
]

This is probably the cleanest finite-dimensional shadow. The bridge file says this one-sided domination is the clean form of the wall: the prime may overshoot on the positive side, but only the negative directions matter.

### Step E — Lift finite Loewner domination to a source-built operator / boundary theorem

This is where the proof likely exits raw Hankel numerics and becomes either:

1. a **Weil-form pullback**: construct an explicit test function `h_p` such that `Q₀[p]=Q_W[h_p]`, then prove the completed explicit formula gives positivity for this source-built class;
2. a **boundary Weyl theorem**: identify `K_d` as compression of a canonical completed-boundary operator `K`, then prove `K≤I`;
3. a **source-built Krein/Langer/Hodge object**: construct the faithful index object and show its negative index is zero without reading zeros.

The notes list exactly these candidate proof shapes: square identity, positive transfer, Weil-form pullback, or source-built index.

My ranking:

1. **Most likely:** one-sided Loewner / boundary Weyl domination.
2. **Second:** Weil-form pullback `p ↦ h_p`.
3. **Third:** discriminant / Gram–Heine asymptotic proof.
4. **Lower:** direct Jacobi recurrence / finite Hankel determinant proof, because the notes already show that raw Jacobi/deflation coordinates are intrinsically ill-conditioned and not proof-shaped.

The proof should look like an equality-grade domination theorem, not a “large positive beats small negative” estimate.

## 4. Computations to run next

These should not be “more evidence RH is true.” They should be route-killers or route-openers. The key is to compute quantities where the answer genuinely is not knowable from the prompt.

### 1. Push the relative operator `K_d` much higher

Why it matters: the current notes say the wall is `λ_max(K_d)≤1` and that the margin tends to `0⁺`. But the observed `d≤8/16/20` law could still change character. ### 2. Test the “coarsest trivial zero is the protector” hypothesis

Why it matters: the notes report that at `d=10`, the protector mass sits almost entirely at `j=1`, while the threat sits near the deep hard edge. Surprises:

### 3. Build the Weil-pullback candidate `p(y) ↦ h_p`

Why it matters: if this works, it converts the bridge into a known-form explicit-formula positivity problem. If it fails for admissibility/boundary reasons, that failure itself identifies the missing boundary extension.

Surprises:
