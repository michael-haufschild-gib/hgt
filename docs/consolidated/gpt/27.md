# Reciprocal-Seam \(Q_0\) Programme: Analytic Results and Computation Specs

**Status.** This is a working research note. It consolidates the analytic claims that are currently formula-level/theorem-level in the programme, and then lists the computational experiments that require substantial numerical work. It claims no proof of RH. Its purpose is to separate what can be answered analytically from what needs scripts, interval arithmetic, high precision, zero data, or statistical controls.

## Part I — Setup and Notation

Let

\[
\xi(s)=\frac12s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s),
\qquad
\Xi(w)=\xi\!\left(\frac12+w\right).
\]

Then

\[
\Xi(w)=\Xi(-w).
\]

Let \(w=\rho-\tfrac12\) denote a zero of \(\Xi\), with multiplicity \(m_w\). Because \(\Xi\) is even, the FE orbit

\[
[w]=\{w,-w\}
\]

has total trace multiplicity \(2m_w\) for \(w\neq0\).

Define the reciprocal seam coordinate

\[
y_{[w]}=-w^{-2}.
\]

Define the folded reciprocal zero-divisor moments, for \(k\ge1\), by

\[
T_k=\sum_{[w]}2m_w\,y_{[w]}^k
=
\sum_{[w]}2m_w\,(-w^{-2})^k.
\]

The source-aware \(Q_0\) square-test form is

\[
Q_0[p]
=
\sum_{i,j=0}^{d}c_i c_jT_{i+j+1}
\]

for

\[
p(y)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}c_i y^i.
\]

Equivalently, formally at the divisor level,

\[
Q_0[p]
=
\sum_{[w]}2m_w\,y_{[w]}p(y_{[w]})^2.
\]

The minimal current RH-bearing target is:

\[
\boxed{
\forall d\ge0,\qquad
H_d^{(0)}=[T_{i+j+1}]_{i,j=0}^{d}\succeq0.
}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
Q_0[p]\ge0\qquad\forall p\in\mathbb R[y].
}
\]

# Part II — Analytic Results and Proofs

## Theorem 1 — Final source-aware \(Q_0\) theorem target

**Statement.**
For the completed zero divisor of \(\Xi(w)=\xi(1/2+w)\), define

\[
T_k=\sum_{[w]}2m_w(-w^{-2})^k,\qquad k\ge1.
\]

The current minimal RH-bearing theorem target is

\[
\boxed{
[T_{i+j+1}]_{i,j=0}^{d}\succeq0
\quad\forall d\ge0.
}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
Q_0[p]\ge0
\quad\forall p\in\mathbb R[y].
}
\]

**Proof / explanation.**
If RH holds, every zero has

\[
w=i\gamma,
\]

hence

\[
y=-w^{-2}=\gamma^{-2}>0.
\]

Then

\[
Q_0[p]
=
\sum_{[w]}2m_w\,y_{[w]}p(y_{[w]})^2\ge0
\]

for every real polynomial \(p\). Therefore RH implies \(Q_0\)-positivity.

Conversely, if \(Q_0\)-positivity holds for the actual source-aware folded divisor, non-real reciprocal atoms and negative real reciprocal atoms are excluded by polynomial isolation. Thus all atoms satisfy

\[
y_{[w]}\in\mathbb R_{>0}.
\]

Since

\[
y=-w^{-2}>0
\]

implies

\[
w\in i\mathbb R,
\]

all zeros lie on the critical line. Hence \(Q_0\)-positivity implies RH.

So the proof task is to prove \(Q_0[p]\ge0\) for all real polynomials \(p\), from completed zeta data, without assuming the zero locations.

## Theorem 2 — Folded reciprocal divisor and trace weight

**Statement.**
If \(w\neq0\) is a zero of \(\Xi(w)\) of multiplicity \(m\), then \(-w\) is also a zero of multiplicity \(m\). In the reciprocal coordinate

\[
y=-w^{-2},
\]

the pair \(\{w,-w\}\) folds to one \(y\)-atom of weight \(2m\).

Therefore

\[
T_k=\sum_{[w]}2m_w\,(-w^{-2})^k.
\]

**Proof.**
The functional equation gives

\[
\Xi(w)=\Xi(-w).
\]

Therefore, if

\[
\Xi(w)=(w-w_0)^m g(w),\qquad g(w_0)\neq0,
\]

then by evenness \(-w_0\) is also a zero of multiplicity \(m\). But

\[
-(-w_0)^{-2}=-w_0^{-2}.
\]

Thus the orbit \([w_0]=\{w_0,-w_0\}\) contributes twice the multiplicity to the same reciprocal atom.

## Theorem 3 — Why \(y=-(\rho-\tfrac12)^{-2}\) is forced

**Statement.**
The coordinate

\[
y_\rho=-(\rho-\tfrac12)^{-2}
\]

is forced by three independent requirements:

1. center \(1/2\) is forced by FE folding;
2. power \(-2\) is forced by parity;
3. the minus sign is forced by positivity for RH-line zeros.

**Proof.**

Let

\[
w=\rho-\frac12.
\]

**Center.** Suppose we use a shifted center \(c=1/2+\varepsilon\). The two FE partners become

\[
w-\varepsilon,\qquad -w-\varepsilon.
\]

Reciprocal folding would require

\[
-(w-\varepsilon)^{-2}=-(-w-\varepsilon)^{-2}.
\]

This is equivalent to

\[
(w-\varepsilon)^2=(-w-\varepsilon)^2.
\]

Expanding gives

\[
w^2-2w\varepsilon+\varepsilon^2
=
w^2+2w\varepsilon+\varepsilon^2,
\]

hence

\[
4w\varepsilon=0.
\]

For nonzero zeros \(w\neq0\), this forces \(\varepsilon=0\). Thus the center is \(1/2\).

**Power.** Odd reciprocal moments vanish by FE symmetry:

\[
\sum_{w}w^{-(2k+1)}=0.
\]

So \(w^{-1}\), \(w^{-3}\), etc. carry no bridge information. The first parity-surviving reciprocal moment is \(w^{-2}\).

**Sign.** On RH,

\[
w=i\gamma.
\]

Then

\[
-w^{-2}=-(i\gamma)^{-2}=\gamma^{-2}>0.
\]

Thus the minus sign is forced if the RH-line divisor is to become a positive Stieltjes measure.

## Theorem 4 — Odd reciprocal moments vanish

**Statement.**

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{w}m_w w^{-(2k+1)}=0.
}
\]

**Proof.**
The divisor is invariant under \(w\mapsto -w\), with equal multiplicity. For each pair \(\{w,-w\}\),

\[
w^{-(2k+1)}+(-w)^{-(2k+1)}
=
w^{-(2k+1)}-w^{-(2k+1)}
=
0.
\]

Summing over FE orbits gives the result.

## Theorem 5 — Even quotient dictionary

**Statement.**
Let

\[
\Phi(z)=\Xi(\sqrt z).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
T_k=-2k(-1)^k[z^k]\log\Phi(z).
}
\]

**Proof.**
Since \(\Xi(w)\) is even, it factors through \(z=w^2\):

\[
\Xi(w)=\Phi(w^2).
\]

For FE orbits \([w]\),

\[
\Phi(z)=\Phi(0)\prod_{[w]}\left(1-\frac{z}{w^2}\right)^{m_w},
\]

where \(m_w\) is the quotient multiplicity per orbit. Then

\[
\log\Phi(z)
=
\log\Phi(0)+\sum_{[w]}m_w\log\left(1-\frac{z}{w^2}\right).
\]

Expanding,

\[
\log\Phi(z)
=
\log\Phi(0)
-
\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{z^k}{k}
\sum_{[w]}m_w w^{-2k}.
\]

But the bridge moments use trace weight \(2m_w\), so

\[
T_k
=
\sum_{[w]}2m_w(-w^{-2})^k
=
2(-1)^k\sum_{[w]}m_w w^{-2k}.
\]

Thus

\[
[z^k]\log\Phi(z)
=
-\frac{1}{k}\sum_{[w]}m_w w^{-2k}
=
-\frac{1}{2k}(-1)^kT_k.
\]

Rearranging,

\[
T_k=-2k(-1)^k[z^k]\log\Phi(z).
\]

## Theorem 6 — \(T_0\) obstruction

**Statement.**

\[
\boxed{
T_0=\sum_{\rho}1=\infty.
}
\]

Thus the ordinary unshifted Hankel tower beginning at \(T_0\) is not an ordinary finite moment matrix for the full reciprocal zero divisor.

**Proof.**
The Riemann-von Mangoldt counting formula implies the number of zeros of \(\xi\) with \(|\Im \rho|\le G\) grows like

\[
\frac{G}{2\pi}\log\frac{G}{2\pi}+O(G).
\]

Thus there are infinitely many nontrivial zeros. Therefore

\[
T_0=\sum_\rho 1
\]

diverges.

By contrast, for \(k\ge1\),

\[
|T_k|
\le
\sum_\rho C|\rho-\tfrac12|^{-2k},
\]

and the zero-counting estimate implies convergence for \(k\ge1\).

## Theorem 7 — Admissible unshifted polynomial domain

**Statement.**
The formal unshifted form

\[
U[p]=\sum_{[w]}2m_w\,p(y_{[w]})^2
\]

is finite on ordinary polynomials only when

\[
p(y)=yq(y).
\]

In that case,

\[
\boxed{
U[yq]=Q_1[q].
}
\]

**Proof.**
The reciprocal atoms satisfy

\[
y_{[w]}\to0
\]

because \(|w|\to\infty\). If \(p(0)\neq0\), then

\[
p(y_{[w]})^2\to p(0)^2\neq0,
\]

so the summands do not tend to zero and the infinite sum diverges.

If \(p(0)=0\), then \(p(y)=yq(y)\) for a polynomial \(q\). Then

\[
U[yq]
=
\sum_{[w]}2m_w\,y_{[w]}^2q(y_{[w]})^2.
\]

Writing

\[
q(y)=\sum_i c_i y^i,
\]

we get

\[
U[yq]
=
\sum_{i,j}c_i c_j
\sum_{[w]}2m_w\,y_{[w]}^{i+j+2}
=
\sum_{i,j}c_i c_jT_{i+j+2}
=
Q_1[q].
\]

So the repaired unshifted form is exactly \(Q_1\), not a third object.

## Theorem 8 — \(Q_0\) source-aware sufficiency

**Statement.**
For the actual folded reciprocal divisor with positive integer multiplicities, if

\[
Q_0[p]\ge0\qquad\forall p\in\mathbb R[y],
\]

then all reciprocal atoms satisfy

\[
y_{[w]}\in\mathbb R_{>0}.
\]

Hence RH follows.

**Proof sketch.**

If a non-real atom \(y\) occurs, its conjugate \(\bar y\) also occurs with the same positive weight. The evaluation map

\[
p\mapsto p(y)
\]

from real polynomials onto \(\mathbb C\) is surjective when \(\Im y\neq0\), since \(1\) and \(y\) span \(\mathbb C\) over \(\mathbb R\). Therefore one can choose \(p\) so that

\[
\operatorname{Re}\big(y\,p(y)^2\big)<0.
\]

Using polynomial interpolation / localization on the discrete support, one can make the contribution of this conjugate pair dominate over all other atoms. Then

\[
Q_0[p]<0,
\]

contradicting positivity. Therefore all atoms are real.

If a real atom \(y<0\) occurs, polynomial localization similarly chooses \(p\) large at \(y\) and small on the rest of the support. Then the contribution

\[
2m_y\,y\,p(y)^2<0
\]

dominates, again contradicting positivity. Thus all atoms must be positive real.

Finally,

\[
y=-w^{-2}>0
\]

implies \(w\in i\mathbb R\). Hence every zero lies on the critical line.

## Theorem 9 — Non-real \(y\)-pair obstruction

**Statement.**
For a conjugate pair

\[
y=u+iv,\qquad \bar y=u-iv
\]

with atom weight \(\mu\), the degree-one shifted determinant is

\[
\boxed{
\det H_1^{(0)}
=
-4\mu^2|y|^2(\operatorname{Im}y)^2.
}
\]

**Proof.**
The local moments are

\[
T_1=\mu(y+\bar y)=2\mu u,
\]

\[
T_2=\mu(y^2+\bar y^2)=2\mu(u^2-v^2),
\]

\[
T_3=\mu(y^3+\bar y^3)=2\mu(u^3-3uv^2).
\]

For \(H_1^{(0)}\),

\[
H_1^{(0)}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
T_1&T_2\\
T_2&T_3
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Thus

\[
\det H_1^{(0)}
=
T_1T_3-T_2^2.
\]

Compute:

\[
T_1T_3
=
4\mu^2u(u^3-3uv^2)
=
4\mu^2(u^4-3u^2v^2),
\]

and

\[
T_2^2
=
4\mu^2(u^2-v^2)^2
=
4\mu^2(u^4-2u^2v^2+v^4).
\]

Subtracting,

\[
\det H_1^{(0)}
=
4\mu^2[-u^2v^2-v^4]
=
-4\mu^2v^2(u^2+v^2)
=
-4\mu^2|y|^2(\Im y)^2.
\]

## Theorem 10 — Off-line quartet obstruction

**Statement.**
For an off-line FE quartet

\[
w=\pm a\pm i\gamma,
\]

with simple zeros, the local \(Q_0\) degree-one determinant is

\[
\boxed{
\det H_1^{(0)}
=
-\frac{64a^2\gamma^2}{(a^2+\gamma^2)^6}.
}
\]

**Proof.**
Take

\[
w=a+i\gamma.
\]

Then

\[
y=-w^{-2}.
\]

Write

\[
w^2=(a^2-\gamma^2)+2ia\gamma.
\]

Thus

\[
y=-\frac{\bar w^2}{|w|^4}
=
-\frac{(a^2-\gamma^2)-2ia\gamma}{(a^2+\gamma^2)^2}.
\]

So

\[
\Im y=\frac{2a\gamma}{(a^2+\gamma^2)^2},
\]

and

\[
|y|=\frac{1}{|w|^2}=\frac{1}{a^2+\gamma^2}.
\]

A simple FE quartet folds to a conjugate \(y,\bar y\) pair with weight

\[
\mu=2.
\]

Using Theorem 9,

\[
\det H_1^{(0)}
=
-4\mu^2|y|^2(\Im y)^2.
\]

Substitute:

\[
-4(4)\cdot
\frac{1}{(a^2+\gamma^2)^2}
\cdot
\frac{4a^2\gamma^2}{(a^2+\gamma^2)^4}
=
-\frac{64a^2\gamma^2}{(a^2+\gamma^2)^6}.
\]

## Theorem 11 — \(Q_0/Q_1\) independence in source-forgotten moment strings

**Statement.**
For arbitrary moment strings not known to come from the completed folded divisor, \(Q_0\ge0\) and \(Q_1\ge0\) are independent.

**Proof by witnesses.**
Let a finite moment string be

\[
m=(m_0,m_1,m_2,m_3).
\]

The two \(2\times2\) Hankel tests are

\[
H_0=
\begin{pmatrix}
m_0&m_1\\
m_1&m_2
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
H_1=
\begin{pmatrix}
m_1&m_2\\
m_2&m_3
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Take

\[
m=(1,0,1,0).
\]

Then

\[
H_0=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0\\
0&1
\end{pmatrix}\succeq0,
\]

while

\[
H_1=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix}
\]

has eigenvalues \(1,-1\), so it is indefinite.

Conversely, one can choose a boundary family where \(H_1\) passes while \(H_0\) fails. Therefore neither condition implies the other in the source-forgotten setting.

The source-aware completed divisor carries additional structure; in that setting all-order \(Q_0\) positivity alone is decisive.

## Theorem 12 — Line-local curvature insufficiency

**Statement.**
The condition

\[
\Phi_2(t)<0\quad\forall t
\]

is necessary under RH but not sufficient for RH.

**Proof / explanation.**
Define

\[
\Phi_2(t)=\sum_\rho\left(\frac12+it-\rho\right)^{-2}.
\]

By harmonicity of \(\log|\xi|\),

\[
\operatorname{Re}\Phi_2(t)
=
\partial_\sigma^2\log|\xi(\sigma+it)|\big|_{\sigma=1/2}
=
-\partial_t^2\log|\xi(1/2+it)|.
\]

Thus \(\Phi_2<0\) says the critical line is a crest of \(\log|\xi|\).

An off-line mirror pair with half-width \(a\) contributes

\[
C_a(\tau)=\frac{2(a^2-\tau^2)}{(a^2+\tau^2)^2},
\]

with peak

\[
C_a(0)=\frac{2}{a^2}.
\]

Near-line zeros are detected because \(C_a(0)\to\infty\) as \(a\to0\). But far-off-line high zeros can be masked by the growing negative on-line background. Therefore \(\Phi_2<0\) is a necessary crest condition, not a source-faithful RH equivalence.

The faithful global condition is \(Q_0[p]\ge0\) for every real polynomial \(p\), not \(\Phi_2<0\).

## Theorem 13 — Local \(D_2\) detector

**Statement.**
For a rectangle \(R\) whose boundary avoids zeros,

\[
M_k(R)
=
\frac{1}{2\pi i}
\oint_{\partial R}(s-\tfrac12)^k\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(s)\,ds
=
\sum_{\rho\in R}m_\rho(\rho-\tfrac12)^k.
\]

Moreover,

\[
\boxed{
D_2(R)
=
\operatorname{Re}M_2(R)
+
\sum_{\rho\in R}m_\rho\gamma_\rho^2
=
\sum_{\rho\in R}m_\rho(\beta_\rho-\tfrac12)^2.
}
\]

Thus

\[
D_2(R)=0
\iff
\text{all zeros in }R\text{ lie on the seam.}
\]

**Proof.**
The first identity is the residue theorem applied to \(\xi'/\xi\), whose residues at zeros are their multiplicities.

Writing

\[
\rho-\frac12=\alpha_\rho+i\gamma_\rho,
\qquad
\alpha_\rho=\beta_\rho-\frac12,
\]

we have

\[
(\rho-\tfrac12)^2=\alpha_\rho^2-\gamma_\rho^2+2i\alpha_\rho\gamma_\rho.
\]

Thus

\[
\operatorname{Re}M_2(R)
=
\sum_{\rho\in R}m_\rho(\alpha_\rho^2-\gamma_\rho^2).
\]

Adding

\[
\sum_{\rho\in R}m_\rho\gamma_\rho^2
\]

gives

\[
D_2(R)=\sum_{\rho\in R}m_\rho\alpha_\rho^2.
\]

Since all multiplicities are positive, \(D_2(R)=0\) iff every \(\alpha_\rho=0\).

## Theorem 14 — Countable rational-rectangle basis

**Statement.**
The condition

\[
D_2(R)=0
\]

for every admissible window \(R\) is equivalent to checking it on a countable basis of rational rectangles whose boundaries avoid zeros.

**Proof.**
If all admissible windows satisfy \(D_2=0\), then certainly every rational admissible rectangle does.

Conversely, suppose an off-seam zero \(\rho\) exists. Because the zero set is discrete, there is a small rectangle isolating \(\rho\) and avoiding all other zeros. We can choose a rational rectangle \(R\) with boundary avoiding zeros and containing \(\rho\). Then

\[
D_2(R)\ge m_\rho(\beta_\rho-\tfrac12)^2>0.
\]

Therefore failure of RH is detected by some rational admissible rectangle. Thus a countable rational basis suffices.

## Theorem 15 — Finite-passport blindness

**Statement.**
Finite low-order moment passports cannot universally certify seam confinement unless they reconstruct the whole local divisor or include a source-width detector such as \(D_2\).

**Proof idea.**
For a finite local divisor with \(N\) atoms, knowing \(p_1,\dots,p_N\) plus \(N\) can reconstruct the divisor by Newton identities. But knowing only \(p_1,\dots,p_K\) with \(K<N\) leaves positive-dimensional fibers of configurations with the same passport.

Concrete example: an off-seam quartet

\[
\{\pm a\pm i\gamma\}
\]

can be matched in \(M_0,M_1,M_2\) by a seam-supported comparison with adjusted height. Thus low-order data can be blind to off-seam width. The detector \(D_2\), or enough moments to reconstruct the divisor, is required.

## Theorem 16 — Low-order quartet counterexample

**Statement.**
FE symmetry plus a low-order second moment can hide an off-line quartet.

**Proof.**
For the quartet

\[
Q_{\rm off}=\{\pm a\pm i\gamma\},
\]

one has

\[
p_2(Q_{\rm off})=4(a^2-\gamma^2).
\]

Choose an on-line comparison with points

\[
\pm i\Gamma
\]

with multiplicity \(2\), where

\[
\Gamma^2=\gamma^2-a^2.
\]

Then

\[
p_2(Q_{\rm on})=4(-\Gamma^2)=4(a^2-\gamma^2)=p_2(Q_{\rm off}).
\]

So \(M_0,M_1,M_2\) agree, but

\[
D_2(Q_{\rm off})=4a^2>0,
\]

while

\[
D_2(Q_{\rm on})=0.
\]

Thus low-order bulk moments can canalize an off-line configuration.

## Theorem 17 — Li–Chebyshev kernel

**Statement.**
For a folded FE pair

\[
\rho=\frac12+w,\qquad \rho'=\frac12-w,
\]

with

\[
y=-w^{-2},
\]

the pair contribution to Li’s coefficient is

\[
\boxed{
L_n(y)=2-2T_n\!\left(\frac{4-y}{4+y}\right),
}
\]

where \(T_n\) is the Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind.

Furthermore, in the expansion

\[
L_n(y)=\sum_{k\ge1}c_{n,k}y^k,
\]

we have

\[
\boxed{
c_{n,1}=n^2,\qquad
c_{n,2}=-\frac{n^2(n^2+2)}{12}.
}
\]

**Proof.**
Let

\[
q=1-\frac1\rho=\frac{\rho-1}{\rho}
=
\frac{w-\frac12}{w+\frac12}.
\]

For the partner \(\rho'=\frac12-w\), the corresponding value is \(q^{-1}\). Thus

\[
L_n=2-(q^n+q^{-n}).
\]

Now

\[
q+q^{-1}
=
2\frac{4-y}{4+y}.
\]

Therefore

\[
q^n+q^{-n}
=
2T_n\!\left(\frac{q+q^{-1}}{2}\right)
=
2T_n\!\left(\frac{4-y}{4+y}\right),
\]

so

\[
L_n(y)=2-2T_n\!\left(\frac{4-y}{4+y}\right).
\]

Using

\[
\frac{4-y}{4+y}=1-\frac{y}{2}+\frac{y^2}{8}+O(y^3),
\]

and

\[
T_n'(1)=n^2,\qquad
T_n''(1)=\frac{n^2(n^2-1)}{3},
\]

the expansion gives

\[
c_{n,1}=n^2,
\]

\[
c_{n,2}=-\frac{n^2(n^2+2)}{12}.
\]

Thus Li is an infinite signed Chebyshev-kernel transform in the \(T_k\) basis, not a coefficientwise-positive transform.

## Theorem 18 — FID shifted Hankel total positivity

**Statement.**
The shifted FID matrix

\[
H_{\rm FID}[i,j]
=
\zeta(2(i+j)+3)
\]

is strictly Hankel totally positive. This positivity is RH-empty.

**Proof.**
We have

\[
\zeta(2(i+j)+3)
=
\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-2(i+j)-3}
=
\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-3}(n^{-2})^{i+j}.
\]

Thus \(H_{\rm FID}\) is a moment matrix of the positive discrete measure

\[
\nu_{\rm FID}=\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-3}\delta_{n^{-2}}.
\]

By Cauchy–Binet, every shifted Hankel minor is a positive sum of products of generalized Vandermonde determinants with positive weights. Since the support points \(n^{-2}\) are distinct and positive, the relevant minors are positive whenever the row and column indices are ordered compatibly.

This certifies the integer divisor, not the zero divisor. Hence it is RH-empty.

## Theorem 19 — Pole cancellation is not Stieltjes positivity

**Statement.**
Finiteness after cancellation does not imply Stieltjes positivity.

**Proof by counterexample.**
Let

\[
\mu_L=L\delta_0+\delta_1.
\]

Its raw moments are

\[
m_0=L+1,\qquad m_1=1,\qquad m_2=1.
\]

The raw moment matrix

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
m_0&m_1\\
m_1&m_2
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
L+1&1\\
1&1
\end{pmatrix}
\]

has determinant \(L>0\), hence is PSD.

If one naively “cancels” the \(L\delta_0\) mass by setting

\[
m_0^{\rm ren}=0,
\]

the matrix becomes

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1\\
1&1
\end{pmatrix},
\]

whose determinant is \(-1\). Therefore cancellation/finiteness alone does not imply positivity.

## Theorem 20 — CO6 host caveat

**Statement.**
If \(R\) is a commutative ring with \(2\in R^\times\), then

\[
g^2=1
\]

is equivalent to

\[
g=2e-1,\qquad e^2=e.
\]

Thus \(g=\pm1\) only in a connected / idempotent-free scalar host.

**Proof.**
If \(g^2=1\), set

\[
e=\frac{g+1}{2}.
\]

Then

\[
e^2=\frac{g^2+2g+1}{4}
=
\frac{1+2g+1}{4}
=
\frac{g+1}{2}
=
e.
\]

Conversely, if \(e^2=e\), then

\[
g=2e-1
\]

satisfies

\[
g^2=4e^2-4e+1=1.
\]

If \(R\) has no idempotents except \(0,1\), then \(e=0\) or \(e=1\), so \(g=-1\) or \(g=1\).

## Theorem 21 — Boundary-extension / Krein threshold

**Statement.**
For a positive invertible bulk operator \(B\) and vector \(v\), a rank-one boundary extension

\[
B_\alpha=B+\alpha vv^\ast
\]

changes inertia at the threshold

\[
\boxed{
\alpha_c=-\frac{1}{\langle v,B^{-1}v\rangle}.
}
\]

**Proof.**
By the matrix determinant lemma,

\[
\det(B+\alpha vv^\ast)
=
\det B\cdot\left(1+\alpha\langle v,B^{-1}v\rangle\right).
\]

The determinant vanishes exactly when

\[
1+\alpha\langle v,B^{-1}v\rangle=0,
\]

hence

\[
\alpha_c=-\frac{1}{\langle v,B^{-1}v\rangle}.
\]

Crossing this value changes inertia by one, assuming a transverse crossing. Thus the bulk alone does not determine the index; the boundary extension is part of the object.

## Theorem 22 — Finite audit amplification failure

**Statement.**
For

\[
H(t)=I-\varepsilon e^{\kappa t}vv^\ast,
\]

one has

\[
\operatorname{neg}H(t)=0
\iff
\varepsilon e^{\kappa t}\le1
\]

and a negative direction appears when

\[
\varepsilon e^{\kappa t}>1.
\]

For any finite audit scale \(T\), one can choose \(\varepsilon=e^{-\kappa(T+1)}\) so that all checks up to \(T\) pass but failure occurs later.

**Proof.**
On the subspace orthogonal to \(v\), \(H(t)\) is identity. On the line spanned by \(v\), assuming \(v\) is normalized, the eigenvalue is

\[
1-\varepsilon e^{\kappa t}.
\]

This is nonnegative iff

\[
\varepsilon e^{\kappa t}\le1.
\]

Given \(T\), set

\[
\varepsilon=e^{-\kappa(T+1)}.
\]

Then for \(t\le T\),

\[
\varepsilon e^{\kappa t}\le e^{-\kappa}<1,
\]

but at \(t=T+1\),

\[
\varepsilon e^{\kappa(T+1)}=1,
\]

and just beyond it, a negative direction appears.

## Theorem 23 — Seam cancellation mechanism

**Statement.**
The phase-carrier defect, the weld \(A+P\), the superzeta values \(S_k\), and the entireness of \(\xi\) are four readings of one pole-cancellation mechanism.

**Proof / derivation.**
Write

\[
\frac{\Xi'}{\Xi}(w)
=
\frac{\xi'}{\xi}\left(\frac12+w\right)
=
A\left(\frac12+w\right)+P\left(\frac12+w\right),
\]

where \(P=\zeta'/\zeta\) and \(A\) includes the archimedean and elementary completion factors.

Near \(s=1\),

\[
A(s)\sim+\frac{1}{s-1},
\]

from the \((s-1)\) completion factor, while

\[
P(s)=\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)\sim-\frac{1}{s-1},
\]

because \(\zeta\) has a simple pole at \(s=1\). These singular terms cancel in \(A+P=\xi'/\xi\), which is why \(\xi\) is entire at \(s=1\).

Taylor expansion at the seam gives

\[
\frac{\Xi'}{\Xi}(w)
=
-2\sum_{k\ge1}S_k w^{2k-1}.
\]

The separate \(A\)- and \(P\)-halves see the singularity at seam distance \(1/2\), so their coefficients grow on the scale \(2^{2k}\). Their sum cancels to the residue set by the nearest zero, on the scale \(\gamma_1^{-2k}\). This is the value-level version of the phase defect

\[
\Delta=\max(\log|A|,\log|P|)-\log|A+P|.
\]

## Theorem 24 — Fused closed form for \(S_k\)

**Statement.**
For \(k\ge1\),

\[
\boxed{
S_k
=
2^{2k}
-\frac14(2^{2k}-1)\zeta(2k)
-2^{2k-2}\beta(2k)
-\frac{1}{2(2k-1)!}
\left(\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}\right)^{(2k-1)}\left(\frac12\right).
}
\]

**Proof sketch.**
Start from

\[
\frac{\Xi'}{\Xi}(w)
=
A\left(\frac12+w\right)+P\left(\frac12+w\right),
\]

and also

\[
\frac{\Xi'}{\Xi}(w)
=
-2\sum_{k\ge1}S_k w^{2k-1}.
\]

Thus

\[
S_k=-\frac12[w^{2k-1}]\left(A\left(\frac12+w\right)+P\left(\frac12+w\right)\right).
\]

The \(P\)-term gives the derivative of \(\zeta'/\zeta\) at \(1/2\):

\[
-\frac{1}{2(2k-1)!}
\left(\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}\right)^{(2k-1)}\left(\frac12\right).
\]

The \(A\)-term is evaluated using the rational completion factors and the polygamma identity

\[
\psi^{(2k-1)}(1/4)=(2k-1)!\,\zeta(2k,1/4),
\]

with

\[
\zeta(2k,1/4)
=
\frac12\left[(2^{4k}-2^{2k})\zeta(2k)+2^{4k}\beta(2k)\right].
\]

Combining the rational pole terms and the polygamma expansion gives the displayed formula.

## Theorem 25 — Why FID is RH-empty

**Statement.**
FID and reciprocal seam moments are the same reciprocal-squared moment instrument applied to different divisors. Integer-divisor positivity is unconditional; zero-divisor positivity is RH.

**Proof.**
For the integer divisor,

\[
\zeta(2k+1)
=
\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{1}{n}\left(\frac{1}{n^2}\right)^k.
\]

Thus it is the moment sequence of the positive measure

\[
\nu=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac1n\delta_{1/n^2}.
\]

Therefore its Hankel positivity is unconditional.

For the zero divisor,

\[
T_k=\sum_{[w]}2m_w(-w^{-2})^k.
\]

This is a positive Stieltjes moment sequence iff all atoms

\[
-w^{-2}
\]

are positive real, which is equivalent to \(w\in i\mathbb R\), i.e. RH.

Thus the two use the same instrument, but on different divisors. Positivity on the integer divisor does not transfer automatically to positivity on the zero divisor. The bridge would have to be the explicit-formula map between them.
