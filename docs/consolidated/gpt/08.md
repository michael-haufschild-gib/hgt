Let the completed divisor be the multiset of zeros

[
\mathcal Z_\xi={\rho}.

After centering at the seam,

[
z_\rho=\rho-\tfrac12=\alpha_\rho+i\gamma_\rho,

RH says:

[
\alpha_\rho=0
\quad
\text{for every nontrivial zero.}
]

So geometrically, RH is:

[
\boxed{
\text{the source measure of }d\log\xi\text{ is supported on the seam.}
}
]

Every APEX-B formulation is a way of asking whether the symmetric-function data of the divisor force that support condition.

The same divisor can be encoded by:

## 2. The cleanest mathematical picture

The wall is a **moment/support problem**.

A moment problem asks:

> Given the moments of a measure, where can the measure live?

Here the measure is the zero/source measure of (\xi). The desired support is the seam

[
\Re s=\frac12.
]

The local version is your contour-moment detector:

[
D_2(R)=\sum_{\rho\in R}m_\rho(\beta_\rho-\tfrac12)^2.
]

This is zero exactly when all sources in the window are seam-confined.

The global version is:

[
\boxed{
\text{prove the regularized global off-seam width is zero.}
}
]

Equivalently:

[
\sum_\rho^{\mathrm{reg}}(\beta_\rho-\tfrac12)^2=0,
]

or the appropriate Li/Weil/Hankel/Jensen positivity condition holds uniformly.

That is the wall.

The hard word is **uniformly**. The big-picture document says the APEX-B wall is the uniform-in-(d) Hankel/Laguerre positivity problem, with fixed/low-order shadows not enough; the open content is the uniformity in (d), not isolated finite tests.

### A. Divisor support wall

This is the best **bridge instrument** version because it is source-accountable and comes directly from (\xi'/\xi).

### C. Li coefficient wall

Li coefficients are symmetric power-sum-type functions of the divisor in a transformed coordinate. Positivity of all Li coefficients is an RH-equivalent global condition.

This is the “one-dimensional sequence” face of the same wall.

---

### D. Jensen / Laguerre–Pólya wall

Jensen polynomials encode coefficient/derivative data of (\xi). Hyperbolicity of all the relevant Jensen polynomials is another way of saying the completed entire function sits in the Laguerre–Pólya class, i.e. all zeros lie on the seam after the (t)-variable transform.

This is the “polynomial approximation / hyperbolicity” face of the wall.

---

### E. Hankel / moment-matrix wall

The Hankel determinants are positivity tests on moment sequences. If the relevant moment matrices are positive semidefinite uniformly, the source measure is constrained in the right way.

This is the “moment cone” face of the wall.

---

### F. Weil positivity wall

The explicit formula gives a quadratic form. RH is equivalent to positivity of the Weil form on an appropriate class of test functions.

This is the “test-function / quadratic-form” face.

---

### G. Krein/Sylvester index wall

[
\kappa_-=0.
]

Here (\kappa_-) is the faithful negative index: off-line zero pairs contribute negative directions. This is the “signed form / inertia” face of the wall.

The big-picture document frames RH as (\kappa_-=0), but also warns that measured positivity is not enough; the index must be faithful, not a scalar reporter.

---

### H. Symmetric-function cone wall

This is the abstraction that unifies all of the above.

The divisor gives a point in an infinite symmetric-function space:

[
(P_1,P_2,P_3,\dots)
]

or equivalently

[
(e_1,e_2,e_3,\dots)

The wall is membership in the cone corresponding to seam-supported positive source measure.

In this language:

[
\boxed{
\text{RH = the divisor’s symmetric-function point lies in the seam-support cone.}
}

## 4. What can be decided analytically?

Several things are analytically reachable and should be written as theorem notes.

### 1. The change-of-basis theorem

Prove carefully that the various (a(n)), (P_k), (M_k), Li coefficients, Jensen coefficients, and Hankel moments are connected by explicit symmetric-function transforms.

This does not prove RH. But it proves:

[
\boxed{
\text{these are not separate obstructions.}
}
]

They are different coordinate charts on the same divisor.

This is probably the first clean analytical milestone.

---

### 2. The finite-passport insufficiency theorem

For any fixed finite number of moments, there should be off-seam positive source configurations that match the same finite passport as seam-confined configurations.

You already have the low-order version: FE symmetry plus (p_2) can hide an off-line quartet, while (p_4) or local curvature exposes it.

A general theorem would say:

[
\boxed{
\text{no finite symmetric-function passport can prove seam confinement.}
}
]

Only an all-order / uniform condition can.

This would explain why the apex is uniform in (d).

---

### 3. The local source-width theorem

This is already basically proved:

[
D_2(R)=\sum_{\rho\in R}m_\rho(\beta_\rho-\tfrac12)^2.
]

So:

[
D_2(R)=0
\iff
\text{all zeros in }R\text{ are seam-confined}.
]

The contour-moment note says this is a proved local detection lemma, with the global statement still open.

---

### 4. The smoothing-failure theorem

One-scale smoothing of source density can merge off-seam pairs into a false seam peak. Contour moments do not.

This is analytically reachable in Gaussian / model kernels and experimentally gatekeeper-certified in the corpus.
