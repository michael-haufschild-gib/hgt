## The blocker

The blocker is **not** that the singular pullback is mysterious algebraically. The formal coefficient extraction is clear:

[
h_p(w)=-w^{-2}p(-w^{-2})^2,
\qquad
y=-w^{-2},
]

so on zeros,

[
h_p(w_\rho)=y_\rho p(y_\rho)^2,

[
\sum_\rho h_p(w_\rho)=Q_0[p].

The blocker is that (h_p) is **not in the Weil/de Branges test space**. It has a pole of order (4d+2) at the seam centre (w=0). Classical Weil positivity works for admissible test functions; this (h_p) lives exactly at the boundary where the space must be enlarged. The corpus already identifies this as the source-aware shifted reciprocal test: (Q_0[p]\ge 0) is the target, but it has to come from completion, explicit formula, and canonical boundary extension, not from ordinary Weil positivity smuggled in through an inadmissible test.

So the honest blocker is:

\boxed{
\text{construct a canonical finite-part domain at }w=0
\text{ in which }h_p
\text{ is admissible, and prove the resulting boundary form is source-built and sign-preserving.}

## The precise obstruction

The singularity at (w=0) produces an infinite jet of counterterms. If

[
p(y)^2=\sum_{n=0}^{2d} b_n y^n,
]

then

[
h_p(w)
======

\sum_{n=0}^{2d}b_n(-1)^{n+1}w^{-2n-2}.
]

A finite-part distribution at (w=0) pairs with these Laurent coefficients. Therefore any seam finite part automatically has Hankel shape:

[
\sum_{n}b_ne_n
==============

\sum_{i,j}c_ic_je_{i+j}.
]

This explains why your (E)-tail is Hankel. But it does **not** yet identify the correct (e_n). Many finite-part prescriptions produce Hankel functionals. The theorem needs the **canonical one** selected by (\Gamma), the pole, the functional equation, and the explicit formula.

That is the central ambiguity:

[
\boxed{
\text{Hankel is forced by locality at }w=0;
\quad
\text{the actual Hankel sequence }e_n\text{ is not forced until the boundary domain is fixed.}
}
]

This is exactly why generic de Branges language is dangerous: naming a Hilbert space or positivity criterion is only RH-equivalent packaging unless the space and boundary are source-built. The challenge file explicitly warns that generic de Branges positivity is not progress without source construction and fake-family separation.

## What makes the finite part nontrivial

There are four separate analytic problems hiding under “make (h_p) admissible.”

### 1. The finite part is not automatically canonical

A Hadamard finite part depends on the subtraction scheme unless one proves invariance. Cut out a disk around (w=0), use a Gaussian regulator, use heat regularization, use analytic continuation in a parameter, or subtract Laurent jets by hand: all of these can give the same divergent terms but different finite constants.

Here, changing the finite constant is not harmless. It changes the Hankel tail (E), and (E) carries the sign-essential information. Your computations already killed low-rank/banded approximations because tiny tails flip the sign. So a finite-part ambiguity of the wrong kind can literally decide RH in the model.

The required theorem is therefore:

[
\boxed{
\operatorname{FP}_{w=0}^{\chi}Q_W[h_p]
--------------------------------------

\operatorname{FP}_{w=0}^{\tilde\chi}Q_W[h_p]
]

is either zero, or a known gauge term already accounted for by the (\Gamma)-boundary atom.}

Anything else means the construction is not canonical.

### 2. The boundary atom is known, but the bulk domain is not

You now know the rank-one boundary atom is exactly the first Γ-trivial-zero term:

[
a_*p(y_*)^2
===========

# (2.5)^{-2}p(-(2.5)^{-2})^2

0.16p(-0.16)^2.
]

That is source-built from the Γ-factor. Good.

But the finite-part construction still has to explain why removing that atom leaves a bulk with exactly one negative square:

[
H_d^- = H_d^0-a_*v_*v_*^T,
\qquad
\operatorname{inertia}(H_d^-)=(d,1).
]

Finite computations show this beautifully, including through Davenport–Heilbronn failure. But analytically, this requires a **Krein/Pontryagin domain theorem**, not just a matrix identity.

The corpus already says the Euler/explicit-formula bulk alone does not determine the index; a rank-one boundary change can cross a Krein threshold, so the boundary extension/domain is load-bearing.

### 3. The test space has to be enlarged without zero-feeding

The easy construction would be: define the finite part using the zero-side moments (T_k), then set

[
\operatorname{FP}Q_W[h_p]=Q_0[p]-a_*p(y_*)^2.
]

But that is circular. It reads the divisor first.

The construction must instead start from

[
\Gamma,\quad \zeta'/\zeta,\quad \text{pole},\quad \text{functional equation},
]

then produce the same finite-part form. The minimal proof obligation in the files is exactly this: construct a source-built completed transport/polarization object from Euler product, Γ, pole, and FE alone, prove its faithful negative index equals the off-line zero count, and prove that index vanishes.

So the blocker is not “define some finite part.” It is:

[
\boxed{
\text{define the finite part before reading zeros, and prove it is the same object after reading zeros.}
}
]

### 4. The no-margin makes ordinary closure arguments too weak

The scalar Weyl gap goes to (0^+). There is no spectral gap, no domination reserve, and no compactness cushion. The files explicitly warn that margin proofs are mis-shaped because RH is a zero-margin statement.

So the finite-part space must be closed in a topology that preserves the **sign at zero margin**. A regularized Hilbert-space approximation such as

[
H+\epsilon I
]

is not acceptable unless one proves it is a congruent regularization of the same Krein object. Otherwise it can manufacture positivity.

This is why the construction must be Krein/de Branges/Pontryagin, not ordinary Hilbert completion.

## Analytical steps that get closer

### Step 1 — Define admissible approximants and prove finite-part invariance

Choose a family of genuinely admissible Weil tests (h_{p,\epsilon,R}) approximating (h_p), for example by cutting out (w=0) at scale (\epsilon) and adding a large-scale decay/window parameter (R). The target is an asymptotic expansion

[
Q_W[h_{p,\epsilon,R}]
=====================

\sum_{m} A_m[p]\epsilon^{-m}
+
B[p]\log\epsilon
+
C_R[p]
+
o(1).
]

Then take (R\to\infty) and show the finite constant

[
C[p]=\lim_{R\to\infty}C_R[p]
]

exists and is independent of the regulator, modulo explicitly allowed Γ/gauge counterterms.

This is the first paper theorem. If regulator independence fails, the route dies or needs a more rigid canonical regularization.

### Step 2 — Prove a local Green identity at the seam

The finite-part construction should produce a Green identity of the form

[
\mathcal Q_{\max}[f,g]-\mathcal Q_{\min}[f,g]
=============================================

## \langle \Gamma_1 f,\Gamma_0 g\rangle

\langle \Gamma_0 f,\Gamma_1 g\rangle
]

with boundary traces at (w=0). For the singular pullbacks (h_p), the relevant trace should collapse to the evaluation vector

[
p\mapsto p(y_*),
\qquad
y_*=-0.16.
]

The Γ-factor must then select the boundary coefficient

[
a_*=0.16.
]

This turns the current finite matrix identity

[
H_d^0=H_d^-+a_*v_*v_*^T
]

into an operator-domain statement.

### Step 3 — Source-identify the Hankel tail

Because (E_{ij}=f(i+j)), define

[
F_E(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}e_nz^n,
\qquad
e_{i+j}=E_{ij}.
]

The analytic target is to derive (F_E) from a finite-part transform of the completed logarithmic derivative:

[
\frac{\Xi'}{\Xi}(w)
===================

A(w)+P(w).
]

In the protector coordinate

[
x=y-y_*=-w^{-2}-y_*,
]

the finite-part distribution should satisfy

[
\mu_E[x^n]=e_n.
]

So the paper lemma should become:

[
\boxed{
F_E
===

\operatorname{FP}_{w=0}
\left[
\text{explicit transform of }
\frac{\Xi'}{\Xi}(w)
\text{ after subtracting Γ-boundary and Jacobi core}
\right].
}
]

This is now the most important identity to derive.

### Step 4 — Prove the one-defect theorem in the limit, not just finite sections

For each (d), computations show

[
\operatorname{inertia}(H_d^-)=(d,1).
]

But the actual theorem needs a projective/infinite-dimensional statement:

[
\kappa_-(H^-)=1.
]

In Krein-Langer language, that means the bulk finite-part functional belongs to a generalized Nevanlinna class with exactly one negative square, probably (N_1), before the Γ-boundary repair.

The repair then says

[
H^0=H^-+a_*vv^T
]

has no negative squares exactly when

[
a_*+\frac1{m(y_*)}>0.
]

This connects the finite matrix story to a genuine Krein/de Branges theorem.

### Step 5 — Prove the scalar Weyl inequality as a source theorem

Once the domain and (m)-function exist, the final sign theorem is:

[
\boxed{
a_*+\frac1{m_d(y_*)}>0
\quad\forall d.
}
]

The corpus’s guardrail is that this must be source-built, not an RH-equivalent restatement. The explicit bridge target is (Q_0[p]\ge0) from completion, explicit formula, and boundary extension; once that holds, RH follows immediately.

# Computations that would actually tell us where to go

These are not more “margin decay” runs. They test the construction itself.

## 1. Regulator-dependence experiment

Define several admissible regulator families:

[
h_{p,\epsilon}^{(\chi)}(w)
==========================

h_p(w)\bigl(1-\chi(w/\epsilon)\bigr)\psi_R(w),
]

with different even cutoff functions (\chi), and also use heat/Gaussian regulators such as

[
h_{p,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{heat}}(w)
=================================

h_p(w)e^{-\epsilon/w^2}\psi_R(w)
]

where meaningful after sector choices. For each regulator, compute the explicit-formula side, subtract divergent powers and logarithmic terms, and extract the finite matrix

[
E_d^{(\chi)}.
]

Then compare:

[
E_d^{(\chi)}-E_d^{(\tilde \chi)}.
]

Possible outcomes:

* **Same (E) to precision:** strong evidence the finite part is canonical.
* **Difference is finite-rank/gauge and explicitly Γ-controlled:** still viable.
* **Difference changes the scalar gap (s_d):** route in current form is dead.
* **Difference is Hankel but not source-identifiable:** finite part is underdetermined.

This is genuinely informative because the outcome depends on the analytic regularization, not on an LLM-predictable identity.

## 2. Order-of-limits experiment

There are at least two limits:

[
\epsilon\to0
\quad\text{near }w=0,
\qquad
R\to\infty
\quad\text{global admissibility/window limit}.
]

Compute

[
\lim_{\epsilon\to0}\lim_{R\to\infty} \operatorname{FP}Q_W[h_{p,\epsilon,R}]
]

and

[
\lim_{R\to\infty}\lim_{\epsilon\to0} \operatorname{FP}Q_W[h_{p,\epsilon,R}].
]

If they disagree, the seam finite part is Stokes/order-contaminated. That would not necessarily kill the route, but it would mean the boundary construction must name the order of limits as part of the domain.

The files already warn that boundary-contaminated analogies fail unless the boundary correction is named.

## 3. Generate (F_E(z)) and do singularity archaeology

From the computed Hankel tail,

[
E_{ij}=e_{i+j},
]

form

[
F_E(z)=\sum_{n\ge0} e_n z^n.
]

Then run Padé/Borel/series acceleration on (F_E). Ask what singularities it sees.

Predictions worth testing:

* A singularity corresponding to the seam centre (w=0), i.e. (y=\infty), would support the seam-distribution story.
* Singularities at the trivial-zero images (y_j) mean (E) still contains Γ-tail leakage.
* Singularities tied to prime scales (\log p) mean (E) is more prime-source than seam-source.
* A natural boundary / unstable Padé cloud means the tail is not a simple finite boundary distribution.

This can genuinely surprise us: we do not know the analytic type of (F_E).

## 4. Compare (E) across ζ, real Dirichlet (L), complex characters, and DH

You already checked the rank-one boundary theorem for real primitive characters. Now compute the Hankel-tail generating function (F_{E,\chi}).

Normalize by the Γ-selected point

[

]

Then compare:

[
F_{E,\chi}(z)
]

after the natural Γ/parity scaling.

Possible outcomes:

* **Same normalized tail class for genuine Euler products:** strong source-universality.
* **DH tail has the same formal Hankel structure but scalar gap crosses:** ideal fake-family language.
* **DH tail differs before the gap:** fake separation occurs at the tail identity, not only at (s_d).
* **Complex characters break the rank-one story:** route is limited to self-dual real cases or needs product completion (\Lambda(s,\chi)\Lambda(s,\bar\chi)).

This is not theatre because the DH (d=62) crossing already shows fake failure in the scalar gap; the question is whether the failure is visible earlier in the tail’s source identity.

## 5. Generalized Nevanlinna (N_1) test

Use finite approximants of the scalar Weyl function (m_d(z)) and test the Pick kernel

[
\frac{m_d(z_i)-\overline{m_d(z_j)}}{z_i-\overline{z_j}}
]

on samples (z_i\in\mathbb C^+). Count negative squares.

The Krein route predicts a stable one-negative-square structure before repair, and zero after the Γ-boundary repair. If the number of negative squares grows with (d), the simple (N_1) story is wrong.

Possible outcomes:

* **Exactly one negative square stably:** strong Krein-Langer evidence.
* **One for ζ/genuine (L), more for DH after threshold:** excellent fake-family separation.
* **Many negative squares despite finite (H^-) inertia ((d,1)):** finite section story does not lift to a clean Nevanlinna carrier.

## 6. Zero-free gauge audit

Multiply the completed function by a zero-free even factor:

[
\Xi(w)\mapsto e^{q(w^2)}\Xi(w).
]

Zeros do not move, but low-order logarithmic derivatives and finite parts change. The files warn that low-order charts are gauge-sensitive and need canonical normalization.

Compute how (E), (m_d), and (s_d) transform under simple gauges:

[
q(w^2)=cw^2,\quad c w^4,\quad c_1w^2+c_2w^4.
]

A valid construction should either:

[
\text{be invariant}
]

or change only by a known finite-rank counterterm removed by canonical ξ-normalization.

If the scalar gap can be moved arbitrarily by a zero-free gauge, the finite-part construction is not divisor-canonical.

If the finite (m_d) do not behave like Padé/Weyl approximants to a single function, the operator-face lift is wrong.

## 10. Test whether (\mu_E) is a distribution at infinity

Since (w=0) corresponds to

[

Try to reconstruct (\mu_E) as:

If this works, the seam-centre story becomes concrete. If (E) instead reconstructs as a signed measure over a finite interval, the seam-centre interpretation is probably wrong.

## Where not to go

Do **not** spend much more time on banded Jacobi truncations, low-rank truncations, or raw condition numbers. Your own runs already show that the tail is mandatory and that every rank-(r<d+1) truncation gives the wrong sign. The bridge files also record that atom deflation and Christoffel transforms worsen conditioning because the reciprocal atoms cluster toward the hard edge.

Do **not** try to prove a margin lower bound. The scalar gap tends to zero; the theorem must preserve sign with no reserve.

Do **not** try to complete the square by inventing a second positive prime measure. The partial square identity is real, but the prime residual is high-rank and predominantly negative; the files explicitly say the prime side supplies no independent positive measure, so a full square identity from source would already be RH.

Do **not** use generic de Branges positivity as the conclusion. The construction has to build the space, domain, boundary, and finite part from source data. Otherwise it is just another equivalent RH criterion.
