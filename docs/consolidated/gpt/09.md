# Atlas v0: one divisor, many coordinate systems

Let

[
\mathcal D_R=\sum_{\rho\in R}m_\rho[\rho]
]

be the finite divisor inside a rectangle (R), with boundary avoiding zeros, and center every zero at the seam:

[
z_\rho=\rho-\frac12=\alpha_\rho+i\gamma_\rho,
\qquad
\alpha_\rho=\beta_\rho-\frac12.
]

The source-accountable bridge moments are

[
M_k(R)
======

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\partial R}
(s-\tfrac12)^k\frac{\xi'}{\xi}(s),ds.
]

By the residue theorem,

[
M_k(R)=\sum_{\rho\in R}m_\rho z_\rho^k.
]

So (M_k) is exactly the divisor power sum

[
p_k(\mathcal D_R)=\sum_\rho m_\rho z_\rho^k.
]

Your contour-moment note already identifies this as the clean Conjecture-C bridge-side representative of the relocated (\mathfrak T_\xi), with (D_2) as the corrected off-seam source-width defect.

The atlas is therefore built over the single coordinate family

[
p_k=\sum_\rho m_\rho z_\rho^k.
]

Everything else is a coordinate transform of ({p_k}).

---

## 1. The atlas between the names

### A. Contour moments

[
M_k(R)=p_k(\mathcal D_R).
]

This is the source-accountable coordinate because it is recovered from (\xi'/\xi), not by handing in zeros.

---

### B. Divisor power sums

[
p_k=\sum_\rho m_\rho z_\rho^k.
]

This is the base coordinate chart.

The generating function is

[
P(t)=\sum_{k\ge0}p_k t^k
========================

\sum_\rho \frac{m_\rho}{1-tz_\rho}.
]

Equivalently,

[
\log E(t)
=========

-\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{p_k}{k}t^k,
]

where

[
E(t)=\prod_\rho(1-z_\rho t)^{m_\rho}.
]

---

### C. Elementary symmetric functions / Newton companions

Define

[
E(t)=\prod_\rho(1-z_\rho t)^{m_\rho}
====================================

\sum_{n\ge0}(-1)^n e_n t^n.
]

Then the (e_n) are the elementary symmetric functions of the centered divisor.

Newton identities give the atlas map:

[
n e_n
=====

\sum_{j=1}^n(-1)^{j-1}e_{n-j}p_j.
]

Conversely,

[
p_n
===

(-1)^{n-1}n e_n
+
\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{j-1}e_j p_{n-j}.
]

So your (a(n)), if defined as elementary/Newton companion coefficients, are not a separate wall. They are just the (e_n) chart, up to sign and normalization.

---

### D. Complete symmetric functions

Define

[
H(t)=\prod_\rho(1-z_\rho t)^{-m_\rho}
=====================================

\sum_{n\ge0}h_n t^n.
]

Then

[
\log H(t)=\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{p_k}{k}t^k.
]

Thus (h_n) are also polynomial functions of (p_1,\dots,p_n).

---

### E. Li coefficients

Li coefficients are power sums in a transformed divisor coordinate. In one standard normalization,

[
\lambda_n
=========

\sum_\rho
\left[
1-\left(1-\frac1{\rho}\right)^n
\right].
]

Expanding,

[
\lambda_n
=========

-\sum_{j=1}^n
(-1)^j\binom{n}{j}
r_j,
]

where

[
r_j=\sum_\rho \rho^{-j}.
]

Since

[
\rho=z_\rho+\frac12,
]

the (r_j) are reciprocal-coordinate power sums of the same divisor. So Li positivity is another symmetric-function chart on (\mathcal D_\xi), not a different object.

---

### F. Jensen polynomials

The completed even function

[
\Xi(t)=\xi\left(\frac12+it\right)
]

has a Taylor expansion

[
\Xi(t)=\sum_{n\ge0} c_n t^{2n}
]

and Jensen polynomials

[
J_{d,n}(X)
==========

\sum_{j=0}^d
\binom{d}{j}c_{n+j}X^j.
]

The coefficients (c_n) are determined by the zero divisor of (\Xi), hence by symmetric functions of the divisor. Under RH, this becomes the usual Laguerre–Pólya / real-zero hyperbolicity chart. Without assuming RH, the same coefficients are still divisor-symmetric data.

So Jensen is another coordinate chart on the same divisor.

---

### G. Hankel determinants

A Hankel matrix is built from a moment sequence:

[
H_d=(m_{i+j})_{0\le i,j\le d}.
]

Depending on the entrance, (m_n) may be a Li sequence, Jensen coefficient sequence, Weil moment sequence, or another regularized divisor moment. But once the moment sequence is fixed, every determinant

[
\det H_d
]

is a polynomial in that sequence, hence ultimately a symmetric function of the divisor.

Thus Hankel is the moment-matrix chart on the same wall.

---

### H. Weil quadratic form

The Weil form is a test-function quadratic form expressed through the explicit formula. Its zero-side terms are sums over the divisor:

[
\sum_\rho \widehat{\phi}(\rho)
]

or equivalent transforms. For finite-dimensional test families, this again becomes a matrix built from divisor moments.

So Weil positivity is the test-function chart on the same divisor wall.

---

### I. (\kappa_-)

The faithful index (\kappa_-) is the signed/inertia chart:

[
\kappa_-=#{\text{off-line zero pairs}}.
]

It is not a scalar measured density. It is a source-sensitive signed integer. The docs frame RH as (\kappa_-=0), while warning that measured positivity can miss density-zero off-line dust.

## 2. Atlas theorem

**Theorem.** For any finite divisor (\mathcal D_R), the following data are mutually determined once enough terms are retained:

[
{M_k}
\Longleftrightarrow
{p_k}
\Longleftrightarrow
{e_n}
\Longleftrightarrow
{h_n}
\Longleftrightarrow
\text{finite divisor polynomial}
\Longleftrightarrow
\mathcal D_R.
]

**Proof.**

1. (M_k=p_k) by the residue theorem.
2. (p_k\leftrightarrow e_n) by Newton identities.
3. (p_k\leftrightarrow h_n) by the exponential generating relation

[
\sum h_n t^n
============

\exp\left(\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{p_k}{k}t^k\right).
]

4. If (N=M_0) is the total multiplicity and (p_1,\dots,p_N) are known, Newton identities recover

[
E(t)=\prod_{j=1}^N(1-z_j t).
]

The roots of (E) recover the divisor.

So all these names are coordinate charts on one finite divisor.

For the global (\xi)-divisor, the same statement holds after choosing the required regularization. The open issue is not the algebraic atlas; it is uniform positivity / all-scale confinement. The documents explicitly frame Conjecture B as the one wall behind (\kappa_-=0), Hankel/Laguerre positivity, Weil positivity, and related formulations, with uniformity in (d) as the open content.

# 3. The local bridge coordinate: (D_2)

The first non-blind off-seam coordinate is the corrected second moment.

Since

[
z_\rho=\alpha_\rho+i\gamma_\rho,
]

we have

[
z_\rho^2
========

\alpha_\rho^2-\gamma_\rho^2
+
2i\alpha_\rho\gamma_\rho.
]

Therefore

[
\operatorname{Re}M_2(R)
=======================

\sum_{\rho\in R}
m_\rho(\alpha_\rho^2-\gamma_\rho^2).
]

Correcting by the height baseline gives

[
D_2(R)
======

\operatorname{Re}M_2(R)
+
\sum_{\rho\in R}m_\rho\gamma_\rho^2
===================================

\sum_{\rho\in R}m_\rho\alpha_\rho^2.
]

Thus

[
D_2(R)\ge0,
]

and

[
D_2(R)=0
\iff
\alpha_\rho=0
\quad
\text{for every zero in }R.
]

So

[
\boxed{
D_2(R)=0
\iff
\text{all zeros in }R\text{ lie on }\Re s=\frac12.
}
]

This is the clean local Conjecture-C detector. It is not a proof of RH because the global/all-window statement remains open. Your contour-moment note states exactly this: the residue identity, (D_2) defect lemma, and smoothing gatekeeper are proved/local, while global confinement is the open RH/Weil positivity core.

# 4. Which finite passports are blind?

A finite passport is “blind” if there exist two positive symmetric source configurations:

[
\mathcal D_{\mathrm{on}}
\quad
\text{all on the seam,}
]

and

[
\mathcal D_{\mathrm{off}}
\quad
\text{with at least one off-seam source,}
]

such that the passport gives the same value on both.

---

## Passport 1: exterior winding / total charge (M_0)

[
M_0(R)=\sum_{\rho\in R}m_\rho.
]

Blind.

A seam pair and an off-seam quartet can have the same total multiplicity. Exterior holonomy sees enclosed charge, not source width.

[
\boxed{
M_0\text{ detects count, not location.}
}
]

---

## Passport 2: first moment (M_1)

[
M_1(R)=\sum_{\rho\in R}m_\rho z_\rho.
]

Blind under symmetric configurations.

For a symmetric off-seam quartet

[
{a+i\gamma,\ a-i\gamma,\ -a+i\gamma,\ -a-i\gamma},
]

one has

[
M_1=0.
]

For a seam-confined symmetric pair with matching multiplicity,

[
M_1=0
]

as well.

[
\boxed{
M_1\text{ detects center of mass, not width.}
}
]

---

## Passport 3: raw second moment (M_2) without height baseline

Raw (M_2) is blind.

For an off-seam quartet

[
Q_{\mathrm{off}}(a,\gamma)
==========================

{\pm a\pm i\gamma},
]

the second power sum is

[
p_2(Q_{\mathrm{off}})
=====================

4(a^2-\gamma^2).
]

Now choose a seam-supported comparison with two seam points

[
+i\Gamma,\quad -i\Gamma
]

each with multiplicity (2), where

[
\Gamma^2=\gamma^2-a^2.
]

Then

[
p_2(Q_{\mathrm{on}})
====================

# 4(-\Gamma^2)

# 4(a^2-\gamma^2)

p_2(Q_{\mathrm{off}}).
]

So (M_0,M_1,M_2) all match, but the off-seam width is different:

[
D_2(Q_{\mathrm{off}})=4a^2>0,
]

while

[
D_2(Q_{\mathrm{on}})=0.
]

This proves:

[
\boxed{
(M_0,M_1,M_2)\text{ is blind unless }M_2\text{ is corrected into }D_2.
}
]

This matches your canalization experiment: FE symmetry plus a low-order moment can hide an off-line quartet until localized positivity curvature or higher moments expose it.

## Passport 4: (p_4) exposes the simple quartet

For the same quartet,

[
p_4(Q_{\mathrm{off}})
=====================

# 4\operatorname{Re}(a+i\gamma)^4

4(a^4-6a^2\gamma^2+\gamma^4).
]

For the seam comparison,

[
p_4(Q_{\mathrm{on}})
====================

# 4\Gamma^4

# 4(\gamma^2-a^2)^2

4(\gamma^4-2a^2\gamma^2+a^4).
]

Therefore

[
p_4(Q_{\mathrm{on}})-p_4(Q_{\mathrm{off}})
==========================================

16a^2\gamma^2.
]

So (p_4) catches this particular fake.

But that does **not** mean finite moments solve the problem. It means this fake only evaded the passport up to order (2). For any fixed finite order (K), one can build higher-complexity fakes that match that finite passport.

## Passport 5: any fixed finite moment list below reconstruction order

Let (N) be the number of sources in a finite window.

If one knows

[
M_0=N
]

and

[
p_1,\dots,p_N,
]

then Newton identities reconstruct the degree-(N) divisor polynomial. This is no longer a blind passport; it is essentially the whole finite divisor.

But if one fixes a passport

[
p_1,\dots,p_K
]

with

[
K<N,
]

then it is generically blind.

Reason: the map

[
(z_1,\dots,z_N)\mapsto(p_1,\dots,p_K)
]

has more source variables than moment constraints. Its fibers have positive dimension generically. Within those fibers, one can move sources off the seam while preserving the first (K) moments, unless the passport includes a source-width constraint equivalent to (D_2) in every relevant window.

So:

[
\boxed{
\text{finite low-order moments can detect particular fakes, but cannot certify global seam confinement.}
}
]

Only all-order / uniform data can be equivalent to RH.

That is exactly why the apex is “uniform in (d)” rather than “check a large but finite (d).” The documents describe the positivity apex as open precisely because uniformity in (d) is the content, not isolated finite tests.

# 6. The finite-passport blindness theorem

Here is the clean theorem I would put in the note.

## Theorem: finite low-order passports are not seam certificates

Let (\mathcal P_K) be any passport consisting of finitely many symmetric moments

[
p_0,p_1,\dots,p_K
]

with (K) fixed independently of the number of sources in the window. Then (\mathcal P_K) is not a universal certificate of seam confinement.

More precisely:

1. For (K=2), there are explicit seam-confined and off-seam positive symmetric divisors with identical (p_0,p_1,p_2).
2. For general (K<N), the moment map from (N)-source divisors to (K)-moment passports has positive-dimensional fibers generically.
3. Therefore finite low-order passports can be matched by off-seam configurations unless they contain enough data to reconstruct the whole local divisor or include a source-width detector such as (D_2) in every relevant window.

**Proof.**

* The (K=2) case is the quartet construction above.
* For general (K<N), the variables (z_1,\dots,z_N) have dimension (N) complex modulo permutation, while the passport imposes only (K) complex equations. Generically the fiber dimension is at least (N-K). Hence the passport cannot determine whether every (\operatorname{Re}z_i=0).
* If (K\ge N) and (p_1,\dots,p_N) are known, Newton identities reconstruct the divisor. That is the boundary between a finite passport and a local source reconstruction.

Thus:

[
\boxed{
\text{finite low-order moment passports are blind;}
}
]

[
\boxed{
\text{full local divisor reconstruction is not blind;}
}
]

[
\boxed{
\text{global all-order positivity is RH.}
}
]
