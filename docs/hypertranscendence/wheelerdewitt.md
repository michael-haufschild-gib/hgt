## 1. The core Wheeler–DeWitt / superspace facts that matter here

**Wheeler superspace is not supersymmetry superspace.** It is not a space with Grassmann coordinates. It is Wheeler’s configuration space for geometrodynamics: for a fixed spatial 3-manifold `Σ`, superspace is

[
\mathcal S(\Sigma)=\mathrm{Riem}(\Sigma)/\mathrm{Diff}(\Sigma),
]

the space of Riemannian 3-geometries modulo diffeomorphisms. Giulini phrases it as “metrics up to diffeomorphisms,” and emphasizes that Wheeler saw superspace as the arena in which geometrodynamics takes place.

**The Wheeler–DeWitt equation is the quantum Hamiltonian constraint.** In canonical gravity, one foliates spacetime into spatial slices with 3-metric `h_{ij}` and momenta `π^{ij}`. The Hamiltonian constraint is schematically

[
\mathcal H(x)=0,
]

and quantization gives

[
\widehat{\mathcal H}(x)\Psi[h,\phi]=0,
]

together with momentum/diffeomorphism constraints

[
\widehat{\mathcal H}_i(x)\Psi[h,\phi]=0.
]

So `Ψ` is not evolved by a Schrödinger equation; it is annihilated by constraints. This is the literal source of the “constraint, not flow” analogy in your programme. DeWitt’s original 1967 paper introduced the canonical theory and the supermetric appearing as the coefficient of the momenta in the Hamiltonian constraint. ([link.aps.org][1])

**The DeWitt supermetric is indefinite.** At each spatial point, the space of positive definite 3-metrics is 6-dimensional; the DeWitt metric on metric variations has a distinguished negative/conformal direction and positive traceless directions, up to sign convention. This is why the Wheeler–DeWitt equation often behaves more like a wave equation than an elliptic ground-state equation. Giulini notes regions where the induced superspace metric is strictly Lorentzian with one negative sign and infinitely many positive signs, though other regions can have infinitely many negative signs.

**The “problem of time” is not an add-on; it is the central structural feature.** In ordinary quantum mechanics, time is an external parameter in

[
i\hbar \partial_t\psi=\widehat H\psi.
]

In canonical quantum gravity, the Hamiltonian is itself a constraint, so the physical equation is closer to

[
\widehat H\Psi=0.
]

Isham’s classic review says the problem comes from the conflict between quantum theory’s use of time and the role time plays in diffeomorphism-invariant GR; the Stanford Encyclopedia likewise describes the issue as stemming from preserving diffeomorphism invariance, which deprives manifold coordinates of direct physical meaning. ([arXiv][2])

**Superspace is a quotient, and the quotient is singular.** Because some metrics have nontrivial isometry groups, `Diff(Σ)` does not always act freely. Thus `Riem(Σ)/Diff(Σ)` is generally stratified rather than a smooth manifold. Giulini discusses the frame-fixing extended superspace `S_F(Σ)=Riem(Σ)/Diff_F(Σ)` as a way to remove stabilizer singularities.

**Superspace carries topology and holonomy-like data.** Since `Riem(Σ)` is contractible, the topology of superspace is inherited from the diffeomorphism group. Giulini points out an Aharonov–Bohm-like ambiguity when a closed one-form on superspace is not exact, and relates `π_1` of superspace to mapping-class groups.

**Finite-dimensional reductions are called minisuperspaces.** In quantum cosmology, one restricts to highly symmetric metrics, reducing the full infinite-dimensional Wheeler superspace to a finite-dimensional configuration space, often with variables like scale factor `a`, anisotropy parameters, and homogeneous matter fields. Isham notes that quantum cosmology heavily uses minisuperspace approximations and that the problem of time is central there. ([arXiv][2])

**Semiclassical time can emerge, but only after solving/approximating the constraint.** A WKB/Born–Oppenheimer ansatz for `Ψ` can yield an approximate Schrödinger equation for matter fields with respect to an internal time defined by the gravitational WKB phase. Isham emphasizes that in this approach time is meaningful only when the quantum state has a semiclassical component able to define it. ([arXiv][2])

**Boundary conditions are not decoration.** Hartle–Hawking and Vilenkin-style proposals try to select a wavefunction of the universe by adding boundary/regularity/tunneling conditions to the Wheeler–DeWitt equation. Hartle and Hawking’s original paper states that the wavefunction obeys the Wheeler–DeWitt second-order functional differential equation and proposes a boundary prescription for it. ([link.aps.org][3])

**There are direct bridges from Wheeler–DeWitt to holography and automorphic/L-function mathematics.** In AdS/CFT, “radial Wheeler–DeWitt” equations are used as quantum versions of holographic RG constraints. Separately, cosmological billiard models reduce Wheeler–DeWitt equations to automorphic/Maass wave equations; recent work even relates certain Wheeler–DeWitt wavefunctions in BKL dynamics to automorphic L-functions evaluated on a critical axis and Euler products over complex primes. These are not RH results, but they show that WDW-type constraints can genuinely touch automorphic and L-function technology. ([arXiv][4])
