      Universal Constraint–Orientation Product Geometry
            and Its Canonical Proﬁnite Completion
         Michael Haufschild            GPT 5.6 Sol           Fable 5         Grok 4.5


                                             Abstract
         We construct one meromorphic Herglotz function whose countable translation
     palette is diﬀerentially algebraically independent over C(z) and which simultaneously
     supports every ﬁnite localized Stanley–Reisner diﬀerential matroid. Over this single
     embedded diﬀerential ﬁeld we then construct, for every ﬁnite set S, a cube of 2|S|
     meromorphic representatives. All vertices generate exactly the same embedded diﬀer-
     ential ﬁeld and realize exactly the same ﬁnite constraint geometries. Nevertheless, the
     Herglotz condition selects one and only one vertex. The number of reversed Cauchy
     atoms is the graph distance from that positive vertex and can be prescribed arbitrarily.
     Thus ﬁnite nonlinear diﬀerential constraint geometry and ﬁnite Herglotz orientation
     occur as independent coordinates of a Cartesian product carried by one hypertranscen-
     dental ﬁeld. The resulting pointing theorem identiﬁes positivity as extra geometric
     data: it is not recoverable from the unpointed diﬀerential ﬁeld together with any ﬁnite
     sewn constraint matroid. The ﬁnite products assemble canonically: their inverse limit
     is the product of the space of abstract countable simplicial complexes with the prime-
     labelled Cantor cube. Every point of the orientation factor has a normally convergent
     signed prime-log Cauchy representative, and Herglotz positivity selects the all-positive
     sequence uniquely. Finite chambers remain in one embedded diﬀerential ﬁeld; no such
     ﬁeld-equality assertion is made for inﬁnitely many sign reversals.


1    Introduction
Hypertranscendence begins with the absence of an algebraic diﬀerential equation. Since
Hölder’s theorem for the gamma function [5], this absence has usually been treated as
a property of an individual special function. The geometric question is diﬀerent: what
structures can coexist on one ﬁeld generated by a maximally free hypertranscendental
source, and which structures remain invisible after the source is forgotten?
    Three established theories touch separate parts of this question. Translation univer-
sality and hypercyclicity provide functions with large orbits [3, 10]; hypercyclic algebras
organize nonlinear orbit phenomena [2, 1]; and Stanley–Reisner theory records ﬁnite hered-
itary constraints by squarefree monomial ideals [9]. Herglotz–Nevanlinna theory, on the
other hand, encodes positivity through Cauchy transforms and their kernels [8, 7]. Ra-
tional changes of Nevanlinna functions have an extensive theory of their own [4]. None
of these parent structures by itself asks whether arbitrary ﬁnite constraint geometry and
analytic orientation can vary independently inside one ﬁxed diﬀerential ﬁeld.
    The construction below answers that question. The source is a positive prime-log

5    Rational gauges do not change the ﬁeld
The following elementary diﬀerential-algebraic fact is the mechanism separating the ﬁeld
coordinate from the orientation coordinate.
Proposition 5.1 (Rational-gauge invariance). Let (fi )i∈I ⊆ K be diﬀerentially alge-
braically independent over C(z) and let ri ∈ C(z) for every i. Then (fi + ri )i∈I is dif-
ferentially algebraically independent over C(z) and

                          C(z)hfi + ri : i ∈ Ii = C(z)hfi : i ∈ Ii                    (11)

inside K.
Proof. Every diﬀerential relation involves a ﬁnite jet packet. On its polynomial ring,
substitution is the automorphism

                                  Xi,k 7−→ Xi,k + ∂ k ri ,

whose inverse subtracts the same rational jet. A nonzero polynomial relation after sub-
stitution would therefore pull back to a nonzero relation among the jets of the fi . This
proves diﬀerential independence. Since every derivative of every ri belongs to C(z), each
side of (11) contains the generators of the other.

8    The universal constraint–orientation product
We now collect the preceding results. All existential choices occur before the ﬁnite data
in the statement.

Theorem 8.1 (Universal constraint–orientation product). There exist one bounded gauge
u∗ , one positive prime-log source g∗ , one countable injective translation palette s : U → C,
and one embedded diﬀerential ﬁeld F∗ ⊆ K with the following simultaneous properties.
     For every ﬁnite S ⊆ P, every chamber J ⊆ S, every ﬁnite simplicial complex ∆, every
common translation denominator d, and every ﬁnite family of numerator classes (Ni ):

 (a) the complete chamber palette (gS,J (z + s(q)))q∈U is diﬀerentially algebraically inde-
     pendent over C(z);

                                                8
 (b) it generates exactly F∗ as an embedded diﬀerential ﬁeld;

 (c) the sewn outputs realize the exact equivalence (23);

 (d) gS,J is Herglotz on the upper half-plane if and only if J = ∅;

 (e) the orientation index is κ(S, J) = |J|, the cubical distance from the unique Herglotz
     vertex.

Consequently, the same ﬁeld carries the Cartesian product of all ﬁnite localized Stanley–
Reisner diﬀerential matroids with all ﬁnite orientation cubes, and the two coordinates can
be varied independently.

Proof. Choose the universal palette of Lemma 4.1 and the single source supplied by The-
orem 3.3; this deﬁnes F∗ by (10). For each S, J, the chamber correction and all its
translated derivatives belong to C(z). Proposition 5.1 and Corollary 6.2 prove (a) and (b).
The universal palette contains the facet–Laurent orbit of every ﬁnite complex, so Theo-
rem 7.3 proves (c) in every chamber. Theorem 6.1 proves (d) and (e). The construction
of the sewn rows does not use S or J, and the construction of the orientation cube does
not use ∆, d, or (Ni ). Hence the two projections vary independently, giving the stated
product.

Corollary 8.2 (Arbitrary prescribed orientation index). For every k ≥ 0, the same ﬁeld
F∗ has a representative which realizes every ﬁnite localized Stanley–Reisner diﬀerential
matroid, has orientation index exactly k, and is Herglotz if and only if k = 0.

Proof. Choose any k primes and take their set for S = J in Theorem 8.1.

Corollary 8.3 (Pointing law). Fix a ﬁnite complex ∆ and a nonempty ﬁnite support S.
No invariant that factors only through

    (i) the embedded diﬀerential ﬁeld generated by the complete translated palette, and

 (ii) the ﬁnite sewn diﬀerential matroid of ∆

can recover whether the distinguished representative is Herglotz. Herglotz positivity is in-
stead the unique basepoint of the orientation cube.

Proof. All chambers J ⊆ S have the same embedded ﬁeld by Theorem 8.1(b) and the
same sewn matroid by Theorem 8.1(c). The chamber J = ∅ is Herglotz, whereas every
nonempty chamber is not, by Theorem 8.1(d). Any invariant factoring through (i) and (ii)
is constant on this cube and therefore cannot equal its Herglotz indicator. The positive
vertex is unique, so it gives a distinguished pointing.

10     Carrier selection and scope
Theorem 8.1 separates three levels of information.
  1. The unpointed diﬀerential ﬁeld records the ambient hypertranscendental capacity.

  2. Facet sewing records an arbitrarily prescribed ﬁnite hereditary constraint geometry.

  3. Herglotz positivity records a distinguished orientation of rational Cauchy directions.
The ﬁrst two levels do not determine the third. This is a positive structural law, not
an absence statement: every ﬁnite orientation ﬁber has a unique positive section, and its
distance function is the exact integer κ.
    The pointing law applies directly to any proposed positivity argument whose input
factors through an embedded diﬀerential ﬁeld and ﬁnitely sewn diﬀerential constraints.
Such an argument must add source-sensitive data capable of selecting a chamber. In
arithmetic applications, possible forms of additional data include an Euler- or divisor-
marking, a polarized kernel, or an arithmetic rule selecting the compatible positive section
through the ﬁnite orientation cubes. Theorem 9.1 constructs the universal analytic section;
it does not identify it with an arithmetic source.
    In particular, the theorem does not assert the Riemann hypothesis, does not identify
the zeta function with the source g∗ , and does not construct an arithmetic section of the
orientation ﬁbers. Its consequence for approaches to the Riemann hypothesis is conditional
and exact: if a route attempts to derive the required positivity solely from the unpointed
hypertranscendental ﬁeld and ﬁnite constraint matroids, then its data are insuﬃcient
within the universal family. The route must positively supply a distinguished marking
that is not erased by rational gauge equivalence.
    Two limitations remain. First, the source u∗ is selected from a dense residual set
rather than by a canonical closed formula. Second, the canonical completion preserves the
common embedded diﬀerential ﬁeld at every ﬁnite chamber, but Remark 9.2 deliberately
makes no same-ﬁeld claim for an inﬁnitely reversed sign sequence. Neither limitation
aﬀects the inverse-limit identiﬁcation or its unique Herglotz pointing.
