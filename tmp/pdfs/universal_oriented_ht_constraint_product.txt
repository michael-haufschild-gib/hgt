     Universal Constraint–Orientation Product Geometry,
 Its Proﬁnite Completion, and the Prime–Dirichlet Tail Law

                                             Abstract
         We construct a universal product between ﬁnite nonlinear diﬀerential constraint
     geometry and analytic orientation. In a Herglotz realization, a dense residual phase
     of positive prime-log Cauchy sources simultaneously supports every ﬁnite localized
     Stanley–Reisner diﬀerential matroid. Every ﬁnite residue cube lies in one embedded
     diﬀerential ﬁeld, while the Herglotz condition selects exactly one vertex. The ﬁnite
     products have a canonical inverse limit, the product of the space of countable simplicial
     complexes with the prime-labelled Cantor cube, and the all-positive sequence is its
     unique Herglotz point.
         We then remove both noncanonical boundaries of this realization     P by an explicit
     arithmetic model. For a sign sequence ε on the primes, let Pε (s) = p ε(p)p−s . Over
     the ﬁnite-prime Dirichlet ﬁeld, a ﬁnite family (Pεj ) is diﬀerentially algebraically in-
     dependent exactly when the sign classes [εj ] are linearly independent modulo ﬁnite
     support. Consequently every signed prime-zeta carrier is hypertranscendental, and
     two generated raw diﬀerential ﬁelds are equal exactly when the signs agree eventually
     or are opposite eventually; otherwise their compositum has diﬀerential transcendence
     degree two. The same equivalence exactly classiﬁes the ﬁelds generated by their com-
     plete explicit integer-shift palettes. Those palettes realize every ﬁnite Stanley–Reisner
     constraint geometry for every orientation, while vertical positive deﬁniteness selects
     exactly the all-positive prime-zeta function. Thus the inverse-limit ﬁeld coordinate is
     not left unspeciﬁed: it is the projective tail quotient of the orientation cube.


1    Introduction
Hypertranscendence begins with the absence of an algebraic diﬀerential equation. Since
Hölder’s theorem for the gamma function [6], this absence has usually been treated as
a property of an individual special function. The geometric question is diﬀerent: what
structures can coexist on one ﬁeld generated by a maximally free hypertranscendental
source, and which structures remain invisible after the source is forgotten?
    Three established theories touch separate parts of this question. Translation univer-
sality and hypercyclicity provide functions with large orbits [3, 13]; hypercyclic algebras
organize nonlinear orbit phenomena [2, 1]; and Stanley–Reisner theory records ﬁnite hered-
itary constraints by squarefree monomial ideals [12]. Herglotz–Nevanlinna theory, on the
other hand, encodes positivity through Cauchy transforms and their kernels [10, 8]. Ra-
tional changes of Nevanlinna functions have an extensive theory of their own [5]. None
of these parent structures by itself asks whether arbitrary ﬁnite constraint geometry and
analytic orientation can vary independently inside one ﬁxed diﬀerential ﬁeld.
    The ﬁrst construction below answers that question without selecting a privileged point
inside its canonical generic phase. For every bounded gauge u consider the positive prime-

We now collect the preceding results. The source is quantiﬁed through its canonical phase
rather than selected from it.

Theorem 8.1 (Universal constraint–orientation product). There exist a countable injec-
tive translation palette s : U → C and a canonically deﬁned dense residual phase GU ⊂ B
with the following simultaneous properties. For every u ∈ GU , let gu and Fu ⊆ K be given
by (1) and (11).

complex ∆, every common translation denominator d, and every ﬁnite family of numerator
classes (Ni ):

 (a) the complete chamber palette (gu;S,J (z + s(q)))q∈U is diﬀerentially algebraically inde-

 (b) it generates exactly Fu as an embedded diﬀerential ﬁeld;

 (d) gu;S,J is Herglotz on the upper half-plane if and only if J = ∅;

 (e) the orientation index is κ(S, J) = |J|, the cubical distance from the unique Herglotz

Consequently, every ﬁeld Fu in the residual phase carries the Cartesian product of all ﬁnite
localized Stanley–Reisner diﬀerential matroids with all ﬁnite orientation cubes, and the two

Proof. Choose the universal palette of Lemma 4.1; Theorem 3.3 makes GU dense residual.

construction of the sewn rows does not use S or J, and the construction of the orientation
cube does not use ∆, d, or (Ni ). Hence the two projections vary independently, giving the
stated product.

11     Canonical objects and scope
The two realizations expose the same product geometry at complementary levels.

   1. The Cauchy model supplies Herglotz representatives and exact local pole orientation.

   2. The prime–Dirichlet model supplies the closed-form source P+ and an explicit uni-
      versal shift palette.

   3. Facet sewing records an arbitrarily prescribed ﬁnite hereditary constraint geometry.

   4. The inverse-limit orientation cube carries two intrinsic positive pointings: Herglotz
      positivity in the Cauchy model and vertical positive deﬁniteness in the Dirichlet
      model.

   5. Its raw arithmetic ﬁeld coordinate is the projective tail quotient

                             {±1}P /(ﬁnite changes and global sign).

Thus the inﬁnite same-ﬁeld boundary is no longer left open, even for the ﬁelds generated
by the complete universal palettes. Equality holds precisely along an eventual-equality or
eventual-opposition class; transverse tail directions contribute a jointly independent pair
of complete palettes. This is a positive structural law: the inverse limit is stratiﬁed by raw
diﬀerential ﬁelds, and its unique positive-deﬁnite section meets one distinguished point of
that stratiﬁcation.

                                               15
    The pointing law applies directly to a proposed positivity argument whose input fac-
tors only through an unpointed hypertranscendental ﬁeld and ﬁnitely sewn diﬀerential
constraints. Such an argument must add source-sensitive data capable of selecting the pos-
itive section. In arithmetic applications, the additional datum can be an Euler marking, a
polarized kernel, or another rule that distinguishes the all-positive tail class. Theorem 10.5
provides exactly such a rule on the half-plane of absolute convergence.
    The result does not assert the Riemann hypothesis. The prime-zeta source is related
to the Euler product by
                                        X1
                           log ζ(s) =             P+ (ks),   <s > 1,
                                        k≥1
                                              k

but positivity on this half-plane does not locate the singularities created by meromorphic
continuation. A route to the Riemann hypothesis must therefore preserve the positive
Euler orientation while adding the functional equation and the continued divisor. What
the present theorem rules out is more precise: no route invariant under ﬁnite Euler gauges
can recover the positive orientation from raw diﬀerential-ﬁeld data alone, because it sees
at most the projective tail class. Conversely, every viable route of this type must carry a
marking that survives the passage from ﬁnite chambers to the tail quotient and remains
compatible with the continued divisor.
    The two limitations of the Cauchy realization are thereby resolved rather than hidden
by a completion. The source P+ and the shift code (32) are explicit, and Corollary 10.4
gives the exact complete-palette ﬁeld law for every inﬁnite orientation.
