Universal Constraint–Orientation Product Geometry
in a Single Hypertranscendental Field

Abstract
We construct one meromorphic Herglotz function whose countable translation
palette is diﬀerentially algebraically independent over C(z) and which simultaneously
supports every ﬁnite localized Stanley–Reisner diﬀerential matroid. Over this single
embedded diﬀerential ﬁeld we then construct, for every ﬁnite set S, a cube of 2|S|
meromorphic representatives. All vertices generate exactly the same embedded diﬀerential ﬁeld and realize exactly the same ﬁnite constraint geometries. Nevertheless, the
Herglotz condition selects one and only one vertex. The number of reversed Cauchy
atoms is the graph distance from that positive vertex and can be prescribed arbitrarily.
Thus ﬁnite nonlinear diﬀerential constraint geometry and ﬁnite Herglotz orientation
occur as independent coordinates of a Cartesian product carried by one hypertranscendental ﬁeld. The resulting pointing theorem identiﬁes positivity as extra geometric
data: it is not recoverable from the unpointed diﬀerential ﬁeld together with any ﬁnite
sewn constraint matroid.

Introduction

Hypertranscendence begins with the absence of an algebraic diﬀerential equation. Since
Hölder’s theorem for the gamma function [5], this absence has usually been treated as
a property of an individual special function. The geometric question is diﬀerent: what
structures can coexist on one ﬁeld generated by a maximally free hypertranscendental
source, and which structures remain invisible after the source is forgotten?
Three established theories touch separate parts of this question. Translation universality and hypercyclicity provide functions with large orbits [3, 10]; hypercyclic algebras
organize nonlinear orbit phenomena [2, 1]; and Stanley–Reisner theory records ﬁnite hereditary constraints by squarefree monomial ideals [9]. Herglotz–Nevanlinna theory, on the
other hand, encodes positivity through Cauchy transforms and their kernels [8, 7]. Rational changes of Nevanlinna functions have an extensive theory of their own [4]. None
of these parent structures by itself asks whether arbitrary ﬁnite constraint geometry and
analytic orientation can vary independently inside one ﬁxed diﬀerential ﬁeld.
The construction below answers that question. The source is a positive prime-log

The universal constraint–orientation product

We now collect the preceding results. All existential choices occur before the ﬁnite data
in the statement.

u∗ , one positive prime-log source g∗ , one countable injective translation palette s : U → C,
and one embedded diﬀerential ﬁeld F∗ ⊆ K with the following simultaneous properties.
For every ﬁnite S ⊆ N, every chamber J ⊆ S, every ﬁnite simplicial complex ∆, every

9

Carrier selection and scope

Theorem 8.1 separates three levels of information.
1. The unpointed diﬀerential ﬁeld records the ambient hypertranscendental capacity.
2. Facet sewing records an arbitrarily prescribed ﬁnite hereditary constraint geometry.
3. Herglotz positivity records a distinguished orientation of rational Cauchy directions.
The ﬁrst two levels do not determine the third. This is a positive structural law, not
an absence statement: every ﬁnite orientation ﬁber has a unique positive section, and its
distance function is the exact integer κ.
The pointing law applies directly to any proposed positivity argument whose input
factors through an embedded diﬀerential ﬁeld and ﬁnitely sewn diﬀerential constraints.
Such an argument must add source-sensitive data capable of selecting a chamber. In
arithmetic applications, possible forms of additional data include an Euler- or divisormarking, a polarized kernel, or a compatible section through an increasing family of ﬁnite
orientation cubes. These are design requirements, not conclusions of the present theorem.
9

In particular, the theorem does not assert the Riemann hypothesis, does not identify
the zeta function with the source g∗ , and does not construct an arithmetic section of the
orientation ﬁbers. Its consequence for approaches to the Riemann hypothesis is conditional
and exact: if a route attempts to derive the required positivity solely from the unpointed
hypertranscendental ﬁeld and ﬁnite constraint matroids, then its data are insuﬃcient
within the universal family. The route must positively supply a distinguished marking
that is not erased by rational gauge equivalence.
Two further limitations delimit the present result. First, the source u∗ is selected
from a dense residual set rather than by a canonical closed formula. Second, the theorem
realizes every ﬁnite constraint geometry and every ﬁnite orientation cube, but it does
not identify a canonical inverse limit of them. Constructing a source-deﬁned, compatible
inﬁnite pointing is therefore a natural next problem.
