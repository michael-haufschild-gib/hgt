# Selective Multiscale Arithmetic Positivity (SMAP)

**One question.** Every positivity the corpus proves on the complete arithmetic source also holds
for the bounded twists \(|\lambda|\le\Lambda\) with the same Gamma and pole terms, yet the Liouville
twin is not Weil-positive. Which inequality is true for \(\Lambda\), false for its twins, and
survives the dyadic renormalization under which the complete source is a copy of itself?

SMAP studies Weil's form \(W=\Gamma+\text{poles}-P\) as a translation-invariant quadratic form on
the complete multiplicative source. The form has three energy sources: the finite places (primes),
the archimedean place (Gamma) and the pole. It also has a renormalization group generated by the
Hecke dilations \(T_{\log p}\). The subject is **selective positivity**: positivity that uses a
property of \(\Lambda\) its twins lack, stated so that it can survive infinite exhaustion. Everything
is unconditional; RH appears only inside equivalences.

Namespace `GaloisForLFunctions.SelectiveMultiscaleArithmeticPositivity`; umbrella
`SelectiveMultiscaleArithmeticPositivity.lean`.

## 1. Place in the research

The surrounding programmes have built a complete source and paid most of it:

- complete logarithmic cells carry every mixed Weil entry;
- the Gamma energy pays every adjacent-pair difference;
- high Haar details carry a horizon-uniform floor;
- ordered off-diagonal entries are centered prime-error integrals;
- narrow atomic families are positive up to an explicit spacing fraction;

that inequality. It proves which statements can be selective, where selectivity lives, how large it
is, and how a level of the complete source is paid.

## 2. Why

**Twin-blindness is a dead end.** The high-detail prime bound is an absolute Young/Schur estimate
with no sign condition. The Gamma payments contain no primes. The narrow stability law and narrow
Hodge index hold for every sign twin. On atomic families at any root of finite overlap order,
however, positivity of a bounded weight *is* RH, and it forces the weight to be \(\Lambda\)
(pillar 1). A positivity proof built from estimates shared by a family of twins containing one
weight different from \(\Lambda\) cannot reach an RH-complete family. On complete cells the
high-detail floor holds, with the same index, for every twist \(|\omega|\le1\) (pillar 10). So the
Liouville twin's negative directions live in the low and cross-level residual. Selectivity is a
necessary ingredient, not a refinement.

**The pole hyperplane is where \(\Lambda\) differs.** On atomic tests the continuous pole moment
\(\int f e^{-u/2}\) is a fixed multiple of the discrete harmonic coordinate \(\sum x_n/\sqrt n\)
(pillar 2). On that hyperplane the pole term vanishes, \(\zeta\)'s prime form has a uniform gap
below its ceiling, and the Liouville twin's prime form reaches the ceiling. Sign twins differ from
\(\zeta\) only in the prime form: the archimedean diagonal and both pole moments cancel exactly. So
the gap transfers to the full Weil forms (pillar 3), and on every atomic test it pays the whole
component orthogonal to the pole (pillar 4).

**Selectivity has a size, and it is \(2\log2\).** No uniform gap of \(\zeta\) on the hyperplane
exceeds \(2\log2\): the conductor-2 Ramanujan mode is the unique first excitation above the pole
(pillar 5). RAPT's sharp Poincaré constant attains it. The Liouville twin's gap is exactly \(0\)
(pillars 6, 7). So the selective margin is \(2\log2\), and no sign gauge has a larger one: \(\zeta\) is
extremal among its sign twins. A positive margin is carried by correlation with the pole: a sign
gauge whose harmonic sums \(\sum_{n\le N}\omega(n)/n\) are \(o(\sqrt{\log N})\) sits at the
ceiling (pillar 8). The prime 2 is the bottleneck, and the same prime generates the
self-similarity of the complete source.

**Multiscale structure is exact.** Two adjacent complete cells fill the translate of one cell by
\(\log2\). The complete source is therefore a sum over levels of translates of one generator cell and
of level details (pillar 11), and every translation-invariant form has level-Toeplitz Grams. A
level is paid when its coupling to the coarser part costs less than its detail reserve, and
summable costs keep positivity under infinite refinement (pillar 9). The pole hyperplane is invariant
under the dyadic step, so on it the pole couplings between levels vanish (pillar 12). Off it they
grow like \(2^{|k-j|/2}\) and form one hyperbolic plane (pillar 13): the pole is never a summable
level cost, and one linear condition removes it.

**The coupling between levels splits into three exact parts** (pillars 13, 14). Between separated
levels the Gamma part costs \(4^{-(k-j-1)}\), a summable cost. The pole part is hyperbolic and
vanishes on the hyperplane. What remains is the centered Chebyshev error at the lags between the
levels. On the pole hyperplane, the entire arithmetic content of the multiscale problem is the
signed Chebyshev error along the dyadic orbit.

## 4. Mechanisms

1. **Selection at every root.** The atomic receiver at every root
   (`weilPositive_iff_atomicFamilies_nonneg_of_growthPow`) combined with Weil cone uniqueness
   (`weilPositiveFull_iff_riemannHypothesis_and_eq_vonMangoldt`). A bounded weight is
   atomic-positive at one root iff RH holds and the weight is \(\Lambda\).
2. **Pole-hyperplane identity.** Translation multiplies an exponential moment by \(e^{ac}\). So
   \(A(f_x)=A(g)\sum x_n/\sqrt n\) and \(B(f_x)=B(g)\sum x_n\sqrt n\).
3. **Twin difference.** \(W_\omega(f_x)-W(f_x)=(\int g^2)(P_N(x)-P_N(\omega x))\) below the
   spacing: arch, Gamma and pole cancel.
4. **Gap transfer.** On the hyperplane, \(W=M\). The Markov lower bound keeps \(c_0D_N\), and
   \(D_N\ge G\,\text{mass}\) there, so \(W\ge(\varphi_0+c_0G)\|f\|^2\). The gap enters the finite
   part additively, with no pole cost.
5. **Two eigenvectors meet a hyperplane.** Actual eigenvectors of the full prime matrix at
   \(\log N-E_1\) and \(\log N-E_2\) span a plane that meets the harmonic hyperplane. On that plane
   the form is diagonal, giving a hyperplane vector at \(\log N-\gamma-2\log2\).
6. **Bottleneck.** \(\log m+\tau(m)\ge2\log2\) with equality only at \(m=2\). The three cases of the
   least prime factor are \(p=2\) (forcing \(m\ge4\)), \(p=3\) (\(27>16\)) and \(p\ge5\).
7. **Level payment.** \((\theta\delta t-\beta s)^2\ge0\) turns a coupling \(|B|\le\beta st\) and a
   reserve \(E\ge\delta t^2\) into \(2B+E\ge(1-\theta)\delta t^2-(\beta^2/(\theta\delta))s^2\).
   Iterating gives the margin recursion \(\mu_{L+1}=\min(\mu_L-c_L,d_L)\).
8. **Schur transparency.** A positive definite detail block leaves exactly the Schur-corrected
   coarse block (`PosDef.fromBlocks₂₂`). The correction \(BD^{-1}B^*\) is positive semidefinite.
9. **Doubled cell.** \(C_{2a}+C_{2a+1}=T_{\log2}C_a\), from \(\log(2a)=\log2+\log a\) and the union
   of adjacent intervals.

## 7. Questions and their status

1. **What is the minimal twin-selective quantity?** *Answered on atomic families:*
   - Positivity at a root selects exactly \(\Lambda\), so no twin-blind quantity works there.
   - On the pole hyperplane the prime-form gap is selective. It is exactly \(2\log2\) for \(\zeta\),
     exactly \(0\) for the Liouville twin, and at most \(2\log2\) for every sign gauge. It is \(0\)
     for every sign gauge with harmonic sums \(o(\sqrt{\log N})\).
   - Matched finite flips keep the main term and are separated only by local witnesses (SP, OWU).
   - On complete cells the high-detail floor is twin-blind, so selectivity must act on the low and
     cross-level residual.

   - Between separated hyperplane levels the coupling is the prime comb up to a summable Gamma
     error, so a selective quantity on generator orbits must be a signed statement about the
     Chebyshev error near the powers of 2.

   *Open:* that signed statement, and a selective quantity on coarse sections.
2. **Does the negative index flow monotonically under dyadic renormalization?** *Answered at the
   positivity level:*
   - A positively paid level leaves exactly the Schur-corrected coarse block.
   - The correction only lowers it.
   - Summable coupling costs below the initial margin keep a uniform positive margin at every level.

   - The coupling between separated levels splits exactly: a Gamma part costing
     \(\pi4^{-(k-j-1)}\), a hyperbolic pole part that vanishes on the hyperplane, and the centered
     Chebyshev error.

   *Open:* signed control of the Chebyshev part, and the coupling of touching levels.
3. **How far does the selective gap carry positivity?** *Answered at spacing scale:*
   - the gap is added to the Markov finite part, giving \(W\ge(\varphi_0+c_0G)\|f\|^2\) on the
     hyperplane;
   - on every atomic test it pays the off-pole component (selective Gårding inequality);
   - the hyperplane, and with it these bounds, is invariant under dyadic renormalization.

   *Open:* coarse-scale transfer beyond the spacing scale.
4. **Why \(2\log2\), and why the prime 2?** *Answered:*
   - \(2\log2=E_2-E_1\), the unique first adjunction excitation.
   - No uniform hyperplane gap can exceed it, and RAPT's sharp Poincaré constant attains it: the
     gap of \(\zeta\) is exactly \(2\log2\), that of the Liouville twin exactly \(0\).
   - The prime 2 is the bottleneck of \(\log m+\tau(m)\) and generates the self-similarity.
5. **Which conservation law bounds critical energy from above?** *Open.* AID's monotone limit and
   index costs are lower laws.
6. **How does the archimedean place couple to the finite places along the RG flow?** *Answered
   for separated levels:*
   - every level repeats the generator energies (`weilQuadTw_level`);
   - Gamma pays every detail with \(\log a\) weight (ACS);
   - the pole couples two copies by \(2A(f)B(f)\cosh\) of half the shift, a hyperbolic rank-two form
     that vanishes on the hyperplane;
   - after the first Gamma mode cancels one pole product, the Gamma coupling of levels at gap
     \(2\delta\) is at most \(\pi e^{-4\delta}\) times their masses.

   *Open:* the coupling of touching levels.
7. **In which topology does exhaustion preserve inequalities?** *Partly answered:*
   - the form is translation invariant at every level, while graph norms of orbits escape every
     exponential (SAHT);
   - on the hyperplane, Gamma couplings are summable in the plain mass norm;
   - absolute Chebyshev costs are not summable in any norm that ignores sign.

   *Open:* a sign-sensitive topology, for example a Toeplitz norm along the orbit, in which the
   Chebyshev couplings are summable.

## 9. Open research topics

1. **Selective transfer at coarse scale.** A full-form lower bound on the generator orbits that uses
   the hyperplane gap or the Chebyshev main-term cancellation, beyond the spacing scale.
2. **Signed Chebyshev costs between levels.** The Gamma and pole parts of the coupling between
   separated levels are settled (pillars 13, 14). The remaining part is the centered Chebyshev
   integral, whose absolute value does not decay with the level distance. Find a signed bound
   (cancellation between levels) that makes the costs fed to `rgMargin` summable.
3. **Generator-orbit Toeplitz symbols.** For a hyperplane generator \(f\), the orbit Gram
   \(W(T_{j\log2}f,T_{k\log2}f)\) is Toeplitz (pillar 11) and pole-free (pillar 13). Its symbol is the
   Gamma symbol plus the Chebyshev increments near \(2^m\). Positivity of that symbol is the
   single-orbit question. An exact prime tower is Toeplitz-blind (ACS), and the two-prime torus is a
   Bochner equivalent of RH (ArithmeticPositiveDuality), so a positive answer must use the windows.
4. **Rigidity of the maximal margin.** Among sign gauges the margin lies in \([0,2\log2]\), with
   \(\zeta\) at the top (pillar 7). Every gauge with harmonic sums \(o(\sqrt{\log N})\) is at the
   bottom (pillar 8). Which gauges attain \(2\log2\)? Is the margin a function of the harmonic
   correlation \(S_N(\omega)/H_N\) alone, and is \(\zeta\) the unique completely multiplicative
   gauge at the top?
5. **A virial identity for critical energy.** An upper side for AID's canonical energy.
6. **Selective input on the low and cross-level residual.** The high-detail floor is twin-blind
   (pillar 10), so the complete-source sign is decided by the low details \(a<A\), the generator
   cell and the couplings between levels. On the hyperplane the couplings are the Chebyshev error
   (pillar 14). Find the selective inequality there.

## 10. Success criteria and contribution standard

**Success** is one of two outcomes:

- a selective inequality on a source-complete family;
- a proof that no inequality of a stated class can be selective there.

The minimal convincing instance is a full-form inequality on a cofinal family that the Liouville
twin violates. A margin or a separation of prime forms alone does not count.

**A contribution** meets all of the following:

- It is unconditional.
- It states its source, form, domain, growing parameter and error currency together.
- It says what it gives on the battery: Liouville, scalings \(c\Lambda\), quadratic mixtures,
  finite flips, the golden curve datum.
- It says whether it is twin-blind or selective.
- It keeps the archimedean term and both pole moments, or says which it drops.
- It records the positive survivor of every no-go.

**Principles.**

- Selectivity is a theorem, not a hope.
- Identities first, sign last.
- Pay each reserve once.
- Work per level and per orbit.
- A reformulation is not a reduction.
