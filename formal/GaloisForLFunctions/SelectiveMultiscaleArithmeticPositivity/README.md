# Selective Multiscale Arithmetic Positivity (SMAP)

**One question.** Every positivity the corpus proves on the complete arithmetic source also holds
for bounded twists \(|\lambda|\le\Lambda\) with the same Gamma and pole terms, yet the Liouville
twin is not Weil-positive. Which inequality is true for \(\Lambda\), false for its twins, and
survives the dyadic renormalization under which the complete source is a copy of itself?

SMAP studies the Weil form \(W=\Gamma+\text{poles}-P\) as a translation-invariant quadratic form
on the complete multiplicative source. It has three energy sources — finite places (primes), the
archimedean place (Gamma) and the pole — and a renormalization group generated by Hecke dilations
\(T_{\log p}\). Its subject is **selective positivity**: a positivity statement that uses a
property of \(\Lambda\) its twins lack, stated so that it can survive infinite exhaustion.
Everything is unconditional; RH appears only inside equivalences.

Namespace `GaloisForLFunctions.SelectiveMultiscaleArithmeticPositivity`; umbrella
`SelectiveMultiscaleArithmeticPositivity.lean`.

- the Gamma energy pays every adjacent-pair difference;

## 2. Why

**Twin-blindness.** The high-detail prime bound is an absolute Young/Schur estimate with no sign
condition. The Gamma payments contain no primes. The narrow sign holds for sign twins. Each of
these is therefore valid for every bounded twist with the same Gamma and pole terms. The Liouville
twin is not Weil-positive and is negative on atomic families at every root. A proof of complete
positivity built only from twin-blind estimates is impossible; selectivity is a necessary
ingredient, not a refinement.

**Self-similarity.** Complete cells \(1,\dots,M\) are the \(T_{\log2}\)-orbits of cell 1 and the
balanced details, and \(W\) is translation invariant. Diagonal blocks repeat at every level, and
cross-level blocks depend only on the level difference. The complete-source problem is therefore a
problem about finitely many generator orbits plus their couplings, governed by a renormalization
group.

**Where the selectivity lives.** On ordered pairs
\[
W(f,g)=-\int_0^\infty\big(e^h-\psi(e^h)-c\big)\Omega_{f,g}'(h)\,dh-\Gamma_{\rm tail}(f,g).
\]
The pole product grows across levels and is cancelled only by \(\Lambda\)'s prime main term;
twins with another main term leave it uncancelled. On atomic tests the continuous pole moment
\(\int f e^{-u/2}\) is a multiple of the discrete harmonic coordinate \(\sum x_n/\sqrt n\). That is
the hyperplane on which RAPT proves a uniform prime gap and the Liouville twin reaches the ceiling.
These are the two selective inputs SMAP develops.

## 3. Objects and language

| Object | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Twin** | Weight \(\lambda\) with \(|\lambda(n)|\le\Lambda(n)\), same Gamma and pole terms; form \(W_\lambda\) |
| **Battery** | Liouville twin, scalings \(c\Lambda\), quadratic mixtures, finite prime flips, golden curve datum |
| **Twin-blind inequality** | Holds for every twin in a stated family |
| **Selective inequality** | True for \(\Lambda\), false for some battery twin |
| **Reserve** | Nonnegative quantity that pays exactly once: Gamma variance, Gamma square reserve, prime defect \(D_N\), detail floor |
| **Pole hyperplanes** | Discrete \(\sum x_n/\sqrt n=0\); continuous \(\int fe^{-u/2}=0\); identical on atomic tests |
| **RG step** | Coarse-graining by \(T_{\log p}\) with its defect recorded; \(p=2\) on complete cells |
| **Generator orbit** | \(\{T^j_{\log2}g\}_{j\ge0}\) of a fixed generator \(g\) |
| **Level-Toeplitz form** | \(W(T^jf,T^kg)=W(f,T^{k-j}g)\) on generator orbits |
| **Cross-level coupling** | Off-diagonal blocks between levels; Chebyshev integrals for separated supports |
| **Index flow** | Negative index as a function of the resolution level |
| **Exhaustion statement** | Names source, form, domain, growing parameter and error currency together |

**Words.** *Twin-blind*: valid for all twins of a stated family. *Selective*: separates
\(\Lambda\) from a named twin. *Paid*: bounded below by a reserve that is not spent elsewhere.
*Hardest member*: extremal in a selective invariant (for example \(\zeta\) maximizes the matched
prime finite part).

## 4. Mechanisms

1. **Selectivity necessity.** An inequality that is eventually nonnegative on atomic families at
   any root, for every twin of a family containing the Liouville twin, is impossible. Atomic
   positivity at a root selects exactly the Weil-positive twins.
2. **Pole-hyperplane identity.** For atomic tests, \(\int f_xe^{\mp u/2}=(\int ge^{\mp u/2})\sum x_nn^{\mp1/2}\).
   The discrete harmonic constraint of RAPT is the continuous pole constraint of SSHA, and the pole
   term of \(W\) vanishes on it.
3. **Selective transfer at spacing scale.** On the pole hyperplane, \(W=M\). The Markov lower bound
   keeps the prime defect \(D_N\), and the uniform gap \(D_N\ge g\cdot\)mass holds there. So the
   gap is added to the Markov finite part and positivity extends beyond the Robin–Euler point,
   while the Liouville twin, which reaches the prime ceiling on the same hyperplane, gains nothing.
4. **Dyadic multiresolution.** The exact decomposition of complete cells into \(T_{\log2}\)-orbits
   of finitely many generators plus balanced details; the level-Toeplitz structure of \(W\) on it.
5. **RG index calculus.** For a block form with positive definite new block \(D\), the negative
   index equals that of the Schur-corrected old block \(A-BD^{-1}B^*\). Adding a paid level can
   only raise the index, and it does so exactly when the coupling correction creates new negative
   squares.
6. **Block dominance.** A Schur/Gershgorin criterion for quadratic forms: diagonal reserves
   dominating weighted cross-level couplings give positivity of the whole form.
7. **Prime-2 bottleneck.** The one-prime tower gap \(p\log p/(p-1)\) increases in \(p\), so its
   minimum \(2\log2\) is at \(p=2\), the prime that generates the self-similarity.

## 6. Traps

| Trap | Truth |
|---|---|
| A strong enough twin-blind floor completes the proof | Twin-blind floors also hold for Liouville, which is not Weil-positive |
| Identifying \(\zeta\) by an invariant gives positivity | \(\zeta\) uniquely maximizes the matched prime finite part: it is the hardest member |
| The pole is harmless across scales | Pole products grow like \(2^{\lvert j-k\rvert/2}\) across levels; only \(\Lambda\)'s main term cancels them |
| Translation invariance makes all levels equal | Diagonal blocks are equal; graph norms escape every exponential, and couplings depend on level difference |
| Exponential renormalization tames the orbit | No fixed exponential amplitude gives uniform graph control |
| Positivity at a fixed overlap ratio is RH | The receiver needs unbounded (polylog) overlap; fixed-ratio positivity is a finite regime |
| The discrete and continuous pole constraints differ | On atomic tests they coincide up to a nonzero profile constant |
| Adding positive levels cannot hurt the index | It cannot lower it; it raises it exactly through the coupling correction |
| One-prime orbits behave like continuous orbits | Sampling at \(m\log p\) can alias; single-tower orientation is an open problem |

## 7. Questions

1. What is the minimal twin-selective quantity — the pole-hyperplane gap, Chebyshev main-term
   cancellation, or something unnamed?
2. Does the negative index of the complete form flow monotonically under dyadic renormalization,
   and which coupling term changes it?
3. How far does the selective gap carry positivity beyond the Robin–Euler point, and what does the
   Liouville twin do there?
4. Why is the conjectured sharp gap \(2\log2\), and why does the same prime generate the
   self-similarity?
5. Which conservation law bounds critical energy from above?
6. How does the archimedean place couple to the finite places along the RG flow?
7. In which topology does exhaustion preserve inequalities?

## 10. Success criteria and contribution standard

**Success.** A selective inequality on a source-complete family, or a proof that no inequality of
a stated class can be selective there. The minimal convincing instance is a full-form inequality on
a cofinal family that the Liouville twin violates.

**A contribution** is unconditional; states its source, form, domain, growing parameter and error
currency; says what it gives on the battery (Liouville, scalings, quadratic mixtures, finite flips,
golden datum); names whether it is twin-blind or selective; and records the positive survivor of
every no-go.

**Principles.** Selectivity is a theorem, not a hope. Identities first, sign last. Pay each reserve
once. A reformulation is not a reduction.
