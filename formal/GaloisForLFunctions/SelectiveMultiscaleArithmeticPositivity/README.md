# Selective Multiscale Arithmetic Positivity (SMAP)

**One question.** Every positivity the corpus proves on the complete arithmetic source also holds
for the bounded twists \(|\lambda|\le\Lambda\) with the same Gamma and pole terms, yet the Liouville
twin is not Weil-positive. Which inequality is true for \(\Lambda\), false for its twins, and
survives the dyadic renormalization under which the complete source is a copy of itself?

SMAP studies Weil's form \(W=\Gamma+\text{poles}-P\) as a translation-invariant quadratic form on
the complete multiplicative source. The form has three energy sources: the finite places (primes),
the archimedean place (Gamma) and the pole. It also has a renormalization group generated by the
Hecke dilations \(T_{\log p}\). The subject is **selective positivity**: positivity that uses a
property of \(\Lambda\) its twins lack, stated so that it can survive infinite exhaustion. Everything
is unconditional; RH appears only inside equivalences.

Namespace `GaloisForLFunctions.SelectiveMultiscaleArithmeticPositivity`; umbrella
`SelectiveMultiscaleArithmeticPositivity.lean`.

## 1. Place in the research

The surrounding programmes have built a complete source and paid most of it:

- complete logarithmic cells carry every mixed Weil entry;
- the Gamma energy pays every adjacent-pair difference;
- high Haar details carry a horizon-uniform floor;
- ordered off-diagonal entries are centered prime-error integrals;
- narrow atomic families are positive up to an explicit spacing fraction;
- the atomic RH receiver holds at every polylog overlap.

What remains is one structured residual. The positive estimates above are **twin-blind**, so the
missing inequality must be **selective**, and it must act on that residual. SMAP is the programme of
that inequality. It proves which statements can be selective, where selectivity lives, how large it
is, and how a level of the complete source is paid.

## 2. Why

**Twin-blindness is a dead end.** The high-detail prime bound is an absolute Young/Schur estimate
with no sign condition. The Gamma payments contain no primes. The narrow stability law and narrow
Hodge index hold for every sign twin. On atomic families at any root of finite overlap order,
however, positivity of a bounded weight *is* RH, and it forces the weight to be \(\Lambda\)
(pillar 1). A positivity proof built from estimates shared by a family of twins containing one
weight different from \(\Lambda\) cannot reach an RH-complete family. On complete cells the
high-detail floor holds, with the same index, for every twist \(|\omega|\le1\) (pillar 7). So the
Liouville twin's negative directions live in the low and cross-level residual. Selectivity is a
necessary ingredient, not a refinement.

**The pole hyperplane is where \(\Lambda\) differs.** On atomic tests the continuous pole moment
\(\int f e^{-u/2}\) is a fixed multiple of the discrete harmonic coordinate \(\sum x_n/\sqrt n\)
(pillar 2). On that hyperplane the pole term vanishes, \(\zeta\)'s prime form has a uniform gap
below its ceiling, and the Liouville twin's prime form reaches the ceiling. Sign twins differ from
\(\zeta\) only in the prime form: the archimedean diagonal and both pole moments cancel exactly. So
the gap transfers to the full Weil forms (pillar 3).

**Selectivity has a size.** Every uniform gap of \(\zeta\) on the hyperplane is at most \(2\log2\),
because the conductor-2 Ramanujan mode is the unique first excitation above the pole. The Liouville
twin has no positive gap (pillars 4, 5). The prime 2 is the bottleneck, and the same prime
generates the self-similarity of the complete source.

**Multiscale structure is exact.** Two adjacent complete cells fill the translate of one cell by
\(\log2\). The complete source is therefore a sum over levels of translates of one generator cell and
of level details (pillar 8), and every translation-invariant form has level-Toeplitz Grams. A
level is paid when its coupling to the coarser part costs less than its detail reserve, and
summable costs keep positivity under infinite refinement (pillar 6).

**Words.**

- *Twin-blind*: valid for every twin of a stated family.
- *Selective*: true for \(\Lambda\) and false for a named twin.
- *Paid*: bounded below by a reserve that is spent only once.
- *Hardest member*: extremal in a selective invariant. For example, \(\zeta\) maximizes the matched

  orbit (AES).
- *Level*: one dyadic refinement step of the complete source.
- *Coupling cost*: the coarse margin a level consumes, \(\beta^2/(\theta\delta)\).

## 4. Mechanisms

1. **Selection at every root.** The atomic receiver at every root
   (`weilPositive_iff_atomicFamilies_nonneg_of_growthPow`) combined with Weil cone uniqueness
   (`weilPositiveFull_iff_riemannHypothesis_and_eq_vonMangoldt`). A bounded weight is
   atomic-positive at one root iff RH holds and the weight is \(\Lambda\).
2. **Pole-hyperplane identity.** Translation multiplies an exponential moment by \(e^{ac}\). So
   \(A(f_x)=A(g)\sum x_n/\sqrt n\) and \(B(f_x)=B(g)\sum x_n\sqrt n\).
3. **Twin difference.** \(W_\omega(f_x)-W(f_x)=(\int g^2)(P_N(x)-P_N(\omega x))\) below the
   spacing: arch, Gamma and pole cancel.
4. **Gap transfer.** On the hyperplane, \(W=M\). The Markov lower bound keeps \(c_0D_N\), and
   \(D_N\ge G\,\text{mass}\) there, so \(W\ge(\varphi_0+c_0G)\|f\|^2\). The gap enters the finite
   part additively, with no pole cost.
5. **Two eigenvectors meet a hyperplane.** Actual eigenvectors of the full prime matrix at
   \(\log N-E_1\) and \(\log N-E_2\) span a plane that meets the harmonic hyperplane. On that plane
   the form is diagonal, giving a hyperplane vector at \(\log N-\gamma-2\log2\).
6. **Bottleneck.** \(\log m+\tau(m)\ge2\log2\) with equality only at \(m=2\). The three cases of the
   least prime factor are \(p=2\) (forcing \(m\ge4\)), \(p=3\) (\(27>16\)) and \(p\ge5\).
7. **Level payment.** \((\theta\delta t-\beta s)^2\ge0\) turns a coupling \(|B|\le\beta st\) and a
   reserve \(E\ge\delta t^2\) into \(2B+E\ge(1-\theta)\delta t^2-(\beta^2/(\theta\delta))s^2\).
   Iterating gives the margin recursion \(\mu_{L+1}=\min(\mu_L-c_L,d_L)\).
8. **Schur transparency.** A positive definite detail block leaves exactly the Schur-corrected
   coarse block (`PosDef.fromBlocks₂₂`). The correction \(BD^{-1}B^*\) is positive semidefinite.
9. **Doubled cell.** \(C_{2a}+C_{2a+1}=T_{\log2}C_a\), from \(\log(2a)=\log2+\log a\) and the union
   of adjacent intervals.

## 5. Pillar theorems

1. **Selection at every root** (`PositivityAtEveryRootSelectsTheVonMangoldtWeight`):
   - `atomicPositiveAt_iff`: a bounded weight is atomic-positive at a root of finite overlap order
     iff RH holds and the weight equals \(\Lambda\) on \(n\ge2\);
   - `not_forall_atomicPositiveAt`: no family containing a weight different from \(\Lambda\) is
     uniformly atomic-positive at a root;
   - `not_atomicPositiveAt_liouville`;
   - `riemannHypothesis_of_atomicPositiveAt`: one weight at one root suffices for RH.
2. **The pole hyperplane is the harmonic hyperplane** (`ThePoleHyperplaneIsTheHarmonicHyperplane`):
   - `integral_atomicTest_mul_exp_neg_half` and `integral_atomicTest_mul_exp_half`: the pole moments
     of an atomic test;
   - `weilQuad_atomicTest_eq_markov_of_harmonic`: \(W=M\) on the hyperplane;
   - `weilQuadTw_atomicTest_sub_weilQuad`: twins differ only in the prime form.
3. **The pole hyperplane separates the full Weil forms** (`ThePoleHyperplaneSeparatesTheFullWeilForms`):
   - `exists_weilQuad_atomicTest_ge_of_harmonic`: \(W\ge(\varphi_0+c_0G)\|f\|^2\) on the hyperplane;
   - `exists_weilQuad_atomicTest_nonneg_of_harmonic`: the corresponding gap-paid positivity;
   - `exists_liouville_weilQuad_add_le_of_harmonic`: some hyperplane vector puts the Liouville
     twin's full form below \(\zeta\)'s by \((G-2\varepsilon)\|f\|^2\), for every narrow profile;
   - `exists_pole_hyperplane_separates_full_weil_forms`: one constant \(G\) does both;
   - `exists_liouville_weilQuad_lt_of_harmonic`: on the hyperplane \(\zeta\) is not the minimum of
     its gauge orbit.
4. **The conductor two is the first excitation** (`TheConductorTwoIsTheFirstExcitation`):
   - `ramanujanAdjunctionEnergy_sub_one_ge`: \(E_m-E_1\ge2\log2\), with equality iff \(m=2\);
   - `eventually_exists_harmonic_atomicWeilForm_ge`: some hyperplane vector has
     \(P_N\ge\log N-\gamma-2\log2-\varepsilon\).
5. **The hyperplane gap is the selective margin** (`TheHyperplaneGapIsTheSelectiveMargin`):
   - `zeta_hasHyperplaneGap`: \(\zeta\) has a positive gap;
   - `le_two_mul_log_two_of_hasHyperplaneGap`: every gap of \(\zeta\) is \(\le2\log2\);
   - `not_liouville_hasHyperplaneGap`: the Liouville twin has none;
   - `hyperplaneGap_selective`: all three together.
6. **A level is paid by its reserve** (`ALevelIsPaidByItsReserve`):
   - `two_mul_add_ge_of_reserve` and `rg_step`: paying a coupling, and one RG step;
   - `rgMargin_ge` and `rgMargin_pos`: summable couplings keep a uniform margin under infinite
     refinement;
   - `fromBlocks_posSemidef_iff_effective` and `effectiveCoarse_le`: Schur transparency, and the
     correction only lowers the coarse block;
   - `sum_quadratic_ge_of_blockDominance`: Gershgorin for forms.
7. **The Haar floor is twin-blind** (`TheHaarFloorIsTwinBlind`):
   - `twistedPrimeSum_haarSectionProfile`: the twisted prime charge of a balanced section is the
     quadratic form of \(\sum_m\omega(m)e_m(a,b)\);
   - `twistedPrimeSum_haarSectionProfile_le`: for \(|\omega|\le1\) it obeys \(\zeta\)'s bound;
   - `haarSectionProfile_roughWeilTw_ge` and `exists_haarTail_roughWeilTw_floor`: one lower index
     gives \(W_\omega\ge\) mass on every high section, for every such twist at once;
   - `liouville_haarTail_roughWeilTw_floor`: the Liouville twin has the floor too.
8. **The complete source is a dyadic multiresolution** (`TheCompleteSourceIsADyadicMultiresolution`):
   - `logCell_two_mul_add`: the doubled cell is the translated cell;
   - `sum_logCell_rg_step`: one renormalization step;
   - `sum_logCell_multiresolution`: the full multiresolution;
   - `orbitPairing_translate_translate` and `weilOrbitPairing_level`: the level-Toeplitz law;
   - `weilQuadTw_level`: every level repeats the generator energies.

## 6. Traps

| Trap | Truth |
|---|---|
| A strong enough twin-blind floor completes the proof | Twin-blind positivity at any root of finite overlap order is impossible (`not_forall_atomicPositiveAt`) |
| The high-detail Haar floor is evidence for \(\zeta\) | It holds with the same index for every twist \(|\omega|\le1\), Liouville included (`exists_haarTail_roughWeilTw_floor`) |
| Identifying \(\zeta\) by an invariant gives positivity | \(\zeta\) uniquely maximizes the matched prime finite part: it is the hardest member, not the most positive |
| The separation on the hyperplane is a sign separation | At spacing scale it is a uniform *margin* (`exists_liouville_weilQuad_add_le_of_harmonic`); narrow regimes are positive for twins too. Sign separation appears at unbounded overlap (every root) |
| \(\zeta\) is the minimum of its gauge orbit | Only on nonnegative atomic tests; on the pole hyperplane the Liouville twin lies below (`exists_liouville_weilQuad_lt_of_harmonic`) |
| The pole hyperplane gap can be made large | Every uniform gap of \(\zeta\) is at most \(2\log2\) (`le_two_mul_log_two_of_hasHyperplaneGap`) |
| The discrete and continuous pole constraints differ | On atomic tests they coincide up to the nonzero factor \(A(g)\) |
| The pole is harmless across scales | Pole products of level translates grow like \(2^{\lvert j-k\rvert/2}\); only \(\Lambda\)'s prime main term cancels them (AID Chebyshev identity) |
| Translation invariance makes all levels equal | Diagonal blocks are equal (`weilQuadTw_level`); couplings depend on the level difference, and graph norms escape every exponential (SAHT) |
| Adding a positively paid level cannot hurt | It leaves exactly the Schur-corrected coarse block, which is lower (`effectiveCoarse_le`) |
| Positivity at a fixed overlap ratio is RH | The receiver needs unbounded polylog overlap; fixed ratios are finite regimes |
| One-prime orbits behave like continuous orbits | Sampling at \(m\log p\) can alias; single-tower orientation is open (AOG) |

## 7. Questions and their status

1. **What is the minimal twin-selective quantity?** *Answered on atomic families:*
   - Positivity at a root selects exactly \(\Lambda\), so no twin-blind quantity works there.
   - On the pole hyperplane the prime-form gap is selective. It is positive for \(\zeta\), at most
     \(2\log2\), and zero for the Liouville twin.
   - Matched finite flips keep the main term and are separated only by local witnesses (SP, OWU).
   - On complete cells the high-detail floor is twin-blind, so selectivity must act on the low and
     cross-level residual.

   *Open:* a selective quantity on coarse sections and generator orbits.
2. **Does the negative index flow monotonically under dyadic renormalization?** *Answered at the
   positivity level:*
   - A positively paid level leaves exactly the Schur-corrected coarse block.
   - The correction only lowers it.
   - Summable coupling costs below the initial margin keep a uniform positive margin at every level.

   *Open:* actual coupling costs of the complete Weil form between levels.
3. **How far does the selective gap carry positivity?** *Answered at spacing scale:* the gap is
   added to the Markov finite part, giving \(W\ge(\varphi_0+c_0G)\|f\|^2\) on the hyperplane. *Open:*
   coarse-scale transfer.
4. **Why \(2\log2\), and why the prime 2?** *Answered as an upper bound:*
   - \(2\log2=E_2-E_1\), the unique first adjunction excitation.
   - No uniform hyperplane gap can exceed it.
   - The prime 2 is the bottleneck of \(\log m+\tau(m)\) and generates the self-similarity.

   *Open:* that \(2\log2\) is attained as a gap. RAPT has the statement, with an unfinished proof.
5. **Which conservation law bounds critical energy from above?** *Open.* AID's monotone limit and
   index costs are lower laws.
6. **How does the archimedean place couple to the finite places along the RG flow?** *Partly
   answered:* every level repeats the generator energies (`weilQuadTw_level`). Gamma pays every
   detail with \(\log a\) weight (ACS). *Open:* Gamma and pole couplings between levels.
7. **In which topology does exhaustion preserve inequalities?** *Partly answered:* the form is
   translation invariant at every level, while graph norms of orbits escape every exponential
   (SAHT). *Open:* a topology in which the cross-level couplings are summable.

## 9. Open research topics

1. **Selective transfer at coarse scale.** A full-form lower bound on the generator orbits that uses
   the hyperplane gap or the Chebyshev main-term cancellation, beyond the spacing scale.
2. **Cross-level coupling costs.** Bound the coupling between level \(j\) and \(k\) of the complete
   form, using AID's centered Chebyshev identity for separated supports, as costs \(c_l\) for
   `rgMargin`. Summable costs below the generator margin would give complete-cell positivity.
3. **Generator-orbit Toeplitz symbols.** Positivity of \(\theta\mapsto\sum_me^{im\theta}W(f,T_{m\log2}f)\)
   for the generators. This is a one-prime orbit problem, linked to AOG's single-tower orientation.
4. **The sharp gap.** Prove that \(2\log2\) is attained as a uniform hyperplane gap. The upper bound
   is here; the lower bound is RAPT work in progress.
5. **A virial identity for critical energy.** An upper side for AID's canonical energy.
6. **Selective input on the low and cross-level residual.** The high-detail floor is twin-blind
   (pillar 7), so the complete-source sign is decided entirely by the low details \(a<A\), the
   generator cell and the couplings between levels. Find the selective inequality there.

## 10. Success criteria and contribution standard

**Success** is one of two outcomes:

- a selective inequality on a source-complete family;
- a proof that no inequality of a stated class can be selective there.

The minimal convincing instance is a full-form inequality on a cofinal family that the Liouville
twin violates. A margin or a separation of prime forms alone does not count.

**A contribution** meets all of the following:

- It is unconditional.
- It states its source, form, domain, growing parameter and error currency together.
- It says what it gives on the battery: Liouville, scalings \(c\Lambda\), quadratic mixtures,
  finite flips, the golden curve datum.
- It says whether it is twin-blind or selective.
- It keeps the archimedean term and both pole moments, or says which it drops.
- It records the positive survivor of every no-go.

**Principles.**

- Selectivity is a theorem, not a hope.
- Identities first, sign last.
- Pay each reserve once.
- Work per level and per orbit.
- A reformulation is not a reduction.
