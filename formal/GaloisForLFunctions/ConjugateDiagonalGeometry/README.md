# Conjugate-Diagonal Geometry

Conjugate-Diagonal Geometry studies arithmetic constructions that evaluate a positive
trace on the conjugate diagonal of a zero divisor. Its central problem is to connect
that trace to the reflection pairing computed by the explicit formula. The desired
connection must come from arithmetic geometry, a trace identity, or a selective count;
assuming the compatibility of the two pairings would assume the missing theorem.

This programme develops the proposal in [the frontier report, §4](../../../docs/reports/review-2610/hard-obstacles-summary.md#4-would-the-b-tier-obstacles-require-a-new-field-of-mathematics).
The name describes a research programme, not an assertion that a new field is necessary.

## Why the conjugate diagonal

Write a nontrivial zero as `ρ = 1/2 + δρ + iγρ`, with multiplicity `mρ`, and let
`f̂` denote the centred transform of a test function. There are two different pairings:

* The **collided form** `D(f) = Σρ mρ |f̂(ρ)|²` is nonnegative by construction.
* The **Weil form** `W(f)` pairs transforms using functional-equation reflection.
  The explicit formula computes it from primes, the pole, and the infinite place.

The existing capacity identity is `Re W(f) = D(f) − Δ(f)`. The nonnegative
defect `Δ` measures disagreement of reflected evaluations. Positivity of `D` alone
does not bound `Δ`. Conversely, an explicit formula for `W` does not make it positive.

Centred translation `T_L` multiplies the transform by `exp(L(ρ − 1/2))`.
Its symmetric defect on the positive form is

```
D(T_L f) + D(T_{−L} f) − 2D(f)
  = Σρ mρ (2 cosh(2δρ L) − 2) |f̂(ρ)|².
```

This is a positive measure of off-line depth. At `L` proportional to `log T`, it
is a weighted zero-density statistic. Vanishing for a separating class of tests
is RH. An arithmetic evaluation or upper bound is therefore a substantive target
even when it falls short of vanishing.

## Objects and conventions

The spectral surface has independent coordinates `(s,t)`. Its conjugate diagonal
is `(ρ, conjugate ρ)`; the reflection graph is `(ρ, 1−ρ)`. Their coincidence
on the zero divisor is the critical-line assertion. Conjugation, holomorphic
reflection `s ↦ 1−s`, and Rosati reflection `s ↦ 1−conjugate s` must remain distinct.

The basic objects are:

1. Two-variable test kernels, their diagonal traces, and sesquilinear forms on
   transforms. Summability and passage from finite divisors to the actual zero
   divisor are part of the construction.
2. The correspondence algebra: normalized Hecke graphs, the archimedean flow,
   reflection, and the ordinate-dependent localizer `σγ(s) = 1+2iγ−s`.
3. The prime gauge torus and the semigroup generated by `n ↦ n+1` and `n ↦ pn`.
   Successor links carry information that multiplicative orbit data forget.
4. Families of arithmetic counts, their section spaces, and their quadratic
   limits. A useful polarization must depend on the fibre and have enough rank
   to represent the tested classes.
5. Completed and height-coupled positive systems, their central orders,
   allowed deformations, anchors, and pole costs.
6. Escape measures and phase kernels. Finite-height boundary mass and mass
   escaping to infinite height are separate components.

A spectral definition of a norm is an object, not an arithmetic computation of
that norm. A model identity is a calibration, not a theorem about the zeta divisor.

## Language that prevents false conclusions

| Distinction | Mathematical consequence |
|---|---|
| Positive / equivariant | Either property can hold without the other. |
| Collided / Rosati | Conjugate pairing and reflection pairing need not coincide. |
| Computed / posited | A formula must evaluate the object without assuming its desired sign. |
| Hyperbolic / elliptic / unipotent | Local type determines what invariant positivity can see. |
| Resonant / localizing | A special scale is not a correspondence that isolates an unknown zero. |
| Even / odd sector | The localizer's even sector is positive at off-line pairs; its odd sector can detect them. |
| Gauge invariant / charged | Orbit information does not select the arithmetic point. |
| Archimedean / non-archimedean charge | The first prime flip is a height twist; the two-prime flip is not. |
| Count / selective count | A positive quadratic limit can be identical on every fibre. |
| Outward / inward deformation | Positivity can split a central double zero without permitting its reverse. |
| Classification / exclusion | Describing an escape state does not rule out its occurrence. |
| Average / individual zero | Exceptional sets must be controlled at the scale required by selection. |

## Mechanisms and foundational pillars

The existing corpus supplies the following pillars. Exact statements and dependencies
must be read in their owning modules before they are used.

* **Polarization and capacity:** `Re W = D−Δ`, together with the Hecke law of `D`.
  This separates the evaluation problem from the missing compatibility.
* **Local-type geometry:** boosts at off-line pairs, resonance restrictions,
  and the affine localizers `σγ`. Their parity sectors distinguish harmless
  positivity from a sign that could force a companion zero.
* **Conductor and forcing thresholds:** twisted positivity below a conductor
  horizon and companion forcing at a larger aperture. Their gap is mathematical
  content, not a range in which either theorem may be extrapolated.
* **Gauge geometry:** multiplicative intertwiners and the successor connection.
  The links `2 → 3 → 4` recover the first two prime phases.
* **Effectivity and counting:** equivalences between the existing currencies of
  the sign, and the fibre-independent theta polarization.
* **Contraction and chirality:** anchor costs, collision obstructions, parity,
  and positive outward deformations of height-coupled systems.
* **Escape geometry:** phase-kernel classifications and Abel boundary measures.

New pillars must add an evaluation, estimate, classification, or construction that
these results do not already provide. The first algebraic target is a quantitative
calculus for the symmetric Hecke defect: its exact kernel, its relation to depth
moments and tails, and its compatibility with finite and infinite diagonal traces.

## Questions and required tools

The programme retains all eleven questions in the source proposal.

| Question | Required advance |
|---|---|
| 1. What is Frobenius at infinity? | Construct an arithmetic correspondence acting as conjugation on the zero divisor. |
| 2. What computes `D`? | An arithmetic formula for conjugate-diagonal traces, or a prime-side upper bound for their Hecke defect. |
| 3. What selects the zeta fibre? | A fibre-dependent counting polarization equal to the Rosati form at that fibre. |
| 4. Where does section rank grow? | Section spaces escaping rank-one and product-lattice collapse. |
| 5. How does elliptic localization cross the horizon? | Odd-detector sign laws at zero ordinates, with explicit exceptional-set control. |
| 6. What carries non-archimedean charge? | Positivity whose separating margin is determined by successor holonomy. |
| 7. What pays the capacity? | A structural proof of `Δ ≤ D`, with uniform depth and aperture control. |
| 8. Is the coupled class bounded at the centre? | Central-order ceilings, including nonvanishing at zero-free ordinates. |
| 9. Where does escape mass go? | Completeness when the real-part supremum is unattained, including infinite-height mass. |
| 10. How does an average select a zero? | Selection estimates resolving the relevant zero spacing. |
| 11. What supplies the base? | A constructed tensor structure with loss independent of the power. |

The associated tool families are conjugate-diagonal explicit formulas; arithmetic
Hermitian correspondence spaces with computed flow laws; selective counting
polarizations; elliptic localization beyond the conductor horizon; non-archimedean
charged positivity; central-order ceilings; oscillatory capacity bounds; and
selection estimates at zero spacing. These are research targets, not available inputs.

Three directions organize the work without replacing this scope:

**Defect and density.** Relate the positive defect to depth distributions. Separate
identities for spectral weights from arithmetic estimates for those weights.
Test two-variable or non-holomorphic carriers, including truncated Eisenstein
norms; a positive scattering norm must be proved to compute the intended diagonal
trace before it can be used as a polarization.

**Elliptic forcing.** Make the conductor/forcing gap precise, then determine
whether exceptional-set estimates for the odd detector yield non-isolation laws.
The sign is needed at actual zero ordinates, for a signed test and at the forcing
aperture; an averaged theorem for nonnegative tests does not supply it.

**Successor charge.** Determine when the one-prime and two-prime flipped twins
lose positivity, then relate any separating margin to additive arithmetic and
its deterioration with aperture. A negative witness without a matching theorem
for zeta does not establish selective positivity.

## Traps and calibration

A positive sum of squares does not establish Hecke invariance. A norm obtained by
declaring spectral vectors orthogonal does not compute an arithmetic metric. An
existence theorem whose object contains the desired sign as a field is circular.
A one-variable explicit formula cannot be applied to an antiholomorphic argument
without a new justification. A finite trace identity does not justify an infinite
trace, and a finite certificate does not justify a cofinal limit.

Every proposed mechanism must name its behaviour on the relevant members of the
source report's selectivity battery: pole-order relaxations, planted quartets,
Beurling systems and the golden datum; Liouville, sign-flipped and dominated twins;
height-coupled deformations; nonzero theta characteristics; and escape models.
Calibrations include Weil for curves with budget `2g`, exterior-count purity,
`P¹ × P¹` effectivity, the `κ=1` collapse, the `2:1` anchor law, the anchored
`κ≥3` floor, the conductor horizon, and the capacity identity.

## What counts as a contribution

A contribution gives an unconditional theorem about explicitly constructed objects,
with a dependency-closed Lean proof. It names the positive and spectral sides, the
local type, the gauge charge, the range of parameters, and the precise arithmetic
content. Definitions and equivalences are useful infrastructure but do not count
as proving their open endpoints. A negative result must include the strongest
nontrivial positive survivor exposed by its proof.

Success at the level of an individual contribution includes a new diagonal trace
formula, an arithmetic bound for the defect, a companion-zero law, a selective
charged margin, a central-order theorem, or an exact structural decomposition.
Success for the full programme requires answering the eleven questions and
constructing the eight tool families above. RH-strength endpoints remain open
until their actual unconditional proofs are checked; foundational infrastructure
alone does not complete the programme.
