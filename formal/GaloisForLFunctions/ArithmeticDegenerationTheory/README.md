# Arithmetic Degeneration Theory (ADT): polarized limits of the sum–product surface

Namespace `GaloisForLFunctions.ArithmeticDegenerationTheory`; umbrella `ArithmeticDegenerationTheory.lean`.
Everything is unconditional. Theorems named `riemannHypothesis_iff_*` are equivalences, not proofs of RH.

## 1. Motivation

The research has reduced RH to one even integer `κ = κ(K_ζ)`, the number of distinct zeros right of
the critical line, read on one hyperplane `v₁^⊥` (pole-orthogonal vectors) of one degenerating family
of finite quadratic forms: the complete Weil matrices `W_{φ,c,N}` (atoms of a profile `φ` of width
`c/N` at `log n`, `n ≤ N`). Three walls stand in front of every route, and each is a question.

- **Σ (sign).** *What decides the sign of a form whose margin has fallen below the contribution of a
  single zero?* At the receiver scale the margin of the complete form and the zero side have the
  same order. A statement proved without zero information holds for every datum sharing that
  information, including data with off-line zeros, so it stops below their edge (EHT). The answer
  cannot be an inequality.
- **Π (precision).** *What produces a count at square-root precision over `ℤ`?* Over `𝔽_q` the count
  is a dimension (sections) or a cardinality (kernel points). Over `ℤ` every candidate count is soft
  or exactly RH (LST, ACS).
- **Γ (selection).** *What canonical operation identifies the reflection pairing `ρ ↔ 1 − ρ` with the
  conjugation pairing `ρ ↔ ρ̄`?* Over `𝔽_q` it is complex conjugation of Frobenius eigenvalues,
  forced by the polarization of the Jacobian. Over `ℤ` it has four RH-equivalent readings (EHT
  Frobenius at infinity) and no construction.

The corpus has proved the logic behind these walls, not merely observed it: estimates stop at the
edge; every integer invariant of the family, read at an RH-complete resolution, is RH; every selector
is a margin on a partial form. What can cross the edge is a statement that holds there **by
structure** — an identity, an inherited polarization, or an integer with a structural bound.

There is a classical theory in which positivity survives a degeneration by structure: Hodge theory
of degenerations. The polarization of a variation of Hodge structure over a punctured polydisc
degenerates in the naive limit, yet Schmid's nilpotent-orbit and `SL₂`-orbit theorems, and the
multi-variable theory of Cattani–Kaplan–Schmid, recover it: after rescaling by the monodromy weight
filtration, the limit is a polarized mixed Hodge structure, polarized on its graded pieces, and the
polarization of the nearby fibres is inherited from it.

**ADT's thesis.** RH is the Hodge–Riemann relation of the degenerating family `W_{φ,c,N}` along the
resolution axis. The degeneration has a monodromy: the dilation semigroup `n ↦ pn`, one branch per
prime, the branches meeting only at the archimedean place. Positivity is to be obtained the way
Hodge theory of degenerations obtains it — not by estimating the limit (which is zero) but by
identifying the rescaled limiting structure, proving its polarization, identifying the extension
data between its graded pieces, and proving (or exactly refuting) inheritance. Counts, traces and
quantized readings are the three ways an identity can carry a sign through the limit; ADT studies
all three on the same degenerating family.

## 4. Language

- **Resolution**: a point `(c, N)`; the family is the section `w = c/N` of the plane of resolutions.
- **Universal regime / edge / RH regime**: as in EHT.
- **Edge-valid statement**: holds at every resolution, edge included: identity, inherited
  polarization, integer invariant with a structural bound.
- **Carrier**: a mechanism that carries a sign through the limit — a **count** (dimension or
  cardinality), a **trace** of a positive operator, or a **quantized reading** (an integer with a
  source-side bound).
- **Weight** of a conductor `m`: the exponent of its degeneration, `E_m(c) ≍ m^β E₁(c)` at large
  `c`. **Graded piece**: the Ramanujan packet `r_m`. **Rescaled limit**: the limit of
  `c^β ⟨W r_e, r_{e'}⟩`.
- **Extension datum**: an off-diagonal pairing `⟨W r_e, r_{e'}⟩`, `e ≠ e'`; nonzero only within an
  lcm class.
- **Inheritance**: positivity of the finite-resolution form deduced from positivity of the rescaled
  limit plus smallness of the rescaled extension data. **Inheritance radius**: the largest schedule
  along which the rescaled extension data tend to `0`.
- **Branch** (prime tower), **cone** (the primes), **glue** (the archimedean hyperplane).
- **Lowering operator** `L_p`: the action of `D_p` on conductors divisible by `p`.
- **Shape constraint**: what a trace carrier must do to reproduce a source term.
- **Fake**: a datum on which a soft law holds and RH fails.

## 5. Mechanisms

**A. The edge logic selects the carriers.** An estimate valid for a class of data is valid for
every member, so it stops below the edge of every off-line member (EHT). Integer invariants read at
an RH-complete resolution are RH (EHT, HKT). Hence a sign producer must be built from the Euler
product, the functional equation and the pole used as identities (selection by construction), and
must carry its sign as an identity, a polarization inherited from a non-degenerate rescaled limit, or
an integer with a source-side bound.

**B. Rescaling.** A family `Q_t` with `D_t Q_t D_t → Q_∞` for diagonal weights `D_t` is eventually
positive definite when `Q_∞` is; when `Q_∞` is only semidefinite, nothing is inherited. In weighted
form, positivity follows from positive graded pieces plus extension data that are `o(√(E_e E_{e'}))`.
This is the finite-dimensional content of the nilpotent-orbit theorem.

**C. The prime cone acts on conductors.** Ramanujan sums satisfy, for a prime `p`,
`c_m(pn) = c_m(n)` if `p ∤ m`, `(p − 1) c_{m/p}(n)` if `p ∥ m`, `p c_{m/p}(n)` if `p² ∣ m`. So the
dilation `D_p` preserves the Ramanujan span: it fixes the `p`-free conductors and lowers the
`p`-valuation of the others. The lowering operators of distinct primes commute; on the tower
`{m'p^k}` each is a Jordan string. The conductor towers are the weight strings of the monodromy.

**D. The rescaled limit is arithmetic.** `E_m(c)` is a Möbius combination of the conductor-one energy
at the star overlaps `c/d`. When the Fourier tail of `φ` varies regularly, `c^β E(c) → K`, so
`c^β E_m(c) → K J_{β+1}(m)/φ(m)`, with `J_k(m) = Σ_{d∣m} μ(m/d)d^k` Jordan's totient. The
degeneration is self-similar (`E_m(c) ≍ E₁(c/m)`), and the surviving structure is a positive
multiplicative form: the limiting polarization is an arithmetic invariant of the conductor, not a
common floor.

**E. Extension data and the inheritance radius.** The extension data are the cross-conductor
pairings. Relative to the margins they are `O(c⁸ log^{1/k} N / log N)` (HWV), so the family converges
to the Jordan-totient form after rescaling along every schedule `c⁸ = o(log^{1−ε} N)`. At
`c ≍ (log N)^{1/8}` the rescaled extension data are of order one and carry the zeros: positivity of
the conductor Gram there is RH (HWV). The inheritance radius is the RH exponent; the sign problem is
the second-order limit at the critical curve.

**F. Gluing by one hyperplane.** No assembly of local poles has one square; the archimedean place
glues dilation data through one hyperplane and absorbs exactly one negative square (AKG): the glued
index is `κ + 1`. On `v₁^⊥` the pole pair is invisible (RIT). ADT reads this as the signature of the
limiting period domain: one negative direction, contributed by `∞`.

**G. Traces.** The archimedean Weil distribution is a trace: on compact-Schwartz tests,
`−W_∞(g ⋆ g*) = Tr(ϑ(g)(1 − P − P̂)ϑ(g)†)` with `P`, `P̂` the time and band cutoffs (RHTower CCM).
The middle operator `1 − P − P̂` of two projections anticommutes with `P − P̂` and satisfies
`(P + P̂ − 1)² + (P − P̂)² = 1`, so its spectrum is symmetric on the generic part: a trace carrier
built from two cutoffs has as many negative as positive directions there, the negative ones in the
prolate directions. The pole terms must pay exactly these.

**H. Counts.** `h⁰` of `Spec ℤ` is a Gaussian lattice count with linear Riemann–Roch (AOG). On the
split surface the Künneth count pays `χ` exactly and `h¹ = h¹_K + ½δ` with `δ` Weil's form (LST);
bidegree counts are blind; the isotropic cone count has Mellin transform `Γ(σ)(2π)^{−σ}ζ(σ)²` and no
Gaussian Poisson pair (LST). The count that would carry the sign must read the primitive part.

**I. Quantized readings.** Horizon defects are monotone integers converging to the number of right
zeros the atom sees; collision charges are Fredholm indices bounding them (EHT). A quantized reading
crosses the edge only with a source-side bound below `2`.

**J. Calibration and fakes.** Over `𝔽_q` the monodromy is finite and the flow trivial. Every soft law
holds on the fakes; a carrier must fail on each fake at an identified place.

## 6. Questions

1. **Limiting structure.** What is the rescaled limit of the complete Weil family, and is it
   polarized? Is it the same along every schedule in the universal regime?
2. **Monodromy.** How does the dilation semigroup act on the graded pieces, and is the conductor
   filtration its weight filtration?
3. **Extension data.** Which off-diagonal data survive rescaling, where, and what do they carry?
4. **Inheritance radius.** Along which schedules is the polarization inherited, and is the boundary
   exactly the RH exponent?
5. **Selectivity of the limit.** Is the rescaled limit zero-blind and twin-blind (so that it cannot
   carry the sign alone), or does it read the arithmetic?
6. **Traces.** What is the spectral structure of the archimedean trace carrier, what must the pole
   pay, and what shape must a carrier at a prime have?
7. **Gluing.** Do local positive carriers at `∞` and finitely many primes glue to a carrier with
   exactly one negative square, and what is the exact non-separable coupling?
8. **Counts.** Which counts read the primitive part, and on which tests does a count equal `½W`?
9. **Calibration.** What do these structures become over `𝔽_q` and on each fake?

## 12. Success criteria

- a limiting polarized structure for the degenerating family with the prime cone as monodromy,
  its polarization on graded pieces a theorem, its extension data identified with the top stratum;
- the inheritance radius determined exactly;
- a positivity statement on `v₁^⊥` past the universal regime by an edge-valid mechanism, or the
  exact statement of what each carrier lacks;
- a count or trace equal to `½W` on an explicit growing family of tests, starting with the prime-free
  window;
- a gluing theorem with exactly one negative square and an exact statement of the coupling;
- exact behaviour on the fakes at every step.

## 13. What counts as a contribution

- A Lean theorem, unconditional, in this folder, wired into the umbrella.
- It is a statement about the degenerating family read through its limit — rescaled limits,
  monodromy, extension data, inheritance, carriers, gluing, calibration — or a tool a pillar uses.
- It says which kind of statement it is: identity, inherited polarization, integer invariant,
  estimate (and in which regime), equivalence with RH, or no-go with its positive survivor.
- Restatements of neighbouring programmes do not count; numerical certificates do not count.

## 14. Big picture

The complete Weil family degenerates: its fixed-overlap limit is a positive diagonal form in the
conductor basis, and it tends to zero along every growing schedule. ADT asks what Hodge theory of
degenerations asks: what survives after rescaling, what acts as monodromy, what the extension data
are, and how far the polarization is inherited. The monodromy is the dilation semigroup acting on
conductors by valuation lowering, one branch per prime; the rescaled limit is the Jordan-totient
form; the extension data are the cross-conductor pairings of the top stratum; and inheritance holds
exactly up to the RH exponent, where the extension data begin to carry the zeros. The sign is
therefore a second-order phenomenon of the degeneration, to be produced by a carrier — a count, a
trace or a quantized reading — that holds at the edge by structure.
