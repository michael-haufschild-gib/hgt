## 1. Motivation

Both proofs of the Riemann hypothesis for a curve `C/𝔽_q` take place on `C × C`.

- **Weil.** The graphs `Γ_n` of `Fⁿ` are divisors on a surface with a Hodge index theorem;
  `Γ_0·Γ_n` is the number of points over `𝔽_{qⁿ}`.
- **Stepanov.** A section of the ideal `I_Δ^ℓ` of small bidegree `(a, b)` exists by Riemann–Roch on
  the surface; restricted to `Γ_n` it has degree `a + qⁿb` and vanishes to order `ℓ` on `Γ_0 ∩ Γ_n`.

Both need a two-dimensional object with a diagonal and spaces of sections. `ζ` is rigid: no family
of twists or covers replaces the base, and the zero motive of `ζ` is the fixed part of every
arithmetic family. So the object has to be a product of `Spec ℤ` with itself.

AGZ does not define `𝔽₁`. It studies the geometries in which `Spec ℤ` has a product with itself
and asks of each: which steps of Weil's and of Stepanov's argument run on it, which are absent,
and what the geometry proves instead. A transported mechanism gets its exact price before it is
used.

Three squares appear, each mapping to the next:

  `Spec 𝒢  →  Spec T  →  S_q = Spec ℤ[Y]`.

- `𝒢 = W(ℤ)` in ghost coordinates, the **arithmetic square**: the cofree ring with commuting
  Frobenius lifts over `ℤ`. It is the union of the Frobenius graphs glued along the primes.
- `T`, the **Adams ring**: the subring generated by the constants (Teichmüller points); the
  integer Dirichlet polynomials.
- `S_q`, the **toric surface of a base** `q`: `Spec ℤ[Y]` with the sections `Y = qⁿ`.

`𝒢` carries the prime part of Weil's off-diagonal exactly and has no diagonal. `S_q` has a
diagonal with a normal direction and a bilinear rank count, and no primitive part. The jets of the
archimedean place are weights of the same Frobenius on the same sequence ring, with a different
Verschiebung. No single object of the three carries contacts, jets and a diagonal together; each
obstruction is a theorem. The fibre of `𝒢` over every prime has infinitely many rational points,
and an object with a finite fibre has contacts that `𝒢` does not have: no object of finite type
carries the contacts of the square. The object that carries them with a count of sections is the
orbit ring with a base, `𝒪_q ⊂ 𝒢`, generated by the orbits `e_d` and the Teichmüller point `[q]`.
It is filtered by levels: at the level `M` it is `τ(M)` lines glued over the primes, each with the
sections of `S_q`. With the weight lines of the pole plane and of the jets it is one ring of
sequences `𝒮_q`, stable under the shifts, with an exact count of sections.

What the square and its fibre carry all the same:

- **The number of the zeros.** The gluing index of the first `N` graphs is `N!`, and `log N!` is
  the count of the zeros below the height `2πN` up to `O(log N)`; at the heights `2π(N + ½)` the
  defect is the remainder `S` of the zero count plus a constant.
- **Weil's archimedean diagonal.** On the comb of the integer degree points the jets are the Lévy
  measure of Weil's archimedean form, and its constant `A(0) = ψ(¼) − log π` is the finite part of
  their rows with the counterterm `log(n/2π)` at the degree `n`. The divergent part `log n` is the
  logarithm of the gluing index of `Γ_n`: the finite places supply the counterterm of the
  archimedean diagonal. The counterterm belongs to the comb and not to the point; it is not
  invariant under the rational torus. A comb invariant under a subgroup `q^ℤ` of the torus, the
  degree points `qⁿ` of `S_q`, has a constant diagonal; after the counterterm `log(1/(2π log q))`
  it tends to Weil's constant as `q → 1`.
- **Weil's archimedean form on packets.** A packet `f ∈ C²_c` in the logarithmic coordinate is
  sampled on the degree points at a scale `N`: the point `n` carries `f(log(n/N))` and the mass
  `1/n`. The pairing of the jets minus the masses times `log(n/2π)` tends to minus the
  archimedean part of Weil's functional of `f` without the pole. The same holds on the combs
  `e^{ℤ/N}` with the counterterm `log(N/2π)`, and the closed form of the finite part on a flat
  packet is the value of the same functional. The counterterm is the logarithm of the density of
  the comb over `2π` and nothing else.
- **Weil's whole form, and its sign.** In the unitary normalization the intersection number of
  `Γ_m` and `Γ_{mk}` is `Λ(k)/√k + ω(log k)`: the contact of the square plus the Lévy density of
  the jets. On a packet the jets carry the masses of both points and a contact carries the mass
  of one. Jets plus contacts minus the counterterm `log(n/2π)` tend to the pole term minus Weil's
  functional of the packet. The Riemann hypothesis is the statement that this limit is at most
  the pole term for every packet: the inequality of Castelnuovo and Severi on the degree points.
- **The reading of the carrier.** The rows of the coupled orbit of the adelic carrier are the
  primitive jets of the square degree points. Its two counterterms, symmetrized, are the
  counterterm of the comb of squares plus Weil's constant: `−A(0) = 4c₀ + γ + log 4π`, density row,
  harmonic collision row and mesh. Twisted by the character `r^{iτ}` of the rational torus the
  density row is `c(τ)`, and `4c(τ) + γ + log 4π = −A(τ/2)`: the density of the orbit sum at every
  frequency is Weil's archimedean symbol, and Weil's archimedean form of a packet is its spectral
  density integrated against these densities. Weil's diagonal is the density of the orbit sum and
  not its finite part.
- **The zeros.** The limit of jets, contacts and counterterm on a packet `f` is `2 d₁ d₂ − Z(f)`:
  `d₁ = ∫ f e^{−u/2}`, `d₂ = ∫ f e^{u/2}` the two degrees, `Z(f)` the sum of
  `m_ρ f̂(γ_ρ) conj f̂(conj γ_ρ)` over the zeros. The jets carry the mean of the zero sum, the
  contacts its fluctuation. Under scaling the contact weights `Λ(n)/√n` have the zeros as
  weights: tested against a packet at the scale `e^a` they are the pole plane, the jets and
  `−Σ_ρ m_ρ ĝ(γ_ρ) e^{(ρ−½)a}`.

**The one inequality.** Bézout on the comb of the integers is the statement `D·D ≤ 2 d₁ d₂` for
the limit of the pairing on every packet. It is the Riemann hypothesis. What is proved without
hypothesis:

- it holds on every packet of length at most `181/250`, of any sign, with the margin `∫ f²/5000`;
  this length exceeds `log 2`, so the contact of `Γ_m` and `Γ_{2m}` enters;
- on every packet it holds up to the defect `Σ_ρ m_ρ |f̂(γ_ρ) − f̂(conj γ_ρ)|²/4`, to which a zero
  on the line contributes nothing;
- it is decided by the contacts: with the contact at the prime `2` reversed it fails on a packet
  of that length, and on the comb of the integers it fails for every system of contact weights
  other than `Λ`;
- it can fail only on a small space: on every finite family of packets the defect
  `2 d₁ d₂ − D·D` has at most as many negative squares as `ζ` has zeros to the right of the line,
  and the Riemann hypothesis is the statement that the intersection form on the pole plane and
  the packets has exactly one positive square;
- the section count does not give it: on the object with the contacts and a count the forced
  zeros of a section on a graph are the powers of its contact with the diagonal, on every ring of
  sections, and Stepanov's inequality is the product formula.

The programme has the object, its section count and its pairing. It does not have a proof of the
inequality beyond the bounded lengths above.

**The aim.** Not a definition of `𝔽₁`: a geometry in which `Spec ℤ` has a product with itself
and the inequality of Castelnuovo and Severi on that product is a theorem. What is missing is
one property of the square that is an inequality: a positive class whose positivity is not the
statement to be proved. The structures of the object are identities (the product formula, the
complementary carry, the payment of the contacts) or receivers, with one exception of bounded
range: the generator of the length of the tree of `2` is a positive form on the packets shorter
than `log 4` (construction I, Q26).

**What `Spec ℤ` over `𝔽₁` is like.** Each clause is a reading of theorems of §5–§8.

1. A curve of infinite genus with a genus function: at the degree point `n` the genus is
   `g(n) = 1 + (n/2) log(n/2π)`, negative at `n = 2, 3` (`combForm_singleton`,
   `genus_two_neg`, `genus_three_neg`).
2. Its Frobenius is a monoid at the finite places and a flow at infinity. No single map is a
   Frobenius at two primes, so there is no auxiliary polynomial with cheap vanishing: order `m`
   on a set `S` of primes costs `m·θ(S)` (construction A).
3. Its square is of infinite type. The prime part of every off-diagonal intersection number is
   a contact order, the archimedean part is a jet, and the diagonal is the density of a comb,
   not the self-intersection of a class with a normal bundle (`frobeniusIntersection_div_sqrt`,
   `not_exists_counterterm_of_two_combs`).
4. Its cohomology has infinite rank and the weights `0, 1` (pole plane), `−2j` (jets) and `ρ`;
   the point count is the logarithm of the order of a fixed scheme plus a trace, and `Λ` is not
   a trace of any order (`contactScale_eq`, `log_card_quotient_graphIdeal_one`).
5. Passing from `𝔽_p[T]` to `ℤ` is a deformation by the carry, and the carry is not a
   coboundary: `𝔽₁` does not remove it (`digitCarry_not_coboundary`).
6. Extensions of the base are covers with ramification, not extensions of the constant field:
   the zeta function of a ring is a product over characters, the conductors are the
   ramification term of the genus, and `ζ` is the fixed part, of weight one over the degree
   (`riemannHurwitz`, `tendsto_ringZeroSum_div_degree_sub`).

## 3. Language

- **Arithmetic square**: `Spec 𝒢`. **Contact order** of two graphs at `p`: the exponent of `p` in
  `J(m, n)`. **Fixed scheme** of `F_n` on the diagonal: `Γ_1 ∩ Γ_n`; its **count** is the logarithm
  of its order.
- **Constant**: `F_p x = x^p` for every `p`.
- **Weight** `z`: `F_n` acts by `n^z`. **Transposition**: `F ↔ V`; on weight `z` it is `z ↦ 1 − z`.
- **Witt Verschiebung**: an additive `V_k` with the projection formula
  `V_k(x · F_k y) = V_k(x) · y`. **Perfect**: `F_k` invertible; then `V_k = k F_k^{-1}`.
- **Bidegree** of a section `Σ c_j [q]^j`: first degree `log max|c_j|`, second degree the number of
  digits.
- **Forced zeros** of an ideal on `Γ_n`: its image under the restriction.
- **Gluing index**: the index of the restriction lattice of a set of graphs in the product of
  their coordinate rings.
- **Carry**: a defect of additivity of a section of `ℤ → 𝔽_p` or of the floor.
- **Comb**: a set of degree points, the point `n` at `log n` with the mass of its cell (`1/n` on
  the comb of all degree points). **Counterterm** of a comb at a point: what is subtracted from
  the total jump rate out of the point to leave Weil's constant.
- **Packet**: a function `f ∈ C²_c` of the logarithmic coordinate, sampled on a comb at a scale.
  Its **degrees** are `d₁ = ∫ f e^{−u/2}` and `d₂ = ∫ f e^{u/2}`; its **length** is the length of
  an interval containing its support, its **aperture** half of that.
- **Bézout on the comb** for a system of contact weights: `D·D ≤ 2 d₁ d₂` for the finite part of
  the pairing on every packet. **Defect** of a packet: `Σ_ρ m_ρ |f̂(γ_ρ) − f̂(conj γ_ρ)|²/4`.
- **Weight under scaling**: an exponent `z` for which the pairing with a packet at the scale `e^a`
  contains the character `e^{za}`.
- **Conductor** of a quadratic ring: the conductor `q` of its character; **twisted part** of its
  pairing: the part with the contacts `Λχ`. **Packet of degree zero**: `d₁ = d₂ = 0`.
- **Level** of a section of the tower: an integer `M` whose divisors index its lines. **Section
  with weights**: `Σ_e χ_e·s_e`, `s_e` a section of the tower.
- **Chain** at `p`: the graphs `Γ_{m p^l}`, `l ≥ 0`, for `p ∤ m`; they pass through one point of
  the fibre over `p`. **Node**: the value of the coordinate `y_p` on a graph of a chain.
  **Planar**: on every chain a polynomial in the node, up to a factor prime to `p`.
- **Paid contact**: a section that vanishes on a set of graphs and not on `Γ_m` is at least as
  large on `Γ_m` as the sum of the contacts of `Γ_m` with the set.
- **Bézout at the aperture `a`**: the inequality on the packets of aperture `a`. **Margin** of a
  system of contact weights at an aperture: `c₀` with `D·D ≤ 2 d₁ d₂ − c₀ ∫f²` there.
- **Genus** of a degree point `n`: one plus `n/2` times the coefficient with which the mass at
  `n` enters the pairing. **Ramification term** of a ring: the sum of the logarithms of the
  conductors of its characters.
- **Superposition** of configurations: the union of their zeros with the multiplicities added.
  **Index** of a square: the largest number of negative squares of the defect `2 d₁ d₂ − D·D`
  on a finite family of tests.
- **Pair**: a deviation `δ` of the contacts together with a constant `c` added to the
  counterterm. **Symbol** of a deviation: `P_δ(τ) = Σ 2 δ(n) n^{-1/2} cos(τ log n)`. **Price** of
  a deviation: the supremum of its symbol, the least constant with which it is not refuted.
- **Busemann function** of the tree of `p` along the comb: the depth `v_p(n)` times the edge
  length. **Edge length** of a glued tree: the weight of its contacts along a chain; `log p`
  on the square.
- **Generator** of the length of a tree: the derivative of the functional of the glued tree
  in its edge length. **Threshold length** `ℓ*(L)` of the tree of `2`: the least edge length
  with Bézout on the packets of length `L ≤ log 4`.

## 4. Mechanisms

1. **Dwork congruences are the gluing.** Two graphs are glued exactly along `Spec ℤ/J(m, n)`; the
   first `N` graphs form a lattice of index `N!`: the graph `Γ_n` carries
   `Σ_{k ∣ n} Λ(k) = log n` conditions, the density of zeros at the height `2πn`.
2. **Necklace decomposition.** After subtracting the orbits of the proper divisors of `n`, the
   congruences give `n ∣ x_n`.
3. **Cofreeness.** `a ≡ b (mod p^k) ⇒ a^p ≡ b^p (mod p^{k+1})` makes `(f(ψ_n a))_n` an element of
   `𝒢` for every ring with Frobenius lifts and every `ℤ`-point.
4. **`F V = n`.** `F_{gn} V_{gm} = g·V_m F_n` for coprime `m, n`: the common factor of two degrees
   is a multiplicity. This is the gcd twist of Weil's prime kernel.
5. **Transposition is the functional equation.** On a weight line `F_n = n^z` forces
   `V_n = n^{1−z}`.
6. **Support.** A Witt Verschiebung is supported on multiples, because `V(y)(e_k − k) = 0`; a
   perfect one is supported everywhere. Conversely `F_{p^j}` raises the `p`-adic precision by `j`:
   an element divisible by every Frobenius is constant.
7. **The restriction to the Frobenius graph.** `x_p = x_1^p + p·(δ_p x)_1`; on the ideal of the
   diagonal `(δ_p x)_1 = b_p(x)`, and the coordinates at different primes are independent.
8. **An ideal power restricts to the power of the restriction**; with the product formula,
   Stepanov's inequality on a graph is an identity.
9. **Chain rule.** For a function `Q` on `S_q` vanishing on the diagonal,
   `b_p(Q([q])) ≡ Q'(q)·(q^p − q)/p (mod p)`.
10. **Carry.** `(a+b)^p ≡ r^p (mod p²)` for `a + b = r + pκ` turns the Witt carry into the digit
    carry plus a coboundary; `log binom(a+b, a) = Σ_k Λ(k)·[k ≤ a mod k + b mod k]`.
11. **Lévy–Khintchine on a comb.** `C_m C_n = ½(C_m² + C_n²) − ½(C_m − C_n)²` turns the
    off-diagonal form of a symmetric kernel into its rows on the squares minus half a Dirichlet
    form. For the jets the kernel is the Lévy density of Weil's archimedean form at the
    displacement `log(m/n)`.
12. **Harmonic rows.** The row of `½(1/|m−n| + 1/(m+n))` is `½(H_{M−n} + H_{M+n}) − 3/(4n)`. Euler's
    constant in Weil's diagonal is the constant of this row; on the row of `Γ_1` it cancels
    against Mertens' constant of the primes. A Riemann sum of a decreasing function lies between
    two increments of its primitive.
13. **The mesh.** The counterterm of a comb is the logarithm of its density in logarithmic scale
    divided by `2π`: `log(n/2π)` on all degree points, `log(k/2π)` at `g·k` on the multiples of
    `g`, `log(n/4π)` at `n²` on the squares, `log(1/(2πh))` on `e^{hℤ}`.
14. **The Lambert swap.** `2ω = 1/(2 sinh) + 1/(2 cosh)` at half the displacement. On `e^{hℤ}` the
    `sinh` part is `Σ_{i≥0} h/(e^{(i+½)h} − 1)`, whose terms are `1/(i + ½) − h·ρ((i + ½)h)` with
    `ρ` decreasing: `γ` and the logarithms of Weil's constant come from the odd harmonic sum
    `2H_{2I} − H_I`, and `π/2` from the `cosh` part, a Riemann sum with primitive
    `2 arctan e^{y/2}`.
15. **Displacement on an invariant comb.** On `e^{ℤ/N}` the pairing depends on the packet through
    its comb autocorrelation `a_N(k)`, which is the autocorrelation `h(k/N)` up to `4RBL/N`
    uniformly in `k`. The form is `a_N(0)·(U(1/N) − log(N/2π))` minus the jump sum; the jump sum is
    a Riemann sum below twice the radius of the support and a tail of rates beyond.
16. **The window and its tails.** The degree points are not a group under multiplication and have
    no autocorrelation. Lévy–Khintchine on the window `N < n ≤ (1+L)N` gives the masses against
    `levyRow n − log(n/2π)`, minus the masses against the rates out of the window, minus half the
    Dirichlet sum inside. The rates out are `Ω(log(n/N))` and `Ω(log(M/n))`, `Ω(y) = ∫_y^∞ ω`, by
    comparison of a monotone sum with the increments of its primitive; the Dirichlet sum is a
    Riemann sum on a square. In the continuum the jump integral splits in the same way: half the
    Dirichlet integral over a window plus the masses against `Ω(c − u) + Ω(u)`, by one exchange
    of integrals on a rectangle and one integration by parts.
17. **The twist.** `cos(τ log r)/(1 − r⁴) = 1/(4(1 − r)) + P_τ(r)` with `P_τ` bounded on `(0, 1]`:
    the collision row does not see a character of the torus, and the density row becomes
    `c(τ) = c₀ − ∫_0^1 (1 − cos(τ log r))/(1 − r⁴) dr`, a Lévy–Khintchine symbol.
18. **Pigeonhole on a fibre.** Graphs of distinct degrees prime to `p` do not meet at `p`, so their
    reductions modulo `p` are distinct points of the fibre of the square. A ring with finitely
    many `𝔽_p`-points identifies two of them.
19. **A contact carries one mass.** The jets are a density on pairs of degree points; a contact
    is a point of two graphs. On a packet `Σ_m f_m f_{mk}/m` is a Riemann sum of `f(u)f(u + log k)`,
    because `log(mk/N) = log(m/N) + log k` exactly: the pairs in divisibility give the
    autocorrelation at the logarithms of the integers, which is where the prime part of Weil's
    functional evaluates it.
20. **The gcd with the level.** On `Γ_n` a section `Σ_{d ∣ M} e_d·P_d([q])` is
    `Σ_{d ∣ gcd(M, n)} d·P_d(qⁿ)`. On the degrees `d(1 + Ms)` the gcd is `d`: the lines are
    separated one at a time, by a polynomial with infinitely many zeros.
21. **The explicit formula on a packet.** Weil's functional of the autocorrelation of a packet is
    the sum over the zeros `Z(f)`; translated by `a`, a test has transform `e^{iza} ĝ(z)`, so each
    zero contributes the character `e^{(ρ−½)a}` of the scale.
22. **`Re(a·conj b) ≥ −|a − b|²/4`.** A summand of `Z(f)` is `m_ρ a·conj b` with `a = f̂(γ_ρ)`,
    `b = f̂(conj γ_ρ)`; on the line `a = b`. The defect is summable because the transform of a
    packet decays like the inverse square of the height on the critical strip.
23. **A window certificate is an inequality of the pairing.** `Z(f)` does not change under a
    translation of the packet, so positivity of Weil's functional on tests supported in
    `[−a, a]` is Bézout on the packets of length `2a`, wherever they sit.
24. **The Weil cone is a point.** A weight with the pole and the archimedean term of `ζ` is
    positive on all tests iff the Riemann hypothesis holds and the weight is `Λ` (corpus); real
    tests suffice. So the comb of the integers accepts one system of contacts.
25. **Stirling on the central binomial coefficient.** `log binom(2N, N) = 2N log 2 − ½ log(πN) +
    o(1)` has no arithmetic in it; the carry term and the window count add up to it.
26. **Covariance of the jets.** The unitary intersection number of `Γ_{gm}` and `Γ_{gn}` is that of
    `Γ_m` and `Γ_n`; a comb enters the pairing through its masses and its counterterm only.
27. **Convexity of `x log x − x`.** Its increment over a part `θ` of a window lies between
    `θ log(n + ½)` and `θ log(n + 3/2)`, within `1/(n + ½)` of the share `θ log(n + 1)` of the
    gluing conditions.
28. **Exponential polynomials in the degree.** `Σ_{e ≤ E} n^e P_e(qⁿ) = 0` for arbitrarily large `n`
    forces every `P_e = 0`: the top power of `qⁿ` carries an integer polynomial in `n`, at least
    `1` in absolute value beyond its roots, and the rest is smaller by `n^E/qⁿ`.
29. **`F_k e_d = g·e_{d/g}`, `g = gcd(d, k)`.** The Frobenius moves a line of the tower to the line
    of a divisor; with `F_k[q] = [q]^k` the tower is stable.
30. **A prime power in a cancellative monoid.** If `d ∣ p·a`, `d ∤ a` and `p^k ∣ a` for a prime
    `p`, then `p^{k+1} ∣ d`. Over a Dedekind domain this makes `π·[𝔡 ∣ 𝔞]` an element of the square
    for every `π ∈ 𝔡`.
31. **The multiples of `6` carry no contact.** `N(6jn) = n²/(n² − (6j)^{−2})`: on these shifts the
    point count is its archimedean part, a rational function of `t = (6j)^{−2}` with poles at the
    squares; a relation among finitely many of them at infinitely many `t` is a polynomial
    identity, and its value at `t = n₀²` isolates one coefficient.
32. **Carries add up along `1 + 1 + ⋯ + 1`.** Among the `p` additions of `1` to `0, 1, …, p − 1`
    one carries; a coboundary `f(a) + f(1) − f(a + 1)` adds up to `p·f(1) = 0`.
33. **The signature law.** The Gram matrix of the pole plane `H, V` and of packets is the block
    matrix with the hyperbolic plane, the degrees and the finite part of the pairing; by
    Haynsworth's inertia additivity it has `1 + κ` positive squares, `κ` the number of negative
    squares of Weil's Gram matrix (corpus). On the kernel of the evaluations at the zeros to the
    right of the line Weil's form is a sum of squares, so `κ` is at most their number.
34. **A window at a height.** Modulating a packet to the height `T` multiplies its
    autocorrelation by `e^{iTx}`: the contacts become the prime polynomial
    `Σ (Λ(n)/√n) k(±log n) n^{∓iT}`, and the zero side becomes the zeros counted through the
    spectral window centred at `T`; a Stieltjes integration by parts turns that count into the
    remainder `S` against the derivative of the window (corpus).
35. **Two combs whose Lévy densities differ by a continuous kernel.** On the comb of squares the
    density is `2ω(2x)`, on the integers `ω(x)`; the difference has no pole at `0`. The pairing
    on one comb is the pairing on the other plus a Riemann sum of the difference, and the
    counterterms differ by the value at `0` of the difference of the primitives, here
    `½ log 2`. On a window the rows of the difference cancel against the primitives of the
    tails.
36. **A quadratic ring on the comb of the integers.** Counted by their norms the ideals of a
    quadratic ring of character `χ` have the contacts `Λ(1 + χ)`, and the zeta function is
    `ζ(s) L(s, χ)`. The functional of the weight `Λχ` on a test is its pole term plus the sum
    over the zeros of `L(s, χ)`, minus `log q` times the mass, minus for an odd `χ` the energy
    of `π/cosh(πτ)`. That kernel is the cosine transform of `1/(2 cosh(x/2))`, the difference of
    the Lévy densities of `Γ_ℝ(s)` and `Γ_ℝ(s + 1)`, which between two degree points is the jet
    `1/(m + n)`. So the twisted part has no pole plane, the counterterm `log(qn/2π)` of a comb
    `q` times as dense, and for a complex place the jets `1/|m − n|`.
37. **Truncated radical tests.** Riemann's kernel lies in the radical of Weil's form and the
    theta kernel of `χ` in that of the form of `L(s, χ)`. The truncations of their convolutions
    vanish at `±i/2`, have zero sums `O(R e^{−R/2})` and a mass bounded below. The real and the
    imaginary part of such a test are packets with both degrees zero, and the zero sums, the
    masses and the energies of the test are the sums of theirs. So on packets of degree zero
    the pairing has no gap, and a counterterm with a larger density is refuted.
38. **The conductor against the archimedean constant.** `L(s, χ)` has no pole term, and its
    archimedean symbol is that of `ζ` plus `log q`, plus `π/cosh(πτ)` for an odd `χ`. The symbol
    of `ζ` is at least `ψ(¼) − log π`, so the symbol of `χ` is nonnegative for `q ≥ 128π`, and
    for an odd `χ` for `q ≥ 4π` (corpus). A packet shorter than `log 2` meets no contact: its
    zero sum is the energy of that symbol.
39. **Two configurations, one kernel.** Weil's form of a tempered configuration is nonnegative on
    the tests whose transforms vanish at its zeros to the right of the line. For the sum of two
    forms the common kernel of both sets of evaluations has codimension at most the sum of the
    two counts, so the negative squares add at most.
40. **Orthogonality places the contacts on a progression.** Modulo a prime `p` every character
    `χ ≠ 1` is primitive, and `Σ_χ χ(n)` is `p − 1` for `n ≡ 1` and `0` otherwise. On a real
    packet the twisted contacts are `Λ Re χ` for every primitive character, so the contacts of
    `ζ` and of the `p − 2` twisted parts add up to `(p − 1)Λ(n)` on `n ≡ 1 (mod p)` and to
    `Λ(n)` on the powers of `p`: the splitting law of the cyclotomic ring.
41. **A chain lies on a line.** In the coordinates `y_p = Σ_j e_{p^j}` the graphs of a chain at
    `p` differ in `Y_p` alone, where they sit at the nodes `p + ⋯ + p^l`, and two nodes `k ≠ l`
    differ by `p^{min(k,l)+1}` times a number prime to `p`. A polynomial that vanishes at some
    nodes is divisible by the product of the linear factors, so its value at another node is
    divisible by the product of the differences: every contact of the chain, counted each.
42. **Fermat quotients separate at a second prime.** `F(z) = (z^ℓ − z)/ℓ` maps `ℤ` to `ℤ`,
    lowers the exponent of `ℓ` in `z` by one, and is a polynomial with only powers of `ℓ` in
    its denominators. Iterated on the difference of two nodes of a chain at `p ≠ ℓ` it gives a
    planar sequence that vanishes on one graph and is prime to `ℓ` on the other.
43. **An ideal of `ℤ` is read prime by prime.** An ideal with an element prime to `ℓ` for every
    prime `ℓ` is the unit ideal; an ideal inside `(r^k)` that contains `r^k s` with `r ∤ s` and
    an element prime to `ℓ` for every prime `ℓ ≠ r` is `(r^k)`.
44. **Indicators regroup a superposition.** A sum over the zeros of a union with added
    multiplicities is the sum over `ℂ` of the indicator series of the members; no disjointness
    is needed. With the exact index law of one tempered configuration (corpus), the sum of
    several Weil forms has as index the number of distinct zeros of the union to the right of
    the line.
45. **Translation and the splitting into real and imaginary parts identify the cone.** A packet
    and its translate have the same functional for every system of weights, and the functional
    of a complex test supported in `[−a, a]` is the sum of those of two real packets of that
    aperture.
46. **Riemann–Hurwitz bookkeeping.** Each twisted part brings one copy of the jets and of the
    counterterm of the integers, and `log q_i` times the mass. The coefficient of the mass at
    `n` is `(2g − 2)/n` for the genus `g(n)`. For the integers `e < π` and
    `e^{2/3} < 2 < 2π/3` make the genus negative at `2` and `3`; `1 + x ≤ e^x` and `2π < 7`
    make it positive from `5` on.
47. **Concentration in the radical.** Tests whose transform is divisible by that of Riemann's
    kernel have a negligible sum over the zeros and can be concentrated at any real frequency
    where the limit of the radical tests does not vanish (corpus:
    `RHTower.vonMangoldt_sub_symbol_violation_of_concentration`). On them the functional of a
    pair is the energy of `c − P_δ` near that frequency, so its positivity is read off the
    symbol point by point; real and imaginary parts give a packet with both degrees zero.
48. **Blocks, and the mean of a valuation.** A sum over a window is `q` times the sum over the
    multiples of `q`, up to the variation inside blocks of length `q`; for the terms of a
    bounded Lipschitz function this is `O(q/N)`. With `v_p(n) = Σ_k [p^k ∣ n]` and the
    domination of level `k` by `p^{−k}`, the valuation has the mean `1/(p − 1)` against the
    masses of a packet.
49. **The two halves of a packet.** At a displacement `y` of at least half the length of a
    packet the two factors of the autocorrelation live on disjoint halves, so
    `2|h_f(y)| ≤ ∫f²`. For the first point of a chain of `p` this bounds the contact by
    `∫f²/√p`, against the mean `∫f²/(p − 1)` of the Busemann function: the difference is
    positive for `p = 2` only.
50. **An affine family with a positive generator.** A family `Z + t·E` with `E ≥ κ·mass`,
    `κ > 0`, and `Z ≥ −C·mass` is nonnegative for large `t`, negative for small `t`, and
    monotone in between: its locus of positivity is a closed half-line, and its end point is
    the only parameter where the form degenerates. The lower bound of `Z` on packets of a
    given length needs no hypothesis: the archimedean symbol is bounded below, the contacts
    and the pole term are bounded by the mass (Cauchy–Schwarz against a cutoff).
51. **Two graphs are an order of rank two.** The functions on `Γ_m ∪ Γ_n` are the lattice of
    the pairs `(u + c v, u)`, `c` the contact number; multiplication by a section `(a, b)` is
    triangular in these coordinates, with the determinant `a b`. The index of a principal ideal
    is the absolute value of this determinant (corpus:
    `LinearMap.card_quotient_range_eq_natAbs_det_all`), so it does not see `c`.
52. **The pole against a pair of distant bumps.** For `f_T = g + s·g(· − T)` the product of the
    degrees is `d₁(g) d₂(g)(2 + s(e^{T/2} + e^{−T/2}))`, while each zero contributes its summand
    for `g` times `2 + s(e^{iγT} + e^{−iγT})`, of size at most `2 + 2e^{|Im γ| T}`. Every zero has
    `|Im γ| < ½`, so by dominated convergence over the zeros the zero sum is `o(e^{T/2})`: the
    prime number theorem. The archimedean energy and a summable deviation stay bounded by the
    mass. So the pole term decides the sign of every functional in which it is not cancelled.

**D. The archimedean fibre of the square.**

**F. The lattice at finite scale.**

The pairing as one matrix on the real divisors of a window, at one scale. It is the subject of

jets plus twice the contacts minus the counterterm on the samples of a packet
(`combPairing_sample`); Zariski's lemma on a comb (`combPairing_eq_potential_sub_dirichlet`);

Riemann hypothesis from a lower bound `−ηΣc_n²` at arbitrarily large scales
(`riemannHypothesis_of_frequently_combForm_ge`). AGZ supplies to it the limit on packets, the
zero sum, the contacts and the comb, and the sharpness of the inequality: on packets of degree
zero no multiple of the mass can be gained (`exists_primitive_packet_zeroSum_lt`).

**G. Number rings.**

- *Kill condition.* The pairing of the square of a number ring needs a datum that is not a
  contact, a jet of an archimedean factor or the density of a comb.
- *Verdict.* Not met for the rings whose zeta function is `ζ(s) Π_i L(s, χ_i)` over primitive
  characters, counted by their norms: the quadratic rings, the cyclotomic rings of prime
  conductor, and every finite family of primitive characters.

**H. Curves.**

The comb `q^ℤ` of a curve over `𝔽_q`: the same pairing without jets and without counterterm is
a Toeplitz form, on one graph it is `2g·e²`, and it is nonnegative on every real divisor iff
the Frobenius eigenvalues are unitary; the golden datum has a negative divisor. It is developed

### The next stage

Constructions A–H fix the object. The constructions of the next stage each ask for a property
that is an inequality, and each has a kill condition.

**I. A Lefschetz operator on the comb lattice.**

- *Deliverable.* An operator on the real divisors of a window with the Hodge–Riemann relations
  at a base point and a transport that is not the Riemann hypothesis.
- *Kill condition.* Every candidate has hard Lefschetz equivalent to the Riemann hypothesis.
- *State.* Met for the three operators tested in the corpus: the dilation, the overlap flow and
  the coupling Laplacian (corpus: `HodgeTheoryWithoutVarieties`,
  `riemannHypothesis_iff_liouville_along_the_overlap_flow`,
  `frequently_exists_primitive_zero_mode_of_not_rh`). A fourth operator is tested here on the
  first selective windows: the edge length of the tree of `2`. On packets of length
  `L ≤ log 4` the functional of the glued tree is `Z(f) + (ℓ − log 2)·E₂(f)` with the generator
  `E₂(f) = ∫f² − √2·h_f(log 2) ≥ (1 − 1/√2) ∫f²` (`pairForm_treeDeviation_eq`,
  `treeGenerator_two_ge`). So a longer tree has a larger functional
  (`treeBezoutAt_two_mono`); all long trees satisfy Bézout there without hypothesis, the base
  point (`exists_treeBezoutAt_two`); all short ones fail (`exists_not_treeBezoutAt_two`); and
  Bézout holds exactly from a threshold length `ℓ*(L)` on, the one point of degeneration
  (`exists_treeThreshold_two`). Bézout for the square on packets of length `L` is the
  statement `ℓ*(L) ≤ log 2`; for `L ≤ 181/250` the threshold is at most `log 2 − 1/10000`
  (`treeBezoutAt_two_of_width`). The transport is not the Riemann hypothesis: it is a
  statement at one length. It does not continue: beyond the length `log 4` the generator is
  indefinite, and on all packets the only length that is not refuted is `log 2`
  (`treeBezout_iff`); for an odd prime the generator is negative on a pair of bumps at the
  distance `log p`, and every long tree fails (`exists_packet_treeGenerator_neg`,
  `exists_not_treeBezoutAt_of_three_le`). Other operators are untested.

**II. The archimedean fibre as a boundary.**

- *Deliverable.* The jets as the trace on an axis of a harmonic object in `ℝ³` or `ℍ³`, glued
  to the trees of the primes so that the glued form is the comb form.
- *Kill condition.* Every invariant gluing has a constant diagonal; a gluing along the comb is
  the pairing itself or fails.
- *State.* Met for the kernels invariant under the adelic group and for the trees glued along
  the comb with an edge length. The two summands have models. The jets are the even multipoles
  of the Newtonian kernel between the points `±n` of an axis, and the two rulings are the
  origin and infinity (corpus: `HodgeTheoryOfAFixedPart.even_newtonianKernel_eq_jetKernel`,
  `hasSum_even_newtonianMultipoles`); the contacts of a prime are a sum of squares over its
  chains minus the diagonal (corpus: `prime_contact_form_eq_chain_squares`). *Invariant
  gluings:* on the adelic carrier the diagonal is the density of the orbit sum and not a finite
  part (`four_mul_twistedMean_add`), and no counterterm is a function of the degree point
  (`not_exists_counterterm_of_two_combs`). *Along the comb:* the counterterm is the sum over
  the primes of the Busemann functions of their trees, `log n = Σ_p v_p(n) log p`, and the
  contacts of `p` have the weight of an edge. A tree glued with another edge length `ℓ` has
  the contacts `ℓ` along the chains of `p` and the counterterm `log(n/2π) − (log p − ℓ) v_p(n)`;
  its pairing tends to `2 d₁ d₂` minus the functional of the pair
  `δ = (ℓ − log p)·1_{p^k}`, `c = (ℓ − log p)/(p − 1)` (`tendsto_busemannMass`,
  `tendsto_treePairing`), and Bézout holds iff the Riemann hypothesis holds and `ℓ = log p`
  (`treeBezout_iff`). So the gluing along the comb exists, it is the pairing of the degree
  points, and it has no modulus at any prime; it gives no inequality. With the archimedean
  constant left free the edge lengths are not determined: contacts are traded against the
  constant at the supremum of their symbol (`combBezoutWith_iff`, Q24). A common factor on
  all trees is not traded: with the contacts `(1 + ε)Λ + δ`, `ε ≠ 0`, Bézout fails for every
  constant (`not_combBezoutScaled`, Q28). A point of the comb moved along the axis fails as
  well (corpus: `RHTower.vonMangoldt_move_atom_not_weilPositive`).

**III. Stepanov on the planar square.**

- *Deliverable.* A section of small height that vanishes on the graphs of a window and not on
  `Γ_1`.
- *Kill condition.* The forced value is the full product of the contact ideals and is
  attained, so vanishing is paid exactly.
- *State.* Met (`planarValues_eq_prod`, `exists_planar_vanishing`): on the planar square
  restriction is an equality of ideals, as on the tower it is the product formula. Neither
  gives a sign.

**IV. Squares of towers.**

- *Deliverable.* The index law for the squares of the rings of a tower; the limit form per
  degree.
- *Kill condition.* The form of `ζ` has the weight `1/[K:ℚ]` in the limit.
- *State.* Met. For rings given by their primitive characters, of degrees tending to infinity,
  the defect per degree differs from the mean of the twisted parts by `Z_ζ(f)/d`
  (`tendsto_ringZeroSum_div_degree_sub`); if the twisted parts are nonnegative on a packet, the
  defect per degree is eventually above `−ε` whatever the zeros of `ζ` are
  (`eventually_neg_le_ringZeroSum_div_degree`, `eventually_ringPairing_div_degree_le`). At each
  finite stage the inequality on the square of the ring implies the Riemann hypothesis
  (`riemannHypothesis_of_ringDefect`); the implication does not survive the normalization by
  the degree. In the cyclotomic tower of prime conductors no contact enters a fixed window
  beyond `p`, the defect is archimedean and ramified, and per degree it tends to infinity
  (`weightedContact_cyclotomicWeight_eq_zero`, `exists_cyclotomicZeroSum_eq`,
  `exists_forall_cyclotomicZeroSum_ge`). The towers with bounded root discriminant are not
  abelian; their twisted factors are Artin `L`-functions, to which the weight statement applies
  once their explicit formulas are in the corpus.

**V. The own comb.**

- *Deliverable.* The pairing of an integer-norm system on its own comb.
- *Kill condition.* The jets of a comb that is not a lattice are not an archimedean factor; the
  pairing is not a sum over zeros.
- *State.* Decided for the system of the squares; open for combs without a density. On the
  comb of the integers every system of contact weights but `Λ` fails (`not_combBezout_of_ne`),
  and a separated comb stable under the Frobenius monoid is one orbit, so the comb of `ℤ` has
  no moduli (corpus: `HodgeTheoryOfAFixedPart`). The system with one generalized prime of norm
  `p²` for each `p` has the zeta function `ζ(2s)` and the comb of squares as its own comb.
  There jets, the counterterm `log(n/4π)` and the contacts `2Λ(k)/k` of the system tend to
  `2 d₁ d₂ − Re W_w(F ⋆ F̃)`, the limit on the comb of the integers with the weights `w` of the
  system (`tendsto_squareForm_add_squareContact`), and Bézout fails on a packet, without
  hypothesis (`exists_packet_squarePairing_gt`). The comb enters through its density, which
  the counterterm removes; the jets are those of the archimedean factor of `ζ` in the
  coordinate of the norms and do not follow the system. So the pairing on the own comb is not
  the sum over the zeros of the system, although these lie on one line with those of `ζ`
  (§13.3).

**VI. A count.**

- *Deliverable.* Natural numbers `K_p(m)` on the rays `Γ_1 + Γ_p` with a quadratic Tate limit.
- *Kill condition.* No definition without the form.
- *State.* Met for the count that the object has. The Riemann hypothesis holds iff a count
  with values in `ℕ` has the integer probes as its Tate limit (corpus:
  `CountingHolography.riemannHypothesis_iff_tateLimit_of_count`). The square has a count on
  every pair of graphs: the functions on `Γ_m ∪ Γ_n` are an order of rank two
  (`range_ghostComponent_pair`), and a section `x` has `K(x) = |x_m x_n|` residues there
  (`card_quotient_graphPair`). The count is exactly quadratic on rays, `K(k x) = k² K(x)`
  (`card_quotient_graphPair_intCast_mul`), so its Tate limit exists and is the count itself:
  the absolute value of the hyperbolic norm of `ℤ × ℤ`. It does not see the contact of the
  two graphs, it is blind to the sign, and it violates the parallelogram law
  (`exists_not_parallelogram_pairRing`). The pairing of the degree points is not its Tate
  limit: there `Γ_1` and `Γ_p` enter through the logarithm `log p` of their contact. A count
  defined on the object without the form is therefore of this kind; a count with the pairing
  as its Tate limit is not known.

### 8.2 Settled questions

The questions of the earlier stages of the specification, with their answers. They fix the
object on which the nine questions are posed.

**Q10. One object.** *Is there a ring of sequences, stable under the shifts `F_k`, that contains
the tower of lines, the pole plane and the jets, and in which the cyclic module of the
prime-counting sequence has the zeros as weights?*

- The ring exists. `𝒮_q` is the real algebra of sequences generated by `𝒪_q`, `χ_1` and `τ`
  (`towerWeightRing`). It contains the tower, `1`, `χ_1`, every `τ^j` and `χ_{−1} = χ_1 τ`, hence
  every integer weight; it is stable under every shift, because the tower is stable under the
  Frobenius (`frobenius_mem_orbitBaseRing`, `shift_mem_towerWeightRing`).
- It has a section count. Lines and weights are independent for `q ≥ 2`, and the sections of the
  level `M` with the weights `0, …, E`, or `−J, …, E − J` with the jets, and `b` digits in
  `[−A, A]` are `(((2A+1)^b)^{τ(M)})^{E+1}` (`eq_zero_of_weightedSection_eq_zero`,
  `card_image_weightedLatticeMap`, `card_image_shiftedWeightedLatticeMap`).
- The zeros are the weights on packets. Tested against a packet at the scale `e^a`, the contact
  weights `Λ(n)/√n` are `e^{∓a/2}` times the two pole values, the jets against the symbol of the
  Gamma factor, and `−Σ_ρ m_ρ ĝ(γ_ρ) e^{(ρ−½)a}` (`contactScale_eq`): in the unitary normalization
  the weights are `±½`, those of the jets, and `ρ − ½`.
- In the topology of a ring of sequences they are not. The shifts of the point count `N`,
  restricted to any finite set of degrees, span all of `ℝ^m`
  (`span_shift_pointCount_eq_top`): the cyclic module of `N` is dense for pointwise convergence,
  so every weight line is a limit of combinations of shifts of `N` and no weight is
  distinguished. Term by term `Λ` is not an element of the square
  (`not_exists_ghostComponent_eq_vonMangoldt`) and not a trace of any order (corpus:
  `InfiniteRankPurityTheory.not_isTrace_vonMangoldt`).

So the answer is: the ring exists, with its count; the cyclic module of the point count has the
zeros as weights against packets at a scale, and has every weight pointwise.

**Q11. Bézout.** *On that object, do classes with sections pair nonnegatively in the comb
pairing?*

- The pairing of two graphs is computed on the object: contact number on `𝒪_q` plus the trace on
  the jets (`frobeniusIntersection_div_sqrt_eq_object`); on packets its finite part is
  `2 d₁ d₂ − Z(f)` (`tendsto_degreePairing_degrees_sub_zeroSum`).
- Bézout holds on the packets of length at most `181/250`, of any sign, with the margin
  `∫f²/5000` (`tendsto_degreePairing_le_degrees_of_width`); the length exceeds `log 2`, so one
  contact enters. Every certificate of positivity on a window is such a statement
  (`tendsto_degreePairing_le_degrees_of_certificate`); the widest in the corpus has an aperture
  strictly between `181/500` and `1/2` (`tendsto_degreePairing_le_degrees_of_enlarged_width`).
- It can fail only on a space of packets of dimension at most the number of zeros to the right
  of the line: the defect has at most that many negative squares on every finite family
  (`negIndex_defect_packets_le`), and the Riemann hypothesis is the Hodge index theorem for the
  pairing, one positive square on the pole plane and every family of packets
  (`riemannHypothesis_iff_one_positive_square_packets`).
- On every packet it holds up to the defect of the zeros off the line
  (`tendsto_degreePairing_le_degrees_add_offLineDefect`), and for all packets it is the Riemann
  hypothesis (`riemannHypothesis_iff_combBezout`, `riemannHypothesis_iff_offLineDefect_eq_zero`).
- Not proved: any aperture beyond that certificate. For all packets the statement is the
  Riemann hypothesis (§13.1).

**Q12. The comb.** *Which density is canonical on a logarithmic window, and how does the pairing
change between combs?*

- The density of the comb at the point. With the counterterm `log(density/2π)` the finite part is
  Weil's form on all degree points (`tendsto_degreeForm`), on the multiples of `g`
  (`tendsto_multiplePairing_sub_density_counterterm`), on the invariant combs `e^{ℤ/N}`
  (`tendsto_combForm`) and on the comb of squares, whose jets are not those of the integers
  (`tendsto_squareForm`).
- Between two combs the jets do not change (`multiplePairing_eq`) and the pairing of a packet
  changes by its mass times the logarithm of the ratio of the densities
  (`tendsto_multiplePairing_sub_point_counterterm`). A counterterm of the scale alone leaves the
  first moment of the mass (`tendsto_degreePairing_sub_scale_counterterm`); none leaves
  `∫f²·log N` (`tendsto_degreePairing_div_log`).
- When the Lévy densities of two combs differ, as for the squares and the integers, they differ
  by a continuous kernel; the pairing changes by a Riemann sum of that kernel and the
  counterterm by the constant of its primitive (`squareForm_eq`, `integral_combDefect`).

- The constant is forced by the inequality. With the counterterm `log(n/2π) − c` Bézout holds
  for all packets iff `c ≤ 0` and the Riemann hypothesis holds (`combBezoutShift_iff`): for
  `c > 0` a packet with both degrees zero has a positive pairing, without hypothesis
  (`exists_packet_shiftedPairing_pos`), and for `c < 0` nothing is gained.

So the answer is: the density of the comb itself, over the constant `2π`, which is the largest
for which the inequality is not refuted; between combs with the same jets a change by the mass
times the logarithm of the ratio of the densities, and between the integers and the squares in
addition the continuous kernel `2ω(2x) − ω(x)`. Other combs are a topic of §13.2.

**Q13. Carries.** *Is the carry cocycle the class of the deformation from `𝔽_q[T]` to `ℤ`, and
does it bound the defect of the window count by anything better than a proportion?*

- It is the class, and the class is not zero. In `𝔽_p[T]` the digits of a sum are the sums of the
  digits; in `ℤ` the second digit of a sum is the sum of the second digits plus the carry `κ` of
  the first (`add_div_eq_add_digit_carry`), so `ℤ/p²` is the pairs of residues with the addition
  of `𝔽_p[T]/(T²)` twisted by `κ`. `κ` is a cocycle (`digitCarry_cocycle`) and not a coboundary
  (`digitCarry_not_coboundary`); the Witt carry differs from it by the coboundary of the Fermat
  quotient (`prime_dvd_wittCarry_sub`) and is not a coboundary either
  (`wittCarry_not_coboundary`). No choice of coordinates removes the carries.
- It bounds nothing. `carryTerm N − (2 log 2 − 1)N + (windowCount N − N) + ½ log N → −½ log π`
  (`tendsto_carryDefect_add_windowDefect`): the defect of the carry is minus the defect of the
  window count, a bound for one is a bound for the other, and transport along the carry is an
  identity (`eventually_abs_carryDefect_le`, `eventually_abs_windowDefect_le`).

**Q14. Integer norms.** *Which additive datum, invisible to the Λ-structure, separates `ℤ` from an
integer-norm monoid with an off-line pair?*

- The comb of the degree points: unit masses on all the integers, with its jets and its
  counterterm. The square sees an integer-norm system through its norms only
  (`ghostLift_adams_X_eq_of_eq`); the pairing takes the jets and the counterterm from the comb and
  the contacts from the system.
- With the comb of the integers and contact weights `w`, Bézout holds iff the Riemann hypothesis
  holds and `w = Λ` (`combBezout_iff`). A system whose weights differ from `Λ` at one integer
  fails on some packet, without hypothesis (`not_combBezout_of_ne`). The weights of an
  integer-norm system are `Λ` only for the rational primes with multiplicity one (elementary; the
  theorem is stated for weights).
So the answer is: the comb. What an integer-norm system does on its own comb is a topic of
§13.3.

**Q17. Number rings.** *What are the comb, the counterterm and the Hodge index on the square of
`𝒪_K`, and what does the class group, which obstructs the orbits, do to the lattice?*

- The square exists over every Dedekind domain: sequences indexed by the ideals with the
  congruences `x_{𝔭𝔞} ≡ x_𝔞 (mod 𝔭^{v_𝔭(𝔞)+1})`. Its graphs meet in the prime ideals with
  `log N𝔭`, and the orbit of `𝔡` exists iff `𝔡` is principal (`idealContact_prime_pow`,
  `exists_idealOrbit_generator_iff`).
- The comb. Counted by their norms the ideals of a quadratic ring sit on the comb of the
  integers, with the contacts `Λ(n)(1 + χ(n))` (`idealWeight`).
- The counterterm. The mass at `n` enters with `log(q n²/(2π)²)`, `q` the conductor
  (`quadraticForm_eq`). The part twisted by `χ` has the counterterm `log(qn/2π)` of a comb `q`
  times as dense as the integers and no pole plane: jets, counterterm and twice the contacts
  `Λχ` tend to minus the sum over the zeros of `L(s, χ)`
  (`tendsto_twistedForm_add_twistedContact`).
- The jets. A real quadratic ring has the jets of the integers twice. An imaginary one has the
  jets `1/|m − n|` between norms: twice the jets of the integers minus `1/(m + n)`, the
  difference of the two archimedean factors (`two_mul_levyKernel_sub_conjugate`,
  `tendsto_conjugateJet`).
- The pairing. Jets, counterterm and twice the contacts of the ideals tend to
  `2 d₁ d₂ − Z_ζ(f) − Z_χ(f)`: twice the product of the degrees minus the sum over the zeros of
  the zeta function of the ring (`tendsto_quadraticForm_add_idealContact`).
- The conductor is forced from below. With `q e^{−c}` in the counterterm, `c > 0`, some packet
  with both degrees zero has a positive pairing (`exists_packet_quadraticForm_pos`).
- The Hodge index. On every finite family of packets the limit is `2 d₁ d₂` minus the
  quadratic form of the two Gram matrices of the zeros
  (`tendsto_quadraticPairing_packetCombination`); the defect has at most as many negative
  squares as `ζ` and `L(s, χ)` together have zeros to the right of the line
  (`negIndex_quadraticDefect_packets_le`), and the twisted part at most as many positive squares
  as `L(s, χ)` has (`negIndex_twistedGram_packets_le`).
- Bounded apertures. For an odd character of conductor `q ≥ 4π` or an even one of conductor
  `q ≥ 128π` the inequality holds on the packets shorter than `log 2`, with the margin
  `∫f²/5000` (`tendsto_quadraticForm_le_degrees_of_short`): the twisted part is nonpositive
  there because the conductor exceeds the archimedean constant
  (`twistedZeroSum_nonneg_of_short`).
- Higher degree. On a real packet the explicit formula is a functional of the contacts
  `Λ Re χ` for every primitive character (`re_weilFullFunctional_twist_packet`). For a prime
  `p` the cyclotomic ring of conductor `p` has degree `p − 1`; its contacts, counted by norms,
  are `(p − 1)Λ(n)` on the progression `n ≡ 1 (mod p)` and `Λ(n)` on the powers of `p`
  (`vonMangoldt_add_sum_twistWeight`), its pairing tends to `2 d₁ d₂` minus the sum over the
  zeros of its zeta function (`tendsto_cyclotomicForm_add_contact`), the mass enters with
  `(p − 2) log p` besides `p − 1` times the counterterm of the integers (`cyclotomicForm_eq`),
  the defect obeys the index law over `ζ` and the `p − 2` characters
  (`negIndex_cyclotomicDefect_packets_le`), and for `p ≥ 128π` the inequality holds on the
  packets shorter than `log 2` (`tendsto_cyclotomicForm_le_degrees_of_short`).
- The class group does not enter this pairing: contacts, jets and counterterm depend on the
  characters alone. It obstructs the orbits and nothing else that the norms see.

So the answer, for the rings with a zeta function `ζ(s) Π_i L(s, χ_i)`, counted by their norms:
the comb of the integers, with the contacts that the splitting law gives; the conductors as the
densities of the combs of the twisted parts; the inequality for all packets is the Riemann
hypothesis of the zeta function of the ring by the same reading as for `ℤ`, and for a quadratic
ring the conductor is its threshold. Not answered: the ideals themselves as degree points, with
the characters of the class group, and the rings that are not abelian (§13.4).

**Q20. An object whose functions pay every contact.** *On the cyclotomic divisors of `𝔾_m`
every contact is paid and the divisor of order `n` has the mass `φ(n)`; on the square the masses
are units and a section pays one contact for each prime above a graph. Which datum carries unit
masses and paid contacts?*

- The two ends. On `𝔾_m` over `ℤ` the divisors `Φ_n = 0` meet with
  `φ(m)Λ(n/m)` for `m ∣ n`, and `Λ(n) = log Φ_n(1)` (corpus:
  `ArithmeticEffectivityTheory.orbitIntersection_formula`,
  `SignFreePurityMechanisms.vonMangoldt_eq_log_eval_one_cyclotomic`); the ring is factorial,
  so a function vanishing on some of these divisors is divisible by their equations. The square
  is the ring of the functions on the torsion points with integer values at every point, the
  invariants of the Galois action [C]: a ring between the two with unit masses is the square.
  In the square `p − e_p` vanishes on every `Γ_{p^j}` and is `p` on the diagonal (corpus:
  `HodgeTheoryOfAFixedPart.log_abs_primePointSection_lt`).
- The datum is a subring of the square. The elements `y_p = Σ_{j ≥ 1} e_{p^j}`, with
  `(y_p)_n = p + ⋯ + p^{v_p(n)}`, are coordinates: `1 + y_p` are the Euler factors of the sum of
  all orbits, whose components are `σ(n)` (`primeCoord_eq_sum_orbit`,
  `prod_one_add_chainNode`). They generate the polynomial ring on the primes
  (`coordHom_injective`), in which `Γ_n` is a lattice point and the graphs of a chain lie on a
  line (`latticePoint_chain`).
- Payment. A sequence that on every chain is a polynomial in the node, up to a factor prime to
  `p`, is a section of the square (`isGhost_of_isPlanar`); if it vanishes on `Γ_{m p^l}`,
  `l ∈ T`, it is divisible on `Γ_{m p^k}` by `p^{Σ_{l ∈ T}(min(k,l)+1)}`
  (`pow_sum_dvd_of_isPlanar`), and if it vanishes on a finite set `S` of graphs and not on
  `Γ_m` then `Σ_{n ∈ S}(Λ(n/m)[m ∣ n] + Λ(m/n)[n ∣ m]) ≤ log|x_m|`
  (`sum_contact_le_log_abs_of_isPlanar`): the contacts of the normal form above and below,
  prime powers counted each.
- The contacts. The polynomial ring on the primes has more contacts than the square: all its
  elements agree modulo `3` on `Γ_1` and `Γ_4` (`three_dvd_sub_of_mem_payingRing`). The planar
  sequences form a larger ring, the planar square (`planarSquare`), whose contact ideal is
  `J(m, n)` for every pair of graphs (`planarContact_eq_graphIdeal`): iterated Fermat quotients
  of the differences of the nodes separate two graphs at every prime that is not their common
  one (`exists_planar_separating`). It is strictly between the polynomial ring and the square
  (`payingRing_lt_planarSquare`, `planarSquare_ne_top`).
- Equality. For `m ∉ S` the values on `Γ_m` of the planar sections that vanish on the graphs
  of `S` are exactly the product `Π_{n ∈ S} J(m, n)` of the contact ideals
  (`planarValues_eq_prod`): the product of sections `y − y_n` with `(y − y_n)_m` a generator of
  `J(m, n)` attains it (`exists_planar_vanishing`), and the payment along the chains forces it
  (`prod_contactNat_dvd_of_isPlanar`). The planar square is glued as the trace of a regular
  surface on its graphs; the square is not (`exists_ghost_vanishing_not_mem_prod`).
- The price. The payment is the product formula: the section `Π_{l ∈ T}(Y_p − node_l)` attains
  the forced zeros, and its size on `Γ_{m p^k}` is the product of the differences of the nodes
  (`ghostComponent_coordHom_nodeProduct`). The archimedean pairing of this object is the
  logarithm of a difference of nodes, not the jets.

So the answer is: the planar square, a subring of the arithmetic square with unit masses, the
contact ideals of the square and every contact paid, with equality in Bézout by restriction; no ring between the square and the
functions on the torsion of `𝔾_m` has unit masses. What it does not carry, and what a section
count on it gives, are topics of §13.5.

**Q21. The exceptional square.** *For a real primitive character `χ` of conductor `q`, is the
twisted part of the square of its quadratic ring nonpositive on windows of aperture
`log log q + C`, for every `C` and all large `q`?*

- The twisted part tends to minus the sum `Z_χ` over the zeros of `L(s, χ)`
  (`tendsto_twistedForm_add_twistedContact`); for a real primitive `χ`, `Z_χ ≥ 0` on the real
  packets of an aperture is the positivity of Weil's form of `L(s, χ)` on that window, and at
  every aperture `log log q + C` it is the generalized Riemann hypothesis of `L(s, χ)`
  (`windowPositiveAt_dirichletZeros_iff_twistedZeroSum_nonneg`,
  `grh_dirichlet_iff_forall_twistedZeroSum_nonneg`).
- The trivial aperture. `Z_χ(f) ≥ (log q − C_χ − 2M(a))∫f²` on packets of aperture `a`, `M(a)`
  the prime mass up to `e^{2a}`; splitting the prime sum at `e^a`, where the two factors of the
  autocorrelation have disjoint supports, gives `M(a) + M(a/2)` in place of `2M(a)`
  (`twistedZeroSum_ge_of_aperture`, `twistedZeroSum_ge_of_aperture_sharpened`); uniformly in
  the character, `Z_χ ≥ 0` when `4e^a + C ≤ log q`, and with any `κ > 2` in place of `4`
  (`exists_uniform_trivial_conductor_aperture`, `exists_uniform_twistedZeroSum_aperture_margin`).
  With the prime number theorem the trivial aperture is `log log q + o(1)`: every system with
  contacts bounded by `Λ` is positive below `log log q − η`, and the systems `Λ`, `−Λ` and the
  Liouville twist, with the conductor `q` and no pole plane, fail at every aperture
  `≥ log log q + η` (corpus: `SelectiveArithmetic.exists_trivialAperture_margin`,
  `SelectiveArithmetic.exists_not_feasible`, `SelectiveArithmetic.tendsto_trivialAperture_sub_log`).
- One square. A real zero `½ + δ` and its reflection contribute at least
  `−(sinh(2δa)/δ − 2a)·m·∫f²` on packets of aperture `a`, and nothing negative on even packets,
  on nonnegative packets and on packets orthogonal to `sinh(δu)`
  (`re_Wsummand_real_zero_pair_ge_neg_two_pairEnergy`, `re_Wsummand_real_zero_nonneg`).
- Repulsion. A real zero close to `1` is unique and simple, forces the zeros of bounded height
  onto the line when it is close enough, and gives a lower bound for every other square in
  terms of its depth (`exceptional_zero_unique_and_simple`,
  `deuring_heilbronn_on_twisted_comb`, `exists_twistedComb_square_margin`).
- Not proved: the sign at the aperture `log log q + C`. It is the Landau–Siegel problem (§13.6).

**Q22. Genus and ramification.** *What is the genus function of a degree point, is the
counterterm of a ring that of its base plus a ramification term, and do the isotypic parts of a
ring of higher degree carry separate indices?*

- The genus. In the normal form of the pairing `2 d₁ d₂ − D·D` on the divisor `c·γ_n` is
  `(2c²/n)·g(n)` with `g(n) = 1 + (n/2) log(n/2π)` (`combForm_singleton`), as the form on one
  graph of a curve is `2g·e²` (corpus: `HodgeTheoryOfAFixedPart.curveCombForm_singleton`). The
  genus is negative at `n = 2` and `n = 3` and positive at every other degree point
  (`genus_two_neg`, `genus_three_neg`, `genus_one_pos`, `genus_pos_of_four_le`).
- Riemann–Hurwitz. For a ring with the primitive characters `χ_i` of conductors `q_i`, of
  degree `d = 1 + #{χ_i}`, the mass at `n` enters the pairing with `d log(n/2π) + Σ_i log q_i`
  (`ringForm_eq`), that is with `(2 g_K(n) − 2)/n` for
  `g_K(n) = 1 + (n/2)(d log(n/2π) + Σ_i log q_i)` (`ringForm_eq_genus`), and
  `2 g_K(n) − 2 = d(2 g(n) − 2) + n Σ_i log q_i` (`riemannHurwitz`): the conductors are the
  ramification, pulled back to the degree point `n` with the multiplicity `n`.
- Higher degree. For every finite family of primitive characters the pairing with the contacts
  `Λ(1 + Σ_i Re χ_i)` tends to `2 d₁ d₂` minus the sum over the zeros of `ζ(s) Π_i L(s, χ_i)`
  (`tendsto_ringForm_add_contact`).
- Separate indices. Each twisted part is a form of its own on the packets, with the exact index
  `#{ρ : L(ρ, χ_i) = 0, Re ρ > ½}` (corpus: `ArithmeticCastelnuovoSeveri.pontryagin_law`). The
  index of the ring is the number of distinct zeros of `ζ` and of the `L(s, χ_i)` to the right
  of the line (`exists_le_negIndex_ringDefect_iff`): at most the sum of the indices of the
  parts, with equality iff no such zero is shared.
- A cover and its base. Negative squares of the square of `ℤ` are negative squares of the
  square of the ring (`exists_le_negIndex_ringDefect_of_zeta`); the inequality on the square of
  the ring holds iff `ζ` and every `L(s, χ_i)` have no zero to the right of the line
  (`forall_negIndex_ringDefect_eq_zero_iff`), and then the Riemann hypothesis holds
  (`riemannHypothesis_of_ringDefect`).

So the answer is: the genus `1 + (n/2) log(n/2π)`, negative exactly at `2` and `3`;
Riemann–Hurwitz with the conductors as ramification; separate exact indices for the isotypic
parts, and for the ring the count of the distinct zeros of its zeta function. Rings that are
not abelian are a topic of §13.4.

**Q23. A square for some weights.** *Which systems of contact weights, with the jets and the
counterterm of `ℤ`, satisfy Bézout at an aperture, and are there any besides `Λ`?*

- The set of these squares at the aperture `a` is the window cone `𝒜(a)` of the corpus, the
  weights whose functional is nonnegative on the tests supported in `[−a, a]`
  (`combBezoutAt_iff_mem_windowCone`). It is convex, decreases with `a`, sees only the weights
  at `n ≤ e^{2a}` and is bounded in those coordinates (corpus:
  `CriticalHardyTheoryOfTheWindowCone.convex_windowCone`, `windowCone_anti`,
  `mem_windowCone_congr`, `abs_le_boxWidth_of_mem_windowCone`).
- Existence decides. The Riemann hypothesis holds iff at every aperture some square satisfies
  Bézout; if it fails, beyond some aperture none does
  (`riemannHypothesis_iff_forall_exists_combBezoutAt`,
  `exists_aperture_forall_not_combBezoutAt`).
- Other squares at a finite aperture. Below the first contact, `2a < log 2`, every system of
  weights satisfies Bézout (`combBezoutAt_of_short`). At the aperture `181/500` the square of
  the integers has the margin `1/5000` (`hasMargin_vonMangoldt`), a margin is an inradius of
  the cone (corpus: `hasMargin_add`, `Icc_subset_bandSet`), and for every `t` within
  `√2/10000` of `log 2` a system of weights with `w(2) = t` satisfies Bézout there
  (`exists_combBezoutAt_weight_two`, `exists_combBezoutAt_ne_vonMangoldt`).
- The limit. Every coordinate of the cone shrinks to `Λ(n)`, and a point of the cone at a large
  aperture obeys the prime number theorem on its range (corpus:
  `exists_forall_abs_sub_vonMangoldt_lt`, `exists_abs_chebyshev_sub_le_of_mem_windowCone`).

So the answer is: at a finite aperture the squares that satisfy Bézout form a convex body with
`Λ` inside it wherever `Λ` has a margin; the body is everything below the first contact and
shrinks to the point `Λ`, or to the empty set, as the aperture grows. A construction of a point
of the body at every aperture would prove the Riemann hypothesis without producing `Λ`.

**Q24. The price of a contact.** *Bézout fails for wrong contacts with the constant `2π`, and
for the contacts `Λ` with a constant above `2π`. Which pairs of contact weights and constants
are not refuted?*

- Take the contacts `Λ + δ` with `Σ |δ(n)| n^{-1/2} < ∞` and the counterterm `log(n/2π) + c`.
  The pairing tends to `2 d₁ d₂` minus the functional of the pair, `Re W_{Λ+δ}(f ⋆ f̃) + c ∫f²`
  (`tendsto_pairPairing`), and `2π` times this functional is `2π Z(f)` plus the energy of
  `c − P_δ` against `|f̂|²`, with `P_δ(τ) = Σ 2 δ(n) n^{-1/2} cos(τ log n)` the symbol of the
  deviation (`two_pi_mul_pairForm_eq`).
- Bézout holds for the pair iff the Riemann hypothesis holds and `P_δ(τ) ≤ c` for every `τ`
  (`combBezoutWith_iff`). Where `P_δ(τ₁) > c`, a packet with both degrees zero violates it,
  without hypothesis (`exists_packet_pairForm_neg`): mechanism 47.
- The symbol has mean zero, so the inequality forces `c ≥ 0`, and with `c = 0` it forces
  `δ = 0` (`nonneg_of_combBezoutWith`, `combBezoutWith_zero_right_iff`).
- One contact changed by `t` at `n₀` is not refuted exactly from the constant `2|t|/√n₀` on
  (`combBezoutWith_contactAt_iff`).

So the answer is: the pairs that are not refuted are the epigraph of `δ ↦ sup P_δ`, a convex
cone whose vertex is the square of the integers with the constant `2π`. A wrong contact has a
price in the counterterm, and a construction that leaves the constant free does not determine
the contacts. The decisive test for a wrong constant has to be read with the contacts fixed.

**Q25. Trees glued along the comb.** *Construction II for gluings that are not invariant: can
the trees of the primes be glued to the archimedean fibre along the comb of the integers in
another way than in the pairing of the degree points?*

- The counterterm of the comb is a sum of local terms on the trees: `log n = Σ_p v_p(n) log p`,
  the Busemann function of the tree of `p` at the depth `v_p(n)` with edges of length `log p`;
  the contacts of `p` are the weight `log p` on each pair of points of a chain.
- A tree glued with the edge length `ℓ` keeps both rules. Against the masses of a packet the
  valuation has the mean `1/(p − 1)` (`tendsto_busemannMass`, mechanism 48), so the pairing
  tends to `2 d₁ d₂` minus the functional of the pair `δ = (ℓ − log p)·1_{p^k}`,
  `c = (ℓ − log p)/(p − 1)` (`tendsto_treePairing`).
- Bézout holds iff the Riemann hypothesis holds and `ℓ = log p` (`treeBezout_iff`). For a
  longer tree the symbol at `τ = 0` is at least `2(ℓ − log p)/√p`, above the constant; for a
  shorter one the constant is negative.

So the answer is: no. Along the comb the trees are glued with the edge lengths `log p`, the
glued form is the pairing of the degree points, and the inequality reads each edge length from
the jets, as it reads the contact orders and the constant `2π`. The gluing is an identity, the
product formula; it carries no sign.

**Q26. A deformation with a positive generator.** *Construction I on the first selective
windows: is there an operator, outside the dilation, the overlap flow and the coupling
Laplacian, with a base point where the inequality holds without hypothesis and a transport that
is not the Riemann hypothesis?*

- The edge length `ℓ` of the tree of `p` (Q25) is a parameter of the pairing. On packets of
  length at most `2 log p` only the first point of each chain is seen, and the functional is
  affine: `Z(f) + (ℓ − log p)·E_p(f)`, `E_p(f) = ∫f²/(p − 1) − (2/√p)·h_f(log p)`
  (`pairForm_treeDeviation_eq`).
- At a displacement of at least half its length the autocorrelation of a packet is at most half
  its mass (`two_mul_abs_packetAutocorr_le_of_length`), so
  `E_p ≥ (1/(p − 1) − 1/√p) ∫f²` (`treeGenerator_ge`). The constant is positive for `p = 2`:
  `E₂ ≥ (1 − 1/√2) ∫f²` on packets of length at most `log 4` (`treeGenerator_two_ge`).
- For the prime `2` and `0 < L ≤ log 4`: the inequality is monotone in the length
  (`treeBezoutAt_two_mono`); it holds for all long trees without hypothesis
  (`exists_treeBezoutAt_two`, from the lower bound `exists_zeroSum_ge_of_length`), fails for
  all short ones (`exists_not_treeBezoutAt_two`), and holds exactly from a threshold `ℓ*(L)` on
  (`exists_treeThreshold_two`). For `L ≤ 181/250`, `ℓ*(L) ≤ log 2 − 1/10000`
  (`treeBezoutAt_two_of_width`).
- The threshold is the margin of the square in another unit: a tree shorter than `log 2` that
  satisfies Bézout gives `Z ≥ (log 2 − ℓ)(1 − 1/√2) ∫f²`, a margin `m` gives Bézout for
  `ℓ ≥ log 2 − m/2`, and the square has a margin at the length `L` iff `ℓ*(L) < log 2`
  (`zeroSum_ge_of_treeBezoutAt_two`, `treeBezoutAt_two_of_margin`,
  `exists_margin_iff_exists_treeBezoutAt_two_lt`). On all packets there is no margin and the
  threshold is `log 2` itself.
- For an odd prime the generator is negative on a pair of bumps at the distance `log p`, and on
  packets of any length above `log p` every long tree fails (`exists_packet_treeGenerator_neg`,
  `exists_not_treeBezoutAt_of_three_le`).

So the answer is: yes, on packets shorter than `log 4`, and nowhere else in this family. The
length of the tree of `2` is a deformation of the pairing whose generator is a positive form,
with a base point that needs no hypothesis and one point of degeneration; Bézout for the square
at such a length is the statement that this point is at most `log 2`. The positivity of the
generator is a property of the object: the mean of the Busemann function of the tree of `2`
exceeds its first contact. It ends where the second point `4` of a chain is seen, and it has no
analogue at an odd prime.

**Q27. The count of the object.** *Construction VI: does the square have natural numbers on
the rays of `Γ_1 + Γ_p` with a quadratic growth, defined without the form, and is their Tate
limit the pairing?*

- The functions of the square on two graphs `Γ_m`, `Γ_n` are the pairs of integers congruent
  modulo the contact ideal `J(m, n)`: every element of `J(m, n)` is the difference of the
  values of one section (`exists_ghostComponent_eq_iff`, `range_ghostComponent_pair`,
  `graphPair_surjective`). For `m ≠ n` this is an order of rank two in `ℤ × ℤ`
  (`graphIdeal_ne_bot`).
- The number of residues modulo a section `x` is `K(x) = |x_m · x_n|`, and `0` when the
  quotient is infinite (`card_pairRing_quotient`, `card_quotient_graphPair`): mechanism 51.
- `K(k·x) = k² K(x)` for every integer `k` (`card_quotient_graphPair_intCast_mul`): the count
  is exactly quadratic on rays and is its own Tate limit.
- On the order of conductor `c ≥ 2`, `K(α + β) + K(α − β) ≠ 2K(α) + 2K(β)` for `α = (1, 1)`,
  `β = (1, 1 − c)` (`exists_not_parallelogram_pairRing`).

So the answer is: the square has such numbers, and their Tate limit is not the pairing. It is
the absolute value of the norm of the split algebra `ℤ × ℤ`, a hyperbolic form under an absolute
value: the same for every contact, and not a quadratic form. In the corpus this is the count of
a fake (`CountingHolography`). The contact of `Γ_1` and `Γ_p` enters the pairing as `log p`; it
does not enter the count at all.

**Q28. The scale of the contacts.** *Can all contacts be multiplied by one factor, the trees of
all primes be rescaled together, at the price of a constant?*

- For the contacts `(1 + ε)Λ + δ` with `Σ |δ(n)| n^{-1/2} < ∞` the functional on a packet is
  `(1 + ε) Z(f) − ε·(pole + archimedean energy) −` deviation
  (`re_weilFullFunctional_scaled_eq`): the pole term `2 d₁ d₂` is no longer cancelled.
- On a pair of bumps `f_T = g + s·g(· − T)` the pole term is `2 d₁(g) d₂(g)(2 + s(e^{T/2} +
  e^{−T/2}))` (`pole_bumpPair`), the zero sum is `o(e^{T/2})` (`tendsto_abs_zeroSum_bumpPair`),
  the archimedean energy is bounded by that of `g` (`abs_weilArchRe_bumpPair_sub_le`) and the
  deviation by the mass (`abs_sum_deviation_le`): mechanism 52.
- With `s` the sign of `ε` the functional plus `c ∫f²` is negative for large `T`, for every
  `c` (`exists_packet_scaled_neg`), so Bézout fails (`not_combBezoutScaled`), without
  hypothesis.

So the answer is: no. Among the contacts `(1 + ε)Λ + δ` with a summable `δ` and any constant,
Bézout holds iff `ε = 0`, the Riemann hypothesis holds and the symbol of `δ` is at most the
constant (Q24). The pole plane fixes the total size of the contacts; the symbol fixes their
distribution up to the constant.

**The decisive test.** A construction whose Bézout holds for an integer-norm system with an
off-line pair, for the Liouville twist at the first rung of the conductor ladder, or for a wrong
constant in the counterterm, is wrong. The pairing of the degree points passes all three without
hypothesis: on the comb of the integers Bézout fails for every system of contact weights other
than `Λ` (`not_combBezout_of_ne`), and with one contact reversed it fails on a packet of length

every aperture beyond the trivial one (corpus: `SelectiveArithmetic.exists_not_feasible`); and
with the counterterm `log(n/2π) − c`, `c > 0`, a packet of degree zero has a positive pairing
(`exists_packet_shiftedPairing_pos`). The test for a wrong constant is meant with the contacts
fixed: wrong contacts with a larger counterterm pass, under the Riemann hypothesis, exactly
from the supremum of their symbol on (`combBezoutWith_iff`).

## 13. Open research topics

The settled questions are in §8.2. The topics below are the open parts of the nine questions
of §8.1 and of the constructions I–VI of §6; each states what is proved, what is not, and what
a decision would be. Questions 5 and 9 are topics of the continuation.

### 13.1 Bézout beyond the proved apertures

*Statement.* For every packet the finite part of the pairing of the degree points is at most
`2 d₁ d₂`. *Proved:* the packets of aperture up to the widest certificate of the corpus, below
`1/2`; every packet up to the defect of the off-line zeros; the equivalence with the Riemann
hypothesis. *Not proved:* any larger aperture.

What a proof may use is delimited by theorems.

- It has to use the contacts with their sign: one reversed contact breaks the inequality inside
  the proved range.
- It has to use the comb of the integers: with the same comb every other system of contact
  weights fails, so an argument that would also run for an integer-norm system is wrong.
- It cannot be the section count alone: on every ring of sections the forced zeros on a graph are
  the powers of its contact with the diagonal, so Stepanov's inequality is the product formula;
  and a count with Riemann–Roch and Bézout exists iff the Hodge index holds (corpus).
- It cannot be an estimate of the defect term by term: the defect of a zero is
  `m_ρ|f̂(γ_ρ) − f̂(conj γ_ρ)|²/4`, positive for every off-line zero and suitable `f`.

- On `𝒮_q` Riemann–Roch is a count, `(((2A+1)^b)^{τ(M)})^{E+1}` sections of the level `M` with
  `E + 1` weights; Bézout is the other axiom, and no property of the tower is known that turns
  the count into an inequality between graphs on different lines.
- The failure, if any, is of finite rank relative to the zeros: at most one negative square of
  the defect for each zero to the right of the line.

Sub-problems in increasing order: (a) the lengths up to `log 3`, where still only the contact at
`2` enters, by a certificate of positivity at the aperture `½ log 3`, which
`tendsto_degreePairing_le_degrees_of_certificate` turns into the inequality; (b) the first length with
two contacts; (c) a bound for the index of the pairing on one logarithmic window, uniform in the
length.

### 13.2 Combs

The counterterm is `log(density/2π)` and the finite part is Weil's form on all degree points, on
the multiples of `g`, on the combs `e^{ℤ/N}` and on the comb of squares. The comparison of the
squares with the integers uses one property: the two Lévy densities differ by a continuous
kernel with an explicit primitive. The constant `2π` is the threshold of the inequality. *Open:* the same for the comb of the `a`-th powers with a
real `a > 0`, where the kernel is `aω(ax) − ω(x)`, and for a comb with a regularly varying
density; and whether a level of the tower singles out a comb on a window.

### 13.3 Systems of integer norms on their own comb

On the comb of the integers every system of contact weights but `Λ` fails. An integer-norm system
has its own counting function, hence its own comb, jets and counterterm. *Open:* whether Bézout
on its own comb holds exactly when its zeros are on the line, and which property of the count
`⌊x⌋` makes the comb of `ℤ` the one for which the jets are those of the Gamma factor. The
corpus separates `ℤ` from the other systems by the distance of the count from `x`
(`SumProductArithmeticOfTheLogarithm.count_distance_trichotomy`); the comb pairing has not been
computed for a count at positive distance.

This is construction V and question 6. *Proved:* for the system of the squares, whose own comb
is the comb of squares, the pairing with the jets between the norms, the density counterterm
`log(n/4π)` and the contacts `2Λ(k)/k` of the system has the limit `2 d₁ d₂ − Re W_w(F ⋆ F̃)` of
the comb of the integers with the weights of the system, and Bézout fails. The mechanism is
general: wherever the jets of a comb have Weil's form as finite part after the counterterm
`log(density/2π)`, as on the integers, the multiples of `g`, the combs `e^{ℤ/N}` and the
squares, the own comb gives the comb of the integers with the contacts of the system, and
that fails for every system but `ℤ`. *Open:*

- an integer-norm one-off whose comb is a lattice with a point removed from the monoid, such
  as the integers prime to `2`: the comb of the odd numbers has the density `½`, and the
  comparison of its jets with those of the even numbers is a translation by one step;
- combs without a density: a periodic comb that is not a lattice, such as the integers prime
  to `3`, where the finite part of the jets has a constant that depends on the gaps; and a
  system with a repeated norm, whose comb is not separated and on which the jets between two
  points of one norm are not defined;
- a system whose jets are its own: an archimedean factor attached to the system and not to
  the norms. For the squares that factor would be `Γ_ℝ(2s)`, and the pairing with it is the
  pairing of `ℤ` in the halved coordinate.

### 13.4 Number rings

Over a Dedekind domain the square is defined by congruences over ideals; its graphs meet in the
prime ideals with the count `log N𝔭`, the diagonal and `Γ_𝔭` are glued along the residue field,
the necklace coordinate at `𝔡` is the ideal `𝔡`, and the orbit of `𝔡` exists iff `𝔡` is
principal. For a ring with a zeta function `ζ(s) Π_i L(s, χ_i)` the pairing of the ideals
counted by their norms is computed on the comb of the integers, with the conductors as the
ramification term of the genus; the index of its square is the number of distinct zeros of the
zeta function to the right of the line; for a quadratic ring the conductor is forced from below;
and for large conductors the inequality holds on the packets shorter than `log 2`.

`TheExceptionalSquareRepelsTheOtherSquares.lean` transfers classical
Deuring–Heilbronn repulsion to the actual twisted-comb squares. A positive
real zero `β` forces the logarithmic margin
`m(Im ρ)≤1/2−|Re ρ−1/2|` on every zero except `β` and its reflection.
Each remaining square is bounded below by
`−mult(ρ) S(max(0,1/2−m(Im ρ)),a)∫|f|²`, where
`S(δ,a)=∫[-a,a]sinh²(δu)du`. `exists_twistedComb_square_margin` combines
these bounds with the proved twisted-comb limit.

*Open:*

- the conductors below `4π` (odd) and `128π` (even) on short packets, and every conductor on a
  packet that meets a contact;
- the ideals themselves as degree points. Two ideals of the same norm are one point of the comb
  of norms; a jet between two ideals is a function of a generator of their quotient and exists
  inside one class only, and the pairings twisted by the characters of the class group are those
  of the Hecke `L`-functions, whose explicit formulas are not in the corpus;
- the rings that are not abelian over `ℚ`, which bring Artin `L`-functions; and, for an abelian
  ring of composite conductor, the identification of the contacts `Λ(1 + Σ_i Re χ_i)` with the
  norms of its prime ideals at the ramified primes [C];
- the exact index on real packets: the families that attain it in the corpus are complex tests,
  each the sum of two real packets;
- the count of the odd characters modulo `p`, which turns the conjugate jets of the cyclotomic
  ring into `(p − 1)/2` complex places;
- the units of a real quadratic ring, which the norms do not see;
- the gluing index of the ideals of norm at most `X` against the zero count of the Dedekind zeta
  function, whose second term contains the discriminant;
- the converse for the conductor: the inequality with the conductor `q` for all packets, which
  is the Riemann hypothesis of the zeta function of the ring.

### 13.5 The planar square

The planar square has unit masses, the contact ideals of the square and paid contacts.
*Proved:* the ring, its contacts, the payment on chains and in the normal form, Bézout by
restriction as an equality, its position between the polynomial ring on the primes and the
square. *Open:*

- the count of its conditions against the zeros. On `Γ_1, …, Γ_n` the last graph carries the
  conditions `Π_{k < n} J(k, n)`; the order of this product is `Π_p p^{v_p(n)(v_p(n)+1)/2}`
  (elementary, not formalized), against `n` in the square. The gluing index `N!` of the square is
  the number of zeros below the height `2πN`; what the larger index of the planar square
  counts is not known;
- the dependence on the nodes. Planarity is defined with the nodes `p + ⋯ + p^l` of the
  coordinates `y_p = Σ_j V_p^j 1`; another sequence of integers with the same differences up to
  units at `p` gives another ring with the same two properties, and no invariant that selects
  one is known;
- a count of sections and its inequality. On the polynomial ring on the primes a section of
  degree `d` in each variable has the size `n^{O(d)}` on `Γ_n`, where a section of the tower has
  `q^{O(bn)}`; whether a count on it gives a bound that is not the product formula is open, and
  by criterion 6 it cannot give the sign;
- the archimedean pairing. The size of a section on a graph is a product of differences of
  nodes; a metric on the planar square whose pairing is the jets is not known, and on the
  projective line none exists (corpus: `SurfaceOfSpecZ.NoMetricOnTheProjectiveLineCarriesTheJets`).

### 13.6 The exceptional square

*Statement.* For a real primitive character of conductor `q` the twisted part of the square of
its quadratic ring is nonpositive on the windows of aperture `log log q + C`, for every `C` and
all large `q`. *Proved:* the sign up to the trivial aperture `log log q − o(1)`, by the sizes
of the contacts alone; the failure beyond it of the systems with those sizes and other signs;
the depth of the one square of a real zero; repulsion of the other squares. *Not proved:* any
aperture `log log q + C`. A proof has to use the signs of `χ` at the primes below `(log q)²`;
it would exclude real zeros near `1`.

### 13.7 A Lefschetz operator and a boundary

*Construction I.* An operator on the real divisors of a window with the Hodge–Riemann
relations at a base point, whose hard Lefschetz property is not the Riemann hypothesis.
*Proved:* for the dilation, the overlap flow and the coupling Laplacian hard Lefschetz at the
top coprime stratum is the Riemann hypothesis (corpus). The edge length of the tree of `2` is
a deformation with a positive generator on packets shorter than `log 4`: a base point without
hypothesis, monotone transport and one threshold `ℓ*(L)`, with `ℓ*(L) ≤ log 2 − 1/10000` for
`L ≤ 181/250` (Q26). *Open:* the value of `ℓ*(L)` for `181/250 < L ≤ log 4`, where
`ℓ*(L) ≤ log 2` is Bézout at the length `L`; a deformation whose generator stays positive when
the second point of a chain is seen; an operator that moves the archimedean fibre. A decision
for the first would be a bound of the Rayleigh quotient `inf Z(f)/E₂(f)` from below without a
certificate.

*Construction II.* The jets are the Newtonian interaction of charges at the points `±n` of an
axis, and the contacts of a prime are chain squares minus the diagonal (corpus). *Proved:* a
kernel invariant under the adelic group has a constant diagonal (corpus); along the comb the
counterterm is the sum of the Busemann functions of the trees, a tree glued with an edge length
other than `log p` fails Bézout, contacts are traded against the constant at the supremum of
their symbol, and a common factor on all contacts is refuted for every constant
(`treeBezout_iff`, `combBezoutWith_iff`, `not_combBezoutScaled`). *Open:* deviations that are
neither summable against `n^{-1/2}` nor a multiple of `Λ` plus a summable one, such as other
edge lengths at infinitely many primes without decay. A decision would be the exchange law for
the deviations bounded by a multiple of `Λ`.

### 13.8 A count

*Construction VI and question 8.* The Riemann hypothesis holds iff some count with values in
`ℕ` has the integer probes as its quadratic Tate limit (corpus). *Proved:* the count that the
square has on two graphs is `|x_m x_n|`, exactly quadratic on rays, blind to the contact and to
the sign, and not a form (Q27); on the tower and on the planar square the counts are exact and
give identities. *Open:* natural numbers on the rays of `Γ_1 + Γ_p` whose Tate limit is the
pairing; a count on three or more graphs, where the rank and the degree of homogeneity are the
number of graphs; a count of sections in an archimedean box on the pair ring, where the
contact enters as the index `c` of the order in `ℤ × ℤ`. A decision would be a count whose
second-order term in the box is the logarithm of that index with the sign of the pairing.

## 15. Success criteria and contributions

**Success** is a property of the square that is an inequality: a positive class, a Lefschetz
operator, a boundary or a count whose positivity is not the statement to be proved. It is
measured on the constructions of the next stage (§6) and on the nine questions (§8.1).

- The object is fixed: the settled questions of §8.2 have theorem-level answers.
- Constructions III and IV are closed by their kill conditions: on the planar square
  restriction is an equality of ideals, and in a tower the form of `ζ` has the weight one over
  the degree.
- Construction V is decided on the comb of squares: the own comb gives the comb of the
  integers with the contacts of the system. Construction II is decided for the trees glued
  along the comb with an edge length: the gluing is the pairing itself and has no modulus.
  Construction I has one candidate that is not killed on short windows: the length of the tree
  of `2`, with a positive generator and a threshold on packets shorter than `log 4`; it does
  not continue beyond. Construction VI: the count that the square has on two graphs is the
  split norm, not the pairing; a count with the pairing as its Tate limit is open. Each
  construction has a kill condition that a contribution may meet instead of the deliverable.
- Question 1, Bézout for all packets, is the Riemann hypothesis, and the sign of the twisted
  part beyond the trivial aperture is the Landau–Siegel problem. Nothing in this folder proves
  either or claims to.

**A contribution** to AGZ

- is unconditional: no hypothesis, no axiom, no statement assumed for `ζ`;
- names what it addresses: a question of §8, a construction of §6 with its kill condition, a
  criterion, or an open topic of §13;
- states its objects in the language of §2–§3: on which ring or comb the Frobenius acts, which
  contacts, which jets, which counterterm, which Verschiebung;
- either supplies a step of Weil's or of Stepanov's argument on the object, or proves with its
  exact price that the step is absent;
- passes the decisive test when it concerns the sign: an argument for Bézout must fail for a
  system of contact weights other than `Λ`, for a reversed contact, for the Liouville twist at
  the first rung and for a wrong constant, and must say where;
- is a statement on the infinite object. Not contributions: finite truncations with no limit,
  better constants in a proved bound that open no new length, a restatement of an equivalence
  with the Riemann hypothesis, a search for a producer inside constructions A and B, which are
  closed.
