## 1. Motivation

Both proofs of the Riemann hypothesis for a curve `C/𝔽_q` take place on `C × C`.

- **Weil.** The graphs `Γ_n` of `Fⁿ` are divisors on a surface with a Hodge index theorem;
  `Γ_0·Γ_n` is the number of points over `𝔽_{qⁿ}`.
- **Stepanov.** A section of the ideal `I_Δ^ℓ` of small bidegree `(a, b)` exists by Riemann–Roch on
  the surface; restricted to `Γ_n` it has degree `a + qⁿb` and vanishes to order `ℓ` on `Γ_0 ∩ Γ_n`.

Both need the same thing: a two-dimensional object with a diagonal and spaces of sections. `ζ` is
rigid (Hamburger, FE-defect isolation, degree one): no family of twists or covers replaces the
base, and the zero motive of `ζ` is the fixed part of every arithmetic family. So the object has
to be a product of `Spec ℤ` with itself.

AGZ does not define `𝔽₁`. It studies the geometries in which `Spec ℤ` has a product with itself and
asks of each: which steps of Weil's and of Stepanov's argument run on it, which are absent, and
what the geometry proves instead. Every transported mechanism gets its exact price before it is
used.

The geometry singled out by the corpus's data is the one in which descent to `𝔽₁` is a commuting
family of Frobenius lifts (a Λ-structure). There the product exists and is canonical,

  `Spec ℤ ×_{𝔽₁} Spec ℤ = Spec W(ℤ)`,

the cofree ring with commuting Frobenius lifts over `ℤ`. In ghost coordinates it is the ring `𝒢`
of integer sequences `(x_n)_{n≥1}` with `x_{pn} ≡ x_n (mod p^{v_p(n)+1})`. It is the union of the
Frobenius graphs glued along the primes: it carries the prime part of every off-diagonal
intersection number of Weil's form exactly, and it is a curve. What it lacks is named by three
further constructions: the archimedean fibre, a second dimension, and the diagonal.

## 3. Language

- **Arithmetic square**: `Spec 𝒢`. **Contact order** of two graphs at `p`: the exponent of `p` in
  `J(m, n)`. **Fixed scheme** of `F_n` on the diagonal: `Γ_1 ∩ Γ_n`; its **count** is the logarithm
  of its order.
- **Effective** element: all necklace coordinates `≥ 0`.
- **Constant**: `δ_p x = 0` for every `p`.
- **Weight** `z`: `F_n` acts by `n^z`. **Transposition**: `F ↔ V`; on weight `z` it is `z ↦ 1 − z`.
- **Witt Verschiebung**: an additive `V_k` with the projection formula
  `V_k(x · F_k y) = V_k(x) · y`. **Perfect**: every `F_k` invertible; then `V_k = k F_k^{-1}`.
- **Cost** of an auxiliary: `log |x_n|` on the graph `Γ_n`, the number of its zeros there counted
  with weight `log p` (product formula).
- **Forced zeros** of a lattice on `Γ_n`: the ideal generated by the restrictions.
- **Carry**: a defect of additivity of a multiplicative section (Teichmüller, digits, floor).

## 4. Mechanisms

1. **Dwork congruences are the gluing.** `𝒢 ⊗ ℚ = Π ℚ`: the square is the union of the graphs; the
   congruences glue `Γ_m` to `Γ_n` along `Spec ℤ/J(m, n)`.
2. **Necklace decomposition.** `x = Σ_d b_d(x) e_d` with integer `b_d`: after subtracting the
   orbits of the proper divisors of `n`, the congruences give `n ∣ x_n`.
3. **Cofreeness.** For a ring `A` with Frobenius lifts and `f : A → ℤ`, `a ↦ (f(ψ_n a))_n` is the
   unique equivariant lift `A → 𝒢` (`a ≡ b mod p^k ⇒ a^p ≡ b^p mod p^{k+1}`).
4. **`F V = n`.** `F_{gn} V_{gm} = g·V_m F_n` for coprime `m, n`: the common factor of two degrees
   is a multiplicity. This is the gcd twist of Weil's prime kernel.
5. **Transposition is the functional equation.** On a weight line `F_n = n^z` forces `V_n = n^{1−z}`.
6. **Support.** A Witt Verschiebung is supported on multiples: `(V_k y)_n = 0` for `k ∤ n`, because
   `V_k(y)(e_k − k) = 0`. A perfect Verschiebung is supported everywhere.
7. **The restriction to the Frobenius graph.** `x_p = x_1^p + p·(δ_p x)_1`: the restriction to
   `Γ_p` is the `p`-th power of the restriction to the diagonal plus `p` times the derivative. On
   the diagonal ideal `(δ_p x)_1 = b_p(x)`: the derivatives at different primes are independent
   coordinates.
8. **Product formula.** `Σ_p v_p(a) log p = log |a|`: on each graph Stepanov's inequality is an
   identity. An ideal power restricts to the power of the restriction.
9. **The carry.** `δ_p(x + y) = δ_p x + δ_p y + C_p(x, y)`, `p·C_p = x^p + y^p − (x + y)^p`;
   `log binom(a+b, a) = Σ_k Λ(k)(⌊(a+b)/k⌋ − ⌊a/k⌋ − ⌊b/k⌋)`.

## 5. Questions

| # | Question | Answer | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Which endomorphisms have `N(n)` fixed points, and on what? | The Witt Frobenius `F_n` on the arithmetic square: its fixed scheme on the diagonal is `Spec 𝔽_p` if `n` is a power of `p` and empty otherwise, and `log #(ℤ/J(1, n)) = Λ(n)`. On every ring with Frobenius lifts and a `ℤ`-point the fixed scheme of `ψ_{p^k}` contains `p`; the cofree ring has nothing more. On the Adams ring `T` the fixed points of `ψ_n` are the primes with `p − 1 ∣ n − 1`. The terms `1 + 1/(n² − 1)` are the trace of `F_n` on the jet ring. | Lean (first part); paper |
| Q2 | What is the multiplicity of a function at a prime over `𝔽₁`? | The order of the `p`-derivation: `v_p(δ_p a) = v_p(a) − 1` on `ℤ`; on the graph `Γ_p`, `v_p(x_p) = 1 + v_p(b_p(x))` for `x` vanishing on the diagonal. Between graphs it is the contact order `1 + v_p(m)` of `Γ_m` and `Γ_{mp^j}`. | Lean (contact); paper |
| Q3 | Why is the diagonal not local? | In the Λ-square the diagonal is one component of a curve. Its conormal module is torsion, supported on the crossings, of order `lcm(1,…,N)` after truncation at `N`. | paper |
| Q4 | What is ampleness, and why would Riemann–Roch imply the Hodge index without assuming it? | — | open; needs the object of Q9 |
| Q5 | Where do the Gamma factor and the pole enter as geometry? | As weights of the same Frobenius on the same sequence ring: the pole plane is the weights `0, 1`, the Gamma factor the weights `−2k`; they are not in `𝒢`, because their Verschiebung is perfect and the Witt one is supported on multiples. | paper |
| Q6 | Why do monoids with integer norms that are not `ℤ` fail? | The canonical map of an integer-norm monoid to the square factors through its norms: the Λ-structure sees the set of norms, not their multiplicities. The dividing line is additive, the count `⌊x⌋` (count-distance rigidity). | paper / corpus |
| Q7 | What is the normal direction to the diagonal? | — | open; construction E |
| Q8 | Which duality exchanges the finite contacts and the archimedean jets? | None exchanges them. Transposition `F ↔ V` is `z ↦ 1 − z` and preserves the contact class and the jet class separately; their sum with the pole plane is symmetric, and that is the functional equation. What separates them is support: contacts live on integer ratios of degrees, jets on all rational ratios. | paper |
| Q9 | Is Weil's diagonal a finite part of the glued object? | — | open; (G) of §5.3 of the report |

## 9. Traps

| Trap | Truth |
|---|---|
| The Λ-square is a surface | it is a curve: the union of the Frobenius graphs |
| The point count is a cardinality | it is the logarithm of the order of a fixed scheme plus a trace; `N(n)` is not an integer |
| `F_n` has about `n` fixed points | at most one on the diagonal; the Adams ring gives the primes with `p − 1 ∣ n − 1`, never a window |
| High δ-multiplicity can be cheap | first order at every prime is free; every further order costs `θ(S)` |
| `δ_p` is a derivation | it is additive only up to the carry, and it is a different operator at each prime |
| The square is translation invariant in `log n` | `J(km, kn) ≠ J(m, n)`; invariance of Weil's kernel comes from `F V = n` |
| The archimedean fibre can be glued with one Verschiebung | Witt's is supported on multiples, the jets' is perfect; they disagree at `1` |
| The FE exchanges primes and Gamma factor | it is `F ↔ V` and preserves each |
| More sections give a count | the forced zeros of `I^ℓ` on `Γ_n` are `ℓ·(Γ_1 ∩ Γ_n)`, attained |

## 11. Success criteria and contributions

AGZ succeeds when each of Q1–Q9 has a theorem-level answer or a named object it waits for, each
construction has its deliverables proved or its kill condition proved with the strongest positive
survivor, and each criterion is decided on every construction that reaches it.

A **contribution** must be unconditional; name the construction (A–E) and the question or
criterion it addresses; state on which ring or space the Frobenius acts, what its fixed points
are and which Verschiebung it uses; and say whether it supplies a step of Weil's or Stepanov's
argument, or proves with its exact price that the step is absent. Searches for a producer inside
constructions A and B are closed.
