Specification: §9 of [the review-0911 report](../../../docs/reports/review-0911/fable.md).

Status column: **Lean** = proved in this folder; **corpus** = proved elsewhere in the corpus;
**paper** = proved on the pipeline card, not yet in Lean; **open** = no proof.

## 1. Motivation

Both proofs of the Riemann hypothesis for a curve `C/𝔽_q` take place on `C × C`:

- **Weil.** The graphs `Γ_n` of the Frobenius `Fⁿ` are divisors on a surface with a Hodge index
  theorem; `Γ_0·Γ_n` is the number of points over `𝔽_{qⁿ}`.
- **Stepanov.** A function on `C × C` of small bidegree, restricted to `Γ_n`, vanishes to high order
  at the points of `Γ_0 ∩ Γ_n`, because `Γ_n` is inseparable over the second factor.

Over `ℤ` the corpus has the numbers (`Γ_1·Γ_n = Λ(n) + 1 + 1/(n²−1)`), two half-products in which a
factor is replaced by `ℙ¹_{𝔽₁}`, the local fibres of the off-diagonal, and the exact Frobenius of
integer auxiliaries (the Adams operations). It has no product of `Spec ℤ` with itself.

`ζ` is rigid: no family of twists, covers or deformations replaces the base (§1.4 (d) of the
report). AGZ therefore does not define `𝔽₁`. It studies the geometries in which `Spec ℤ` has a
product with itself, and for each asks one question: does Weil's or Stepanov's argument run on it,
and if not, which step is missing and what does the geometry prove instead?

The geometry singled out by the corpus's own data is the one in which descent to `𝔽₁` is a
commuting family of Frobenius lifts (a Λ-structure). In it the product exists and is canonical:

  `Spec ℤ ×_{𝔽₁} Spec ℤ = Spec W(ℤ)`,

the big Witt vectors of `ℤ`, the cofree ring with commuting Frobenius lifts over `ℤ`. AGZ develops
this object in ghost coordinates, where it is the ring `𝒢` of integer sequences `(x_n)_{n≥1}` with
`x_{pn} ≡ x_n (mod p^{v_p(n)+1})`, and tests it against the three constructions and ten criteria
of §9.

## 3. Language

- **Arithmetic square**: `Spec 𝒢`. **Graph**: a ghost component. **Contact order** of two graphs at
  `p`: the exponent of `p` in `J(m, n)`.
- **Fixed points of `F_n` on the diagonal**: the scheme `Γ_1 ∩ Γ_n`. Its **count** is the logarithm
  of its order, not its cardinality.
- **Effective** element: all necklace coordinates `≥ 0` (the point counts of a set with Frobenius).
- **Constant** at `p`: `δ_p x = 0`. **Constant**: constant at every prime.
- **Inseparable at `p`**: `F_k` is inseparable at `p` if it agrees with a power of the `p`-Frobenius
  modulo `p`; this holds exactly for `p ∣ k`.
- **Cost** of an auxiliary `x ∈ 𝒢`: `log |x_1|`, its archimedean size on the diagonal; by the
  product formula this is the number of its zeros on the diagonal, counted with weight `log p`.
- **Carry**: any defect of additivity of a multiplicative section (Teichmüller, digits, floor).

## 4. Mechanisms

1. **Dwork congruences are the gluing.** `𝒢 ⊗ ℚ = Π ℚ`: the square is the union of the graphs
   `Γ_n`, each a copy of `Spec ℤ`; the congruences glue `Γ_m` to `Γ_n` along `Spec ℤ/J(m, n)`.
2. **Necklace decomposition.** `x = Σ_d b_d(x) e_d` with integer `b_d`, by induction on `n`: after
   subtracting the orbits of proper divisors, the congruences give `n ∣ x_n`.
   `e_d e_{d'} = gcd(d, d')·e_{lcm(d, d')}`.
3. **Cofreeness.** For a ring `A` with Frobenius lifts and a point `f : A → ℤ`, the sequence
   `(f(ψ_n a))_n` satisfies the Dwork congruences (`a ≡ b mod p^k ⇒ a^p ≡ b^p mod p^{k+1}`), and
   `a ↦ (f(ψ_n a))_n` is the unique equivariant lift `A → 𝒢` of `f`.
4. **`F V = n`.** `F_n V_m = g·V_{m/g} F_{n/g}`, `g = gcd(m, n)`: the Frobenius of degree `g` and its
   transpose compose to the degree. This is the gcd twist of Weil's prime kernel.
5. **Product formula.** For a nonzero integer `a`, `Σ_p v_p(a) log p = log |a|`: on the diagonal
   Stepanov's inequality `#zeros · order ≤ degree` is an identity.
6. **Constants are the monoid.** `F_p x = x^p` for all `p` forces `x_n = x_1ⁿ`.
7. **The carry is the defect of additivity.** `δ_p(x + y) = δ_p x + δ_p y + C_p(x, y)` with
   `p·C_p(x, y) = x^p + y^p − (x + y)^p`; `δ_p` lowers the `p`-adic order by exactly one.

## 5. Questions of the programme

| # | Question (§9.5) | Answer | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Which endomorphisms have `N(n)` fixed points, and on what? | The Witt Frobenius `F_n` on the arithmetic square. Its fixed scheme on the diagonal is `Spec ℤ/e^{Λ(n)}`: the point `p` if `n = p^k`, empty otherwise; `Λ(n)` is the logarithm of its order. This holds on the cofree ring; on every ring with Frobenius lifts and a `ℤ`-point the fixed scheme of `ψ_{p^k}` contains `p`. On the Adams ring `T` the fixed points of `ψ_n` are the primes with `p − 1 ∣ n − 1`. The terms `1` and `1/(n²−1)` of `N(n)` are archimedean and are not in `W(ℤ)`. | paper |
| Q2 | What is the multiplicity of a function at a prime over `𝔽₁`? | The order of the `p`-derivation: `v_p(δ_p a) = v_p(a) − 1` for `p ∣ a`, so the `δ_p`-order of an integer is `v_p`. `δ_p` is not additive; its defect is the carry. Between graphs the multiplicity is the contact order `1 + v_p(m)` of `Γ_m` and `Γ_{mp^j}`. | paper |
| Q3 | Why is the diagonal not local? | In the Λ-square the diagonal is one component of a curve: its conormal module is torsion, supported on the crossings with the other graphs, of order `lcm(1,…,N)` after truncation at `N`. There is no normal direction, hence no self-intersection; Λ-geometry gives the off-diagonal only. | paper |
| Q4 | What is ampleness, and why would Riemann–Roch imply the Hodge index without assuming it? | — | open |
| Q5 | Where do the Gamma factor and the pole enter as geometry? | Not through a Λ-structure: `W(ℤ)` is affine and carries the prime part of every intersection number exactly; the cusp and the jets `min/(1 − (min/max)²)` are the complement. | paper (first half) |
| Q6 | Why do monoids with integer norms that are not `ℤ` fail? | The canonical map of an integer-norm monoid to the square factors through its norms: the Λ-structure sees the set of norms, not their multiplicities. The dividing line is additive: the count `⌊x⌋` (count-distance rigidity, corpus). | paper / corpus |

criterion is read as: which input of the criterion is present in the square, which is absent.

## 9. Traps

| Trap | Truth |
|---|---|
| The Λ-square is a surface | it is a curve: the union of the Frobenius graphs |
| The point count is a cardinality | it is the logarithm of the order of a fixed scheme; `N(n)` is not an integer |
| `F_n` has about `n` fixed points | it has at most one on the diagonal; the Adams ring gives more (`p − 1 ∣ n − 1`), none of them a count of a window |
| High δ-multiplicity can be cheap | the cost is the product formula, an identity |
| `δ_p` is a derivation | it is additive only up to the carry |
| The square is translation invariant in `log n` | `J(km, kn) ≠ J(m, n)`: `F_k` is not an automorphism; invariance is restored only by `F V = n` |

## 11. Success criteria and contributions

AGZ succeeds when each of Q1–Q6 has a theorem-level answer, each construction has its deliverables
proved or its kill condition proved with the strongest positive survivor, and each criterion
C1–C10 is decided for every construction that reaches it.

A **contribution** must be unconditional; name the construction (A, B, C) and the question or
criterion it addresses; state on which ring or space the Frobenius acts and what its fixed points
are; and say whether it supplies a step of Weil's or Stepanov's argument, or proves that the step
is absent.
