# Arithmetic Coupling Theory (ACoT)

Namespace `GaloisForLFunctions.ArithmeticCouplingTheory`; umbrella `ArithmeticCouplingTheory.lean`.
Working name in the review: *the sum–product Hodge theory of the scaling surface*.

## 1. Motivation

Every RH route in the corpus reads one integer: `κ = κ(K_ζ)`, the number of zeros right of the
critical line, as the negative index of Weil's correlation `K_ζ(u) = W(B, T_{log u} B)` on `ℚ_{>0}`.
Three corpus results say where that integer lives.

1. **The index is carried by the coupling.** Restricted to a two-prime lattice `⟨p, q⟩` the
   correlation keeps its full glued index `κ + 1`. Its canonical split into an axial (separable)
   part and an inter-tower coupling `C(u, v) = K(uv) − K(u) − K(v) + K(1)` puts all zero information
   in the coupling: separable assemblies are blind or diverge, and a continuous profile without
   coupling is affine (`AdelicKreinGeometry/TowerCoupling`).
2. **The prime side is an incidence calculus.** In prime-incidence coordinates the prime part of the
   complete Weil form is made of nonnegative divisibility counts, with one coherent logarithmic
   excitation that the prime side cannot cancel (`CriticalScaleArithmeticRigidity`).
3. **Successor and dilation are jointly rigid.** Additive shift and prime dilation together see every
   nonconstant conductor with the sharp constant `3/4`; their joint kernel is the constants.

All three are statements about how the additive structure of `ℤ` (successor, divisibility, windows
of the prime count) couples its multiplicative towers. Over `𝔽_q` this coupling is geometric: the
Frobenius graph couples the two factors of `C × C`, and its intersection numbers are point counts.
Over `ℤ` there was no theory of this coupling at the level of positive forms. ACoT is that theory.

Edge Hodge theory measures where positivity fails. ACoT asks which mechanisms can *produce*
positivity across coupled towers, and answers with theorems in three registers: stability
(approximately separable ⇒ near separable, with constants), incidence identities (positivity as a
count), and rigidity (kernel theorems that forbid crossings). Every statement is unconditional;
where a mechanism ends in an RH equivalence, the theorem says so and the trap table records it.

| Object | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Kernel on a group | `K : G → ℂ`, Hermitian if `K(u⁻¹) = conj K(u)`; Gram forms `Σ c̄_a c_b K(x_a⁻¹x_b)`, negative index `κ(K)` |
| Coupling across `(G₁, G₂)` | `C_K(u, v) = K(uv) − K(u) − K(v) + K(1)`, `u ∈ G₁`, `v ∈ G₂`; the mixed second difference |
| Axial kernel | `K(uv) = K(u) + K(v) − K(1)` on `G₁G₂`: the coupling-free kernels |
| Künneth splitting | `ℂ[G₁ × G₂] = ℂ ⊕ I₁ ⊕ I₂ ⊕ I₁ ⊗ I₂` (augmentation ideals); a kernel meets `I₁ ⊗ I₂` only through its coupling |
| Mixed-difference test | `f_{u,v} = (T_u − 1)(T_v − 1) f` for a test `f` and scales `u, v` |
| Coupling form | the Weil form on mixed-difference tests; its kernel is the coupling Laplacian `L_u L_v K`, `L_u = 2 − τ_u − τ_{u⁻¹}` |
| Antidiagonal | the pairs `(u, u⁻¹)`; `C_K(u, u⁻¹) = 2(K(1) − Re K(u))` for Hermitian `K` |
| Triangle box | `T_L = {(x, y) : |x|, |y|, |x + y| ≤ L}` in log-coordinates; the natural domain of local stability |
| Coupling growth | `M_K(L) = sup_{T_L} |C_K|` over a pair of lattices |
| Two-prime log-lattice | `log⟨p, q⟩ = ℤ log p + ℤ log q`, dense in `ℝ` |
| Prime-incidence packet | `F_b(n) = Σ_{p ∈ P} b_p (1_{p|n} − 1/p)`: the coherent prime sector |
| Incidence event | `[p ∣ k ∧ p ∤ n]`, the dilation jump of an incidence packet |
| Dilation jump energy | `J_N(F) = Σ_k Λ(k)/k Σ_{kn ≤ N} (F(kn) − F(n))²/n` |
| Mertens defect | `μ(x) = Σ_{k ≤ x} Λ(k)/k − log x`, tending to `−γ` |
| Prime dilation form | `P_N(F) = Σ_k Λ(k)/k Σ_{kn ≤ N} 2F(n)F(kn)/n`: the prime part of the Weil form on narrow atoms at `log n` |
| Affine incidence energy | mean of `(F(kn + h) − F(n))²`: the successor–dilation coupling of divisibility events |
| Rigid kernel | a subspace that contains every null vector along a path of Hermitian forms |
| Curve coupling | `C(a, b) = Σ_j (β_j^a − 1)(β_j^b − 1)` for a curve datum with normalized eigenvalues `β_j` |

## 3. Language

- **Separable / axial**: a sum of single-tower functions; **coupled**: anything else. **Coupling-free**:
  zero coupling across the chosen pair of subgroups.
- **Coprime** pair of lattices: generated by disjoint prime sets, so `G₁ ∩ G₂ = 1` and `G₁G₂ ≅ G₁ × G₂`.
- **Stable**: approximate coupling-freeness implies near-axiality, with explicit constants.
- **Lossless transfer**: two-sided comparison of a coupling functional with a kernel functional with
  absolute constants and no loss in the height parameter.
- **Coupling-complete invariant**: vanishes on axial kernels and still determines the index.
- **Incidence square**: a squared difference of packet values that is an indicator of divisibility
  events; **Mertens term**: the diagonal `Σ F(n)²/n · (log N + μ(N/n))`; **running counterterm**: its
  `log N` part, cancelled by the archimedean diagonal.
- **Crossing**: a time at which a path of Hermitian forms is degenerate; **crossing-free**: none.
- **Calibration**: the same statement over `𝔽_q[T]`, where log-lattices are discrete and the scaling
  group has rank one.

## 4. Mechanisms

**A. Coupling calculus.**
1. *Künneth splitting.* Pairing a kernel with `c ⊗ d`, `c ∈ I₁`, `d ∈ I₂`, sees only its coupling:
   `Σ c(u)d(v)K(uv) = Σ c(u)d(v)C_K(u, v)`. Axial kernels are exactly the kernels annihilated by
   `I₁ ⊗ I₂`.
2. *Polarization.* For a correlation `K(g) = W(f, T_g f)` of an isometric action,
   `C_K(u, v) = W((T_{u⁻¹} − 1)f, (T_v − 1)f)`: the coupling is the Weil pairing of difference tests.
3. *Characters.* The coupling of a character is a product, `(χ(u) − 1)(χ(v) − 1)`; the trivial
   character has none. A sum over zeros `Σ_ρ w_ρ u^{ρ − 1/2}` has coupling
   `Σ_ρ w_ρ (u^{ρ−1/2} − 1)(v^{ρ−1/2} − 1)`.
4. *Antidiagonal.* For Hermitian `K`, `C_K(u, u⁻¹) = 2(K(1) − Re K(u))`: the self-coupling of a
   scale is the kernel itself.

**B. Stability.**
5. *Local Hyers–Ulam.* If `|ψ(x + y) − ψ(x) − ψ(y)| ≤ M` on the triangle box `T_L` and `ψ` is bounded on
   `[−L, L]`, then `|ψ(x) − (x/L)ψ(L)| ≤ 3M` on `[−L, L]`.
6. *Density transfer.* Two coprime two-prime lattices have dense logarithms, so the coupling of a
   continuous profile on their product box controls its coupling on the whole real triangle box,
   including the antidiagonal.
7. *Losslessness.* Combining 4–6: `sup_{|log w| ≤ L} |Re K(w) − K(1)| ≤ ½ M_K(L) ≤ 2 sup_{|log w| ≤ L}|K(w)|`.
   The coupling is exactly as large as the kernel.

**C. Completeness.**
8. *Mixed differences preserve the seen zeros.* The transform of `f_{u,v}` is that of `f` times
   `(e^{iuγ} − 1)(e^{ivγ} − 1)`, which never vanishes at an off-line ordinate. The coupling form
   annihilates axial kernels and constants, yet carries every negative square of `K`.

**D. Incidence.**
9. *Mertens polarization.* `2F(n)F(kn) = F(n)² + F(kn)² − (F(kn) − F(n))²` and `Σ_{k | m} Λ(k) = log m`
   give, for every `F` and `N`, the exact identity
   `P_N(F) = Σ_{n ≤ N} F(n)²/n · (log N + μ(N/n)) − J_N(F)`: the `log n` parts cancel exactly.
10. *Incidence squares.* On prime-incidence packets every jump is an event:
    `(F_b(p^j n) − F_b(n))² = b_p² [p ∤ n]`, so `J_N(F_b)` is a weighted count of nondivisibility.
11. *Affine incidence.* Divisibility events of distinct primes are independent over complete periods
    (CRT), so the mean affine incidence energy is an exact sum of squares.

**E. Rigidity.** A path of Hermitian forms whose null vectors stay in a rigid kernel on which the forms
vanish has constant index modulo that kernel.

**F. Calibration.** Over a curve the coupling of the `q`-tower with itself is
`Σ_j (β_j^a − 1)(β_j^b − 1)`; its antidiagonal is hit exactly by discrete lattices.

## 5. Questions

1. What is the size of the inter-tower coupling of `ratCorr`, as a four-window prime sum, and which
   cancellations does coprimality force?
2. Is there a local Hyers–Ulam inequality for kernels on `ℚ_{>0}` with polynomial constants?
3. Is the complete Weil form on the coherent prime sector a sum of incidence squares minus an exact
   Mertens term?
4. Do near-edge negative directions localize in the coherent sector?
5. Which arithmetic rigidity theorems make source paths crossing-free?
6. What is the arithmetic analogue of the self-intersection of the Frobenius graph: a count attached
   to the coupling of two prime towers?

## 6. Tools

- Local Hyers–Ulam inequalities on intervals and on dense subgroups of `ℝ`.
- Coupling calculus on products of groups: Künneth splitting, polarization, antidiagonal, Laplacian.
- Lossless coupling–kernel transfer and coupling growth rates.
- Mixed-difference tests and the coupling form; zero-visibility under mixed differences.
- Mertens polarization and prime-incidence coordinates; incidence-square certificates.
- Affine incidence (sum–product) energies for divisibility events.
- Rigidity-to-noncrossing transfer for Hermitian paths.
- Curve couplings for the `𝔽_q` calibration.

## 11. Success and contribution criteria

The programme succeeds when its invariants are coupling-complete, its stability theorems carry
explicit constants, its incidence identities make every prime-side quantity on the coherent sector a
count with the pole entering only through Mertens' constant and the running counterterm, its
positivity statements are identities or stability theorems rather than estimates against a ground
energy, it calibrates over `𝔽_q`, its rigidity theorems are strong enough to forbid crossings, and it
separates `ζ` from flip twins.

A contribution is an unconditional Lean theorem about explicitly defined coupling, incidence or
rigidity objects that either (a) proves an identity or inequality with explicit constants, (b)
answers one of the questions in §5, or (c) closes a mechanism with an exact obstruction together
with its strongest positive survivor. Renaming an existing identity is not a contribution.
