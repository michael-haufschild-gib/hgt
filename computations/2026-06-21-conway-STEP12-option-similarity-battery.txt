3 reverse: phase-similarity, not amplitude-similarity, predicts valid suppression.
4 viewpoint: the option space is a feature space, not a scalar magnitude list.
5 visual: similar distractors crowd the target only in the metric they share.
6 action: suppression is local normalization by neighboring alternatives.
7 essentials: two feature maps are enough for the boundary test.
8 similar objects: finite Euler and random-phase controls are the neighbors tested.

12 invariant: left-strip random-phase is amplitude-similar but phase-dissimilar.
13 monotone: raw value and false-similarity decline moving rightward.
14 weaken: claim only metric-dependence of competition, not a new phase statistic.

16 minimal counterexample: random-phase preserves amplitudes but loses phase-neighbor status.
17 representation: feature-metric representation is the crux.
