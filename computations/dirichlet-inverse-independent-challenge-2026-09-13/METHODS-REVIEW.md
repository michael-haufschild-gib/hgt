# Mechanical method audit

This is a retrospective applicability audit. The original pre-run PROTOCOL.md and METHODS.md remain unchanged. Concrete family-specific variations and falsifiers are those frozen in PROTOCOL.md; this audit does not invent additional runs.

1. **Split the question.** Separate validity of roots or critical points, continuation, endpoint classification, and global interpretation.

2. **Attack it as false.** Use the explicitly frozen competing explanation in PROTOCOL.md; retain every failed or inconsistent result.

3. **Reverse it.** Study maps from zeros to values and back from critical values to zero endpoints; reversals are controls when derived.

4. **Change viewpoint.** Treat zeros as inverse branches or graph vertices, while keeping literal function and access path explicit.

5. **Make it visual.** Use saved domain trajectories, endpoint tables, graphs or access plots; the mixed join needs only exact component data.

6. **Identify the action.** Transport roots, join graph components, or compare independently measured access classifications, as specified by this family.

7. **Strip to essentials.** Use the literal zeta or declared character L-function. No uncomputed physical action is substituted.

8. **Compare similar objects.** Cross-carrier comparisons use only the verified conductor4/5 completion data. Generic local branching is a control.

9. **Use small cases.** Discovery pilots share the family clock; validation sizes use measured parent costs. The graph join is already a small exact calculation.

10. **Test extremes.** Retain trivial roots, near-critical starts, approach to1 and explicit domain escapes when applicable; do not extrapolate limits.

11. **Run experiments.** Execute only the frozen jobs and concrete variations in this family. The guard bounds the entire worker group.

12. **Find invariants.** Check function residual, endpoint identity under independent validation and declared component counts. Do not assume homotopy avoidance.

13. **Test monotonicity.** Height order, loop-size behavior and access type are tested where relevant; no monotonicity theorem is imported.

14. **Keep weaker bridges separate.** Treat finite connections and graph consistency as observations. They are not substitutes for a global RH rigidity theorem.

15. **Freeze new hunches independently.** Graph/access matching and missing-edge explanations were frozen as new hypotheses in their own protocols; failures remain in earlier reports.

16. **Use minimal counterexamples.** Retain the smallest reported differing label or exceptional component, without claiming global minimality beyond the sample.

17. **Change representation.** Compare complex paths, critical-value incidences and finite graphs without conflating these representations.

18. **Search applicable methods.** Use archived corpus deep/source-method evidence and inspected source statements. Partial retrieval is not absence, and source text is not a fresh kernel certificate.

19. **Expose the fake proof.** The invalid shortcut is to infer zero-fibre confinement from a finite graph or an asymptotic formula valid only in an already-entered regime.

20. **Name the missing lemma.** Missing ingredients are a controlled full-path lift, complete critical/asymptotic classification and a source-derived rigidity implication.

21. **Trace dependencies.** Trace numerical evaluation -> controlled finite observation -> independent validation -> finite interpretation; all arrows to global RH remain unproved.

22. **Test nearby false statements.** Compare the actual interventions declared in this protocol; do not relabel predictable controls as discoveries.

23. **Classify boundaries.** Distinguish escaped Newton starts, unresolved access labels, unclassified endpoints, incomplete census and successfully validated finite observations.

24. **Extract positive residue.** Use each failure to isolate the surviving connection, component or missing mechanism described in REPORT.md. Do not claim a formalized theorem.

Big-picture interpretation, limits and positive residue are stated in REPORT.md. No universal-shadow conjecture is promoted from finite numerical evidence. The ontological change is the inverse-branch viewpoint; it is a research representation, not a new universal law.
