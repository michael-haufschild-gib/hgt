D. method ledger for scientific-method moves
1 split: entry/on-rate and off-hazard/lifetime are reported separately.
2 attack-false: high occupancy alone is insufficient; true often has lower entry but longer survival.
3 reverse: finite controls can match occupancy but not long-run survival at sigma=0.3.
4 viewpoint: pointwise Delta is re-represented as a run-length survival process.
5 visual: proofreading depth becomes the length of a visible uninterrupted positive-defect run.
6 action: repeated rejection tests phase persistence, not amplitude size.
7 essentials: binary positive-defect runs are enough for the lifetime discriminator.
8 similar objects: finite Euler and random-phase controls are the similar objects.
9 small cases: finite prime_count=30 already has short-run behavior.
10 extremes: threshold=0.5 and sigma=1.2 are boundary stresses.
11 experiments: threshold, sigma, and prime-count sweeps are the probes.
12 invariant: true left-strip survival-depth advantage persists across windows.
13 monotone: survival advantage decreases moving right in sigma.
14 weaken: claim only left-strip lifetime/off-rate advantage.
15 strengthen: universal-depth or right-strip claim is false.
16 minimal counterexample: sigma=1.2 gives true and finite similar lifetimes.
17 representation: run-length survival is the required representation change.
18 theorem-tool resemblance: needs a persistence/hazard bound for phase-defect sign.
19 fake-proof gap: positive median Delta does not imply long-run survival without dependence control.
20 missing lemma: left-strip positive-defect run-length tail lower bound for true zeta.
21 dependency: prove entry/off decomposition, then survival-tail separation, then finite-control bound.
22 nearby false: more amplitude or more finite primes does not create true-like long survival.
23 boundary cases: left strip survives; right strip and high thresholds delimit the claim.
